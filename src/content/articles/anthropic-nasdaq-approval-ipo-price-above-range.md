Anthropic has been cleared to list its shares on the Nasdaq stock exchange under the ticker symbol ANT, according to a regulatory filing on Tuesday, taking the artificial-intelligence company a step closer to one of the largest public offerings on record — with reports suggesting it could price above its marketed range.

The Economic Times reported the approval, citing the filing, and said the proposed price range of $70 to $78 a share is unchanged. The offering covers 825 million Class A shares. At the top of the range it would raise about $64 billion, with a further $26 billion coming through a concurrent private placement.

Demand has been running far ahead of supply. Bloomberg News reported that bids for the shares total nearly seven times the number on offer, and that Anthropic is giving priority to existing investors when allocating shares, meaning some new buyers may get smaller holdings than they asked for.

CNBC reported on Tuesday, citing multiple sources, that Anthropic is likely to price above the $70-to-$78 range. The company planned to close its order books at 4 p.m. Eastern Time on Tuesday to lock in total demand, with the final price expected to be set on Wednesday and trading to begin on Thursday.

At $78 a share, Anthropic would be valued at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis — up from the $965 billion valuation set in a private funding round in May.

The filing shows that growth in stark terms. Revenue for the nine months to the end of September reached $33.24 billion, roughly 1,200 percent higher than the same period last year, driven in large part by demand for Claude Code, its coding tool. The annualised revenue run rate hit $81 billion by October 31, up $10 billion in a single month.

The same filing shows the other side of the ledger. Anthropic posted a net loss of $104 billion over the nine months, against $25.1 billion a year earlier, largely from changes in the value of convertible notes and derivatives as its valuation rose. It also faces about $71 billion of commitments falling due in the year to September 30, 2027, mostly for computing capacity and semiconductors.

The private placement is part of the same bet: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC have committed to buy $26 billion of a separate class of shares at the final IPO price. The money is earmarked for general corporate purposes, including computing capacity, AI infrastructure and hiring.

Anthropic has been courting investors in the run-up to pricing, including a livestreamed conversation on Friday between chief executive Dario Amodei and JPMorgan chief Jamie Dimon. Billionaire investor Bill Ackman, who attended in person, told Bloomberg he was impressed, calling Amodei "a very thoughtful, measured CEO."

Nothing is final until Wednesday evening, when the price is set. But with the books nearly seven times covered, the year's biggest AI listing is already taking shape.

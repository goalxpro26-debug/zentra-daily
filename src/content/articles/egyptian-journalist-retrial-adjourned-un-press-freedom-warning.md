A criminal court in Cairo has adjourned the retrial of Lina Attalah, editor-in-chief of Egypt's leading independent news site Mada Masr, until 18 November, after her lawyers asked for the presiding judge to step aside.

The defence argued the judge had already made up his mind against her, Mada Masr said, citing its lawyer Hassan al-Azhar. The judge took a recess at Wednesday's opening hearing and then adjourned proceedings for a week to consider the request.

## A fine, a shutdown order — and a sentence she did not know about

The retrial stems from a case in which Attalah was sentenced in her absence in 2024 to a year in prison for spreading false news and running a website without a licence. She only learned of that sentence after last month's separate trial, even though she was in Egypt when it was issued. She is now being retried in that case before the same judge.

Last month, the New Cairo Misdemeanours Court convicted her of operating a website without a licence — a licence she said she had tried for years to obtain without success. It fined her 3 million Egyptian pounds (about $56,000) and ordered Mada Masr to close. Her appeal against that conviction is due to be heard on 25 November.

## The UN warning

On Tuesday, the United Nations human rights office warned of "widening assaults on press freedom by Egyptian authorities, including the targeting, persecution and harassment of independent journalists." Its spokesperson, Thameen Al-Kheetan, said Attalah's case raised fresh concerns about due process and a fair trial, and called on Egypt to end the harassment of journalists and media outlets and to overturn the measures suppressing Mada Masr and the fact-checking platform Matsda2sh.

In September, six journalists from Matsda2sh — the platform's entire newsroom — were arrested in home raids and held incommunicado for nearly a week. They have since been charged with joining the Muslim Brotherhood and financing terrorism. Their lawyers say several were beaten and subjected to electric shocks in detention, and that prosecutors have presented no evidence linking them to the Brotherhood.

## A shrinking space for independent news

Attalah told the BBC the crackdown was meant to "send a chilling message" to "the very few newsrooms that are left." Mada Masr was set up 13 years ago and is widely regarded as Egypt's leading independent news outlet.

Egypt ranks 169th out of 180 countries in Reporters Without Borders' 2026 press freedom index, which says at least 26 journalists arrested in recent years remain in detention.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet known whether the court will agree to replace the judge when the hearing resumes on 18 November, or how the appeal on 25 November will rule. The charges against the Matsda2sh journalists have not yet been tested in court.

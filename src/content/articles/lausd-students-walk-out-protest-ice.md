More than 7,000 students in the Los Angeles Unified School District walked out of class on Monday, November 16, 2026, to protest federal immigration enforcement operations and demand stronger protections for immigrant families.

Organisers had expected students from around 60 schools to take part, and reports indicated some schools outside the district joined as well. About 200 students walked out at Thomas Jefferson Senior High School in South Los Angeles, and dozens more at Huntington Park High School. The district itself did not immediately provide participation figures; the estimate of more than 7,000 came from a school police source.

Hundreds of students reached Los Angeles City Hall in downtown Los Angeles and marched through the surrounding streets for much of the day. More than 100 others reached Huntington Park City Hall in south-east Los Angeles County before continuing on to Pacific Boulevard.

The students called for an end to all Immigration and Customs Enforcement operations and for the city and the school district to do more to protect immigrant communities. A central demand was the establishment and funding of campus "dream centers" on every campus — spaces that provide food, clothing, information on immigration law, mental health support and room for student organising. Some students at Bravo Medical Magnet High School also used the walkout to oppose reinstating the SAT and ACT as requirements for University of California admissions.

The district already operates 19 dream centers on high school campuses, funded by $5 million approved by the Los Angeles Board of Education in June 2025. In recent months it has also offered know-your-rights training, home-to-school bus pickups, alerts when immigration agents are reported near campuses, and neighbourhood patrols by staff and volunteers.

The march was described as largely peaceful, though a brief brawl broke out near the downtown Federal Building. Those involved and what caused it were not immediately clear.

Students taking part cited fear for undocumented relatives, against a backdrop of record immigration arrests and deportations reported under the Trump administration. It was not the first such action this year: thousands of students walked out in January and February over similar fears.

## What is still unknown

No final participation count has been released, and the district has not said whether it will expand the dream center programme to every campus, as the students demanded. It is also unclear whether students who left class without permission will face any consequences, though California law allows an excused absence for a civic or political event when a school is notified in advance.

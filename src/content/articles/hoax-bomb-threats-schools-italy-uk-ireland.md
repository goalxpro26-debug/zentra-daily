Thousands of schools across Italy, the United Kingdom and Ireland received threatening emails on Wednesday, forcing more than 300 Italian schools to evacuate as a precaution. Police in all three countries quickly assessed the messages as hoaxes, and no credible danger was found.

In Italy, the disruption was the widest. Schools in Rome, Naples, Florence, Pisa, Trieste and Vicenza were among those to receive the emails, and pupils were sent home while police checked the buildings. Parents were called to collect their children, and classes were interrupted for much of the morning.

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said the alerts posed no danger and that police and other public bodies were monitoring the situation. Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara told Italian television that about 300 schools had received the threats, and described them as presumed hoaxes linked to cyberattacks rather than a genuine risk.

## The UK and Ireland were hit too

The same wave reached schools well beyond Italy. Police Scotland said at least 470 schools received the email, while more than 300 schools in Northern Ireland reported the same message. Police in the Republic of Ireland said a generic message had been sent to a number of schools and assessed it as not credible, advising against evacuations.

The Police Service of Northern Ireland said the email originated outside the United Kingdom and was being treated as a hoax. "Our enquiries are ongoing, however, at this stage we consider risk to the public as low," Assistant Chief Constable Anthony McNally said.

Police Scotland likewise told schools that the email alone did not warrant evacuation or keeping children at home, though a small number of schools activated their emergency plans anyway. Pupils at Queensferry High School in Edinburgh were sent home, and a primary school in County Down evacuated children to a nearby church.

## A familiar signature, no attribution

A copy of the message seen by Reuters was signed "Mikhail Andropov, leader of the Vilayat of Misoginistan," and included a threat against Prime Minister Meloni. The same purported author claimed responsibility for email bomb threats sent on Friday to newsrooms in Ireland and Bulgaria.

Italian Defence Minister Guido Crosetto said Wednesday's threats were part of a clear strategy "to spread fear and create anxiety," according to the Adnkronos news agency. But authorities have not attributed the emails to any group or state, and no evidence has emerged linking them to a government.

The incident is the latest in a series of threats and cyberattacks targeting infrastructure and organisations across Europe, including supermarkets, blood banks, air traffic and television broadcasters. Northern Ireland schools were hit by a similar hoax wave last month, when more than 300 schools received fake bomb threats.

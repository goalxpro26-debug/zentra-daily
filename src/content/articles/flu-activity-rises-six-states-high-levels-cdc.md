The US flu season is off to an unusually early start, and it is gathering speed. New data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention shows six states now at high or very high levels of flu activity, with illness spreading fastest across the South and West.

Five states — California, Tennessee, Oregon, South Carolina and Texas — are in the CDC's high-activity category. Louisiana stands alone in the very-high category. Three more states — New York, Massachusetts and Hawaii — are at moderate activity. The figures come from the CDC's weekly FluView update for the week ending November 7.

The agency said seasonal flu activity is higher than typically observed at this time of year, and that it continues to increase in many parts of the country. About 11% of flu specimens tested at clinical laboratories were positive in the week ending November 7, and that share was still rising. More than 3% of doctors' office visits were for flu-like symptoms.

The CDC's first estimates of the season put the toll so far at at least 2.6 million illnesses, 36,000 hospitalizations and 2,100 deaths. Five flu-associated deaths among children have been reported this season. The most frequently reported virus in the week ending November 7 was influenza A(H1N1)pdm09, a descendant of the strain behind the 2009 swine flu pandemic that has circulated seasonally ever since.

Health experts cautioned that the early surge is real but its ultimate severity is not yet known. The CDC noted that an earlier increase in activity does not necessarily lead to a higher seasonal peak or greater overall severity, and said it is too early to know which flu viruses will dominate this season. In an October media briefing, Dr. Anna Durbin of Johns Hopkins University said the flu hospitalization rate in mid-October was the highest recorded that early in autumn in more than 15 years.

Dr. Fred Lopez, an infectious diseases specialist at the LSU Health Sciences Center in New Orleans, said the season was taking off and was only likely to increase over the next month or two. Louisiana, where he practices, is seeing the worst of it so far.

The CDC recommends an annual flu vaccine for everyone aged six months and older, and says prescription antiviral drugs should be started as early as possible, especially for people at higher risk of complications.

Grand Theft Auto VI took in $3.79 billion in retail sales in its first 24 hours, developer Rockstar Games said on Friday, making it the biggest entertainment launch in history by a wide margin.

The figure, announced by Rockstar and its parent company Take-Two Interactive, covers sales through the end of Thursday, November 19, the game's first full day on sale worldwide. It sets a record for any entertainment release across any medium — ahead of the biggest film openings and far ahead of any previous video game launch.

The game also sold 32.5 million copies before its official release, a record for pre-orders across all platforms, according to the companies. The launch comes after thirteen years of anticipation since the previous entry in the series, and the game earned a near-perfect score of 99 on review aggregator Metacritic.

The official total is considerably higher than the estimates that circulated on launch day. Analytics firm Sensor Tower had put first-day sales at about $2.36 billion, based on 24.58 million copies sold by Thursday evening. The Rockstar figure is the company's own estimate of what shoppers spent at retail, and it includes the record pre-order volume that built up before release.

For comparison, Grand Theft Auto V, the previous game in the series, sold about $800 million worth of copies on its first day in 2013 — then a record for the industry. GTA VI has nearly tripled the number of copies its predecessor sold in its first 24 hours. The $3.79 billion total is also more than the highest-grossing film in history has earned across its entire theatrical run.

The scale of the launch reflects one of the most expensive entertainment projects ever made, with production costs estimated at $1 billion or more. The game went on sale for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, and Take-Two said demand had been unprecedented since pre-orders opened.

Rockstar president and founder Sam Houser said the studio was grateful to players as it begins a new chapter with the release. Attention now turns to what follows: investors are watching for Take-Two's plans for a PC version and for the game's online multiplayer mode, which analysts expect in 2027 and which could turn a one-off purchase into a longer-running revenue stream.

For now, the first-day numbers alone put GTA VI in a league of its own — a launch thirteen years in the making, and one that has already rewritten the record books for the entertainment industry.

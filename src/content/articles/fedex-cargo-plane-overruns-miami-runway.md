A FedEx cargo jet ran off the end of a runway at Miami International Airport on Wednesday evening, forcing the airport to close all but one of its runways on the eve of Thanksgiving, one of the busiest travel days of the year. No one was hurt.

The aircraft, a McDonnell Douglas MD-11 operating as FedEx Flight 910 from Memphis, overran runway 26R at about 8:40 p.m. local time, according to the airport. It came to a stop roughly 1,000 feet past the end of the runway, on grass near the airport's perimeter fence and short of a public road. The two crew members on board were unhurt and walked off the plane.

FedEx said the aircraft had "an issue upon landing" and that the company is cooperating with investigators. The Federal Aviation Administration said it will investigate the incident.

The disruption rippled through the airport quickly. The FAA issued a ground stop for flights heading to Miami, and all but one runway — 9R — was temporarily closed while emergency crews responded. Flights began moving again later in the evening, with the ground stop lifted at about 10:30 p.m. The timing was unwelcome: more than 500 flights at Miami had already been delayed before the incident, according to FlightAware figures, as millions of Americans travelled for the holiday.

Weather is likely to be part of the investigation. Thunderstorms and heavy rain were moving through the Miami area at the time, and a flood watch was in effect. Air Cargo News, citing meteorological data from the airport, reported rain and crosswinds around the time of the landing. What exactly caused the jet to run long remains unknown.

The incident is the second time in about three months that a cargo jet has overshot a runway at Miami. In September, an Amazon cargo jet overran a different runway, crossed a road and struck a van carrying a plane-cleaning crew, killing five people on the ground. Wednesday's jet landed on a different runway, but three of Miami's runways converge at the airport's west end, so it ended up close to the same area.

For travellers, the episode was a reminder of how quickly a single incident can snarl a holiday. For investigators, the focus now turns to the runway, the weather and the aircraft — and to why Miami, of all airports, has seen this happen twice in a season.

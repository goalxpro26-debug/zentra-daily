Kyle Larson is a three-time NASCAR Cup Series champion. The 34-year-old from Elk Grove, California, clinched the 2026 title early in the season finale at Homestead-Miami Speedway on Sunday, November 8, and he did it in the most Larson way possible: by going to the front at a track where he has led more laps than any other driver.

Larson entered the weekend with a comfortable lead over Christopher Bell and Denny Hamlin. All he needed was a handful of stage points — or, without them, a finish of 28th or better — to secure the championship. He left no doubt. He finished fourth in Stage 1 of the 400-mile finale, scoring seven stage points that put Hamlin and Bell out of reach. From that moment, the championship was his, whatever happened in the remaining laps.

Ryan Blaney won the race itself, capturing his fourth victory of the season for Team Penske after a chaotic restart on lap 260 carried him to the lead, which he held to the finish. Tyler Reddick finished second, followed by William Byron, Christopher Bell, Bubba Wallace and Denny Hamlin. Larson finished 12th, a result made mostly moot by the early clinch — but the points told the story of his season: he ended 49 points ahead of Bell and 56 ahead of Hamlin.

## A champion at his best track

Homestead-Miami Speedway has long been Larson's place. It is, by the reporting of the day, arguably his best track on the circuit, and he drove it that way on Sunday, putting his No. 5 Hendrick Motorsports Chevrolet to the front and clinching the title before the race was even half run. There is a particular kind of confidence in a driver who can win a championship in the first stage of the final race, and Larson has now done it in the revived Chase format for the first time in his career.

The title is Larson's third in six years, and it makes him the first back-to-back champion since Jimmie Johnson in 2010 — Johnson won five in a row beginning in 2006. Larson is now the 11th driver in NASCAR history to own at least three Cup Series titles, joining a list that includes seven-time champions Richard Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Johnson. That is the company a third title buys, and it is not given lightly.

Larson's path to this championship was not a straight line. His career has included early struggles and the kind of setbacks that end lesser drivers' title ambitions. The 2026 season, though, was built on the consistency that championships require: the stage points, the finishes when the car was not at its best, and the ability to be at the front when it mattered most. He entered the finale as the overwhelming favourite to lift the Bill France Cup, with a 67-point lead over Bell, and he needed only nine points to wrap up the title. He got them in the first stage.

## The race behind the championship

While Larson's title was decided early, the race itself still had to be run, and Blaney made it worth watching. The Team Penske driver raced to the lead after the lap-260 restart and held it to the chequered flag, winning the season finale and finishing fourth in the final points standings. Reddick, driving for 23XI Racing — the team co-owned by NBA legend Michael Jordan, who watched from pit road — finished second, with Byron third.

For Bell and Hamlin, the two drivers who arrived at Homestead with a mathematical chance of stopping Larson, the day ended in the only way the arithmetic allowed once Stage 1 was over. Bell finished fourth in the race and second in the points; Hamlin finished sixth in the race. Both had strong seasons. Neither had an answer for Larson's points lead.

The finale also closed out the lower series. Kaden Honeycutt won the Truck Series championship at Homestead, and Justin Allgaier, racing for Dale Earnhardt Jr.'s team, won his second O'Reilly Auto Parts Series title on Saturday night by placing seventh. It was a full weekend of championships at Homestead, with Larson's the headline.

## What three titles mean

Three championships in six years is a statement. In NASCAR's modern era, sustained excellence is hard: the cars change, the formats change, the competition is deep, and a single bad race in the playoffs can end a season. Larson has now navigated that gauntlet three times, and the back-to-back nature of this latest title — his second consecutive — puts him in the conversation with the sport's greats rather than merely its good drivers.

The question now is what comes next. At 34, Larson is in the prime of a driver's career, with a team in Hendrick Motorsports that has shown it can give him championship-winning cars year after year. A fourth title would put him in still rarer company. For now, though, the 2026 season belongs to him: clinched in Stage 1, celebrated at Homestead, and sealed with a points margin that left no argument about who the best driver of the year was.

Britain has imposed sanctions on eight Israeli construction companies involved in building settlements in the occupied West Bank, in a move London says is aimed at protecting the two-state solution.

The measures, announced on Tuesday by Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, include asset freezes and disqualifications for company directors. Britain said the action would disrupt the firms' access to finance and support for further construction in the West Bank.

Among the companies named are Har Hevron Development Company Ltd, Hemdat Shomron Ltd, and S.A.G. (Velvel) Construction and Development Ltd, which build and develop homes in the territory.

## Timed to the E1 tender

The announcement came shortly before the final deadlines for the E1 tender, a settlement project that would bisect the Israeli-occupied West Bank and cut it off from East Jerusalem, fragmenting land Palestinians hope will form the heart of a future state.

Miliband said protecting the two-state solution was the guiding principle of the government's approach, and warned companies against taking part in the project: "do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution."

## Aid-blocking group also sanctioned

Britain also sanctioned Tzav 9, an Israeli activist group the government said had regularly blocked humanitarian aid trucks carrying food, water and fuel to the Gaza Strip.

The move drew a sharp response from Israel. Foreign Minister Gideon Saar described the sanctions as a hostile act against private, law-abiding companies and said Israel would respond with countermeasures.

The Palestinian Authority welcomed the decision and called on other countries to take similar action.

The sanctions are Britain's first targeting Israeli firms working in the West Bank, and they come as international pressure grows over settlement expansion, which most countries consider illegal under international law — a position Israel disputes.

Two passenger trains collided south of Cairo on Sunday, killing seven people and injuring at least 125, Egyptian authorities said. The conductor of one of the trains was among those killed.

The crash happened in al-Ayyat district in Giza province, about 52 kilometres (32 miles) south of the capital. According to the Transportation Ministry, a train travelling from Giza to the southwestern province of Fayoum ran into the rear of a train heading from Cairo to the southern city of Aswan. Several carriages derailed in the impact.

Egypt's Health Ministry gave the casualty toll in a statement, saying more than 70 of the injured had already been discharged after receiving treatment. Earlier reports had put the number of injured at 112, before the ministry updated the figure to at least 125 as hospitals continued to treat passengers.

## What investigators found

The Chief Prosecutor's office said its investigators reached the site late on Sunday. A preliminary inspection indicated that the Giza–Fayoum train struck the last carriage of the Cairo–Aswan train, which was stationary on the same track and travelling in the same direction, officials said.

Residents of Kafr Girza, near the crash site, rushed to help before emergency crews arrived, pulling survivors from the wreckage and calling ambulances. One resident, Ayman Gamal, said he saw around 80 injured people taken away by ambulance. Some passengers were trapped inside a carriage of the stationary train and were dead when they were pulled out, he said.

Survivors taken to al-Wasta Central Hospital in Beni Suef province described passengers in shock, struggling to breathe in the dust thrown up by the collision. Farha Gamal, a survivor who had been on the Fayoum train, said some passengers appeared dead while others struggled to find their way out of the carriage. "There were other cars that were completely mangled," she said.

Emergency teams and cranes were sent to the scene to clear the wreckage and restore services on the line connecting Cairo with Upper Egypt, the Egyptian Railway Authority said.

## Investigation ordered

President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi said in a social media post that he had ordered a prompt investigation into the crash. The public prosecutor's office has opened an inquiry to determine the technical reasons behind the collision.

No final account of the cause has been announced. Authorities have not said whether anyone has been detained or held responsible.

## A troubled rail record

Train derailments and crashes are a recurring problem in Egypt, where the railway system has a long history of mismanagement and poor maintenance. The government has announced modernisation programmes in recent years, but serious accidents continue.

Last September, a train driver and his assistant were injured after two freight trains collided in the same district. Egypt's deadliest rail disaster was in 2002, when more than 300 people were killed after a fire broke out on an overnight train travelling from Cairo to southern Egypt.

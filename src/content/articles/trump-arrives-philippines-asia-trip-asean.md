President Donald Trump arrived in the Philippines on Tuesday, opening a three-day Asia trip that mixes trade deals and alliance meetings with a planned three-way summit in China alongside Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin.

Manila, the Philippine capital, is the first stop. The visit is Trump's first travel abroad since his party suffered deep losses in the midterm elections, and it comes as the White House contends with the fallout from the departure of Nvidia chief Jensen Huang and a major disruption to the United States aviation industry.

After landing, Trump met the leaders of the Quad — the United States, Australia, India and Japan — for the first time since his return to the White House. The group will conduct an exercise featuring maritime patrol aircraft and launch an accelerator to speed the development of artificial intelligence, the president announced.

On Tuesday evening, Trump is the guest of honour at a gala for the annual summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations. Before the dinner, he will join a special summit with ASEAN leaders to discuss deeper trade and security ties, the long-running violence in Myanmar and disputes in the South China Sea, which Beijing claims almost in its entirety. ASEAN leaders have been working on a non-aggression pact with China over the contested waters, where clashes between Chinese and Philippine forces have escalated in recent years.

## Trade deals on the agenda

Trump is scheduled to sign a trade deal with Thailand, one of the agreements he has pursued since launching a trade war last year. Under a framework announced last October, Thailand would remove tariffs on most American goods, while spending more than $18 billion on American aircraft and buying farm goods and liquefied natural gas.

The deal follows a winding path. The United States Supreme Court overturned in February the 19 per cent tariff Trump had placed on goods from Thailand; the administration then sought replacement tariffs in July, levying a 12.5 per cent tax on Thai imports for not taking steps to oppose the use of forced labour.

## Protests in Manila

About 1,500 left-wing protesters held a noisy rally in a downtown Manila square ahead of Trump's arrival, calling on Philippine leaders not to take their foreign policy cues from Washington. They waved red flags and held a large streamer reading "Resist U.S. war and plunder!" and planned to burn an effigy of Trump, as riot police with shields and wooden truncheons stood nearby. Nearly 23,000 police personnel and other law enforcers, including SWAT teams, were deployed to secure the ASEAN summit.

On Wednesday, Trump receives a red-carpet welcome and full military honours from President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., followed by a state banquet at the Malacañang presidential palace. From Manila, he heads to Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit and the three-way nuclear talks with Xi and Putin. He has also said a meeting with North Korea's leader Kim Jong Un is possible, though nothing has been confirmed.

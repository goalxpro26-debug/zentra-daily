French police searched the Ritz hotel in Paris and two other properties linked to the late billionaire Mohamed Al Fayed in mid-October, the Paris prosecutor's office confirmed on Tuesday, as part of an investigation into an alleged trafficking network.

The searches took place on October 13 and 14 at the Ritz in Paris, at Villa Windsor in the city's 16th arrondissement, and at Villa Sainte-Thérèse in Saint-Tropez on the French Riviera. Officers from London's Metropolitan Police were present during the operation in France, though they did not take part directly. The French investigation is being conducted in coordination with British authorities.

Several people were taken into police custody for questioning following the searches and have since been released pending further investigation. Investigators seized computers, phones and other digital material, which prosecutors said is being analysed. No current member of Ritz staff was arrested, according to The Observer.

## An inquiry into alleged accomplices

The French investigation was opened in the summer of 2025 into allegations of aggravated human trafficking, procuring prostitutes and rape. Its focus is on identifying possible accomplices of Mohamed Al Fayed and his late brother, Salah Al Fayed, rather than on the businessman himself, who died in 2023 aged 94 without ever being charged.

France's specialist anti-trafficking unit has interviewed at least five complainants. Victims cited the Ritz and Villa Windsor as locations where alleged abuse took place, according to prosecutors.

The inquiry runs parallel to a British investigation. More than 20 women told the BBC in 2024 that Al Fayed had sexually abused them, some alleging rape, and Harrods — which he owned until 2010 — apologised the same year. Since the BBC documentary aired, more than 400 allegations of sexual misconduct have been made against Mohamed Al Fayed and his brother, and at least 157 people have reported allegations directly to the Metropolitan Police taskforce, known as Operation Cornpoppy.

## What remains unknown

No charges have been announced in the French case, and prosecutors have not disclosed what the seized digital material contains. The searches themselves do not establish that anyone assisted the alleged offending.

The Ritz has condemned the alleged behaviour. The investigation continues under the supervision of the Paris prosecutor's office.

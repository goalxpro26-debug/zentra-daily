The scandal that ended Jensen Huang's 33-year run at Nvidia has exposed a backdoor route that has kept advanced American AI chips flowing to China despite years of US export controls.

At the centre of the story is Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based company that in three years went from an inactive offshoot of a Chinese gaming firm to one of Asia's biggest buyers of cutting-edge US semiconductors. Trade records show it has bought more than $4 billion in chips and equipment from Nvidia since 2023.

US officials suspect Megaspeed of exploiting a gap in the controls Washington imposed in 2022 to stop China's access to its most advanced chips. Instead of selling chips onward, Megaspeed builds data centres across Southeast Asia and rents out the computing power. "If you put me on the restricted list to drive a Ferrari… I can just go to a car dealership and rent a Ferrari," said Alex Capri of the National University of Singapore. "It's the car dealership that you have to control."

The scale of the wider problem is hard to pin down, but researchers estimate that between 290,000 and 1.6 million advanced US semiconductors reached China in violation of export controls last year. Blacklisted buyers have kept obtaining chips directly: Northwestern Polytechnical University, on the US Entity List since 2001, bought four H200 chips in October for $250,000, according to procurement records on its own website that have since been deleted.

Megaspeed is also at the heart of the scandal that cost Huang his job. He resigned on Thursday after admitting to an affair with Alice Huang — no relation — a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials believe to be a Chinese intelligence asset. Beijing calls the claim groundless, and US intelligence has not established that she acted as a spy. The FBI has investigated for at least a year without reaching conclusions. Megaspeed says it has cooperated with Commerce Department inspections and hired a US law firm to verify its compliance; it says Alice Huang left the company in August 2025.

The case has also exposed a rift in Washington. Megaspeed has not been placed on the Entity List or subjected to purchase limits — a decision by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick that led to a confrontation with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent last week, CNN reported. Lawmakers are now demanding greater oversight, with Huang called to testify behind closed doors on December 1 and Lutnick due before the Senate Banking Committee next month.

Nvidia's shares fell 12.75 per cent in the week to Friday's close, wiping out about $573 billion in market value. Finance chief Colette Kress has been named interim chief executive.

What happens next is unclear. Singapore is investigating Megaspeed and its controllers over possible false declarations about server purchases and their final destinations, and Taiwan is moving to criminalise unauthorised exports of advanced AI chips to China. The scrutiny comes days before President Donald Trump is due to meet Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

The San Francisco Bay Area is bracing for the highest king tides in 18 years, with forecasters warning of major to extreme coastal flooding through Thanksgiving week as unusually high tides combine with a storm and a surge of warm El Nino water.

The National Weather Service has issued a Coastal Flood Warning for the San Francisco and San Pablo Bays from 6 a.m. Monday, November 23, through 4 p.m. Thursday, November 26. A Coastal Flood Advisory covers the Pacific coast through Thursday. The warning means flooding of low-lying roads, trails, parking areas and other shoreline infrastructure is expected, along with coastal erosion.

The tides are driven by a rare alignment: the monthly king tide cycle, the strongest El Nino conditions in years, and warmer ocean water that lifts sea levels through thermal expansion. At the San Francisco tide gauge, high tide is expected to reach about 7.8 feet on Sunday, 8.0 feet on Monday, 8.4 feet on Tuesday, 8.6 feet on Wednesday and 8.2 feet on Thursday. The U.S. Coast Guard said water levels could run 1 to 2 feet above normal high tides, and higher where storm surge adds to the peak.

A second storm arriving Tuesday and Wednesday will make matters worse. Forecasters expect 1 to 2 inches of rain in the North Bay and up to an inch across much of the Bay Area, with runoff draining into bays already at historically high levels. The storm will also bring hazardous beach conditions, with breaking waves of 14 to 18 feet on Tuesday building to 18 to 24 feet on Wednesday, along with sneaker waves and strong rip currents.

Local agencies are already preparing. The Southern Marin Fire Protection District urged residents and businesses to protect property, review emergency plans and prepare for road closures, warning that moderate flooding is expected this weekend before major to extreme flooding develops during Thanksgiving week.

The timing adds pressure to one of the busiest travel periods of the year. The Weather Service said the storm and the flooding could delay travel on roads, railways and flights, and officials have urged residents to reconsider non-essential travel along the coast. The Coast Guard advised mariners and beachgoers to stay clear of the water, warning that king tides can bring dangerous surf and sneaker waves that catch people off guard.

The king tide season is only beginning. The Coast Guard said the highest tides of the season are expected around December 22 to 25, when observed heights could reach 7.8 to 8.2 feet, with another cycle following in late January.

Pete Hegseth's former chief of staff has publicly called on the defence secretary to resign, saying Hegseth has lost the ability to lead the Pentagon effectively.

Joe Kasper, who served as Hegseth's chief of staff during the first three months of the Trump administration, made the call in an interview with Politico published on Monday. He said Hegseth's capacity to do the job was nearly exhausted, and that it was time for him to step aside.

Kasper criticised Hegseth's handling of the war in Iran, his dismissal of dozens of senior military leaders, and his blocking of promotions for military officers. He also said Hegseth's standing with Congress had collapsed, arguing that the secretary had destroyed his relationships on Capitol Hill and would soon find himself unable to function in the role.

Kasper said he decided to speak out after an incident earlier this month at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery, where Hegseth and a group of his advisers were rebuked by an Army sentinel for breaking decorum. A day later, Hegseth awarded the soldier, Spc. Thomas Iglesias, the Joint Service Commendation Medal for carrying out his duties.

Kasper, an Air Force veteran, left the chief of staff post in April 2025 and briefly remained at the Pentagon as a special government employee. During his tenure he oversaw the dismissal of three senior Pentagon officials, senior adviser Dan Caldwell, former deputy chief of staff Darin Selnick and Colin Carroll, a former chief of staff to the deputy secretary of defence, who were accused of leaking sensitive information to the news media. All three denied the accusations, and an administration official later told The Hill that Caldwell had been investigated and cleared, with no evidence released to suggest he had leaked anything.

The Pentagon did not immediately respond to The Hill's request for comment. President Donald Trump has continued to stand by Hegseth, praising him for removing generals he described as "woke". The Hill also reported that Trump's decision to elevate adviser Stephen Miller to White House chief of staff is likely to help Hegseth, given the close relationship between the two men.

Kasper's intervention comes shortly after Hegseth survived an impeachment effort in the House led by outgoing Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican. The Hill previously reported that the failed effort followed the president asking allies and advisers about Hegseth.

It is not yet clear whether Kasper's criticism will change anything. Trump has shown no sign of dropping Hegseth, and the secretary himself has not responded directly to his former aide's call to quit.

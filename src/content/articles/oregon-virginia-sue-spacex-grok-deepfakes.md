Virginia and Oregon filed lawsuits on Monday against SpaceX's AI division, alleging its Grok chatbot generated non-consensual sexual images — including images depicting children — while the company misled users about how safe the tool was.

The two states are the first to sue over Grok, though several other attorneys general had already announced investigations. Both cases were filed under consumer protection laws and seek damages, penalties and court orders requiring the company to stop breaking state law.

At the centre of the complaints is a feature in Grok's image tools known as "spicy mode", which the lawsuits say lets a user turn an existing photograph of a person into a sexual or nude image or video. The states say SpaceX promoted it to attract users and gain ground on rivals, even though its safeguards were not strong enough to prevent abuse.

Virginia's complaint cites Center for Countering Digital Hate research estimating that between December 2025 and January 2026, Grok generated and posted millions of sexualised images on X, including more than 20,000 depicting children.

"In the absence of federal action, AGs have stepped into the breach," Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones told Reuters before the filings, describing state attorneys general as a regulatory force in a lightly regulated industry.

Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said the lack of needed safety measures "was considered a feature, not a bug". Virginia is asking for $2,500 for each wilful violation of its consumer protection law. Its case was filed in Richmond; Oregon's in Multnomah County.

The suits name SpaceX, the social platform X and SpaceX Super Intelligence, the AI subsidiary that now owns Grok. SpaceX acquired xAI, Grok's original developer, in February and renamed the AI division in October.

In a statement, SpaceX Super Intelligence said sexual content involving minors, whether real or AI-generated, "is abhorrent. It has no place on X or in Grok, and neither do explicit images of real people made without their consent."

The suits follow a backlash that began in January, after Grok's image-editing tools were opened to all users in December and it became clear they could create realistic fake sexual images of real people. The company tightened restrictions in mid-January, but Reuters reported in early February that Grok was still producing such images even when users said the subjects had not consented.

SpaceX already faces more than a dozen lawsuits from individuals who say Grok generated non-consensual sexual images of them, as minors or adults. In Britain, lawmaker Jess Asato is suing the company over sexualised images users generated of her; the company denies those claims. SpaceX is also suing at least two people in the US, alleging they misused Grok to create child sexual abuse material.

The two states say they worked together and hired the same outside firm to help litigate the cases. The filings mark an escalation: regulators are no longer just investigating AI image tools — they are asking judges to hold their makers to account.

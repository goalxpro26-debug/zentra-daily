The COP31 climate summit collapsed on Sunday in Antalya, Turkey, after more than two weeks of negotiations ended without an overarching agreement — the first time United Nations climate talks have broken down without a deal since Copenhagen in 2009.

At the closing plenary, COP31 president Murat Kurum conceded that the draft "Antalya Pact" could not be adopted. "It is clear that there are parties in this hall who oppose this text. We recognize that there is no consensus on this document," he told delegates. The summit had already run a record 44 hours past its scheduled Friday close, making it the longest climate conference ever held.

Fossil fuels were the breaking point. The first draft of the agreement, published on Monday, had called for an "immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems." But every reference to coal, oil and gas was stripped from later versions after objections from Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers, despite a push by more than 100 countries to keep the language in.

The European Union, Panama, the Marshall Islands and Norway were among those that refused to endorse the weakened text. Ireland's climate minister Darragh O'Brien, speaking for the EU, said the bloc "will not endorse a cover decision, or an Antalya Pact, that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change." "We will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuel," he added.

The draft also backed a major expansion of carbon capture technology, with a goal of capturing and storing one billion metric tons of carbon, and dropped a $120 billion target for money to help poorer countries adapt to climate change. The United States was not part of the talks, after the Trump administration pulled out of international climate action.

The failure stands in sharp contrast to COP28 in Dubai in 2023, when countries agreed for the first time to "transition away from fossil fuels." Delegates in Antalya did make progress on smaller technical matters, but without a cover decision the summit leaves the central question — how fast the world moves away from the fuels driving warming — unanswered, with the 1.5C target under the Paris agreement looming ever closer.

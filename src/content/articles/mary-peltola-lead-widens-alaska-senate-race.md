Democrat Mary Peltola widened her lead over Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska's Senate race on Friday, as the latest release of ballots moved her closer to a majority — but the contest, the last uncalled Senate race in the country, is still not decided.

With about 96 percent of expected ballots counted, according to the Associated Press, Peltola held 138,240 votes, or 49.8 percent, against 127,828 votes, or 46.1 percent, for Sullivan — a lead of 10,412 votes. Friday's update added roughly 22,500 ballots to the count, and Peltola's margin has grown with each release since election night, when she led by just a few hundred votes.

The race remains uncalled. Under Alaska's ranked-choice system, if neither candidate finishes with more than 50 percent of first-choice votes, the lowest-placed candidates are eliminated and their supporters' next preferences are counted. That official tabulation is scheduled for November 18, after the deadline for ballots mailed from overseas to arrive, and the state aims to certify the result by November 25. A New York Times analysis found Peltola on track to eventually prevail, and her campaign has said victory is near. Sullivan's campaign is waiting for every ballot to be counted. "We're still waiting for every ballot to be counted. And we'll see what happens from there," a Sullivan spokesman, Nate Adams, said on Friday night.

Two other candidates were on the ballot alongside Peltola and Sullivan: Gerald L. Heikes, a Republican, and Dan J. Sullivan, an unaffiliated candidate whose name closely resembles the senator's. Together they have taken about 4 percent of the vote so far.

## What a Peltola win would mean

Peltola, a former congresswoman who campaigned as a centrist under the slogan "fish, family and freedom," would be the first Alaska Native ever elected to the United States Senate, and the first Democrat to win a Senate seat from Alaska in nearly two decades.

The stakes extend beyond Alaska. Democrats have already won 55 Senate seats in the midterm elections, and a Peltola victory would give them 56 — their largest Senate majority since 2008. The result is striking in a state Donald Trump carried by about 14 percentage points in 2024, though Alaska has drifted gradually leftward in presidential elections over the past quarter-century.

## Why the count has taken so long

Alaska's slow count is partly a matter of time allowed for mail ballots to arrive and be verified, and partly a feature of the state's ranked-choice system, which requires a full tabulation once the first-choice count is complete. Voters rejected a ballot measure that would have repealed the system, so it remains in place for this election.

Until the tabulation runs, the outcome is a projection, not a certification. But with her lead now above 10,000 votes and only a small share of ballots outstanding, Peltola's position has strengthened with every update — and the count, eleven days after the midterms, is nearly done.

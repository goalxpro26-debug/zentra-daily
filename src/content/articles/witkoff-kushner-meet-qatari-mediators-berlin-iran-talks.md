President Donald Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met senior Qatari officials in Berlin on Thursday to discuss mediation between the United States and Iran, Axios reported, citing two sources familiar with the meeting. The talks were the latest attempt to revive a ceasefire and restart nuclear negotiations — and, according to the report, they made no significant progress.

Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani and his envoy, Ali al-Thawadi, attended the meeting, the sources said. The session coincided with a visit to Germany by Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, who held talks with Chancellor Friedrich Merz and called for diplomatic efforts to de-escalate conflicts across the Middle East.

Qatar is the main mediator between Washington and Tehran and has spent weeks trying to broker a deal that would restore the ceasefire, restart nuclear negotiations and prevent a return to war. The Berlin meeting was part of a flurry of diplomacy in recent days: an Iranian technical delegation visited Doha earlier in the week, al-Thawadi met Iran's foreign minister in Turkey last week, and a Qatari delegation visited Tehran on Friday evening for talks with Iranian officials, according to Iran's deputy foreign minister.

Trump, in a brief phone interview with Axios on Thursday, signalled he is in no hurry to decide his next step. "We will see what happens after the beginning of the year," he said. At a White House news conference on Friday, he said Iran wanted an agreement but not one he could accept. "We want to make a real deal, not a fake deal," he said, adding that Iran could not be allowed to have a nuclear weapon.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said Qatar and Pakistan are the only two mediation channels with the Trump administration. He said Tehran's two-day proposal — resuming a ceasefire, reopening the Strait of Hormuz and relaunching nuclear negotiations — remains on the table, and that Iran is open to shortening its timetable. Tehran's conditions include a ceasefire on all fronts, an end to the naval blockade, waivers allowing Iran to sell oil and the release of frozen Iranian funds. Araghchi said Iran will not allow its enriched uranium stockpile to leave the country, though he believes "a fair solution" can be found, and that Iran is prepared to hold expert-level talks.

The diplomatic push comes after the Qatari prime minister visited Washington last week and met Trump and Vice President JD Vance. After that meeting, Trump told reporters a renewed military campaign against Iran was "quite unlikely" and "doesn't seem to be necessary." Since then, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has pressed Trump to launch a new operation, Axios reported. No deal has been reached, and the mediators are still passing proposals between Washington and Tehran.

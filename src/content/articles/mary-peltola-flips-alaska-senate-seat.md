Democrat Mary Peltola has defeated Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in Alaska's Senate race, the last contest of the 2026 midterms to be decided — and a result that gives Democrats a 56-44 majority in the chamber.

The Associated Press called the race on Tuesday night, two weeks after Election Day. With 99 percent of the vote counted, Peltola held 50.1 percent to Sullivan's 45.8 percent, clearing the majority threshold Alaska's ranked-choice voting system requires for an outright win.

Peltola, who is Yup'ik, becomes the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate. She previously made history in 2022 as the first Alaska Native elected to Congress, winning the state's sole House seat in a special election. She lost that seat in 2024, then rebuilt her campaign around a deliberately local message: lower costs, high fuel prices, and putting Alaska first.

Her victory is the ninth Senate seat Democrats flipped from Republicans in this year's midterms, the party's largest midterm Senate gain since 1958. Republicans entered the election with a 53-47 majority; Democrats now emerge with a 56-44 margin when the new Congress convenes in January. It is also the first time in more than a decade that a Democrat has won a Senate seat in Alaska — the last was Mark Begich, whom Sullivan unseated in 2014.

The race was one of the most closely watched and most bitterly fought of the cycle. It drew national attention after an investigation alleged that people linked to Peltola's campaign had helped recruit a retired teacher named Dan J. Sullivan Jr. — who shares the incumbent's name — to run and siphon votes from the Republican senator. Peltola has denied any knowledge of the alleged decoy-candidate plan. Days before the result was called, the Department of Justice filed a lawsuit seeking to decertify the results if Sullivan lost; Sullivan had pledged to accept the election outcome.

In a statement after the race was called, Peltola said there was "no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs and put Alaska first."

Alaska also elected a Democrat as governor for the first time since 1998, with former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins defeating three Republicans, including one endorsed by President Donald Trump. The Associated Press has not yet called the state's House race, where independent Bill Hill is challenging Republican Representative Nick Begich.

Legal challenges to the Senate result are still possible, and the final certified count may adjust the margins. But the outcome itself is now settled: the Senate's last open seat has gone to the Democrats, closing out a midterm that delivered the party its strongest position in the chamber in 16 years.

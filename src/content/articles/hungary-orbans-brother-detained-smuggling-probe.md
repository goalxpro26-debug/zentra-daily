Hungarian authorities reportedly detained Áron Orbán, the younger brother of former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, on Monday as part of a people-smuggling investigation, in the most politically charged arrest since the country's new government took office.

The news was first reported by Kontroll, a news site owned by Márton Magyar, brother of Prime Minister Péter Magyar. The prime minister shared the report on Facebook, writing that, according to press reports, Áron Orbán had been taken into custody on suspicion of human trafficking. He later confirmed the detention during a speech in Berlin.

Police and Hungary's anti-graft body, the National Asset Protection and Recovery Office, carried out coordinated raids in Budapest and the surrounding Pest county, searching homes and properties linked to Áron Orbán's business associates. Authorities said four people had been detained on suspicion of people smuggling, but they did not name any of the suspects — and police have not confirmed whether Áron Orbán was among those held.

## The case behind the raids

According to the authorities, the four detained suspects — one woman and three men — are accused of helping Vietnamese citizens reach Western Europe over several months in 2024. Investigators say the group charged visa applicants a so-called recruitment fee at Hungarian missions in Vietnam and shared the proceeds.

The case has roots in earlier reporting. Last year, an investigation by Hungarian news site Telex linked Áron Orbán, a 49-year-old businessman, and his business associates to an alleged scheme involving visas for foreign guest workers. Counterterrorism officers were reported to have searched a property in Fótliget linked to a company associated with him.

The anti-graft body was set up to investigate alleged corruption during Viktor Orbán's 16 years in power, which he has denied. Three of his former ministers are already in custody, according to Reuters.

## Orbán calls it political revenge

Viktor Orbán dismissed his brother's reported detention as political persecution, writing on X that the government was using it to divert attention from other issues.

"I am the real target. My younger brother is simply being used to get to me," he wrote.

The arrest lands at a sensitive moment. Péter Magyar, a pro-European Union conservative, won a landslide election victory in April that ended Orbán's 16-year rule, after promising to investigate alleged corruption and abuse of power under his predecessor. His government has since launched a broad accountability drive, and the new anti-graft body has taken over several cases involving the sale of political connections to obtain work and residence permits.

## What is still unknown

The central fact remains unconfirmed by the authorities: police and the anti-graft body have not said whether Áron Orbán himself was detained, formally identified as a suspect, or charged. What is confirmed is that four people were detained and searches took place at several sites in Budapest and Pest county. Further details from the investigation are expected to follow, and the question of whether the reported detention becomes a formal charge will shape how far the case reaches into the former prime minister's circle.

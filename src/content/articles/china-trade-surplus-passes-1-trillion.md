China's trade surplus passed $1 trillion in the first ten months of 2026, official data showed on Tuesday, reaching the milestone a month earlier than last year's record-breaking total — and putting the country on course to beat the $1.2 trillion record it set in 2025.

Exports jumped 35.2 percent year on year in October to $412.3 billion, according to the General Administration of Customs, while imports expanded 41.6 percent to $307.1 billion. The two figures together brought China's cumulative surplus for January to October to $1.04 trillion, up from $964.8 billion over the same period last year.

The engine behind the surge is the global rush to build artificial intelligence infrastructure. Demand for computer chips, data-centre servers and networking equipment has driven overseas sales of high-technology goods, alongside semiconductors, electric vehicles and other advanced products. Semiconductors and computer parts accounted for about 40 percent of the increase in China's exports this year, according to Bloomberg's tally of the data.

## Imports grew faster than exports

The monthly picture was more mixed. Because imports grew faster than exports in October, the surplus for the month alone narrowed to $105.3 billion, down from $133.5 billion in September and the smallest monthly gap since April. Imports rose 41.6 percent, far above the roughly 32 percent economists had expected in a Reuters poll, buoyed by purchases of technology products, critical minerals and energy supplies.

Exports to the United States surged 32.9 percent in October, the customs data showed, even as Washington and Beijing remain locked in their long-running trade confrontation. China's surplus with the European Union reached $310.7 billion over the first ten months, up from $241.6 billion a year earlier.

## Why it matters

A second straight year above $1 trillion underlines how central China has become to global supply chains — and how large the imbalance has grown. Trading partners have pressed Beijing for greater balance, and the size of the surplus is likely to sharpen tensions and protectionist pressure, including fresh tariff risks.

The figures landed just ahead of another high-stakes meeting between President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in China next week, where trade is expected to be high on the agenda.

Analysts expect exports to stay resilient through the rest of the year, led by electronics and vehicles, according to Moody's Analytics. On current trends, China is well placed to finish 2026 with a larger surplus than last year's record — a result that would keep its export engine, and the global debate over it, at the centre of the world economy.

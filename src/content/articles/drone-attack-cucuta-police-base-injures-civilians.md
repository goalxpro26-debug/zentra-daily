Explosive-laden drones struck a police headquarters in the Colombian city of Cúcuta early on Monday, injuring three civilians — including an 18-month-old baby — and damaging more than 60 homes, in what local reporting described as the first drone attack on a major Colombian city.

The attack hit the Norte de Santander police department, known as DENOR, in the Tasajero neighbourhood of Cúcuta, a city of about half a million people near Colombia's border with Venezuela. Police said the drones struck at around 2:30 a.m. local time. No officers were reported injured, and officials did not immediately say who was responsible.

The three injured civilians were a couple and their baby, hurt inside their home near the police compound, according to the Cúcuta metropolitan police commander. Local officials said more than 60 homes were damaged, most of them close to the headquarters. Reporting in Colombia suggested the police department's signal-jamming systems may have thrown the drones off course, so that some of the explosives fell on surrounding houses instead of the compound itself.

Cúcuta is a major trade hub and a centre for criminal groups that profit from the drug trade and other smuggling. Armed groups including the National Liberation Army (ELN) operate in the nearby Catatumbo region, where they tax local businesses and promote coca cultivation, and the ELN has carried out repeated drone attacks there. No group had claimed the Cúcuta attack by Monday, and no authority had named those responsible.

The Cúcuta strike was not an isolated incident. On the same day, drones also attacked a police station in the town of Curumaní, in the department of Cesar. And on Saturday, one soldier was killed and 12 others were injured when drones and rockets hit a military base in La Jagua de Ibirico, also in Cesar. Officials said both incidents remained under investigation.

The attacks come as Colombia's armed groups increasingly turn to small drones, homemade rockets and roadside bombs. President Abelardo de la Espriella, a conservative who took office in August, has stepped up military offensives against rebel groups and drug gangs, including more air strikes, and cancelled the peace negotiations his predecessor Gustavo Petro had been pursuing with rebel groups.

According to the Ideas for Peace Foundation, which monitors Colombia's conflict, illegal armed groups carried out 272 drone attacks during the first nine months of 2026 — a 71 percent increase on the same period last year. For years, drone strikes in Colombia were largely confined to rural areas and jungle fronts. Monday's attack on a city police headquarters shows the tactic has now reached an urban centre.

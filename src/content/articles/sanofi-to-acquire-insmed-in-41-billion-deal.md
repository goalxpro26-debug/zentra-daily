French drugmaker Sanofi said on Tuesday it has agreed to buy U.S. biotech Insmed for $41 billion in cash, its largest bet yet on respiratory medicine and one of the biggest pharmaceutical deals of the year.

Sanofi will pay $175 per Insmed share, a premium of about 46 percent to Insmed's unaffected closing price on November 18, the day before media reports of a possible transaction emerged. The deal values Insmed at roughly $41 billion, or €37.6 billion, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals.

The acquisition gives Sanofi two marketed medicines and a late-stage pipeline asset, strengthening a respiratory business that is set to become more important as patent protection on its best-selling eczema and asthma drug Dupixent begins to run down.

## What Sanofi is buying

The centrepiece is Brinsupri, the brand name for brensocatib, a once-daily oral drug approved in the United States, the European Union, Britain and Japan for non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis, a chronic lung condition in which the airways widen and become prone to infection. It is the first drug of its kind approved for the disease.

Sanofi also gains Arikayce, an inhaled antibacterial used alongside other drugs to treat a serious lung infection known as MAC lung disease. Arikayce brought in $433.8 million in revenue in 2025, up 19 percent on the year before, according to Sanofi.

Behind the two marketed products sits treprostinil palmitil inhalation powder, known as TPIP, a once-daily inhaled treatment in phase 3 trials with potential uses across several rare respiratory diseases. Sanofi said the deal would add to its earnings per share by the end of 2028.

## Why now

The deal follows a familiar pattern in the drug industry: large companies buying growth as their biggest products approach patent expiry. Dupixent, jointly developed with Regeneron, has been Sanofi's main growth engine for years, and the company has been building its respiratory and rare-disease portfolio to carry it beyond that franchise.

Reports of a possible Sanofi–Insmed deal surfaced last week, lifting Insmed's shares before Tuesday's announcement confirmed the terms. Sanofi will brief investors on the transaction at a conference call later today.

If the deal closes on schedule, Insmed's two commercial products and its pipeline will sit inside one of the world's largest drugmakers, with the resources to expand their reach beyond the markets they serve today.

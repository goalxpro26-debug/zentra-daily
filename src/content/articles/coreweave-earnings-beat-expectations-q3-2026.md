CoreWeave, the cloud provider that rents out Nvidia-powered computing for artificial intelligence work, reported stronger-than-expected third-quarter results on Tuesday, easing some of the market's recent nerves about the AI infrastructure boom.

Revenue for the quarter reached $3.66 billion, ahead of Wall Street's expectation of $3.54 billion and up 168 percent on the year. Adjusted operating income rose 75 percent to $379 million, although the adjusted operating margin narrowed from 16 percent to 10 percent.

The results landed after a rough stretch for the stock, which had fallen by roughly half over the previous month as investors questioned whether the company's debt-fuelled expansion could keep pace with demand. Shares rose in after-hours trading following the report.

## A backlog that keeps growing

The headline that mattered most to investors was the order book. CoreWeave's revenue backlog — signed contracts not yet delivered — grew to $133 billion, up from $104 billion at the end of the second quarter.

The company's growth rests on its close relationship with Nvidia, which is a 9 percent shareholder, a customer and its most important supplier. CoreWeave rents out Nvidia servers in the cloud, and that access has translated into large contracts with heavyweight customers including Microsoft and Meta Platforms.

The company also gave solid guidance for the fourth quarter and the full year, particularly for adjusted operating income, suggesting management expects the momentum to continue into the end of 2026.

## The cost side of the boom

The report also laid bare the price of growing this fast. Interest expenses reached $846 million in the quarter, up from $310 million a year earlier, more than wiping out the operating profit. Debt and lease liabilities rose by $14.6 billion in a single quarter, to $66 billion.

Chief executive Michael Intrator said the company was moving away from unsecured public debt in favour of delayed-draw facilities it has used before, a shift aimed at lowering its borrowing costs.

That financing question is now the central test for CoreWeave. Demand for AI computing shows no sign of slowing — the backlog proves that — but the company must convert those contracts into revenue on schedule while keeping its borrowing under control. This quarter, the top line did its part. The balance sheet remains the thing to watch.

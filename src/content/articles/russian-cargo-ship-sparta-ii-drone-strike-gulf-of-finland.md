A Russian cargo ship bound for the Baltic exclave of Kaliningrad was hit by two drones in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday morning, catching fire and drifting out of international waters and into Finnish territorial waters, Russian and Finnish officials said.

The vessel is the Sparta II, a Russian-flagged roll-on/roll-off cargo ship sailing from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad. Russian officials said the attack happened about three nautical miles from Russia's border, west of Gogland Island, in the eastern part of the gulf. Finland's defence ministry said its forces detected the strike at about 8:40 a.m. local time.

A fire broke out on board and the ship lost manoeuvrability. A photograph taken by a Finnish patrol plane showed flames and thick smoke coming from the rear of the vessel. The Finnish Border Guard, which took charge of the rescue operation, set up a restricted area around the ship and said the situation posed no danger to other maritime traffic or to people.

By Saturday afternoon the ship had anchored in Finnish waters south of the town of Loviisa. The Border Guard said rescue units were on site, the crew had not been forced to abandon the vessel, and no environmental damage had been detected. Finnish officials said the ship's 16 crew members were evacuating into rescue craft, and that Russian rescue vessels were expected to arrive later in the day, with Russia intending to tow the ship back into its own waters.

Finland temporarily restricted air and sea traffic in the eastern gulf as a precaution, lifting the restrictions in the early afternoon. Separately, Finnish authorities said a Russian helicopter was suspected of entering Finnish airspace near Loviisa shortly after midday, reaching about four kilometres into Finnish territory. Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen said the suspected violation was possibly connected to rescue or tracking operations around the damaged ship, and the Border Guard is investigating.

The Finnish Defence Forces said on Saturday afternoon that the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine. Kyiv has not publicly claimed responsibility. The Sparta II is known to Finnish authorities as a vessel with links to Russia's armed forces, and it is under US sanctions. Finnish officials said there was no confirmed information about what cargo was on board, and that military materiel could not be ruled out.

The incident comes barely a week after a similar strike in the same waters, when Finland said it detected an attack on a Russian warship in the gulf. It also underlines how exposed the Baltic has become: Kaliningrad, a Russian exclave wedged between Poland and Lithuania, depends on sea routes through the gulf, and the war in Ukraine keeps spilling into waters shared with NATO members.

Russia's Leningrad region governor, Aleksandr Drozdenko, said 80 drones had been shot down over the region and lifted a drone threat warning. Kaliningrad's governor, Alexei Besprozvannykh, said the incident posed no threat to supplies to the region.

Cam Schlittler is the American League's best pitcher, and nobody who voted disagreed. The New York Yankees right-hander was named the unanimous winner of the 2026 AL Cy Young Award on Wednesday, collecting all 30 first-place votes from the Baseball Writers' Association of America.

Schlittler, 25, went 14-6 with a 1.95 ERA across 33 starts in his first full major league season. He led the American League in ERA and in pitching WAR (7.0), and struck out 239 batters in 193⅔ innings. His 1.95 ERA was the lowest in the league since 2022 and the third-lowest in Yankees history among qualified pitchers, behind Spud Chandler's 1.64 in 1943 and Ron Guidry's 1.74 in 1978.

Toronto Blue Jays right-hander Dylan Cease finished second with 110 points, and Cleveland Guardians rookie Parker Messick placed third. Schlittler finished with 210 points, the maximum possible under the voting system.

The award puts Schlittler in rare company. He is the seventh Yankees pitcher to win the Cy Young, joining Bob Turley (1958), Whitey Ford (1961), Sparky Lyle (1977), Guidry (1978), Roger Clemens (2001) and Gerrit Cole (2023). He is the youngest Yankee ever to win it, and only the third in club history to do so unanimously, after Guidry and Cole. Clemens, a seven-time winner himself, announced Schlittler as the winner during the awards ceremony in Las Vegas.

## A rapid rise to the top of the rotation

Schlittler's path to the award was fast. A seventh-round draft pick, he debuted on July 9, 2025, and made 14 starts that season before stepping into a full-time rotation role this year. Since his debut, he has posted the majors' best ERA (2.23) while tying for the most starts in baseball, and he allowed one run or fewer in an MLB-best 21 starts this season.

The numbers tell the story of a pitcher who took over the Yankees' rotation in his second year. He pitched the second-most innings in the American League, and his 239 strikeouts ranked among the league's best. He is also the first homegrown Yankees Cy Young winner since Guidry in 1978.

The other half of the night belonged to the National League. Milwaukee Brewers right-hander Jacob Misiorowski also won unanimously, taking all 30 first-place votes in his first full season. It is only the second time both Cy Young winners were in their second major league seasons.

For the Yankees, the award caps a season in which Schlittler went from a promising arm to the undisputed ace of the staff. He remains under team control through the 2031 season, with two seasons still to go before he is even eligible for arbitration — a detail that will shape the club's offseason planning as much as the trophy itself.

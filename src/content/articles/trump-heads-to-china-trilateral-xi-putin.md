President Donald Trump departs on Wednesday for China, where he will join Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin for an unprecedented three-way meeting, with nuclear weapons and the war in Ukraine at the top of the agenda.

The meeting takes place in Shenzhen, the skyscraper-filled technology hub hosting world leaders for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Trump arrives from Manila, where he attended the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit, and will take part in the APEC summit on Thursday before sitting down with Xi and Putin, then returning to the United States.

Trump has signalled that he wants to take an improvised approach to the trip, leaning on personal relationships rather than a scripted agenda. It is a bet that his unscripted style of diplomacy can produce a legacy-defining moment in the final two years of his presidency, at a time when much of the world is on edge over the wars in Ukraine and Iran and the rapid transformation brought by artificial intelligence.

The president said he plans to talk with Xi and Putin about limiting nuclear weapons and bringing an end to the war in Ukraine, which began in 2022 with Russia's invasion. That raises a question about what is actually on the table: before the trip, Trump's aides had told reporters that the war in Ukraine would not be part of the trilateral meeting. It is not yet clear how that difference will be resolved once the three leaders are in the room.

Analysts are not expecting substantive breakthroughs, despite the unprecedented nature of the meeting. A first-ever three-way gathering of the American, Chinese and Russian leaders is historic in itself, but the gaps between them — on Ukraine, on trade, on the military use of artificial intelligence — are wide, and a single session is unlikely to close them.

Trump has also floated the possibility of a private meeting with Putin and a separate sit-down with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un while he is in Asia. Neither has been confirmed beyond the president's own remarks, and it remains unclear whether either will happen.

The trip comes at an awkward moment at home. A series of technical outages back in the United States has put the administration on the defensive, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio stepping in to reassure the public on Trump's behalf. Trump, meanwhile, has been keen to project strength abroad, presenting the China visit as a demonstration of American technological leadership.

What the meeting produces, if anything, should become clear on Thursday, when the three leaders meet and then face reporters. Until then, the most that can be said with confidence is that the meeting itself — three leaders, one table, no precedent — is the news.

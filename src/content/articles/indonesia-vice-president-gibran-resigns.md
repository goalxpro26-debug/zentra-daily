Indonesia's vice president, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, resigned on Thursday, ending two years in office after court rulings cast doubt on his education credentials and lawmakers moved toward impeaching him.

Gibran, 39, handed his resignation letter to parliament leaders in Jakarta on Thursday morning after a meeting of about 20 minutes, Ahmad Muzani, speaker of the People's Consultative Assembly, told reporters. He had also sent one to President Prabowo Subianto, who said hours later at a press conference alongside Gibran that he had accepted it with a "heavy heart."

Gibran is the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo, known as Jokowi, whose backing helped carry Prabowo to the presidency in 2024. His resignation closes a chapter on the alliance between the two men and leaves the government under pressure amid widespread public dissatisfaction over the economy, corruption and wildfires.

The immediate cause was a dispute over his eligibility. Last month, the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court ruled that the documents he submitted about his overseas education did not meet election requirements, fuelling questions over whether he held a valid senior high school-equivalent diploma. On Wednesday, 120 lawmakers from all eight parties in parliament filed a motion that could have led to his impeachment.

## A swift reversal

The resignation came a day after Gibran had vowed to fight the court rulings in the State Administrative Court and the Supreme Court. But at a meeting with Prabowo on Tuesday, he offered to resign, saying he wanted to avoid a prolonged political controversy.

Muzani, reading from the letter, said Gibran had acted "through my own volition ... for the interests of the government and nation." The five-paragraph letter cited "growing public sentiment" and personal reasons, senior ministers said. Muzani added that Gibran gave no reason in the letter he sent to Prabowo.

Jokowi moved quickly to steady the fallout. Speaking outside his home in Solo, central Java, the former president said he would continue to support Prabowo's administration, "especially programmes that serve the interests of the broader public." The show of support may soothe signs of strain, though some observers say Gibran may have been pressed to step down rather than quitting freely.

## What happens next

Under Indonesia's Constitution, a vice-presidential vacancy does not trigger a new election. The People's Consultative Assembly must first hold a session to formally declare the office vacant, and the president will then nominate two candidates. The assembly elects a replacement within 60 days.

Gibran had served two years of a five-year term after taking office in October 2024. His candidacy was controversial from the start: a Constitutional Court ruling changed the election rules so he could stand, and Jokowi was accused of influencing that decision. The questions that surrounded him — about eligibility, courts and political dynasties — are likely to shape the contest to replace him.

A bomb found at an ethanol plant in central Illinois forced more than 5,000 people to evacuate part of a city for about a day, as police and federal agents searched a sprawling industrial site for further threats. The evacuation order was lifted on Saturday afternoon, roughly 24 hours after the device was discovered, and no injuries were reported.

The device was found on Friday at the BioUrja Renewables facility in Peoria, a city of about 110,000 people roughly 130 miles southwest of Chicago. The plant sits in an industrial area along the Illinois River, just southwest of downtown, and makes ethanol — a highly flammable liquid used in fuel, beverages and hand sanitiser.

The incident began shortly after 1 p.m. local time on Friday, when the plant told police it had received a bomb threat by email. Officers found a suspicious package at about 2 p.m. and, at about 6:45 p.m., determined it posed a credible threat. Bomb technicians rendered the device safe at about 9:12 p.m.

Because of the materials stored at the plant, officials set a one-mile blast radius around the site. An evacuation order took effect at 7:02 p.m. on Friday, covering parts of downtown Peoria and the Warehouse District and crossing the Illinois River into neighbouring East Peoria. Shelters were opened at Manual Academy High School in Peoria and the EastSide Centre sports complex in East Peoria.

The order stayed in place through Saturday morning while investigators searched the facility, which Peoria Police Chief Brad Dixon said is the size of about 44 and a half football fields. By Saturday afternoon the search was complete, nothing further had been found, and the evacuation order and blast radius were cancelled.

The FBI and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives assisted in the investigation, including with bomb technicians and explosives specialists. Officials said they were still searching for whoever was responsible. No arrests have been made, and no motive or suspect has been identified.

Dixon said he would not call the device a bomb while the process of determining its composition was still under way, a caution that reflects how much of the investigation remains open. BioUrja, based in Houston, Texas, bought the plant from Archer Daniels Midland in late 2021. The company did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

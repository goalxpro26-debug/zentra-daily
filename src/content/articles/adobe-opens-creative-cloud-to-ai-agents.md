Adobe has announced it will open its flagship creative apps to outside AI assistants, letting tools such as ChatGPT and Claude edit projects directly inside Photoshop, Premiere Pro, Illustrator and After Effects. The change, unveiled on Tuesday at Adobe MAX in Miami Beach, is the most significant opening of Creative Cloud since the subscription service launched in 2012.

The connection uses the Model Context Protocol, an open standard that lets AI assistants plug into other software. It will run locally on the user's own computer, so an assistant works directly with native project files — layered Photoshop documents, Premiere sequences, vectors and bins — and those elements stay editable afterwards. Adobe says the integrations will enter private beta in early 2027.

## A wave of tools arriving now

Most of the other announcements are available from today. In Photoshop, a new Relight tool lets users add, move and resize light sources in an image non-destructively, while Generative Restyle generates style variations from a text prompt that can be fine-tuned with sliders. The beta app gains features including Mockups, Camera Angles and a tool that converts text in a flattened image into an editable type layer.

Following Adobe's acquisition of Topaz Labs, two new enhancement models arrive: Wonder 4, which combines upscaling, denoising, deblurring and sharpening in one step, and Image HDR, which converts standard images to HDR.

Premiere Pro gets a new colour-grading tool, Color Mode, starting today, alongside a Generative Media tool that can create missing b-roll, sound effects, soundscapes and music from inside the timeline, drawing on Adobe's Firefly models or partner models from Google, Kling, Runway and Luma. Audio tools including Enhance Audio and Separate Crosstalk aim to clean up messy recordings, and a Generative Remove tool in beta can erase unwanted objects such as boom microphones directly on the timeline.

## Firefly and the agent push

Adobe is also reorganising Firefly, its generative AI brand, around new pieces: Firefly Video for organising scenes and assembling clips, Firefly Design, a single canvas combining illustration, image editing, animation and 3D, Firefly Graph for reusable creative workflows, and Firefly Builder, in private beta, which turns a natural-language idea into a working website or app prototype.

The company's own Adobe Creative Agent is expanding across Creative Cloud, with AI assistants now in beta in Illustrator, Premiere, InDesign, After Effects and Frame.io, as well as the Photoshop beta app. The assistants handle multi-step jobs from natural-language prompts — in Premiere, for example, applying effects across a whole sequence. Adobe is also previewing always-on agents in Firefly that can handle recurring chores such as organising assets, with a beta due in early 2027.

## What is still unknown

Adobe has not said whether any of the new tools will cost extra or require a higher subscription tier, and the third-party integrations are still months from their private beta. Whether outside assistants editing native files becomes a daily workflow, or a niche for power users, will only become clear once the beta opens in early 2027.

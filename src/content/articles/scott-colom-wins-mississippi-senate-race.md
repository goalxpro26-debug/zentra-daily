Democrat Scott Colom has won election to the U.S. Senate from Mississippi, according to a race call by the Associated Press, narrowly defeating Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in the country's closest Senate contest.

The call came on Wednesday, more than a week after election day, once officials had finished counting the outstanding absentee and provisional ballots that had left the outcome in doubt. Colom won by just a few thousand votes.

## A week of counting

The race was too close to settle on the night itself. With ballots still being counted for days afterwards, both campaigns waited while local officials worked through the remaining absentee and affidavit ballots. When the count finished, the margin was small enough to make it the tightest Senate race in the country — and a result few forecasters had predicted. The Cook Political Report had rated the contest as Likely Republican going into election night.

## A historic first

Colom's victory carries two historic firsts. He is the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Mississippi in more than 40 years, and the first Black senator elected from the state since Reconstruction.

Mississippi has the highest share of Black voters in the country, at close to 40% of the electorate, yet President Donald Trump carried the state by 23 points in 2024. NPR described the result as part of a midterm full of upsets for Democrats, driven in the South by a surge in Black voter turnout.

## How the race turned

Colom, a district attorney, made the cost of living the centrepiece of his campaign, branding his opponent "Cindy High-Prices" and arguing that rising prices had hit Mississippi's rural, agriculture-heavy economy especially hard.

A remark by Hyde-Smith — a cattle rancher and former state agriculture commissioner — became a turning point. Asked earlier this year about high beef prices, she suggested voters had "so many other proteins to choose from." Colom's campaign turned the line into a viral advertisement, with voters reacting in disbelief before the ad closed on its message: it was time to "pick another senator."

Unlike most other contested Senate races, Mississippi drew little national money or attention until the closing days. That changed in the final week, when Trump flew in to headline a rally for Hyde-Smith, while former Vice President Kamala Harris and Georgia Sen. Raphael Warnock campaigned for Colom.

Colom himself had criticised the national Democratic Party's late arrival. "The reality is, Mississippi has never been a lost cause, we've been a betrayed cause," he told NPR earlier this month, saying the party had spent hundreds of millions of dollars in other states and "almost nothing in Mississippi."

## What is still unknown

The final certified vote totals have yet to be published, and Hyde-Smith has said little publicly since election night beyond expressing confidence while votes were still being counted. What the result means for the balance of the Senate, and how Colom positions himself once seated, will become clearer in the weeks ahead.

German prosecutors said on Thursday that police had arrested two Iraqi nationals suspected of belonging to a terrorist organisation that plotted attacks on Jewish and other targets in Europe, including a synagogue in Cologne.

The two men, identified only as Mostafa Al-R. and Ameer J. under German privacy rules, were detained in Heidelberg and Cologne after officers searched 17 properties across the states of Baden-Wuerttemberg, Hesse and North Rhine-Westphalia. Prosecutors said more than 250 officers took part in the operation.

Prosecutors say Mostafa Al-R. was closely associated with Mohammad Al-Saadi, who is facing terrorism charges in the United States and is described as a key figure in a pro-Iranian group known as Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiyyah, or HAYI. Prosecutors believe the group is behind a series of attacks on US, Israeli and Jewish institutions in Europe since March.

According to the arrest warrants, Al-Saadi and Mostafa Al-R. formed a separate organisation with others at the end of 2025 to prepare further attacks in Germany and elsewhere in Europe. Ameer J. was a member of that group, prosecutors said. The group raised funds and recruited people to carry out bomb attacks, according to the warrants.

The warrants say that on two consecutive nights in late March, unidentified people acting on the group's behalf travelled to a synagogue in Cologne and to another location in Europe, apparently carrying explosive devices. The attacks were not carried out, for reasons that remain unclear.

The two suspects were due to appear before a judge on Thursday, who will decide whether they remain in pre-trial detention. Prosecutors said the investigation was launched on intelligence supplied by Germany's domestic intelligence agency, the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution.

In the United States, prosecutors unsealed an eight-count indictment earlier this month charging Al-Saadi and two other men with terrorism-related offences, in a case linked to the same network.

Jewish institutions in Germany are under heightened protection after warnings of threats linked to tensions in the Middle East, and Germany has faced a series of attacks and plots in recent years. Prosecutors have not said whether further arrests are expected.

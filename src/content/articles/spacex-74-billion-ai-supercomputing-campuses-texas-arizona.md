SpaceX said on Thursday it plans to invest more than $74 billion in two supercomputing campuses, one at its Starbase site in Texas and another in Cochise County, Arizona, in a major expansion of its artificial intelligence business.

The company expects to spend more than $24 billion on the Starbase campus and roughly $50 billion on the Arizona site, it said in a statement. The campuses are designed to train frontier AI systems, the large models used for research, manufacturing and national security work.

## Two sites, two different builds

The Starbase facility, near the company's rocket base on the Gulf Coast, would generate its own electricity rather than draw from the public grid. SpaceX has described a gas-fired power plant for the site, and the Starbase City Commission is due to consider a property-tax abatement for the project on December 9.

The Arizona campus is the larger of the two. The company says it would draw up to 6 gigawatts of power at full build-out, delivered in four phases across nine large compute buildings. It would create about 5,500 construction jobs at peak and 1,000 permanent jobs, according to SpaceX. The site would use dry cooling, with no planned industrial use of groundwater, and the company says it could cut power bills for local cooperative members by up to 20%.

## Approval still to come

Neither project is a done deal yet. The Arizona campus needs approval from Cochise County, and a petition with about 2,400 signatures has asked officials to deny the permit. The county's planning commission is scheduled to hold a public hearing on December 9.

The announcement underlines how central AI computing has become to SpaceX. The company's AI segment generated more than half of its revenue in the third quarter, Reuters reported, alongside its rocket launches and Starlink satellite internet business.

SpaceX has not disclosed which chip suppliers it will use, or a commissioning schedule for either campus. The $74 billion figure is the company's planned investment; it is not contracted capacity or revenue, and both sites remain subject to the local decisions due in December.

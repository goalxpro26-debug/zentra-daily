Anthropic's shares slipped below their $83 initial public offering price on Tuesday, less than a week after the Claude developer's record-breaking Nasdaq debut — a swift reversal for the most anticipated listing of the year.

The stock was down 6.5% at $82 in premarket trading, according to Reuters, and later touched $80.10 shortly after the market opened. It is a steep fall from the November 19 debut, when shares opened at $111.50, reached $115.10 on their first day, and the company's fully diluted valuation briefly topped $2 trillion.

The drop came a day after Anthropic missed out on a broad rally in AI-linked stocks. Nvidia surged nearly 30% on Monday after reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive, but Anthropic barely moved. The shares fell sharply on Friday, their second day of trading, and have since spent most of their time below $90.

Filings also showed that chief executive Dario Amodei sold more than 3.37 million shares worth over $280 million on November 20, the day after the debut, at the $83 IPO price. The sale was disclosed late on Monday.

## A record listing, quickly tested

Anthropic raised $93.5 billion in the largest IPO on record once underwriters exercised their option to buy extra shares, passing SpaceX's $85.7 billion listing in June. As the first frontier AI company to go public, it is widely seen as a proxy for the sector — which is why its slide has fed a wider debate about stretched AI valuations and the vast sums being poured into computing infrastructure, with rising interest rates threatening to push those costs higher.

The broader market has offered little shelter. The S&P 500 and the Dow ended last week in correction territory, and Nvidia posted its steepest weekly loss since 2008, underscoring the sell-off that has swept Anthropic along with it.

## History offers a mixed guide

Anthropic is not alone among blockbuster listings in quickly giving up its early gains. SpaceX took about a month to fall below its offer price, while Facebook — now Meta — slipped below its offer price on its second trading day in 2012. The experience of past high-profile listings suggests an early break below the offer price is not necessarily a guide to longer-term performance.

Some support may be on the way. Index provider MSCI will add Anthropic to its global standard indexes at the November 30 close, and index rebalance analytics firm Intropic estimates passive funds could buy up to 30% of the shares offered by year-end.

The US government has confirmed it is investigating Megaspeed, the Singapore-based computing firm suspected of helping advanced Nvidia AI chips reach China despite American export controls.

In a joint statement on Monday, the Department of Justice and the Department of Commerce said the investigation was under way and that it was in the public interest to provide an update. The departments said the government would pursue the matter to the full extent of the law, describing semiconductors as among the most valuable and strategic technologies.

Megaspeed is described as the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in South East Asia. US officials suspect the company of diverting high-end AI chips to China, in breach of controls Washington imposed to limit Beijing's access to the most advanced semiconductors.

The investigation began in 2024 over potential export-control breaches, according to the BBC. In May 2025, Commerce Department investigators asked Nvidia for records about direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. Agents from the Bureau of Industry and Security later acted alongside Singaporean law-enforcement officers, after which the Justice Department took the lead in the criminal investigation.

## A scandal that has already cost Nvidia its chief

The confirmation comes days after the resignation of Nvidia founder Jensen Huang, who stepped down as chief executive last week after revelations about two affairs, including one allegedly with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US authorities suspect of spying for Beijing. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress, including the House Select Committee on China.

The case has also exposed a rift inside the US government. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Friday blamed the Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, for failing to oversee the chip exports properly. Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month.

The White House said on Monday that it would further restrict China's access to AI chips from firms such as Nvidia.

## What is still unknown

No monetary figures or chip quantities were given in the announcement, and Megaspeed itself was not quoted in the BBC report. A related report notes the company saying the suspected spy left the firm in 2025. Singapore does not prohibit transferring advanced AI chips to companies abroad, but such transfers can still breach US export controls — and it is that gap, rather than Singaporean law, that the US investigation is testing.

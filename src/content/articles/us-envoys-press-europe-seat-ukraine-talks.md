The second day of U.S.-mediated peace talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday with no breakthrough on the war's core questions — but with a new push from Washington to bring Europe into the room next time.

The session lasted just under two hours behind closed doors, after an opening day on Tuesday that ran about four and a half hours. It was the first direct meeting between the two sides in nine months.

The one concrete result was humanitarian: agreement on a large prisoner exchange, with figures of 1,000 for 1,000 or 1,500 for 1,500 under discussion, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. On the bigger issues — territory, a ceasefire and security guarantees — the gap remains wide.

## Europe's missing seat

The most pointed question was not what was discussed, but who was allowed to discuss it. European security advisers from Britain, France, Germany and Italy, along with EU and NATO officials, were in the city for separate consultations — but Russia kept them out of the main talks.

That is now the focus of the American effort. Politico reported, citing U.S. officials, that Trump's envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, will press Moscow to accept European participation in the next round — and want this week's meeting to be the last one held without Europe at the table.

"We need Europe at the table," one official told Politico, describing a proposal to bring European representatives into the discussions after this trilateral round. The envoys are also trying to speed up the process, leaving European governments little time to decide who would speak for them.

Moscow has shown no sign of agreeing. Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov accused European leaders of trying to "buy time for Ukraine" and suggested they "stay at the hotel" in Abu Dhabi, and the Kremlin said its delegation held no contacts with European representatives.

## What happens next

Zelenskyy said Europe must decide by the end of November who will represent it, and expressed hope the decision could be reached at a November 19 meeting of the coalition of the willing in Germany. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen has ruled herself out, saying European institutions should take part instead. Finnish President Alexander Stubb has been mentioned as a possible representative; he and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre both spoke to Putin by phone on October 27.

Witkoff and Kushner could travel to Moscow and then Kyiv after the Abu Dhabi round, Western media reported, as Washington tries to keep the momentum going.

Both sides spoke positively without claiming progress on substance. Kushner said he was "encouraged by the constructive discussions," Zelenskyy called Wednesday "the most crucial day for negotiations," and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who did not attend, called the opening day productive but cautioned that much work remained.

For now, the war goes on while diplomacy circles the same obstacles: Russia's demand that Ukraine withdraw from the Donbas, the shape of any security guarantees, and whether Europe gets a seat at a table deciding its own security.

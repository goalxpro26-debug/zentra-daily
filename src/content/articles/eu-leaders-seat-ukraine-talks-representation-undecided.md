European Union leaders meeting in Dublin on Friday agreed that Europe must speak with one voice in any future Ukraine peace talks, but they left open the question of who would do the speaking.

The leaders said the structure of European security should not itself be a subject for negotiation between Ukraine and Russia. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz put the position plainly to reporters: "There will be no discussions about Ukraine without Ukraine, and no discussions about Europe without Europe. Furthermore, there is no need to talk about European security architecture. We have that already."

Russia has pressed for a broad agreement on Europe's security framework before tackling the specifics of the war, and U.S. negotiator Jared Kushner said on Thursday that he had begun discussions with European partners on the subject. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said the Russian calls risked diverting attention from the need for a ceasefire, and Latvian Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs called such discussions "a trap."

## A seat at the table, still undefined

The question of representation remains unresolved. EU leaders broadly agreed that Europe needed a single voice, but they did not settle on one person to provide it, and Merz said discussions would continue.

Merz pointed to the options already in use. "There are established institutional forms of (EU) representation — Kaja Kallas, for instance, is part of this — but the E3 format has also proven very effective," he said, referring to the informal grouping of France, Germany and Britain. "Moreover, we always include Italy and Poland," he added.

Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said Britain, one of Europe's top three military powers, certainly had to be included, and that five countries plus EU institutions would make sense. "I'm quite flexible on that, so I think what we actually say is more important than the exact configuration of the people in the room," he said.

A day earlier, forty European countries meeting as a broader group in Dublin signed a declaration saying that any matter affecting European security should be decided with Europeans involved.

## Moscow's conditions

The Kremlin said on Friday that it would be open to European countries taking part in the talks if it saw they were ready to contribute to the peace process. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov said a decision on an expanded format would come once Moscow understood their "genuine intention and readiness to contribute."

That position stops short of a commitment. U.S. negotiators had said on Thursday that President Vladimir Putin had agreed to European participation, but Peskov said no concrete discussions about European involvement had yet taken place. Putin said on Thursday that the United States must work with its European allies to understand whether they wanted peace or war in Ukraine.

Representatives from Britain, France, Germany and Italy, along with the EU, attended Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi earlier this week — the first such meeting in nine months. Both Moscow and Kyiv cited some progress, though there was no sign of a breakthrough on key issues such as territory.

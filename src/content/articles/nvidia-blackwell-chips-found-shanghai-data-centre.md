Nvidia's most advanced AI chips have turned up inside a Shanghai data centre linked to Alice Huang, the businesswoman at the centre of the scandal engulfing the chipmaker, according to documents reviewed by Bloomberg.

Filings submitted to Chinese regulators in November show two Chinese firms installed Blackwell-based hardware at the Yangtze River Delta Project. Shanghai Shuoyao listed an Nvidia GB300 NVL72 rack as collateral for a 96 million yuan loan — about $14 million — from Shanghai Innovation Bank. A second firm, Shanghai Kezhan, controlled by a Tokyo-based parent company, described a GB200 server at the same site. Both machines run on Blackwell chips, Nvidia's most powerful AI processors.

The filings are the first evidence that Blackwell hardware has reached a Chinese data centre connected to Huang and to Megaspeed, the Singapore-based cloud company she once ran. Bloomberg also found evidence of earlier Hopper-generation Nvidia chips at the facility, which are likewise restricted.

A standard GB300 NVL72 rack is about two metres tall, weighs more than 1,500 kilograms, and holds 72 Blackwell Ultra chips alongside 36 Grace processors. Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang once argued that equipment of that size was too big to smuggle. Shipping Blackwell systems to China without a U.S. export licence is illegal, and it is not clear how the rack or the GB200 server reached Shanghai.

Bloomberg traced part of the rack's journey through its serial number, which a public warranty database showed was manufactured by Gigabyte Technology of Taiwan, which assembles Nvidia chips into servers and racks. Gigabyte did not say who it had originally sold the hardware to.

The site is vast: local media have described the Yangtze River Delta Project as designed to house as many as 800,000 servers. Megaspeed used renderings of the project in a 2024 investor presentation that did not list China as its location. Kang Wei, who has been described in reporting as close to Huang, is listed as the facility's president, according to Bloomberg.

Shanghai Shuoyao, which procured the GB300 rack, is active at the data centre and has significant overlap with Megaspeed. It confirmed this month that it is one of Megaspeed's service providers, describing the relationship as limited to ordinary commercial cooperation. Bloomberg said it has no evidence that Megaspeed itself diverted Nvidia equipment to the site, and the company denies any wrongdoing. The U.S. Justice Department is investigating whether Megaspeed broke American export rules.

The discovery lands as the scandal around Nvidia widens. Huang took a controlling stake in Megaspeed in 2023 and left the company in August 2025; The New York Times reported in October that U.S. intelligence agencies had been monitoring her for about a year. Jensen Huang admitted to an affair with her and resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on November 12, and he is scheduled to testify before Congress on December 3.

For Nvidia, the filings raise a question it has not answered: how its most restricted hardware keeps reaching China despite export controls designed to stop exactly that.

The White House has withdrawn Heidi Overton's nomination to lead the Food and Drug Administration, a White House official told the Guardian on Tuesday — ending her bid to run the agency a day before the Senate health committee was due to vote on whether to advance it.

Overton, a physician and deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, was nominated by President Donald Trump in August. Her selection was seen as an attempt to bring a stabilizing Trump loyalist to the agency after a tumultuous year under the president's first FDA commissioner, Marty Makary. Overton has spent most of her professional career working directly for Trump or for conservative groups aligned with his agenda.

Her confirmation prospects had been narrowing for weeks. Senators from both parties questioned whether she would act independently of the president, particularly on his unsupported claims about the dangers of childhood vaccines. Senator Bill Cassidy, the Louisiana doctor who chairs the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee, had called her support for Trump's plan to break up combination vaccines — including the measles, mumps and rubella shot — "almost disqualifying," and pressed her during a September hearing on her scientific independence and her willingness to push back against Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a longtime vaccine critic.

The arithmetic was unforgiving. With Democrats expected to oppose her, Overton needed the votes of all 12 Republicans on the 21-member committee to advance to the full Senate. Cassidy said earlier on Tuesday that he remained undecided ahead of the vote scheduled for Wednesday. After the withdrawal, he said the White House had abandoned a viable confirmation path, adding that his conversations with Overton had convinced him she would follow the science.

Abortion policy also hung over the nomination. As FDA commissioner, Overton would have overseen the agency that regulates medication abortion. In a 2023 brief for the America First Policy Institute, she argued that Congress should strictly regulate the abortion pill mifepristone, criticized the mailing of abortion pills, and wrote that abortion "is corrosive to children, women, and society broadly."

The withdrawal leaves the FDA without a permanent leader. The agency is currently being run on an acting basis by Kyle Diamantas, according to Reuters, and the White House has given no timeline for naming a new nominee. Senator Roger Marshall of Kansas, who recently lost his reelection bid, has expressed interest in the role, according to people familiar with the matter.

Overton is the latest health nominee the White House has pulled amid crumbling Republican support, following nominees to lead the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the surgeon general's office. Republicans are racing to confirm the president's remaining nominees before handing power to Senate Democrats in January, and the FDA vacancy now looks likely to stretch well into next year.

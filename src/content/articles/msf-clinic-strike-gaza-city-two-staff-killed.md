Two staff members of Doctors Without Borders were killed on Friday night in an Israeli strike on one of the aid group's clinics in Gaza City, in an incident where the two sides give sharply different accounts of what happened and who the men were.

Doctors Without Borders, known by its French initials MSF, said the two men were watchmen employed at the clinic and described the strike as a "direct hit". In a statement posted on X on Friday, the group said it was "devastated and outraged" by the killings, which it said bring the number of its staff killed in Gaza to 17.

"Our premises were clearly marked with MSF logos, and their location had been communicated to and was known by the Israeli military," the group said. It called for those responsible to be held accountable. The head of the World Health Organization, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, called the attack "outrageous and unacceptable", writing that "those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked."

The Israeli military gave a different account. It said the two men were members of Hamas who worked as security guards at the clinic, and named them as Ahmed Sufyan Muhammad Ali Abu Hasira and Moaz Yousef Alitsbach, describing the latter as an artillery cell commander in Hamas's military wing. The military said the men had been planning attacks against Israeli troops and civilians and working to restore Hamas's military capabilities, in violation of the ceasefire. It did not provide evidence for the claim in its statement.

The military also said the strike had not targeted a hospital and had caused no damage to medical infrastructure. Palestinian media reported that the strike hit near an MSF clinic close to Shifa Hospital in Gaza City.

The incident comes against a backdrop of months of strain between Israel and MSF over the group's operations in Gaza. In January, MSF said Israel had blocked its supplies and denied entry to all of its international staff since the start of the year. Israeli officials have alleged, without proof, that aid groups are linked to Hamas, while aid organisations say such accusations have helped normalise attacks on humanitarian workers.

A ceasefire in Gaza took effect in October 2025, but strikes have continued. What remains unresolved in this case is the central dispute itself: MSF says two of its watchmen were killed in a direct hit on a marked clinic, while the military says the same two men were Hamas members targeted for their militant activity. Neither account has been independently verified in the reporting so far, and MSF has not addressed the military's allegations about the men's affiliations.

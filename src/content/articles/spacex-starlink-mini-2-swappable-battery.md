SpaceX has unveiled the Starlink Mini 2, a new version of its smallest portable satellite internet terminal — and the headline change is power. For the first time, the laptop-sized dish carries its own battery, so it can get online without an outlet, a vehicle, or an external power bank nearby.

The terminal combines a satellite dish, a Wi-Fi router and now a removable 71 Wh battery in a single unit. SpaceX says the pack delivers up to 4.5 hours of connectivity on a full charge, and owners can hot-swap in a spare while the terminal stays online. The battery clips on with a quick-release mechanism that needs no tools, has its own LED indicators, and charges over USB-C at up to 60 watts. It can also send up to 60 watts back out over USB-C to charge other devices.

The redesign is slimmer and tougher than the original Mini, which launched in June 2024 at $599 and always needed an external power source. The Mini 2 is 23.1 mm thick, down from 38.5 mm, weighs about 3.5 pounds with the battery and kickstand attached, and carries an IP67 rating against dust and water. SpaceX says its built-in Wi-Fi can connect a user within 60 seconds.

## Price and availability

Preorders opened on November 10 by invitation only, for select customers in the United States, at $999 plus tax. That price covers the hardware and a full year of Roam Unlimited service, which SpaceX values at $2,100. Customers reserve with a $100 refundable deposit, with the remaining $899 charged when the device ships. SpaceX expects the first shipments to begin in mid-December.

Most buyers will have to wait. SpaceX has not announced when general sales will open, what the terminal will cost without the year of service, or what it will cost in other countries. Its website lists more customers and countries as "coming soon", with no dates attached.

## Why it matters

The original Mini's biggest practical limit was its dependence on power: in the field it had to stay tethered to a vehicle, a power station, or a wall. A built-in, swappable battery makes the terminal genuinely portable for travellers, campers, remote workers, and anyone needing connectivity during a power cut — a use case SpaceX has leaned into in its announcement. The bundle pricing also matters: with a year of service valued at more than twice the $999 package price, the launch is effectively a service-led offer, and the standalone hardware price remains unknown.

## What is still unknown

The 4.5-hour figure is SpaceX's own claim, and no independent test of it has been published yet. Spare batteries will be sold separately, but SpaceX has not said what they will cost. Separately, code spotted on Starlink's website before the announcement hinted at a $249 kit and a $50 spare battery, but SpaceX has not confirmed either figure, and they should not be confused with the $999 invitation package.

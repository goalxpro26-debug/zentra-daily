Rescuers in Ukraine have pulled a seven-year-old boy alive from the rubble of a collapsed apartment building in Dymer, about 14 hours after a Russian strike hit the town in the Kyiv region.

The boy was in stable condition and was taken to hospital, Ukrainian authorities said. His rescue was confirmed by President Volodymyr Zelensky and by Ukraine's national police.

The strike hit the five-storey residential building at about 05:00 local time on Sunday, while families were still asleep, according to Ukraine's Minister of Internal Affairs, Ivan Vyhivskyi. The building's apartments collapsed across all five floors, and emergency crews have been searching the wreckage for survivors ever since.

At least 11 people were killed in Dymer, Ukrainian officials said. A 79-year-old woman was also pulled alive from the same building and taken to hospital. Six people were injured in the attack, according to Kyiv regional head Tymur Tkachenko, and others were still missing as the search continued.

More than 130 police officers, explosives experts, sniffer dog teams and medics are working at the site, Zelensky said, adding that the operation would continue until there was complete information about every person in the building. He said the rescue effort was being complicated by constant new air-raid alerts and renewed threats.

The Dymer strike was part of a wider overnight attack across the Kyiv region. A strike on a private home in Bila Tserkva killed one person and set the building ablaze, officials said. In Vyshneve, three people were killed and 13 were injured when a grocery store was hit on Sunday, and the town's mayor, Illya Dikov, said three children were among the injured.

Vyhivskyi said that by Sunday evening, more than 70 strikes had been launched across 10 Ukrainian regions.

The rescue in Dymer drew praise from Ukrainian officials, who thanked everyone involved in saving the boy's life. The search for people still trapped under the rubble was continuing on Monday.

Russia and Ukraine held their first U.S.-mediated talks in nine months on Tuesday, meeting in Abu Dhabi for nearly four hours in a renewed push to end a war that began with Moscow's full-scale invasion in February 2022.

The session, the first direct meeting between the sides since February, is scheduled to continue for a second day on Wednesday. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the opening day "very productive," while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described it as "constructive and quite substantive."

Neither Rubio nor Zelenskyy attended the talks in the capital of the United Arab Emirates. Washington was represented by President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and envoy Steve Witkoff, who are trying to broker a settlement. Russia's chief negotiator is Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky; the Ukrainian delegation is headed by Rustem Umerov.

## Humanitarian questions first

According to Zelenskyy, the first day was largely about humanitarian issues, including prisoner exchanges and the return of Ukrainian civilians held in captivity, as well as children taken from the country. The two-day session was also expected to take up future security guarantees for the warring countries, the White House said before the discussions began.

Hopes of a breakthrough are tempered by the record so far. Three previous rounds of talks earlier this year produced no significant breakthroughs, and the two sides remain far apart on the core questions of territory and the shape of any deal. Russia wants Ukraine to pull back troops and abandon its bid to join NATO — terms Zelenskyy has rejected.

## Fighting goes on

The talks did not pause the war. Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile at Ukraine overnight, Ukraine's air force said, and analysts and humanitarian organizations say the conflict has killed hundreds of thousands of soldiers and thousands of civilians.

The Trump administration has signalled it believes ending the war may require a broader agreement with Moscow on Europe's security — a position European officials see as an early concession to Russia. Trump said last week that a wider deal was in the works and would "involve more than Ukraine."

Whether a second day of talks produces anything concrete remains to be seen. For now, the fact that the two sides are in the same room again — after nine months apart — is the news.

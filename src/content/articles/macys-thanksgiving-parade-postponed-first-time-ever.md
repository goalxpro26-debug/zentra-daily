New York's Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will not march on Thanksgiving Day this year. Organisers have postponed the 100th edition to Saturday, the first postponement in the parade's century-long history, as a powerful storm bears down on the city.

Macy's announced the decision on Wednesday, after consulting city officials, saying the parade had been moved "out of an abundance of caution." The rescheduled parade will begin at 8:30 a.m. ET on Saturday, November 28.

The storm is a nor'easter expected to bring intense wind and heavy rain to the region through the holiday. The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for New York City for Thursday into Friday, with gusts potentially reaching 60 mph. City officials banned empty tractor-trailers from bridges on Thursday morning because of the wind.

The danger was clearest for the parade's giant balloons, which were already being filled on Wednesday when the postponement was announced; workers were then seen deflating them. High winds can push the balloons off course between Manhattan's skyscrapers and over the crowds below, and the city has long had rules for lowering or grounding them when the wind gets too strong.

At a news conference, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday's winds would be strong enough to snap tree limbs and bring down power lines, and that rescheduling was the right call. "I know that this change of plans will cause inconveniences, yet safety has to come first," he said.

NBC, which broadcasts the parade, said it would instead air a repeat of the 2025 edition on Thursday.

It was not immediately clear how much of the parade's large lineup could shift to the new date. Representatives for the Broadway shows "The Rocky Horror Show," "Wicked" and "Hamilton" said their performers would take part on Saturday, and the University of Connecticut's marching band, which had been due to perform in the parade for the first time, will also make the rescheduled event. The University of Alabama's Million Dollar Band, however, will head home to perform at a football game.

Macy's executive producer Will Coss said organisers were working with bands and other performers and were "optimistic that we're going to be able to maintain the parade that we set out to produce." A company spokesperson confirmed it was the first time the event had ever been postponed.

The parade began in 1924 and was suspended from 1942 to 1944 during World War II, but it had never before been moved because of weather. Its balloons have been grounded only once, in 1971. The city's wind rules were tightened after high winds sent a balloon into a metal pole during the 1997 parade, leaving a woman in a coma.

For at least one family, the delay was good news: Sarah Collins and her youngest son had not planned to see the parade because their flight from Florida arrives on Friday. Now, she said, it is part of their plans — and he is excited.

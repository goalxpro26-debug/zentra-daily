US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia should look outside the company for its next chief executive, arguing that the chipmaker needs a "dramatic shakeup" after the sudden resignation of founder Jensen Huang last week.

"This makes me think that as the board conducts a search for a new leader that they need to go outside the company, that just with what was going on inside the company, they need a dramatic shakeup," Bessent told Fox News' "Sunday Morning Futures" show.

Huang stepped down on Thursday after 33 years at the helm, following revelations of extramarital affairs, including one involving a suspected Chinese spy. The company's board named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive and appointed longtime director Harvey Jones as its first-ever board chair. Nvidia has said it will consider both internal and external candidates for the permanent job.

## Ten days to answer on export compliance

Bessent also put a deadline on a second front. He said Nvidia's board and its outside counsel should answer within 10 days questions about the company's culture of compliance with export restrictions — including whether it pursued chip sales to China that appeared to break the rules.

Bloomberg reported that the questions concern sales routed through Singapore shell companies, and that Bessent blamed the Commerce Department for stalling the trade-blacklist listing of Megaspeed, the Singapore firm at the centre of the affair. Huang has acknowledged a relationship with Alice Huang, the former chief executive of Megaspeed, who has been identified in media reports as an alleged Chinese intelligence asset. Megaspeed has said Alice Huang left the company in 2025 and denied wrongdoing.

The Financial Times reported on Friday that Nvidia's board has approached Rene Haas, chief executive of British chip designer Arm Holdings, as a possible successor.

## A company in transition

The intervention is the sharpest yet from Washington since Huang's departure plunged the world's most valuable chipmaker into crisis. Nvidia, valued at about $4 trillion, reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday — its first report without Huang at the helm.

Huang has also agreed to a closed-door interview with three House committees on December 1, and the Senate Banking Committee has asked him to testify the same week.

What happens next rests with the board: whether it follows Bessent's advice and hires from outside, and what its answers on export compliance show when the 10-day clock runs out around November 25.

The Trump administration quietly encouraged Venezuela and Iraq to consider leaving OPEC earlier this year, in a campaign to weaken the oil cartel that was only halted after Saudi Arabia's crown prince stepped in, according to a Reuters investigation published on Friday.

Washington urged the two countries — both founding members of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries — to weigh quitting the group, pressing them at the same time to raise oil production and investment. Some U.S. officials were concerned that OPEC's production limits could constrain those ambitions, the report said, citing people familiar with the matter.

The pressure on Venezuela went further. The administration prevented Caracas from paying its annual OPEC membership dues, blocking a bill of about 2.8 million euros, roughly $3.08 million. The State Department did not say directly whether it had blocked the payment, but confirmed that Washington reviews disbursement requests from Venezuela's interim government against approved and prohibited uses of foreign government deposit funds held in a U.S.-controlled account, following the ousting of Nicolás Maduro in January. Reuters said it could not establish exactly when the dues payment request was rejected.

OPEC is not planning to expel Venezuela over the unpaid fee, according to two OPEC sources. Neither Venezuela nor Iraq has left the organisation.

The campaign appears to have been stopped by an intervention from the top of the cartel itself. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman stepped in this summer to persuade the Trump administration to stop pressuring Venezuela over its OPEC membership, according to two people familiar with the matter. Saudi Arabia is OPEC's largest producer and its de facto leader.

Iraq came closer to acting on the idea. Prime Minister Ali Faleh al-Zaidi raised the possibility of leaving OPEC at a government meeting this summer, after discussions with U.S. officials who had encouraged the move. Iraq's oil ministry pushed back, persuading al-Zaidi to keep Iraq in the group and instead seek a higher production quota. The issue could return: some elements of Iraq's ruling coalition favour leaving OPEC and its wider OPEC+ grouping, the report said.

The episode marks an unusually direct challenge to an organisation that President Donald Trump has long accused of keeping oil prices high. OPEC was founded in 1960 by Iraq, Venezuela, Iran, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, and its production decisions still shape global energy prices. Washington, now the world's largest oil producer, has spent decades criticising the cartel — but this year, according to the report, it went beyond criticism and tried to pull two of its founders out.

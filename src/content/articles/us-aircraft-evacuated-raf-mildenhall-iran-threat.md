The United States military evacuated aircraft from a second airbase in Britain on Monday after a reported threat from Iran, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter.

Two U.S. refuelling tankers operating from RAF Mildenhall in Suffolk were diverted to Germany early on Monday, and several more tankers stationed at the base were moved to Germany and other locations later in the day, according to the report.

It is the second British base from which American aircraft have been withdrawn in recent weeks. The move follows the removal of U.S. bombers from RAF Fairford in southwest England after a suspected Iran-linked security incident. According to The National, that incident involved seven arrests.

## Security raised at two bases

The U.S. also raised the security posture at RAF Mildenhall and the nearby RAF Lakenheath to Force Protection Condition Charlie, the second-highest defensive threat level in the U.S. Department of Defense system. The level is used when intelligence indicates that a form of terrorist action is likely.

RAF Mildenhall hosts U.S. Air Force personnel and units, including the tankers that support long-range operations. RAF Fairford had been used by U.S. bombers to carry out strikes on Iran, and Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps had previously declared Fairford and similar bases legitimate targets, The National reported.

The precise nature of the threat to RAF Mildenhall remains under discussion among U.S. officials, according to the Wall Street Journal. It is not clear whether the threat was imminent or whether the move was precautionary.

## What is still unknown

No U.S. or British official has publicly described the threat in detail, and neither government has confirmed the exact number of aircraft moved. What is confirmed is that tankers left Mildenhall for Germany and other locations on Monday, and that the alert level at Mildenhall and Lakenheath has been raised. Further clarity is likely to come from official statements in the coming days.

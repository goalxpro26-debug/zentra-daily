Options traders are pricing in a roughly $335 billion swing in Nvidia's market value after the chipmaker reports third-quarter earnings on Tuesday afternoon — the latest sign of how much uncertainty now hangs over the world's most closely watched stock.

The options market implies an 8.5% move in either direction on Wednesday, the day after results, according to Susquehanna. That is more than double Nvidia's average post-earnings move over the past eight quarters, and only just short of the 8.74% jump that followed its previous report. The implied swing is larger than the entire market value of about 90% of the companies in the S&P 500.

Nvidia arrives at earnings in the middle of a steep slide. Its market value stood at about $3.9 trillion on Friday, down from roughly $5.8 trillion in early October, according to LSEG data. Shares fell a further 4% on Monday and are down nearly 17% this year, while the S&P 500 is up 8% over the same period.

## A leadership crisis on top of a market one

The selloff accelerated after chief executive Jensen Huang resigned last week, following reports that he had affairs with an alleged Chinese spy and with a subordinate at the company. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim chief executive while the board searches for a permanent successor.

President Donald Trump has said he would not oppose Huang's return, and a petition calling for him to come back has gathered more than 178,000 signatures, including from prominent Silicon Valley executives. But Nvidia's board is not weighing a return for Huang, three people told Reuters.

Investors have responded by trimming their holdings ahead of earnings — while continuing to buy call options and call spreads, contracts that would pay off if the stock rebounds, said Chris Murphy, co-head of derivatives strategy at Susquehanna. "Earnings are the main catalyst over the next three days, but that pricing also captures ongoing headline risk — including new company developments and Trump comments — rather than earnings alone," he said.

## What investors will be listening for

Sentiment toward chipmakers and the AI infrastructure buildout has turned negative, with rising borrowing costs and concern that growth in the sector could slow after Democrats swept the midterm elections. The question now is whether Nvidia can reassure the market that demand for AI infrastructure remains strong.

"It will be forward-looking statements about the order book, customers possibly canceling and pushing out, whether data center regulatory issues mean that data centers are going to take longer than we thought," said Chris Grisanti, chief market strategist at MAI Capital Management.

Grisanti added that the underlying case for the stock may be simpler than the noise suggests: "There's less here than meets the eye, in the sense of data center growth will continue in '27. Nvidia is the monopoly provider of chips, and it's not hard to connect the dots to a higher stock price with those two things."

## What is still unknown

Tuesday's report is the company's first since Huang's departure, and the first earnings call led by Kress. Whether the numbers and guidance are enough to steady a stock that has lost nearly a third of its value from its October peak — or whether the options market's big swing materialises to the downside — will be clear by Wednesday's open.

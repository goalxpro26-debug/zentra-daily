Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told the Senate Armed Services Committee on Tuesday that the war with Iran is straining the United States Navy, as senators from both parties pressed him on long deployments, shrinking weapons stockpiles and the welfare of sailors at his confirmation hearing.

"This war, again, I know it's a strain on our Navy," Cao said, while arguing that the joint military campaign had "really set China back on their heels". The hearing was his bid for the permanent top civilian job. President Donald Trump nominated him in September, and he has served as acting secretary since April, after the departure of his predecessor, John Phelan.

The war is now in its ninth month. Senator Elizabeth Warren cited estimates that the naval blockade of Iranian shipping costs about $32.5 million a day, and that Iranian strikes on the Navy's regional headquarters in Bahrain caused at least $400 million in damage. Cao confirmed the blockade figure but said he could not assess the Bahrain damage in full.

Much of the hearing focused on the crew of the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln. Cao confirmed that eight sailors in the carrier strike group attempted suicide during an extended deployment supporting the war — a figure he had disclosed to lawmakers in a letter in September. "You see it as eight suicide attempts; I see it as eight suicide mitigations," he told Senator Richard Blumenthal, pointing to the mental-health staff aboard. The Lincoln returned to San Diego in early October after a record-breaking deployment of more than eight months, and the United States is currently massing three carrier strike groups in the region.

Senators also pressed Cao on weapons. The conflict has drawn down stockpiles of air defence interceptors and Tomahawk cruise missiles, and lawmakers questioned whether the Navy can keep supplying the blockade while preparing for a possible conflict with China. Cao said production had risen sharply — the Navy now makes "well above 60" Tomahawks a month, against 125 a year when he was in uniform — and said unmanned vessels could eventually relieve destroyers on blockade duty.

Cao's path to confirmation is not straightforward. He retired from active duty in October 2021, so he needs a congressional waiver from the law requiring seven years out of uniform before a former officer can lead a military service — an exemption that needs 60 votes in the Senate. He also faced questions about the Navy quietly removing the entire leadership team of the submarine USS South Dakota after an underwater collision in October, without telling Congress. Committee chair Roger Wicker voiced support for the nomination; ranking Democrat Jack Reed said he was troubled by Cao's leadership record.

The hearing came days after Trump said further strikes on Iran were "quite unlikely" and the military phase of the war "effectively over", while adding that US forces remain "locked and loaded" if conditions change. Whether the Senate grants Cao the waiver is still to be decided.

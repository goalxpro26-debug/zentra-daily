European leaders closed ranks in Berlin on Thursday, telling Washington and Moscow that no peace deal for Ukraine can be struck without Europe and Kyiv at the table — hours after Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping held a three-way summit in China.

The Coalition of the Willing, the group of nearly 40 countries backing Ukraine, met in the German capital with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, French President Emmanuel Macron, British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk and Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, alongside NATO chief Mark Rutte, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner.

In a joint statement, the leaders of Germany, France, Britain and Ukraine said they were committed to a comprehensive, just and lasting peace, and that the security of Europe depended on it. They condemned Russia's missile and drone attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure, pledged long-term support with air defence the priority, and promised more pressure on Russian oil and gas revenues and the shadow fleet carrying them.

The statement also set out what Europe wants in a settlement: robust and legally binding security guarantees once a ceasefire takes effect, a European-led multinational force for Ukraine backed by the United States, and direct talks between Kyiv and Moscow with the US and Europe involved. As a first step, the leaders called on Russia to agree to an energy ceasefire for the winter. They also insisted that Ukraine's right to choose its own security arrangements must be fully respected.

Merz was blunt about the Shenzhen meeting, calling it a "photo op" and warning that a "Yalta format cannot bring peace". He said the war's outcome would shape European security for years to come, and pointed to 45 billion euros earmarked by the European Commission for Ukraine next year. Kushner, speaking after Trump joined the Berlin meeting by phone, said Europe was "very united and very, very strong" in its support, and that Russia was "comfortable" with the idea of NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine.

Macron said the immediate priority was helping Ukraine defend itself, including air-defence systems and interceptors, and argued that the question of Ukraine's accession to NATO should be reopened. Zelenskyy said it was crucial that the United States use its influence on Russia, which he said had rejected every proposal for de-escalation. Britain announced a 260 million pound winter support package, including 1,000 long-range strike drones and long-range cruise missiles.

No date has been set for negotiations, and the leaders acknowledged they were preparing for the possibility that talks fail. But the message from Berlin was consistent: Europe intends to be in the room when the war's end is negotiated — while preparing Ukraine for a winter of war.

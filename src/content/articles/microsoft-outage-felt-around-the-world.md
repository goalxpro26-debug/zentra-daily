A Microsoft outage that began on Thursday has now touched at least 74 countries, and the list of places feeling it reads like a tour of public life: courts, hospitals, parliaments, police forces and stock exchanges.

The failure was first detected after midday on the U.S. East Coast on Thursday, 12 November. Microsoft 365 services — including Outlook email, Teams and the AI assistant Copilot — have been suffering connectivity problems ever since, and the company had not restored full service by Friday morning.

Microsoft has said the cause was "elevated load" on its infrastructure across several regions, after a number of servers became "unexpectedly unavailable" and pushed extra traffic onto the machines still running. A Microsoft executive, Charles Lamanna, said the incident was not caused by a cyberattack. The company has also warned that its early fixes were not working "as expected".

## The disruption, country by country

In the United States, staff in the House of Representatives were unable to use Outlook, and court communications were delayed from Los Angeles to Texas. The City of Austin reported its Outlook email was unavailable, though phone lines and the 911 emergency service were unaffected. Workhorse Group, an electric-vehicle maker, pushed back its third-quarter results from 12 November to 16 November.

In Canada, Unity Health Toronto declared a "code grey" — its alert for infrastructure failure — while stressing that patient care was unaffected. In Japan, some companies went back to the fax machine, and government systems were disrupted in both Japan and Singapore. The Philippines Supreme Court reported delayed email responses, and users in India reported disrupted Teams calls and email.

Australia's spy and federal police agencies were affected, though the impact was described as minimal and largely confined to email. In Europe, the stock-exchange operator Euronext advised listed companies to use the phone, while in Britain, GPs, police, pharmacies and members of parliament were among those hit. The UK Parliament said it was working with Microsoft to resolve the matter as quickly as possible, with alternative ways of communicating in place.

The outage also spilled beyond Microsoft's own products: both OpenAI and Anthropic reported problems, because their tools connect to workplace files and email through Microsoft 365.

## Why one failure travelled so far

More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps, according to Microsoft, so a fault in the shared infrastructure behind Outlook and Teams reaches far beyond office workers. The same cloud services now carry court filings, hospital administration, government correspondence and the tools other companies build on top of.

That is also why the recovery is slow. Microsoft says it is carefully reintroducing traffic as it brings servers back, and that capacity has not yet returned to normal. There is still no time given for a full restoration.

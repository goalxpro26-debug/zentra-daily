President Donald Trump said on Monday he would not object to Jensen Huang returning as chief executive of Nvidia, adding his voice to a campaign to reinstate the chipmaker's founder less than a week after Huang stepped down.

Speaking to reporters as he left the White House for a trip to the Philippines and China, Trump said Huang had "learned a big lesson" and that he would welcome a comeback. "I would certainly not mind seeing that," he said. "He could return. There's a big group of people that would like him to return."

Huang resigned last Thursday after reports of extramarital affairs, one of them with Alice Huang, a Chinese national and former executive at the chip distributor Megaspeed whom US officials reportedly suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. The two share a surname but are not related. China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy.

Trump said he did not know whether she was a spy and that he would investigate, but insisted the relationship had not endangered the country. "It was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before," he said. "You know, China plays games with us, and we play games with China."

The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Huang was considering a comeback and had discussed the possibility with confidants. An online petition calling for his reinstatement has gathered more than 177,000 signatures, according to the site hosting it, including those of Elon Musk, the head of SpaceX and Tesla, Replit chief executive Amjad Masad and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney. More than 8,400 signatories identified themselves as Nvidia employees and nearly 90,000 as shareholders.

Whether Huang returns is not Trump's decision to make. Only Nvidia's board can reinstate him, and its own investigation into the circumstances of his departure is still under way. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim chief executive. The BBC reported that the likelihood of a return is seen as low while the internal inquiries continue, and Trump said the FBI was also investigating the affair.

The episode has divided the administration. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has said Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" and should hire its next chief from outside the company, while Trump floated Apple's Tim Cook as a possible successor last week. The scandal has also put pressure on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over whether he moved fast enough to restrict AI chip shipments to China; Trump said on Monday he had no plans to replace Lutnick, who he said was "doing a very good job."

Trump said he might raise the matter with Chinese President Xi Jinping during this week's trip. Nvidia, meanwhile, reports its third-quarter results on Tuesday, its first earnings announcement since its founder's departure.

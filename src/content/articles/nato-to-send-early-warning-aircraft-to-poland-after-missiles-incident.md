NATO will send one of its AWACS early-warning aircraft to Poland after three Russian missiles landed in the country last week, Poland's deputy defence minister said on Monday.

Pawel Zalewski told reporters in Brussels that the aircraft would be made available to Poland following a meeting of NATO allies. "This is a very good system that gives us even deeper... operational awareness which allows us to respond better to this kind of threat," he said.

Three Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles came down in south-eastern Poland on Friday, during a large overnight Russian attack on Ukraine. Prosecutors confirmed the missiles had landed on Polish soil, adding to fears that the war in Ukraine could spill into NATO territory. No one was injured and no infrastructure was damaged, according to Polish officials.

NATO's AWACS fleet — 14 aircraft with distinctive nine-metre-wide radar domes — has served as the alliance's eyes in the sky since 1982. Based at Geilenkirchen air base in Germany, the planes have played a central role in surveillance along NATO's eastern flank since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. In a conflict, they can build a shared radar picture for allied fighters, ships and command centres, and direct combat jets to their targets.

The deployment is the first concrete allied response since the incursion. Poland had asked NATO for more help guarding its airspace, including AWACS aircraft and F-35 fighter jets, and the alliance's North Atlantic Council met in Brussels on Monday to discuss the incident. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called the airspace violation a "dangerous incident" and said the alliance "will defend every inch of Polish territory."

The incident was the latest in a series of Russian weapons straying into the airspace of NATO's eastern-flank states during the war. Poland and the Baltic countries have been the most affected, and Warsaw revised its air-defence procedures earlier this year to let fighter pilots decide for themselves whether to shoot down intruding objects.

What happens next is not yet clear. Warsaw has not said when the AWACS aircraft will arrive or how long it will stay, and NATO has not announced any further reinforcements. Russia has not publicly commented on the missiles landing in Poland.

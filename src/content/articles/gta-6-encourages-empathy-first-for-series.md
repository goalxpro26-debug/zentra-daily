Grand Theft Auto VI will be the first game in the series designed to encourage empathy, Rockstar Games has said, five days before the long-awaited release on November 19.

In a hands-on interview with The Guardian, published in the paper's Saturday magazine, Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson said the change grows out of the story the game is telling rather than from any rule imposed on the player. "We've tried for the first time in a GTA to provide mechanisms that encourage it, for those who want to," he said.

The heart of that approach is the relationship between the game's two protagonists, Jason and Lucia, a young couple trying to make their way in the fictional state of Leonida. "The relationship is yours to nurture or yours to lose," Nelson said — a marked shift for a series long defined by consequence-free chaos.

## A slower kind of crime

The interview describes a more deliberate opening than fans may expect. Jason and Lucia begin as small-time criminals, hotwiring cheap cars and scraping by, rather than arriving as established kingpins. In the demo shown to The Guardian, the first person the pair meet outside their apartment is a woman walking her dog — and Jason asks her permission before petting it.

Players can also listen in on passers-by's conversations, and interrupting them can draw a puzzled reply or a stream of abuse. The game, the report says, steers players toward acting in character rather than going on aimless rampages, in a similar spirit to Red Dead Redemption 2.

That does not mean the old Grand Theft Auto is gone. Nelson was clear that crime remains fundamental to the game and that Rockstar is not punishing players for playing the way they always have. The empathy mechanics are optional, and actions will affect how the game plays out rather than locking anyone into a moral path.

## Not an honour system

The approach is distinct from the honour system in Red Dead Redemption 2. A separate system, called the Criminal Profile, tracks how violently and maliciously players commit crimes over the course of the story — independently of Jason and Lucia's relationship. Rockstar has not explained in detail how the two systems interact, or how far the consequences of a player's choices will reach.

Jennifer Kolbe, Rockstar's head of publishing, told The Guardian the studio resisted making a safer sequel. "They could have made GTA 5.5, and done it faster," she said. Take-Two confirmed development was complete on November 9, and the game launches on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S on November 19, thirteen years after GTA V.

U.S. negotiators raised the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from its eastern Donbas region in exchange for joining NATO, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Saturday — the first time Washington had put the question to Kyiv in talks aimed at ending the war with Russia.

"The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?'" Zelenskyy told reporters in remarks released by his office. "This was the first time this had been raised."

The question came after two days of U.S.-brokered talks between Ukrainian and Russian delegations in Abu Dhabi, which ended on Wednesday without a major breakthrough. Zelenskyy said the idea put NATO membership back on the negotiating agenda — while warning the conversation would be extremely difficult, given President Vladimir Putin's longstanding position that Ukrainian membership of the alliance is a red line for Moscow.

## Kyiv's conditions

Zelenskyy repeated that Kyiv is not prepared to give up territory. But he said that if Western countries confirmed a comprehensive package — security guarantees, a strong and well-funded Ukrainian army, and money to rebuild — he would put the question to the Ukrainian people.

"If the American side confirms precisely this to us, together with other NATO partners, then I will ask our Ukrainian society what it thinks: do we accept it or not?" he said. "I have no right to make that decision on my own."

The account rests on Zelenskyy's description of the exchange. The United States has not publicly confirmed it, and the remarks were given to reporters on Friday under embargo until Saturday, after Zelenskyy met European leaders in Dublin following the Abu Dhabi round.

## A week of shuttle diplomacy

The Abu Dhabi talks did produce one concrete result: the two sides agreed to a prisoner exchange involving more than 1,000 people, though final numbers were still under discussion. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner then met Putin in Moscow on Thursday, and Zelenskyy in Ireland later the same day, having pledged to raise an energy ceasefire with Putin — but there was no news of progress. Zelenskyy said Putin "personally does not support a ceasefire" and that Russia had rejected a halt to strikes on energy targets.

Zelenskyy also pushed back against Moscow's call for a broad agreement on European security to come before the specifics of ending the war, echoing European leaders who met in Dublin on Friday. "We cannot turn everything upside down and start by talking about changing the security framework in the world or across the continent overall, and only then talk about ending this war. This war must be ended first," he said.

## What is still unknown

Whether Washington will confirm the security package Zelenskyy described, whether any referendum would ever be held, and whether Moscow would accept any arrangement involving NATO membership are all unresolved. The U.S. envoys told Zelenskyy that all sides had agreed Europe would have a seat at the table in forthcoming talks, and EU leaders in Dublin agreed a team of five major military powers — Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — would speak for Europe. No date or format for the next round has been announced.

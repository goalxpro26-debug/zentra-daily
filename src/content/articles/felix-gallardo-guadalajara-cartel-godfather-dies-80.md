Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, the drug lord known as "The Godfather" who built the Guadalajara Cartel into the dominant force in Mexico's drug trade, has died in hospital in his native Guadalajara aged 80.

Félix Gallardo had been admitted to hospital earlier in the week, with Mexican prison police securing the building. He had been in state custody since 1989.

As head of the Guadalajara Cartel, he presided over Mexican drug trafficking at the height of the cocaine boom of the 1980s. He is credited with building the alliance between criminal groups in Colombia and his own cartel — a partnership with the Medellín Cartel that combined production in the Andes with Mexican transport routes across the border into the United States. The arrangement created a new way of working in the illegal drug trade, one that endures to this day.

At its height, the cartel's influence reached deep into Mexican politics and law enforcement, controlling entire police networks and state governments. Félix Gallardo, known as "The Godfather" or the "Boss of Bosses", became one of the biggest drug smugglers in history.

His downfall began with the murder of a US federal agent. In 1985, Drug Enforcement Administration agent Enrique "Kiki" Camarena was tortured and murdered after destroying a vast marijuana plantation in Chihuahua belonging to the criminal group. The killing set off a major US manhunt. In 1989, Félix Gallardo was captured and sentenced to decades in prison, and his organisation broke apart.

He spent the rest of his life in custody. In ill health, he was granted house arrest in 2022, but technical problems with electronic ankle monitoring prevented his transfer, and he remained under guard until his death.

Félix Gallardo's life has been portrayed in a number of television programmes, including the Netflix series Narcos: Mexico. His death closes the chapter on the first generation of Mexico's modern drug kingpins — but the trafficking model he pioneered, a federation of groups moving drugs from South American producers to US markets through Mexico, outlived him by decades and still shapes the trade today.

Amazon and Google could soon own more than a third of Anthropic, with a combined stake worth roughly $400 billion at the price the artificial intelligence company is expected to set for its stock market debut.

An updated prospectus filed on Tuesday lays out the scale of the two technology giants' holdings. Google will own 1.8 billion Anthropic shares once the initial public offering and a concurrent private sale are complete, according to Barron's analysis of the filing. At $74 a share, that stake would be worth about $133 billion.

Amazon will start with 1.9 billion shares. It also holds Anthropic debt that it can choose to convert into stock. If it exercises that option in full, Amazon would hold as many as 3.5 billion shares, valued at about $260 billion at the same $74 price.

Together, the pair will own 28.4 per cent of Anthropic's outstanding capital stock, and an estimated 36 per cent if Amazon converts the bonds. Both companies will hold Class N shares, a class designed for Anthropic's business partners.

The stakes are the product of years of backing. Alphabet, Google's parent, first funded Anthropic by buying convertible debt that has since been converted into equity, and this year agreed to invest up to $30 billion more. Amazon agreed earlier this year to invest $20 billion, of which $5 billion has already gone in, with a further $15 billion tied to computing capacity milestones. About $6.2 billion of Amazon's earlier convertible debt was still outstanding at the end of June.

The relationship runs both ways. Amazon and Google are also Anthropic's critical cloud-service providers, supplying the data-centre capacity that trains and runs its Claude models. Anthropic is committed to more than $475 billion in compute capacity agreements over the next seven to 10 years, and Alphabet and Amazon are each set to receive around $110 billion of that spending.

The filing lands as Anthropic's listing reaches its final hours. The company is telling prospective investors it expects to price its shares at the top end of its $70 to $78 range, or even above it, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, citing people familiar with the matter. The final price is expected late on Wednesday, with the shares due to begin trading on Thursday.

Anthropic is also selling shares at the IPO price to a group of large technology companies, including Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Micron Technology, Microsoft and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing.

What remains uncertain is whether Amazon will convert its debt in full — the decision that determines whether the pair's combined stake stops at 28.4 per cent or rises to about 36 per cent — and the final IPO price, which will fix the dollar value of both holdings.

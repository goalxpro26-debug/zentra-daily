About 1.5 million people gathered in and around Chiclayo, in northern Peru, on Friday as Pope Leo XIV celebrated an outdoor Mass in the city where he once served as bishop. The Vatican estimated that roughly one million people lined the streets along his route, while about 500,000 more attended the Mass itself at the Pampas de Pimentel, a wide open space outside the city.

It was the central event of the pope's 12-day tour of South America, and by several accounts its most emotional stop. Leo, born Robert Prevost in Chicago, spent about 20 years as a missionary in Peru and was bishop of Chiclayo from 2015 to 2023. Earlier in the week he described the country as his homeland, and crowds in Chiclayo still call him by his old name, Father Robert.

In his sermon, Leo said it was a blessing to be back in his adopted home exactly one month after undergoing surgery for lung cancer. He had an operation for lung adenocarcinoma, the most common form of lung cancer, on October 13. Addressing the crowd, he thanked God that he was alive to see them, and told them that all Christians are called to be missionaries.

People arrived on foot and in lines of vehicles at the arid field outside Chiclayo, enduring heat of about 31 degrees Celsius (87 Fahrenheit) to attend. Leo arrived from Lima on Friday morning aboard a plane decorated with "#LeoXIVFliesHome" stickers. The stop in Peru is the final leg of a three-nation tour that also took him to Uruguay and Argentina; the Vatican estimates that about 12 million people have turned out for public events across the trip so far.

Leo is due to remain in Chiclayo until Sunday before the tour moves on. For the city, the visit was a homecoming as much as a papal event: the diocese he led has about 1.5 million inhabitants, nearly all of them Catholic, and his return had been anticipated for days.

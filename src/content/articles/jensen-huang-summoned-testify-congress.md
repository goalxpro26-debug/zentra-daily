Former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has been summoned to testify before the United States Congress about his reported affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset, days after the revelations forced him out of the chipmaker he led for more than three decades.

Senate Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, wrote to Huang and to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Thursday, asking both men to appear before the panel in December. Scott warned that the committee could issue a subpoena if Huang does not appear voluntarily. Separately, three House committees — Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and the panel on China — have called Huang for a closed-door interview on 1 December. The Senate has asked him to appear voluntarily on 3 December.

## The affair at the centre of the inquiry

Huang, 63, admitted on Sunday that he had been unfaithful to his wife of 40 years, Lori Huang, after reports of an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data-centre company. The two are not related. American intelligence has reportedly suspected Alice Huang of being an asset of Chinese intelligence — an allegation Beijing has rejected as a smear and which has not been established. The Justice Department said on Thursday it had not yet reached any conclusions about whether she was engaged in espionage for Beijing.

A second alleged affair was reported on Wednesday, involving Kimberly Powell, a long-time Nvidia executive who reported directly to Huang. Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation into whether its former chief executive was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." The company said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised," while pledging that the inquiry would examine all available information.

## Fallout for Nvidia and Lutnick

The scandal has shaken the chipmaker at the centre of the AI boom. Nvidia has lost about $550bn (£415bn) in market value in five days — its steepest five-day fall since it went public 27 years ago. Chief financial officer Colette Kress was named interim chief executive on Thursday.

Lutnick faces questions of his own, over whether the Commerce Department stalled efforts to tighten export controls on advanced AI chips bound for China. A department spokesperson said he had "proactively" agreed to appear before the Senate committee. Unnamed administration officials have told American media that Lutnick slowed moves to toughen the controls.

## What is still unknown

Much remains unresolved: whether subpoenas will be issued, what lawmakers knew and when, and whether any Nvidia technology or secrets were compromised. Nothing published so far establishes that secrets were passed on, and the espionage allegation against Alice Huang remains unproven.

Wall Street staged one of its wildest turnarounds in years on Wednesday, as the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 1,100 points in early trading and then recovered the entire loss to close higher — its biggest intraday reversal into positive territory since 2022.

The Dow finished up 164.25 points, or 0.33%, at 50,182.95. At its low of the day it had been down about 2.2%. The S&P 500 also clawed back most of its losses but still ended 0.25% lower at 7,182.78, after falling as much as 3.2% during the session. The Nasdaq Composite closed 0.62% lower at 23,585.80, having been down as much as 4.6% at its worst.

The selling began with a batch of morning economic data that gave investors two worries at once. The second estimate of third-quarter growth came in at a 4.3% annual rate, well above the earlier 3.4% reading, while the core inflation measure watched by the Federal Reserve hit a three-year high. Strong growth paired with sticky inflation raised doubts about further interest-rate cuts, and a fresh tariff threat added to the pressure.

Chip stocks bore the brunt of it. Nvidia, the market's most watched artificial-intelligence name, ended about 4% lower after a far steeper drop earlier in the day, and the technology sector was the only one of the market's eleven major groups to finish in the red — down just 0.8% after being off 5.5% at its low.

The turn came around midday, when bargain hunters stepped in. Eight of the eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher, and advancing stocks outnumbered decliners by nearly three to one on the New York Stock Exchange. The breadth of the recovery was the session's most encouraging sign: most stocks rose even as the headline indexes stayed mixed, suggesting the selling was concentrated in the big technology names rather than spreading across the whole market.

The rebound also carried the Dow to a second straight close above 50,000, a level it had only recently reached for the first time.

Whether the calm holds is far from settled. The Nasdaq remains close to its lows of the correction, and the same questions that drove the selloff — whether spending on artificial intelligence will pay off, and whether inflation will stay high enough to keep rates elevated — are still unanswered. Volatility stayed high, with the market's fear gauge, the VIX, up more than 7% on the day.

Trading now pauses for Thanksgiving. US markets are closed on Thursday and reopen for a shortened session on Friday, closing at 1 p.m. Eastern time.

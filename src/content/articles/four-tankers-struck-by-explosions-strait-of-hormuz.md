Four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's military said on Thursday, adding that the vessels had ignored warnings and sailed into areas it considers restricted.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two supertankers were disabled by explosions on Wednesday after they ignored warnings against using what it described as an unauthorised route. Iranian state media reported that the two vessels exploded after hitting naval mines and broadcast what it said was video footage of the incidents. The Guards said the two supertankers were carrying a combined total of more than four million barrels of oil and were left ablaze.

Later on Thursday, Iran's army said two other tankers had hit mines in the eastern part of the strait after ignoring warnings and disabling their radar systems, according to state television. The statements did not name the tankers or give their nationalities, and no casualty figures were reported.

Reuters, which first reported the Iranian statements, said it could not immediately verify the Iranian account.

## A strait still under attack

The incidents come despite a rebound in shipping traffic through the strait and expectations of a de-escalation in the conflict between the United States and Iran — expectations that had been reflected in falling oil prices. The Strait of Hormuz normally carries about one-fifth of the world's oil, and the United States has responded to the attacks with a naval blockade of Iranian ports while helping tankers cross the waterway.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency said in reports on Thursday that a vessel had been hit by an unidentified projectile about 14 nautical miles northwest of the United Arab Emirates emirate of Ras Al-Khaimah on Wednesday while leaving the strait.

Attacks on shipping in the strait have continued at a rapid pace. At least 10 ships were hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October — the highest monthly tally since the conflict began in late February, according to data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy.

## What is still unknown

The cause of the explosions has not been independently established, and the names and nationalities of the four tankers have not been released. It is also unclear whether the vessels are still burning or have been towed, and whether the latest incidents will disrupt traffic through the strait or push oil prices higher in the coming days.

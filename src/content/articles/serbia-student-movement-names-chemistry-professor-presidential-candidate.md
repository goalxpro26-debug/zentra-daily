Serbia's student movement on Friday named chemistry professor Radomir Saicic as its candidate for the early presidential election on December 27, betting that the academic who stood with its street protests can carry its October parliamentary victory into the presidency.

Saicic, 65, teaches organic chemistry at the University of Belgrade and is a member of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts. He was among the first academics to back the student protests in public, spoke often at their rallies, and stood as one of 250 candidates on the Students List in the October parliamentary vote. Announcing his candidacy in Belgrade, he said it was time to restore dignity to the presidency, arguing that former president Aleksandar Vucic had overstepped the office's authority, and promised to stick to the constitutional mandate and explain his decisions in public. The movement is campaigning under the slogan "students win again."

The vote comes weeks after the student-backed list defeated Vucic's right-wing Serbian Progressive Party in an early parliamentary election on October 25, ending 14 years in which Vucic dominated Serbian politics. Vucic resigned the presidency in September to run for prime minister, a bid that failed; having served two near-complete five-year terms, he was barred by law from seeking a third.

The presidency is largely ceremonial under Serbia's constitution — it commands the army and has a say in foreign policy but holds no executive power — though Vucic concentrated authority in the office during his time there and faced accusations of authoritarian rule. Analysts expect his party would struggle against the Students List candidate, and Vucic has suggested the party could boycott the vote. It is not yet clear who else will stand.

The movement grew out of two years of street protests that began after a train station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad, killing 16 people. Many blamed corruption and negligence in the station's renovation. Since losing power, Vucic has accused the election winners of imposing "revolutionary terror", while the outgoing government remains technically in charge until a new one is formed. The change has already led to detentions of officials and police officers suspected of abusing their positions under his rule.

Parliament Speaker Aleksandar Cupic, serving as acting president, formally called the election for December 27 the day before the announcement.

Mexican military forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro," in a nighttime raid in Sinaloa on Friday, in one of the most significant cartel arrests in Mexico in years. Meza Flores, 44, was one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives; his detention set off hours of cartel violence across the state.

Mexico's defence ministry said special forces from the army and the National Guard, working with US intelligence, used "stealth and surprise" to surround a house in Guasave, a city in northern Sinaloa. As troops established a security perimeter, they came under attack with gunfire and explosives from suspected cartel gunmen, then entered the house and detained him. He was flown to Mexico City. Security officials identified the two others as German "N," known as "Serafin," said to be his second-in-command, and Jose "N," described as the organisation's financial operator.

Meza Flores leads the Beltran Leyva criminal organisation and is an ally of the Los Mayos faction of the Sinaloa Cartel, according to Mexican authorities. The US State Department says he and his organisation spent more than 20 years moving heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States. The FBI added him to its Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list in February 2025, offering a $5 million reward, and he has been indicted in the US on drug-trafficking charges since 2012. Despite the shared nickname — "Chapo" means "shorty" — he is not related to Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman, the former Sinaloa Cartel leader now jailed in the United States.

The arrest set off a wave of violence across Sinaloa. Gunmen set up flaming roadblocks, torched cars, trucks and petrol stations, and attacked federal forces in several areas. In Guasave, crowds fled a baseball game as gunfire rang out, and videos showed helicopters circling over homes. Businesses were burned, including convenience stores, kiosks and pharmacies, according to local officials. Security Secretary Omar Garcia Harfuch said armed forces would remain deployed in the state to protect civilians, calling the detentions "an important step in pacifying Sinaloa."

The capture is the latest in a string of high-profile blows against Mexico's cartels. In February, the military killed Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes, known as "El Mencho," the leader of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, in an operation that also set off a burst of violence. President Claudia Sheinbaum has pursued the cartels more aggressively than her predecessors, and homicides have fallen during her two years in office — but she is under intense pressure from US President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly threatened unilateral military action inside Mexico if her government does not do more against the cartels. The Mexican government said US intelligence helped in the Guasave operation but gave few details about how the two countries coordinated.

Meza Flores is wanted in Mexico on organised-crime and drug charges and is subject to a US extradition order. US agencies did not immediately comment on the detention, and no timetable for any extradition has been announced.

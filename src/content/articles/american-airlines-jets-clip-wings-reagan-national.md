Two American Airlines Boeing 737s clipped wings while taxiing for departure at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport on Friday evening, in an incident that tore a winglet off one of the jets. No one was hurt, and the Federal Aviation Administration said it would investigate.

The FAA said American Airlines Flight 638, bound for Dallas/Fort Worth, clipped the wing of Flight 2152, bound for Chicago, at about 5:10 p.m. local time. The Dallas-bound jet was preparing to take off when its pilot reported the contact with the Chicago-bound plane waiting next in line, according to recordings of air traffic control radio calls. "We are slightly over the hold short line and unfortunately we swapped paint with the American behind us, next to us," the pilot told controllers.

The winglet — the upturned tip at the end of a wing — was broken off one of the aircraft, leaving debris on the taxiway. "We got debris off our right... the whole winglet is there," a pilot told the tower. The tower told an arriving aircraft to abandon its landing, and other incoming flights were warned of possible delays. Once airport workers recovered the detached wingtip, both jets were able to taxi back to the gate, where they were checked by the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority Fire and Rescue Department.

American Airlines said the wingtips of the two Boeing 737-800s had made contact, and that both aircraft had returned to the gate and been removed from service for inspection by its maintenance team. Passengers on the two flights were due to depart later on Friday evening, and the airline apologised for the inconvenience. A passenger on one of the planes told CNN the contact could be felt through the cabin. "It was really strong. The whole plane like rocked," they said.

The incident happened just off Runway 19, the airport's longest runway. It is the second time in less than 20 months that two American-branded aircraft have collided while waiting to depart from Reagan National, after a similar wingtip collision in April 2025. The airport was also the scene of the deadliest commercial aviation accident in the United States in decades in January 2025, when an American Airlines regional jet and a US Army helicopter collided in mid-air, killing 67 people.

Friday's incident came during the Thanksgiving travel period, when about 6 million Americans were projected to fly domestically, according to AAA, and about two weeks after a nationwide ground stop caused thousands of cancellations across the country.

## What is still unknown

The FAA has not said what caused the two jets to come so close, or how long its investigation will take. It is not yet clear whether the National Transportation Safety Board will join the inquiry, or how much damage the aircraft sustained beyond the torn-off winglet.

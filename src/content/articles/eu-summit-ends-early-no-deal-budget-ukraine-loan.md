European Union leaders ended their extraordinary summit in Brussels a day early on Thursday, after more than 12 hours of talks failed to produce agreement on either the bloc's next seven-year budget or a new €45 billion loan for Ukraine.

The summit had been called to settle two linked questions: how to fund the EU's 2028–2034 budget, worth nearly $1.76 trillion, and how to cover Ukraine's financing needs for 2027. Both were deferred to their next meeting on December 17–18.

On Ukraine, 24 of the 27 member states backed the proposed €45 billion ($49 billion) loan, split between €20 billion in budget support and €25 billion in military aid. But there was no agreement on how the loan should be financed, and several governments oppose using EU funds for military assistance at all. Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia have all objected, with Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš insisting that Ukraine's financing "should be negotiated separately."

The stakes are high. Ukraine faces an estimated €70 billion financing gap in 2027, even after a €90 billion EU loan agreed last year to cover most of its needs for 2026 and 2027. The draft budget sets aside up to €100 billion for Kyiv over seven years, alongside about €740 billion for agriculture and poorer regions, as Russian attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure strain Kyiv's finances.

The budget talks exposed a deeper split over what the EU should spend its money on. Some leaders pushed for tighter belts and new priorities such as defence and competitiveness; others defended the traditional spending that consumes most of the budget. "Europe needs solid finances. We can't and we mustn't take on more debt," German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said. Earlier this month Merz threatened to hold up any budget deal unless all member states made a "collective commitment" to Ukraine, "without exception."

Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni was blunt before the talks began. "We're light-years away from a solution," she said. "We are not willing to sign off on a budget under which we would have to give more to receive less." Hungary's Prime Minister Péter Magyar put the chance of an agreement at "virtually zero" and said a Christmas deal did not look likely either.

French President Emmanuel Macron set a year-end deadline, warning that after December 31 he would be too close to France's presidential election in April to negotiate on his country's behalf. Any budget requires unanimous approval from all 27 member states plus the consent of the European Parliament.

European Council President António Costa, who chaired the summit, had urged leaders to approach the debate with "an open mind, a constructive approach and the sense of compromise." He now has three weeks to produce a compromise before the December meeting. December is the last scheduled chance to settle both negotiations on the terms set by the French and German leaders. In Moscow, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said EU plans to fund Ukraine would make Kyiv "less inclined toward a peace process."

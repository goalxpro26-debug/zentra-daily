Canada has given automaker Stellantis 30 days to fix alleged breaches of a 2022 funding agreement — or face a lawsuit to recover hundreds of millions of dollars in public money. The ultimatum follows the company's decision to sell its idle assembly plant in Brampton, Ontario, and move planned vehicle production to the United States.

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said on Thursday that Ottawa had served the world's fourth-largest carmaker with a formal notice of default. "We sent a notice of default and, basically, they have 30 days or else. Of course, we'll sue them," Joly told reporters in Toronto. She is seeking a response by December 12.

The dispute centres on a 2022 agreement under which the federal government committed up to C$529 million — about US$380 million — to help Stellantis retool its Brampton and Windsor plants for electric and gasoline-powered vehicles, with Ontario pledging a further C$513 million. About C$222 million in federal money had been disbursed as of December 2025.

## What Ottawa says Stellantis did wrong

Joly's letter to Stellantis Canada chief executive Trevor Longley lists several alleged breaches: the suspension of the Brampton retooling in February 2025, the relocation of planned Jeep Compass production to Illinois, and the planned sale of the plant itself. The agreement required the company to maintain its footprint in Ontario through 2035, and barred closing Brampton before 2033 unless the plant was commercially unviable.

Stellantis announced on Monday that it had agreed to sell the Brampton site to Roshel, a Canadian maker of armoured vehicles, for an undisclosed sum. The plant has been idle since 2023, when production stopped for retooling, and the shift leaves more than 2,000 Canadian workers without jobs.

The company has offered to voluntarily return $24 million in federal support received for the original Brampton retooling plan — far less than the hundreds of millions Ottawa is seeking. If Stellantis stays in breach, Joly warned, it could face termination of the funding deal and a demand to repay all or part of the money it received, plus interest.

## A flashpoint in the trade war

The row has become one of the sharpest flashpoints of the US–Canada trade war. Prime Minister Mark Carney has accused Stellantis of betraying Canadian workers, and Unifor — the union representing more than 9,000 Stellantis workers across Canada — said the sale broke the federal government's promise to protect autoworkers. Its president, Lana Payne, said the company had "betrayed" its unionised workforce.

Joly met the company, the union and Ontario Premier Doug Ford in Toronto before issuing the notice.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear whether Stellantis will try to remedy the alleged breaches within 30 days, offer a larger repayment, or let the dispute go to court. The company has not disclosed the price of the Roshel deal. What is certain is the deadline: December 12.

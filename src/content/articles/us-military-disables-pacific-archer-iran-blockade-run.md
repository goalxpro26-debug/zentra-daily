The U.S. military said on Friday it disabled a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman after its crew tried to run the American naval blockade of Iranian ports, the latest interdiction in a confrontation that has now stretched across most of the year.

A U.S. military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer on Thursday, U.S. Central Command said, disabling the vessel's propulsion. The civilian crew was unharmed. The ship had departed Bandar Abbas, a major port city on Iran's southern coast, and ignored repeated warnings to turn back before the strike.

Ship-tracking sites list Singapore as the vessel's destination. Although some early reports described the ship as a tanker, shipping records list the Pacific Archer as a bulk carrier. U.S. Central Command did not identify the cargo it was carrying or say whether the vessel was towed after the strike.

The blockade is the sharp end of Washington's pressure campaign on Tehran. The United States first imposed the naval blockade on Iranian ports in April, lifted it during talks over the summer, and reimposed it in July. Since the blockade resumed, U.S. forces have redirected 158 commercial vessels that complied with instructions and disabled six that refused to — the Pacific Archer is the sixth. It is at least the 15th vessel disabled since the blockade began in the spring. The previous one, the merchant vessel Huda, was disabled on November 7.

Earlier this month the United States also announced Task Force Iron Gate, an effort to restrict the movement of weapons and sanctioned materials across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea, widening the embargo beyond the ports.

Tehran answered the strike with defiance. Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's supreme leader, said Iran would keep the Strait of Hormuz shut and would not allow the blockade to remain in place, according to Iranian state media. He gave no details of how Iran planned to break the embargo.

President Donald Trump defended the blockade while speaking to U.S. service members on Thanksgiving, calling it the most successful naval blockade in history.

What happens next is unclear. Iran has threatened military action at sea and in the air but has not said what form it would take, and it is not known whether the Pacific Archer will be seized, towed, or left where it stopped. For now, the episode underlines how the blockade has become a routine — and still escalating — instrument of the standoff.

At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the war in Ukraine began in 2022, according to Russian government statistics analysed by the BBC — a figure Moscow has never published itself.

The number comes from leaked records of Rosstat, Russia's federal statistics agency. BBC Russian obtained access to more than 11 million death records from 2021 to September 2026, after a hack by Belarus's Cyber Partisans and Ukraine's Silent Crow. Rosstat has admitted a cyber-attack took place, but denies that any protected information was leaked.

With the independent Russian outlet Mediazona, BBC Russian checked the files against its own list of confirmed military deaths, compiled from open sources. The two sets matched in 90% of cases.

The records show how deeply the war has reshaped mortality in Russia. In 2025, 65% of Russians aged 18 to 24 who died lost their lives in the war; for those aged 25 to 34, the figure was 59%. Only among 45- to 54-year-olds does another cause, cardiovascular disease, claim more lives.

The data also put numbers to the fighting in specific places. About 20,000 Russian soldiers died fighting for control of ten Ukrainian villages whose combined pre-war population was 12,000. In the fight for one small village, Andriivka, south of Bakhmut, 1,122 Russians died. Before the war, 74 people lived there.

The dead have grown older as the war has gone on: the average age of those killed was 34 in the first year and 40 by 2025, as prisoners and contract soldiers joined the ranks. The burden has fallen unevenly: men from some of Russia's poorest regions in eastern Siberia — Tyva, Buryatia and Zabaykalsky Krai — are 20 to 25 times more likely to die in the war than residents of Moscow and St Petersburg.

The files also counted at least 5,017 foreigners who died fighting for Russia, including 1,031 Uzbeks and 720 Tajiks. The BBC separately identified 2,304 North Koreans who died repelling Ukraine's 2024 incursion into Russia, from satellite imagery and photographs of a new memorial in Pyongyang.

Russia has released no public figures for its losses since February 2022, and two years ago Rosstat stopped publishing deaths from external causes, the figures analysts used to estimate losses. The Kremlin has not commented.

The 490,334 figure is a floor rather than a ceiling. The dataset covers only officially recorded deaths; it cannot count soldiers listed as missing, bodies never recovered, or men who died later of their wounds. BBC Russian and Mediazona's own open-source count of confirmed deaths stands at 290,012, and military experts cited by the BBC estimate that figure may represent only 45 to 55% of the true toll — putting it between roughly 527,000 and 644,000. Counting the missing as well, Meduza and Mediazona estimate the toll has likely passed 700,000. Ukrainian losses are contested too: Zelensky put them at about 50,000 in July 2026, while the open-source UA Losses list counts 115,952.

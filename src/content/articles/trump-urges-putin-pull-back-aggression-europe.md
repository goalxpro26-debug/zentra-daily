President Donald Trump said on Thursday that he used his talks with Vladimir Putin in China last week to press the Russian leader to pull back military aggression across Europe, framing the request as a step toward a quick deal to end the war in Ukraine.

"During my conversations in China with President Vladimir Putin, of Russia, I spent a lot of time talking about the fact that I would like to see President Putin pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe, in the hopes that a Deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly," Trump wrote in a post on his Truth Social network.

"I really believe this is a strong possibility — Thousands of people are being killed each and every month, A COMPLETE BLOODBATH," he added.

The wording was notably broader than Trump's usual calls for an end to the fighting in Ukraine. He did not say which territories he meant, and he did not specify what conduct he wanted Putin to halt. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in 2022, other countries have repeatedly accused Moscow of provocative behaviour beyond Ukraine's borders. Poland said this week that Russia deliberately violated its airspace, and Estonia recently linked an arson attack to Russia's military intelligence service.

The post came a day after Trump hosted NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte at his Mar-a-Lago club in Florida on Wednesday night. Trump said he updated Rutte on the talks with Putin, and the two leaders issued a joint statement calling on Russia to avoid actions that could expand the war. The tone was a sharp change from a week earlier, when Trump met Putin in the Chinese city of Shenzhen alongside President Xi Jinping and described the Russian leader as his "friend" — a warm reception that drew unease among European leaders.

Speaking to reporters at Mar-a-Lago on Thursday, Trump struck a less certain note about the path to a settlement. He said the biggest obstacle was the animosity between Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who he said "really hate each other." He also said he was unsure whether Putin would attend the G20 summit in Miami next month, and acknowledged that the question of territory in eastern Ukraine claimed by Moscow remained stuck. His envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, have reported that the territorial issue is "a little bit of a deadlock."

The backdrop to the diplomacy remained violent. Zelenskyy warned in his nightly address on Thursday that Ukraine had information Russia was preparing a new mass strike in the coming days, and urged people to heed missile warnings. Overnight, Russia struck Kyiv with ballistic missiles, wounding seven people and hitting a shopping centre and two other buildings, according to the city's mayor. Ukraine, meanwhile, said its drones had hit an oil refinery in Russia's Krasnodar region and other targets deep inside Russia.

The US Department of Justice has charged three men it says were involved in a string of plots in the United States and Europe, including an alleged terrorist plot at RAF Fairford in England in September.

An eight-count indictment was unsealed on Tuesday. It names Mohsen Mohajeri, an Iranian national; Mohammad Ali al-Hassani, an Iraqi national; and Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, a dual Iranian-Iraqi citizen. The charges have not been proven in court.

## What the US alleges

According to the Justice Department, the three men planned attacks in Europe carried out under the name of a purportedly new group, Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya — the Islamic Movement of the People of the Right Hand, known as HAYI.

The department said 18 attacks in the spring of 2026 in Europe were claimed in HAYI's name, seven of them in the UK. Those attacks involved arson and explosives, and in one case a near-fatal stabbing of two Jewish men in Golders Green, north London, in April — one of them a dual US-British citizen. A suspect in that stabbing is due to stand trial next year.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the accused were involved in nearly 20 attempted attacks in support of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and the Iran-backed Iraqi militia Kataib Hezbollah. He said the indictment showed Iran's determination to commit terrorist attacks in Europe and the US.

One of the men, a dual Iranian-Iraqi national, is accused of communicating with contacts in the UK from a prison in New York, where he is awaiting trial on previous charges. The other two are accused of communicating from Iran. Patel said one of the three allegedly sent coded messages from detention to assist in future acts of violence.

## The RAF Fairford incident

A major incident was declared on 27 September near RAF Fairford in the Cotswolds, Gloucestershire, after three suspicious vans were seen near the airbase. No explosive materials were found in the vans, but a quantity of petrol was seized.

The Justice Department said Iranian agents most recently orchestrated a terrorist plot at an air force base in the United Kingdom. It alleges that in August, al-Hassani directed and paid associates in the UK to take preparatory steps for the plot, including paying one person on 22 August to carry out surveillance of the base's perimeter.

In September, the department alleges, he paid several people to help prepare — procuring vans and barrels of fuel, researching chemical accelerants including large quantities of potassium chloride, and buying metallic objects that could be used as explosive shrapnel. The vans were made to look as though they belonged to a fuel delivery business and a swimming pool supply business.

On 26 September, al-Hassani allegedly told individuals to drive the three vans, some carrying barrels of fuel, to the base, staying in frequent contact with the drivers. At least two of the vans met a security roadblock on the way to the perimeter and were turned back. In the early hours of 27 September, a local farmer spotted the vans and called the police.

Seven men have been arrested in the UK over the incident, and two British nationals appeared in court last month in connection with the alleged plot.

## Wider context

RAF Fairford has been used by the US Air Force for decades, and in recent months the US flew B-1 and B-52 bombers from the base to the Middle East for strikes on Iran. The US removed all its bombers from the base last month after the incident, with President Donald Trump citing threats to the base. In July, Iran said any base used for aggression against Iranian territory was a legitimate target.

Iran has strongly denied any involvement in the Fairford incident. The Iranian embassy in London said it categorically rejected what it called unfounded and malicious speculation linking Iran to it.

Measles cases in the United States have climbed past 5,400 this year, and the number of deaths linked to the disease has reached 10, according to state health departments — the worst outbreak the country has seen in more than three decades.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported 5,412 cases for 2026 as of November 12, an increase of 230 in a single week. It was the 14th consecutive week in which the CDC recorded a triple-digit weekly rise. Cases have now been reported in 45 states and the District of Columbia.

The death toll tells a more complicated story. State health departments have reported 10 measles-associated deaths this year, while the CDC's own count stands at six. The federal agency has consistently lagged behind state reports and says its total is subject to change.

Two of the deaths were reported this week. Wisconsin's health department said a child in Grant County, in the state's southwestern corner, had died of measles complications, a week after a baby in the state also died. Pennsylvania officials confirmed that an unvaccinated resident of Montour County had died after testing positive for measles. Each state has now recorded seven measles-associated deaths this year, and New York has recorded one. At least four of this year's deaths were children.

## Pennsylvania remains the epicentre

Pennsylvania is still the hardest-hit state, with 1,542 cases this year — more than any other state. Wisconsin reported the most new infections over the past week, with 174, bringing its total to 651. Ohio added 61 cases this week for a total of 372.

The outbreak is being driven overwhelmingly by gaps in vaccination. People who have not been vaccinated, or whose vaccination status is unknown, account for 96 percent of reported cases, according to the CDC. The hospitalization rate remains at 11 percent, with 591 people hospitalized this year.

There are signs the surge in Pennsylvania may be slowing. The state's vaccination campaign has pushed monthly measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) doses far above normal — about 56,000 in September and 61,000 in October, against a typical monthly average of roughly 25,000 — including home visits to under-immunized communities.

## Elimination status at risk

The outbreak now threatens a public health milestone the United States has held for more than a quarter of a century. Measles was declared eliminated in the country in 2000, meaning the virus was no longer spreading continuously within its borders. Cases have now risen above 5,000 for the first time since 1991.

A commission of the Pan American Health Organization is meeting this week to decide whether the United States — and Mexico — should lose that elimination status, following Canada, which has already lost it. A decision is expected on November 17. Infectious disease experts say the virus is effectively spreading again across North America, driven by falling vaccination rates.

At a summit in Shenzhen on Thursday, President Donald Trump pointed at Vladimir Putin, smiled, and called him "my friend" as the two men shook hands. The greeting came at the start of the first formal meeting of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia — a gathering Trump later rated "12 out of 10".

The meeting took place on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen. Trump and Putin spoke face to face for about 40 minutes before joining their host, Chinese President Xi Jinping, for a 90-minute three-way session around a single table. Trump greeted Xi warmly too, telling him: "You're my friend and we have a very special relationship." Afterwards the three leaders issued a short "Declaration toward Peace". It made no mention of Ukraine, and it condemned the International Criminal Court, which has issued an arrest warrant for Putin over the alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children.

The reaction from America's allies was swift. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk posted on X: "God, protect us from such friends." His foreign minister, Radosław Sikorski, said the optics "cause us some concern", and asked whom Trump considered the closer friend — Putin or Poland. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called the meeting "very incomprehensible", and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz warned against a rerun of the 1945 Yalta Conference, at which the victorious powers carved up Europe. In Washington, Democratic Senator Jeanne Shaheen called Putin a "war criminal and murderer", and Senator Tim Kaine said he is "no friend". Republican Senator Thom Tillis also criticised the summit. Asked about the remark at a news conference in Berlin, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy only shrugged and walked off the stage.

Trump stood by the greeting the next day. At a White House news conference on Friday, he said his "fantastic relationships" with Putin and Xi kept the United States safe. Asked whether Putin was a friend to America, he replied: "I can't speak to America", pointing instead to their "very good relationship". He told Reuters it was "nice to have people that have 4,000 nuclear weapons as a friend as opposed to an enemy", and repeated that Putin wants to end the war in Ukraine. Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended the engagement, noting that the three countries hold the vast majority of the world's nuclear weapons, and that the idea of the three leaders not meeting regularly was "not just absurd, it's dangerous".

Putin's own message, however, was very different. Hours after the summit, he told reporters that Russia would not negotiate a partial ceasefire with Ukraine following Ukrainian strikes on the Crimean Bridge. Poland's defence minister said Russia had violated Polish airspace overnight with three objects, probably two missiles and a drone. Trump, meanwhile, called into a meeting of European leaders in Berlin and told them Putin was ready to end the war — a claim the events of the day did little to support.

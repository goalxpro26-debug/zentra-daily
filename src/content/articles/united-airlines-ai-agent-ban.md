United Airlines said on Wednesday it will stop personal artificial-intelligence agents from booking flights on its customers' behalf, making it the first major US airline to draw a line against the fast-growing class of AI travel assistants.

The carrier is updating its booking and ticketing policies to block what it calls unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents and assistants. The restriction applies directly on United's own website and app, and indirectly through travel agencies and flight aggregators that sell its tickets.

United framed the move as a matter of security and compliance. Letting an AI assistant act for a traveler means handing it the keys to the account: login credentials, payment details and frequent-flyer miles. The airline said that sharing those credentials with a third-party assistant can expose sensitive information and lead to purchases a customer never intended, changes to an account, or miles spent without the traveler's knowledge.

There is also a regulatory angle. Some steps in air travel legally require the traveler to act in person — checking in, for example, involves confirming that checked luggage holds no banned items. United said an automated tool performing those steps on a customer's behalf could run afoul of federal aviation and transportation rules.

## The assistants keep growing

The ban lands as AI agents move from answering questions to carrying out transactions. Millions of consumers now use assistants such as Meta's Muse and OpenAI's dots to research products, track down deals and complete purchases, and travel is one of the most obvious next frontiers.

For United, there is a commercial motive as well as a safety one. Aviation analyst Henry Harteveldt of Atmosphere Research Group told CBS News that the airline wants to keep control of its own booking process and avoid a surge in automated flight searches, which cost carriers money with every query. "Flight searches cost airlines money," he said.

Travelers will not lose the ability to compare United's fares. Its tickets will still be bookable through flight aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights, Harteveldt said, and finding a good fare should not get any harder. What changes is that an AI agent cannot complete the purchase on United's systems — and, by blocking the bots, United will not make it any easier for them to find its fares either.

The move puts United on a different path from at least one rival. Southwest Airlines has made its flight inventory viewable to ChatGPT users, who can reach the airline's website through the AI app — a reminder that the industry has not yet settled on a single answer to the agent question.

## What is still unknown

United has not published a timetable for the new restrictions or explained how it will detect and stop unauthorized agents, including those reaching it through agencies and aggregators. It is also unclear whether other major carriers will follow with outright bans or take a different approach, such as authorizing selected assistants under formal partnerships. For now, United says it has no partnerships with third-party AI agents.

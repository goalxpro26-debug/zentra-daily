Microsoft said on Saturday that its services had fully recovered, bringing to an end a global outage that cut millions of people off from their email and workplace tools for more than 24 hours.

"We have completed our efforts to address the underlying issue of the incident and restored service to the remaining impacted customers and regions," the company posted on X. The message covered Teams, Outlook, Copilot and its other products, and Microsoft's service-health page showed all products operational on Saturday morning.

The outage was one of the longest in the company's history. Reports began climbing at about 5:25 p.m. GMT on Thursday, according to Ookla, the parent company of outage tracker Downdetector. By the end of the incident, Downdetector had logged more than 7.4 million reports worldwide, peaking at nearly 20,000 a minute — more than 1,500 times normal levels. The disruption reached users in 74 countries, hitting businesses, government offices, hospitals and courts.

## Servers "unexpectedly unavailable"

Microsoft said the failure was tied to Exchange Online, its cloud-based email and scheduling hub, which left Outlook, Teams and Copilot struggling while unconnected applications kept working. The company said a large number of servers had become "unexpectedly unavailable" after an earlier network-capacity upgrade, and a further change to its systems then left the remaining servers unable to cope with the load.

On Friday, Microsoft said an earlier round of fixes had not worked as expected, and it was rolling out broader software fixes to stabilise the system. Consumer services were reported fully recovered at 4:10 p.m. UTC on Friday, while enterprise services continued to come back through the night before the company confirmed full restoration on Saturday.

Microsoft has said there was no indication of a cyberattack or malicious activity, and it ruled out artificial intelligence as a cause of the incident. That distinction mattered to customers: the outage was a failure of capacity and configuration, not of security.

## A second disruption this year

The incident is the second disruption to Microsoft's services in 2026, and its scale underlined how much of daily work now runs through a handful of cloud platforms. Outlook and Teams are among the most widely used workplace applications in the world, with hundreds of millions of users, and the outage blocked basic tasks such as sending email, managing calendars and hosting virtual meetings.

Microsoft shares closed flat at $523.88 on Friday, a muted market reaction to a disruption that, for a day, left much of the working world waiting on its inbox.

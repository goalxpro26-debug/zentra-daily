SoftBank Group reported a net loss of ¥1.12 trillion — about $7.1 billion — in its fiscal second quarter on Tuesday, its first quarterly loss in almost two years and a sharp reversal from the profit analysts had expected.

The Tokyo-based investor, one of the biggest backers of OpenAI, had been forecast to post a profit of ¥129 billion on average. Instead, it absorbed a $3.7 billion writedown on its American data-centre projects and watched the value of several of its largest listed holdings fall.

The writedown relates to non-refundable advance payments SoftBank made to secure power-generation equipment for its US data-centre build-out, including an eight-gigawatt campus in Ohio. Having rushed to reserve manufacturing capacity amid fears of shortages and long lead times, the company later revised its procurement plans as the requirements of its existing and planned projects changed. In total, it had paid about $9.5 billion upfront for power-generation equipment by the end of September.

## Falling holdings added to the damage

The data-centre charge was not the only drag. Declines in the share prices of chipmakers Arm Holdings and Intel, along with South Korean e-commerce group Coupang, further weighed on the bottom line. SoftBank recorded a loss of about ¥270 billion on its Intel holdings and related derivatives.

The result underlines the tension at the heart of founder Masayoshi Son's strategy: building the infrastructure needed to support artificial intelligence at enormous speed, even as the cost of hardware and financing rises. SoftBank has committed roughly $65 billion to OpenAI, and a potential new funding round that could value the chatbot maker at $1.25 trillion has not yet been reflected in its results — any revaluation will wait until that round closes, chief financial officer Yoshimitsu Goto said.

## A proxy for AI sentiment

SoftBank is increasingly treated by investors as a proxy for sentiment towards OpenAI and the wider AI sector, and its shares have fallen by more than half from their record high in June. They rallied 13.9 percent on Tuesday, alongside broader strength in Asian technology stocks, before the results were released.

The loss is one of the first hard writedowns on AI data-centre spending by a top-tier backer, and it lands in a market already asking whether the vast sums being poured into AI infrastructure will generate the returns to justify them. SoftBank's answer, for this quarter at least, is that the bill has arrived before the payoff.

## What is still unknown

It is not clear how much of the equipment SoftBank has already paid for can still be used, or what rebates it may recover on future purchases. The company has also not said whether it will slow the pace of its data-centre spending, or how the Ohio project will proceed now that its procurement plans have changed.

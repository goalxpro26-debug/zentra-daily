Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday as president, chief executive and a member of the board of Nvidia, effective immediately, ending a 33-year run as the founder and only chief executive the chipmaker has ever had. The board named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive and said it had begun a search for a permanent successor, considering internal and external candidates. It also appointed longtime director Harvey C. Jones as its first board chairman.

The resignation came a day after Nvidia's board met to consider removing Huang, following reports in the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal about two extramarital affairs. One involved a Singapore-based technology executive whom United States intelligence officials suspect of spying for China; the other involved a senior Nvidia executive who reports to him. On Sunday, Huang told employees in a letter that he had been unfaithful to his wife, without giving details.

## An inquiry that is still running

On November 8, Nvidia said its independent directors, assisted by outside legal counsel, would oversee an investigation into whether Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence operation. The inquiry is being led by the law firm Wachtell Lipton, and the FBI is also examining the matter. The board had previously said it had no reason to believe company secrets or national security had been compromised, and had expressed confidence in Huang.

The political fallout has spread beyond the company. Lawmakers in Washington have called for briefings and new restrictions on advanced chip exports to China, and at least one senator has publicly called for Huang's resignation. President Donald Trump said on Monday that he had spoken to Huang, who told him he had no idea the woman was a spy, and that he believed him.

Investors have reacted swiftly. Nvidia's shares have fallen sharply this week, wiping hundreds of billions of dollars from its market value, and the stock slid again after Thursday's announcement, rattling other technology stocks. Huang, 63, owns about 3.6 percent of the company and is one of the world's richest people.

## What happens next

Kress joined Nvidia in September 2013 and has led its finance operations since. She will keep her roles as executive vice president and chief financial officer while serving as interim chief executive. Nvidia said it would still report its third-quarter results for fiscal 2027 on November 17, as previously scheduled.

The board's statement gave no reason for the resignation and disclosed no terms of any agreement with Huang. The independent investigation continues, and the board has given no timeline for naming a permanent successor — a notable gap, since Huang has said he does not believe in succession planning and Nvidia has no public plan for replacing him.

An explosion and fire aboard a Russian warship docked at the port of Benghazi was likely caused by a drone attack, Libya's eastern-based government said on Sunday, in the latest incident to drag the war in Ukraine into the Mediterranean.

The government said a preliminary investigation indicated the blast happened aboard a Russian ship at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya, and that a drone attack was suspected. The vessel had stopped at the port to refuel and resupply on its way back to Russia, under maritime cooperation agreements between the two countries.

Two Libyan military officials told the Associated Press that the damaged vessel was the Russian Navy corvette Merkury. They said several Ukrainian sea drones had been launched at the ship from a base in western Libya, and that at least four were intercepted before two struck the warship, severely damaging it and starting a fire. The officials spoke on condition of anonymity, and the account has not been independently verified.

Naval News, a specialist defence outlet, reported that the vessel was almost certainly destroyed by the blast and fire, though it was not clear whether it had sunk at its berth or burned out above the waterline. The ship had been at the port for several days while escorting a convoy, the outlet said.

Two medical sources told Reuters that no deaths had been reported, but that there were injuries. The number of people hurt and their nationalities were not immediately known. There was no immediate reaction from the Russian authorities, and no one had claimed responsibility for the suspected attack.

The eastern-based government said port and naval base facilities, including warehouses, were safe, and that normal operations would resume once the fire was brought under control and the site secured. It condemned the suspected attack as a violation of Libya's sovereignty and a threat to maritime safety, and said it would take legal and diplomatic measures against anyone found responsible.

"The Libyan government will not allow Libyan ports, territory or territorial waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts," it said in a statement.

Not every account of the blast agrees. Services in Benghazi under the Libyan National Army, which controls the east of the country, attributed the explosion to a detonation at an ammunition depot inside the naval base rather than a direct strike on the ship. The exact cause remains unconfirmed.

The incident came a day after the Russian-flagged cargo ship Sparta II was hit by two drones in the Gulf of Finland on 21 November, in a separate attack that also remains unattributed. Together, the two strikes point to a widening campaign against Russian shipping far beyond the Black Sea, where Ukraine has repeatedly targeted Moscow's fleet.

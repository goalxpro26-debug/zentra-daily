Colombian security forces stormed a supermarket in the Caribbean port city of Cartagena in the early hours of Wednesday, freeing all the hostages who had been held inside for about 17 hours and bringing a day-long siege to an end.

Police said around 76 shoppers and employees had been held at the Éxito supermarket in the La Castellana neighbourhood. One gunman was killed in the raid and four others were arrested. Officials said all of the hostages were freed unharmed.

The gunmen, who a rescued employee said were disguised as police officers, stormed into the store at about 08:00 local time on Tuesday. President Abelardo de la Espriella said they had fled into the supermarket after a failed attempt to rob an armoured car carrying cash at a nearby shopping centre, and then seized the people inside while trying to negotiate their getaway.

Ten hostages, including several elderly women, were released during the day after hours of negotiations. Police said around 20 people were still inside when the rescue operation began. Some hostages had been locked in a cold storage room, face down with their hands tied, and told the exits had been rigged with explosives. Early reports put the number held far lower, and the police figure was revised upwards during the day. Local media reported that the gunmen had demanded about $2 million, armoured vehicles and safe passage out of the store, though officials did not confirm those terms.

De la Espriella travelled to Cartagena on Tuesday evening to oversee the operation and gave the green light for the raid. In a video message early on Wednesday, he announced that the remaining hostages had been rescued in a joint operation by National Police commandos and the armed forces, using stun grenades to overpower the gunmen. He called it "an extraordinary joint operation", carried out "flawlessly", and said the gunmen had been given the chance to surrender but had refused. He described them as "simple bandits" with no political affiliations, not a terrorist group.

Cartagena's mayor, Dumek Turbay, said the freed hostages were given medical checks before being reunited with their families. Flights into the city were briefly disrupted during the standoff, with some planes diverted to nearby Barranquilla.

The siege unfolded on a tense day for Colombia. It came on the 10th anniversary of the peace deal with the FARC rebel group, and on the same day a car bombing elsewhere in the country injured more than 20 people. Authorities have not said who the gunmen in Cartagena were or whether they had any connection to organised armed groups, and the identities of the hostage-takers have not been released.

Cartagena, a major tourist destination on Colombia's northern coast, rarely sees violence of this kind in its commercial districts. The supermarket chain said the situation at its Cartagena store was being handled by the authorities and did not comment further.

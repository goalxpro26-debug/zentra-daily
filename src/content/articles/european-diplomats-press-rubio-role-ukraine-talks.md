European foreign ministers told US Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Paris on Monday that Europe must have a say in any deal to end Russia's war on Ukraine, as US-mediated talks between Moscow and Kyiv got under way in Abu Dhabi.

The appeal came at a two-day G7 foreign ministers' meeting — Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States — which ends on Tuesday. Ministers from Saudi Arabia, Egypt, South Korea and Brazil are also attending.

It was Rubio's first meeting with allies since last week's midterm elections, in which Trump's Republican Party suffered heavy losses. It gave Washington's allies a chance to raise their concerns directly, amid unease about Trump's talk of a new security architecture for Europe.

## "Europe belongs at this table"

Germany's Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul was blunt after his talks with Rubio, saying it was "certainly neither necessary, nor right, nor appropriate to make a fundamental change" to Europe's security architecture.

"Europe belongs at this table. There can be no solution without Europe — only with Europe," he added.

France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot had said on Sunday that Europeans would be represented at the Abu Dhabi talks. The Paris meeting overlaps with the talks between Russia and Ukraine, scheduled for Tuesday and Wednesday.

Wadephul said the Abu Dhabi talks must show whether Russia is "genuinely prepared to negotiate", urging Moscow to "give up maximalist demands" and warning against any "negotiation show". With Russian attacks on Ukraine's civilian infrastructure continuing, he said, "we see no substantial contribution whatsoever toward negotiations gaining any serious momentum" from Moscow.

## Ukraine: "No one has the right to decide our fate behind our backs"

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha is set to join a session on Ukraine in Paris on Tuesday. Before departing, he said: "No one has the right to decide our fate behind our backs."

The Paris meeting comes as G7 members have grown uneasy with the direction of US policy, including Washington's scaling back of support for Ukraine and of sanctions on Russia, and its increasingly unilateral course. Europe fears Kyiv could be forced to accept disadvantageous terms, and that Washington could offer Russia new security guarantees over the Europeans' heads.

Iran is also expected to feature prominently on Tuesday. Washington's G7 allies have repeatedly voiced scepticism about the US and Israeli war on Iran, fearing further US military action could deepen the crisis rather than resolve it.

## Why it matters

The two tracks are now running in parallel, and the question hanging over both is whether Europe will have a formal role in whatever emerges. Al Jazeera's correspondent in Paris reported that day one ended with little clarity on US policy after the midterms — and that the bigger question is the future of the multilateral system that has shaped relations among these countries for decades.

What remains unknown is whether the Abu Dhabi talks produce anything concrete — and whether Europe's demand is answered with a place at the table or with assurances alone.

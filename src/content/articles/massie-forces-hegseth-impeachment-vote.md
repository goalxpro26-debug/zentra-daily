Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, moved on Monday to force a House vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war in Iran without congressional approval and of ordering unlawful military strikes abroad.

Massie read eight articles of impeachment aloud on the House floor and filed them as a privileged resolution, a procedural move that requires House leaders to act within two legislative days. A vote could come as soon as Wednesday. It would be the first House impeachment vote against a Pentagon chief in 150 years.

## Eight articles

The resolution centers on the war in Iran, which Massie says Hegseth waged without a declaration of war, statutory authorization, or an imminent threat to the United States — and continued even after both chambers of Congress passed a resolution directing U.S. forces to leave. The articles cite three alleged violations of the War Powers Resolution of 1973, the post-Vietnam law limiting how long U.S. forces can remain in hostilities without Congress's approval.

Other articles cover lethal U.S. strikes on suspected drug-trafficking boats in the Caribbean, military action in Yemen, and the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in a military raid, which Massie called an act of war against a sovereign country.

One article concerns a U.S. airstrike on February 28 on the Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school in Iran, which killed at least 168 people, most of them children. The Pentagon has not yet released the findings of its investigation into that strike.

## A second attempt

This is Massie's second try. He first sought to force an impeachment vote in September, but House Speaker Mike Johnson sent lawmakers home early and the resolution expired before anyone had to vote on it. Massie had to reintroduce the measure to restart the two-day clock.

The new effort comes just days after Republicans suffered historic losses in the midterm elections, which President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance have attributed to the unpopular war in Iran and the rise in gas prices that followed. Democrats take control of both chambers when the new Congress convenes in January.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday he was a "hard yes" on the measure, and Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer has endorsed it. Johnson, meanwhile, said Monday it was a "terrible, terrible look" to impeach a defense secretary during hostilities. Massie acknowledged he does not know whether the resolution has enough votes to pass, and warned Johnson could try to sidestep a vote.

## What happens next

Even if the House votes to impeach Hegseth, removal is unlikely: a Senate trial would require a two-thirds majority to convict, and Republicans still hold the majority in both chambers until January. Leadership could also try to table the resolution, killing it without a direct vote on the charges — though that, too, would be a recorded vote.

Hegseth, who has vowed to fight the effort, plans a rare press conference at the Pentagon on Tuesday morning — his first time taking questions from the briefing room since May 5. Massie, who is leaving Congress at the end of the term after losing a Trump-backed primary, said impeaching Trump himself over war powers was "an impossible hurdle" and that Hegseth was "the better target."

Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo Subianto on Tuesday that he intends to resign, a presidential spokesperson said, as lawmakers weigh whether the education documents he submitted to stand for office disqualified him.

Gibran, 39, raised the matter during a meeting with the president, spokesperson Prasetyo Hadi said. The president did not decline the request, Hadi added. It was not immediately clear whether a formal resignation letter had been submitted, or when any resignation would take effect.

## A probe parliament is weighing

Parliament reconvened on Tuesday with lawmakers considering an investigation into Gibran's eligibility, amid a widening rift between his father, the hugely popular former president Joko Widodo, and Prabowo. House Speaker Puan Maharani said lawmakers had received many opinions on the issue during a one-month recess and that the next steps would be discussed. Asked about the vice president's future, she declined to speculate, saying it was better to respond calmly and avoid raising public concern over things that had not yet happened.

The dispute centres on Gibran's qualifications. Last month, the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court ruled that the education documents he submitted did not meet election requirements. The Constitutional Court added that it did not have the power to disqualify him, saying that responsibility belonged to other institutions. Some lawmakers are now seeking an investigation, which analysts believe could be the first step towards impeachment proceedings.

## A partnership under strain

Gibran's presence on the ticket helped Prabowo win the 2024 election by a large margin, but he has kept a low profile as vice president, and his public appearances alongside Prabowo have been infrequent during their first two years in office. The eligibility question has now become a visible strain on the partnership between the president and the family of his predecessor.

It remains unclear whether Prabowo will accept the resignation, whether parliament will press ahead with its probe, or what either decision would mean for the coalition that brought the pair to power. What is certain is that the question hanging over the vice presidency — whether Gibran was ever eligible to hold it — is now being asked in the open, by the institutions that would have to answer it.

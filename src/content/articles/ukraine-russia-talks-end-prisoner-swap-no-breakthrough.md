Two days of U.S.-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday with agreement on a large exchange of prisoners of war — but no breakthrough on ending a war that is nearing its fifth year.

The meeting was the first direct encounter between the two sides since February. Hopes for a significant step toward peace had been low, and the delegations left still far apart on the question that matters most: territory.

## The one concrete result

Both sides confirmed they had agreed in principle to a large-scale prisoner swap. Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said mutual understanding had been reached on some issues, while U.S. envoy Jared Kushner called the planned release "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners" and said the numbers would be finalised on Thursday.

Russia's state-run news agency TASS reported the exchange should take place within two to three weeks. A Russian military intelligence official said the aim was to go past 1,000 people on each side — which would make it the largest swap of the war, topping the 1,000-for-1,000 exchange in May last year. Prisoner swaps have continued through the war even as diplomacy stalled: the two sides freed 200 prisoners each in March, and large exchanges followed three rounds of talks in Istanbul from May to July.

## No movement on the core dispute

On the substance of a settlement, neither side moved. Kushner said "progress" had been made on a post-war security framework for Ukraine, but added that no deal could be signed until the territorial dispute is settled — and he described territory as the hardest issue to resolve. Russia continues to demand that Ukraine give up land it still holds, including parts of the Donbas, a demand Kyiv has refused.

Ukraine's peace negotiator, David Arakhamia, said there was "good momentum" toward reaching a solution before the end of the year, and argued the next round should be multilateral, with Europe represented. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who did not attend the talks, said on Wednesday that European security could not be discussed "without Europe". Medinsky said Moscow was not opposed to European participation in future talks, though he questioned what Russia and the EU would discuss.

## What happens next

Kushner and U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin and relay what was discussed, then fly to Dublin to meet Zelenskyy. Kushner said the envoys saw "some openings and some opportunities" in Moscow, and that the meetings would also address whether Europeans can be brought into the format.

The round's modest outcome keeps the diplomatic process alive without resolving it. The prisoner exchange, if it goes ahead at the scale discussed, will be a real humanitarian step — but the war itself, and the territorial question at its centre, remains where it was when the delegations arrived.

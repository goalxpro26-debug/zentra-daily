The US Commerce Department has added Megaspeed International and Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions to its trade blacklist, escalating Washington's crackdown on suspected diversions of advanced Nvidia AI chips to China.

Announced on Monday, November 23, the move places seven companies in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand on the Entity List. Firms on the list need a US government licence to buy American exports, which is usually refused. The other five are linked to Megaspeed, including its owner Swiftdata and Malaysian subsidiary Speedmatrix.

Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company and Nvidia partner, has been under investigation by US authorities since 2024 over suspected diversions of AI chips to China. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security said the companies were added because of "the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorised end uses or end users."

## The Alibaba connection

Apex, which buys computing power for Alibaba in Southeast Asia, paid around $4.2 billion to Megaspeed and others as prepayment to rent computing capacity there. Speedmatrix, its Malaysian subsidiary, pledged its entire business as collateral and held about $2.14 billion in assets at end-2024.

Alibaba rejected the decision. "Adding Apex Enterprise Solutions to the Entity List is erroneous because Apex has never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions," the company said, adding that it would "seek all legal avenues to protect our rights and reputation."

Megaspeed says its business is legitimate and lawful and complies with US export controls. It said Alice Huang, a former executive at the centre of the scandal, left the company in August 2025.

## A scandal that reached Nvidia's top

Megaspeed came under intense scrutiny about two weeks ago after reports that Nvidia chief Jensen Huang had an affair with Alice Huang, whom US authorities suspect of being a Chinese spy. China has called the accusations groundless. Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief and has since been reinstated.

The department had faced growing criticism over its handling of the case. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly complained on November 13 that the company had still not been listed, and Senator Pete Ricketts said the department "still needs to explain to Congress why this took so long." Senator Maria Cantwell called for a broader investigation.

A separate rule unveiled last week requires data centre operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil to get a US licence before exporting advanced AI chips to customers there. The Justice and Commerce departments also said they would "bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred."

## What happens next

Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said on Tuesday his country would continue its own investigation. US and Singapore law enforcement visited in 2025 and interviewed Alice Huang.

It was the first Entity List addition since October 2025, and it shows how far the fallout has spread.

Chinese technology stocks have shed a combined $1.7 trillion in market value since the end of June, according to data compiled by Bloomberg, turning one of the year's hottest trades into a rout.

The three main tech-focused indexes have all given up their gains for the year, while major peers in the United States, Taiwan and South Korea remain in positive territory. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Tech Index, home to Alibaba and Tencent, has slumped to a two-year low. The onshore ChiNext Index reached a record high this summer but is now down 31% on the year, and the chip-heavy Star 50 Index has similarly wiped out a record rally.

The selloff is a sharp reversal for a trade that took off after DeepSeek showed Chinese firms could build competitive AI models far more cheaply than US rivals. Breakthroughs from Huawei and Beijing's drive for national tech self-sufficiency drew investors in. Now frothy valuations and a reliance on policy support have left the sector exposed as cracks appear in the global AI boom.

Investors are worried both about a slowdown in AI capital spending and about whether US-China supply-chain business can be sustained, according to Bush Chu, investment director of China equities at Aberdeen Investments, who said the year's wild swings and second-half selloff had left fund managers "pretty speechless".

The latest pressure comes from Washington. Draft rules released by the US earlier this month would effectively block Chinese firms from renting cutting-edge processors from Nvidia and others through overseas data centres, closing a vital backdoor for training next-generation models. Chinese AI is already slipping: none of the current 10 most advanced models in the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index are from China, a sharp contrast with four months ago, when Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 rivalled OpenAI and Anthropic.

The chip crunch is reaching company results. Tencent warned in last week's earnings call that the next version of its HY4 model could be delayed, though it said the domestic supply chain should help over time. Alibaba said on Thursday that it expects cloud sales growth to slow to 30% to 45% next year before bouncing back above 50% in 2028. Chinese internet giants also face the same high AI development costs pressuring US hyperscalers, alongside sluggish domestic consumption and persistent price wars.

"The key issues are weak AI monetization, compute bottlenecks, intense competition and margin pressure," said Nick Ferres, chief investment officer at Vantage Point Asset Management.

A KraneShares exchange-traded fund tracking China's Star 50 gauge fell more than 4% in US trading. On November 20, China's four major A-share indexes all moved lower, with the Shanghai Composite down 1.41%, the Shenzhen Component down 1.71%, the ChiNext down 2.15% and the STAR Composite down 2.99%.

What happens next is unclear. It is not yet known whether domestic chipmakers can fill the gap left by US restrictions, whether Beijing will step in with further support, or whether the selloff deepens when markets reopen on Monday.

Estonia will close every border crossing with Russia from December 1, its government announced on Sunday, after what Tallinn says was an attempted arson attack on a drone manufacturer ordered by Russian military intelligence.

Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna announced the closure in a post on X, writing that Estonia would shut "all Estonian-Russian border crossing points as of 1 December" and citing what he called "clear evidence" that Russia's GRU military intelligence service had ordered the attack. He did not say how long the closure would last.

The decision follows an attempt on Saturday night to set fire to the premises of Threod Systems, a defence company in Viimsi, just north of the capital, Tallinn. The company makes reconnaissance and attack drones. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said the GRU had ordered a "sabotage attempt" at the plant, adding that "extensive damage was averted thanks to heightened security measures and a swift reaction by law enforcement" and that "the attackers have been detained."

Security forces arrested eight people who live in Latvia, chief prosecutor Astrid Asi told journalists. Tsahkna said he had informed his Finnish, Latvian and Lithuanian counterparts of the closure decision.

Estonia currently has three open crossings with Russia — at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula — which admit pedestrians and freight. All three will close under the December 1 measure.

Tallinn has blamed Moscow for a pattern of sabotage against its defence industry. Michal described the weekend incident as "part of Russia's broader hybrid war against Europe, with the aim of decreasing support to Ukraine and pressure on Russia," adding: "We will not be intimidated." Tsahkna said "Russia must understand that hostile actions against Estonia come with consequences" and that "attempts to intimidate, destabilise, or undermine our security will not go unanswered."

Moscow has not commented on the latest accusations or the border decision. Russia regularly denies involvement in sabotage operations in Europe.

It is the second attempted attack on an Estonian defence firm that Tallinn has attributed to Russia. In August, the defence subcontractor Milrem, based in Tallinn, was targeted in an attempted arson attack, and Latvian nationals were arrested.

## What is still unknown

Estonia has not said when — or whether — the crossings will reopen, and no end date has been set. The government has also not released further details about the eight people detained, or the evidence behind its claim that the GRU ordered the attack. The attribution rests on the findings of Estonia's Internal Security Service and has not been independently verified.

House Republicans have chosen Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their next leader, handing the party's top House post to one of its most combative conservatives a week after voters stripped the party of its majority.

Jordan, the chairman of the House Judiciary Committee and a founder of the conservative Freedom Caucus, defeated Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana in a closed-door secret ballot on Tuesday. The vote was 106 to 73, according to two Republican lawmakers in the room.

Jordan will serve as minority leader in the next Congress, when Democrats take control of the chamber after winning at least 11 Republican-held seats in last week's midterm elections. The Associated Press projected Democrats will hold at least 255 seats to at least 166 for Republicans, with some races still uncalled.

## A changing of the guard

The result is a clear break with the party's recent leadership. Speaker Mike Johnson of Louisiana announced on Monday that he was stepping down from the leadership team after three years in the top role, and Jordan now succeeds him as the House's senior Republican.

Scalise, who had spent a decade in leadership and held the No. 2 post since 2019, was the establishment choice. Jordan's victory signals that the conference, now facing life in the minority, preferred a fighter known for confrontation with Democratic leaders and for his close alliance with President Donald Trump.

Rep. Tom Emmer of Minnesota was re-elected as whip and will be the second-ranking House Republican in the next Congress.

## A rematch with history

Tuesday's contest was a rematch of the leadership struggle that followed the ouster of then-Speaker Kevin McCarthy in 2023. Scalise defeated Jordan 113 to 99 in that closed-door race for the party's nomination, but withdrew the following day after failing to secure enough support to win a vote of the full House. Jordan's own bid for the speakership then collapsed as well.

This time the path was simpler: the minority leader is chosen by the party conference alone, so Jordan needed only a majority of his colleagues, not the full House.

## What comes next

The new Congress is sworn in in January, when Jordan will formally take up the minority leader's post and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become speaker. The immediate test for Jordan will be holding together a smaller, bruised conference while Democrats, with a commanding majority, set the House agenda — and while the party decides how to rebuild after a midterm defeat that cost it both chambers.

For now, the message from the conference is unmistakable: after a sweeping loss, House Republicans have chosen their most prominent brawler to lead them.

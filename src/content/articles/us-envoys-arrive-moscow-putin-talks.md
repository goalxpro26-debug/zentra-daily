U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow on Thursday for talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin, the latest leg of a fast-moving American diplomatic push to end the war in Ukraine.

The two men were met at the airport by Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev and driven to central Moscow, where their meeting with Putin was scheduled for the first half of the day. It is the tenth time Witkoff has met Putin in person. After Moscow, the envoys planned to fly to Dublin to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy later the same day.

The visit follows two days of U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi between Russian and Ukrainian delegations — the first direct negotiations between the two sides in nine months. That round ended on Wednesday with agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange, but no breakthrough on the questions that have blocked a settlement for more than four years.

## What Abu Dhabi produced — and what it did not

The one concrete result from Abu Dhabi was humanitarian. Both sides agreed in principle to a large-scale exchange of prisoners of war, which Kushner described as potentially the biggest release of the conflict. The numbers were still being finalised, and no date was announced.

On the substance of a settlement, neither side moved. Russia continues to demand that Ukraine give up territory in the eastern Donbas, including land Kyiv still holds — a demand Ukraine has refused. Kushner said progress had been made on a post-war security framework for Ukraine, but added that no deal could be signed until the territorial dispute is settled, describing it as the hardest issue to resolve.

The Moscow meeting is the American attempt to carry that unfinished business forward. Kushner said on Tuesday that the U.S. had seen "some openings and some opportunities" and would try to make progress with Putin. Zelenskyy, speaking in Ireland, said there was a "window of opportunity" to reach a deal this year, and said he hoped the Americans would return from Moscow with "specifics."

## Europe's seat at the table

The other question the envoys are carrying to Moscow is who gets to take part in the next round. Kushner said the U.S. wants European representatives included in future discussions, particularly on questions of continental security, and urged Europe to pick a single person to speak for it in the negotiations.

Russia has opposed European participation, pointing to Europe's support for Kyiv. European security advisers were in Abu Dhabi for separate consultations this week but were kept out of the main talks, and Moscow has shown no sign of changing that position.

Whether Putin agrees to let Europe in — and whether the Moscow session produces anything beyond the prisoner-exchange plan — will shape what happens next. The envoys' same-day trip to Dublin means Zelenskyy will hear the outcome within hours, but the core dispute over territory remains unresolved, and neither side has signalled a concession on it.

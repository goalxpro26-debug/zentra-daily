A powerful storm swept across the United States on Thanksgiving Day, leaving almost a million homes without power, cancelling hundreds of flights and flooding coastal communities on both coasts.

The storm hit the eastern US on Thursday with winds of up to 70 miles per hour, torrential rain and major coastal flooding, at the height of one of the busiest travel weeks of the year. More than 400 flights were cancelled on Thursday and another 2,200 were delayed, according to the tracking site FlightAware, while the Federal Aviation Administration had already warned that delays were possible throughout the week.

By Thursday evening, more than 980,000 households were without power, with Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland and New Jersey hit hardest. Utility companies in Virginia and New Jersey warned it could take days for power to return, as crews had to wait until it was safe to reach damaged areas.

New Jersey's governor, Mikie Sherrill, declared a state of emergency as several communities along the Jersey Shore saw widespread flooding. New York's governor, Kathy Hochul, declared a state of emergency for New York City, Long Island and Westchester County. Bridges closed in Maryland because of severe winds, and waves battered beaches in Delaware as water seeped onto the boardwalk.

In Maryland, almost two million water customers were asked to use water for essential purposes only, because of power cuts at treatment plants. In Washington DC, it was the wettest Thanksgiving on record, and tidal waters soaked runways at Reagan Washington National Airport.

President Donald Trump said on Thursday afternoon that he would consider releasing emergency funds for the east coast as it faces down the holiday storm.

The west coast was hit at the same time. Pacific storms brought rain, mountain snow and strong winds, while king tides peaked along the coast. Water levels along California's coastline could reach over eight feet in some areas, according to the National Weather Service, breaking records set just last month. San Diego and Los Angeles had already reported tides of over eight feet earlier in the week. Amtrak cancelled several Pacific Surfliner trains along the California coastline because of washout conditions from the high tides.

In Miami, travel delays persisted into Thursday after a FedEx plane overshot the runway the night before, prompting the closure of all but one runway at the airport for a brief period. No injuries were reported. An airport employee told CBS News, the BBC's US partner, that rainy conditions may have been a factor.

The American Automobile Association had predicted 81.5 million Americans would travel at least 50 miles from home over the seven-day Thanksgiving period. It also forced the postponement of New York City's Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, for the first time in its 100-year history.

The eastern storm was expected to move out into the Atlantic on Friday — the second major blow to US air travel in two weeks, after a failure in air traffic control communications forced a nationwide ground stop on November 13.

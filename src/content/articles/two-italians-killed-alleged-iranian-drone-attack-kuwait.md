Two Italian nationals were killed in Kuwait on Thursday when drones struck a military air-defence site, in an attack that Kuwait and Italy blame on Iran. Tehran denies any involvement.

The two men were technicians for Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian arm of German defence group Rheinmetall. They had been maintaining detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system at the site when it was hit, Italy's foreign ministry said.

Kuwait's defence ministry said four people were wounded by falling debris as air defences intercepted incoming drones at what it called a "vital facility". Alongside the two Italians, a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national were injured. All four were taken to hospital, and the two Italians later died of their injuries.

Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, confirmed the deaths and told the Senate that "several drones of Iranian origin targeted Kuwaiti military installations", including an air-defence detection station. He said the drone used in the attack was manufactured in Iran, while noting that "numerous pro-Iranian militias operate in the region".

Iran rejected the accusation. Brigadier General Abolfazl Shekarchi, a spokesperson for Iran's armed forces, said no military or offensive operations had been conducted by Iranian forces in the region, and described the claims as a cover for further insecurity. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also denied Iranian involvement in calls with his Italian and Kuwaiti counterparts, and offered a joint investigation into the incident.

The attack drew swift condemnation. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for more pressure on Tehran, while Gulf Cooperation Council secretary-general Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi condemned what he called a "brutal" attack and a dangerous escalation.

The strike is the latest in a series of Iranian drone attacks on Gulf states since September. Gulf governments have said an attack against one of them is an attack against all, and the incident came as an Italian delegation — including the commander general of the Carabinieri, Lieutenant-General Salvatore Lungo, and Italy's ambassador to Kuwait — was visiting the country.

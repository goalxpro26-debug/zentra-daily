The United States has recorded 14 measles deaths this year, with the national case count nearing 6,000 — the worst outbreak the country has seen since 1991.

Pennsylvania reported its ninth death on Monday, an unvaccinated resident of Centre County, and Wisconsin reported its third death on Tuesday, an adult in Lafayette County. The two deaths, announced on consecutive days, pushed the national toll to 14, according to Ars Technica's tally of state health department reports.

Cases are now approaching 6,000 for the year, the highest annual total since 1991, when more than 9,600 were recorded. The virus has been reported in 47 states and jurisdictions. There have been 43 outbreaks this year, accounting for 96 percent of all cases, and four states have each recorded more than 500 cases. Pennsylvania leads the country with 1,638 cases as of November 23. Nearly 650 people have been hospitalised nationwide.

The deaths have not been confined to the two hardest-hit states. New York reported the death of a 7-month-old baby, its first measles death in more than 30 years, and Virginia reported the death of a child aged between 0 and 4 on November 20. Wisconsin's earlier deaths were an infant and a child.

The rising toll comes a week after international health authorities formally revoked the United States' measles elimination status, which it had held since 2000. The Pan American Health Organization acted on November 17 after determining that measles had been transmitted without interruption for more than 12 months, meaning the virus is once again considered endemic in the country.

State tallies and the federal count do not agree. As of mid-November, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's official count listed six measles-related deaths, against 14 reported by state health departments. The gap has become a point of dispute: Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has asked the CDC not to count the deaths in the official tally, questioning whether they were caused by measles, while state officials say they count a death as measles-associated when a person with confirmed measles dies within 30 days of developing symptoms, unless the death had an unrelated cause.

Measles is one of the most contagious viruses known, and it is preventable with the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine. Before widespread vaccination, the disease sickened 3 million to 4 million Americans a year and killed 400 to 500. Health officials say the current outbreak is concentrated in communities with low vaccination coverage, and that the reported case count is likely an undercount of the true total.

With cases still climbing week by week, officials expect the final tally for 2026 to keep rising — and the question of whether transmission can be interrupted before a second full year of endemic spread is now open.

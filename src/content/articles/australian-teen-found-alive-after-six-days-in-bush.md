A 17-year-old boy missing for six days in dense Australian bushland has been found alive, ending a search that drew in more than 100 people across Victoria's east.

William, whose surname has not been released, was spotted by a Victoria Police AirWing helicopter crew on Thursday morning, six days after he strayed from his camp near Walhalla, about 180 kilometres east of Melbourne. He was lying in a dry creek bed when the crew found him, and was winched to safety before being flown to a search base at Cowwarr and reunited with his family.

## A week in the bush with almost nothing

William disappeared last Friday afternoon while on a camping trip with a friend at Walhalla East, a former gold-mining town in the Gippsland region. He was last seen near Stoney Creek Road, between Cowwarr and Walhalla, at about 4pm on November 6. He had walked to another part of the campsite and failed to return.

He was wearing only shorts and work boots, and had left his food, water and phone behind at the camp. Overnight temperatures in the area fell as low as 3C during the six days he was missing, and police said they held grave fears for his welfare. William has autism, and police said he may have become disoriented and lost his way in the steep, heavily forested terrain.

The search grew into one of the biggest the region has seen in recent years. Police divers, the dog squad, mounted officers and helicopters worked alongside State Emergency Service volunteers, combing the bushland around Walhalla for six days.

## "Grounded for life"

His mother, Elizabeth Barkley, was at the police staging ground in Cowwarr when the helicopter brought him in, and the two were reunited on Thursday morning. She told reporters her son had "only a few scratches" and seemed fit and healthy, joking that he was now "grounded for life" and would never be leaving the house again.

Victoria Police said William was "in good spirits and, for the most part, in good health". Rescuers gave him a doughnut and a bottle of water before he was taken to hospital for a check-up.

Officers involved in the search said they were emotional at the outcome. "It doesn't happen all the time," Victoria Police Acting Inspector Jason Benbow said.

## What is still unknown

Police have not said how William survived the six nights, including whether he found water or shelter, or exactly how far he travelled from the campsite. His hospital check-up was precautionary, and no details of any treatment have been released.

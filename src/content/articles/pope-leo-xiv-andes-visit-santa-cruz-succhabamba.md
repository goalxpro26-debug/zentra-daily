Pope Leo XIV returned on Saturday to a remote town high in Peru's Andes mountains, where he once served as a bishop, and used the visit to speak about the poverty and migration that are emptying the region's villages.

The pope arrived by helicopter in Santa Cruz de Succhabamba, a town of about 6,000 people in the Cajamarca region of northern Peru, and was greeted by singing and cheering crowds before touring in a popemobile and celebrating an outdoor Mass. About 20,000 people attended the visit, according to the Vatican — more than three times the town's population.

In his sermon, the pope spoke of the "suffering of those who are forced to leave their homeland in search of a better future" and of "declining birth rates that are emptying villages." He also criticised the unequal distribution of the land's resources, and said he felt joy at visiting the small town again.

The visit was a homecoming of sorts. Leo, who was born in the United States and elected pope in May 2025, lived in Peru for long periods between 1985 and 2023, became a Peruvian citizen in 2015, and served as bishop of Chiclayo — the diocese that includes Santa Cruz de Succhabamba — from 2015 to 2023.

The town's struggles are the region's struggles. More than 40 percent of people in Cajamarca live in poverty, the highest figure in Peru, according to official data. The region has also lost more people to internal migration than any other in the country: some 729,000 people born in Cajamarca now live elsewhere in Peru, many of them in coastal cities such as Lima and Trujillo.

Peru is the longest stop on the pope's first tour of Latin America, a 12-day trip that also included Argentina and Uruguay. He was scheduled to leave Chiclayo on Sunday for Cusco, then visit a Peruvian sector of the Amazon rainforest, before the tour concludes on Monday with his return to Rome.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear what, if anything, will follow from the pope's remarks on migration and rural poverty, or whether the visit will bring new attention — or funding — to the Cajamarca region. The Vatican has not announced any new initiatives for the area tied to the trip.

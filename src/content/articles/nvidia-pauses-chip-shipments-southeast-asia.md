Nvidia has told its manufacturing partners to pause shipments of AI servers to four Southeast Asian countries while it checks whether they are complying with new US export controls, according to reports published on Thursday.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia instructed partners including Dell and Super Micro to halt server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for an unspecified period. The Financial Times, citing three people familiar with the matter, reported the same move, and said partners had told buyers in the four countries that Nvidia would not allow their orders to be shipped.

The pause follows a US Commerce Department rule issued on November 16 and in force since November 19, which requires a US export licence to send advanced AI chips to those four countries and to Brazil. The rule is aimed at a workaround Washington says Chinese companies have been using: renting computing power remotely from data centres abroad instead of importing the chips directly.

Those four Southeast Asian countries have become favoured destinations for Chinese AI developers seeking access to Nvidia's most advanced chips, whose direct export to China is restricted. The think tank Institute for Progress estimates that remote data centres of this kind supply about 24 percent of China's AI computing power, according to the Journal's reporting.

Under the new rule, cloud providers that were already supplying large amounts of computing power to companies in China or other covered locations have 90 days from November 19 to obtain a licence, by showing their end users do not include restricted entities.

It is not yet clear whether the halt is temporary. Nvidia declined to comment, and Dell and Supermicro did not immediately respond to requests for comment, according to the reports. Bloomberg reported that a $480 million loan sought by Singapore cloud company Aolani to buy Nvidia graphics processors for a data centre in Malaysia has been put on hold.

The disruption is already rippling beyond the chipmaker. Data centre operators in the region that had planned to sell GPU computing power to Chinese customers are reassessing projects, and financing tied to those plans is under pressure.

The halt comes days after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, on November 23. For buyers in the four affected countries, the immediate question is how long the pause lasts — and what compliance will look like when shipments resume.

Waymo has filed two safety recalls covering its self-driving system, days after California regulators ordered its robotaxis off the state's freeways.

The Alphabet-owned company said on Friday that it had filed the recalls with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration on Thursday. Both are software recalls for its automated driving system, aimed at improving how its cars handle closed freeway lanes and intersections where police or emergency workers direct traffic by hand.

The first recall covers situations in which a Waymo car may drive into a closed freeway lane and keep going. Waymo said no crashes or injuries had been reported in connection with those events, and that it was still developing the fix.

The second covers how the cars respond to manual traffic control at intersections. It follows a collision in which a Waymo car initially followed an officer's directions, but the officer was then temporarily hidden from the car's view. Waymo said the software update for that issue was finished on 6 November.

The recalls come a little over a week after the California Department of Motor Vehicles barred Waymo's driverless cars from all state freeways, for both testing and passenger service, effective 11 November. The regulator said the cars posed an "imminent hazard to public safety" after three of them entered an active construction closure near Burlingame, and a separate incident involving California Highway Patrol vehicles in San Francisco. Waymo said its cars continue to operate normally on surface streets, and it has paused freeway rides in every market where it offered them.

Waymo described the recalls as voluntary and said they do not require any vehicles to be taken off the road. A company spokesperson said Waymo holds its technology to high safety standards and acts proactively to improve it.

The recalls add to a difficult stretch for the company. Last week, NHTSA opened a separate recall query after reports that Waymo cars had passed stopped school buses, a probe covering nearly 3,000 vehicles. NHTSA and the National Transportation Safety Board are also investigating after a Waymo vehicle struck a child in January near an elementary school in Santa Monica.

What remains unclear is how many vehicles the two new recalls cover — no figure was given — and when the closed-lane fix will be ready or freeway rides will return. The California ban stays in place until Waymo shows its corrective actions work.

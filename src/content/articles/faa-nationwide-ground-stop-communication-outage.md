The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on Friday, halting every departing flight in the United States after a communication problem spread across the country's air traffic control centers.

The order, issued just before 4 p.m. Eastern time, told all departing aircraft to remain on the ground until further notice. In its advisory, the FAA cited "extraordinary circumstances" and "reasons of safety." Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed the stop at 4:16 p.m., saying it followed a communication issue affecting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and that FAA technicians were investigating.

Flights already in the air were allowed to continue to their destinations and land. International flights heading to the United States were to be handled on a case-by-case basis. Flight-tracking service Flightradar24 reported that about 3,100 aircraft were airborne and bound for U.S. airports when the order took effect, with no additional aircraft taking off.

The disruption rippled through the system quickly. Reuters reported that nearly 7,000 flights across the country had been delayed or canceled because of the stop. The FAA's advisory listed an effective period beginning at 3:54 p.m. Eastern and running to 11:30 p.m. Eastern, but the directive itself said departures should stay grounded until further notice, and the agency gave no estimate for when normal operations would resume.

Reports pointed to problems beginning at about 3:30 p.m. Eastern at the FAA's Kansas City Air Traffic Control Center before spreading to other centers. Flightradar24 linked the order to an equipment outage, and the National Weather Service also reported a national radar outage. The FAA has not named a cause, and officials have not said whether a cyberattack is suspected.

## Only the third stop of its kind

A nationwide halt of all departures is exceptionally rare. The only comparable events were September 11, 2001, when U.S. airspace was closed after the terrorist attacks, and January 11, 2023, when the FAA's system for distributing critical flight safety notices failed and departures were grounded for hours.

That history underlines how seriously the agency treats a loss of reliable communication with aircraft. Controllers depend on radio contact to keep planes separated in the sky, and a failure that touches every control center at once leaves the system with no safe way to launch more flights.

## What is still unknown

This is a developing story. The FAA has not said what caused the communication failure, how long the ground stop will last, or when departures will resume. Passengers with flights scheduled for Friday evening were advised to check with their airlines, as delays and cancellations were expected to continue even after the stop is lifted.

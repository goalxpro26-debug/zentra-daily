Ontario prosecutors on Friday asked a court to send Kenneth Law to prison for 25 years, arguing that the scale of his crimes was without precedent.

Law, 61, appeared at a sentencing hearing at the Ontario Superior Court of Justice in Newmarket, six months after pleading guilty to 14 counts of counselling or aiding suicide in Ontario. Crown prosecutor Katie Doherty told Justice Michelle Fuerst that a 25-year term would reflect the global reach of his offending and his moral blameworthiness.

The proposed sentence is built from 12-and-a-half-year terms on two of the counts, served one after the other, with the remaining terms running at the same time. Because aiding suicide carries a maximum of 14 years per count in Canada, the Crown's request depends on stacking the terms. With credit for the time Law has spent in custody since his arrest in May 2023, he would have roughly 19 years and eight months left to serve.

The defence asked for 12 years. Law's lawyers called the Crown's request a de facto life sentence and described their client as a misguided believer in the right to die rather than a mercenary predator. Law himself apologised to the victims' families from the prisoner's box, saying his actions were "categorically wrong" and promising never to repeat them.

The case turns on the sheer number of people affected. Prosecutors said 93 people, including 12 teenagers, died after using products Law sold them. Fourteen of those deaths were in Ontario; 79 were in the United Kingdom. The youngest victim was a 14-year-old boy in Britain, and just under half of the 93 were in their twenties.

According to an agreed statement of facts, Law ran several websites selling sodium nitrite, a meat-curing chemical that is lethal in concentrated doses, along with related products. He shipped about 1,200 packages to people in more than 40 countries, including 330 packages to Britain, and earned roughly $300,000 from the sales. Prosecutors said he entered online forums where people discussed ending their lives, using the alias "Greenberg" to steer them toward his shops.

Law was arrested in May 2023, a week after an investigation by The Times exposed his operation. The court heard he had first sent out his products in 2020, and that 79 of the 93 deaths occurred after police in Surrey challenged him about his sales in 2022.

He had faced 14 counts of first-degree murder as well as the aiding-suicide counts. Those murder charges were dropped as part of the plea deal that produced his guilty plea in May. Prosecutors have said the deal reflected uncertainty over the legal test for when a seller's conduct amounts to murder.

Justice Fuerst adjourned the sentencing decision until February 26. The hearing also heard victim impact statements from families, delivered at an earlier session in September, describing lives shattered by grief.

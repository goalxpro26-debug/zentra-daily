Russia violated Polish airspace overnight, with three objects — probably two missiles and a jet-powered drone — coming down in south-eastern Poland, Warsaw said on Friday. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incursion appeared to be a deliberate provocation.

"There has been a violation of Polish airspace and an incursion by objects from the Russian Federation," Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz told a press conference. One of the objects was a Kh-101 cruise missile, he said; a second was probably also a missile, and the third was most likely a jet-powered drone.

The objects came down at three sites: Nowy Lipowiec, Mołodycz and Rudnik nad Sanem. At Nowy Lipowiec, about 60 kilometres from the Ukrainian border, an object about seven metres long fell at 05:40 GMT, sending up black smoke. A resident described hearing a whistling sound, then a dull thud. Debris was scattered across farmland at Mołodycz, about 50 kilometres to the south. At Rudnik nad Sanem, a traffic controller heard a loud bang near railway premises, though no damage to the railway was found, and air traffic controllers were evacuated at the airport in Rzeszów. No injuries were reported in Poland, and schools near Nowy Lipowiec suspended classes for the day.

The timing is what Warsaw is pointing to. The objects fell as Poland prepared to host "Coalition of the Willing" military exercises on Friday, with defence ministers from France, the United Kingdom and the Baltic states and eight chiefs of staff in the country. Kosiniak-Kamysz said the convergence of the incursion and the exercises "is no accident". Tusk said there was no doubt the objects were Russian, and added: "It seems we are dealing with an intentional provocation." He stressed, however, that the link to the exercises remained a hypothesis.

Tusk said NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte would visit Warsaw on Saturday, and Poland would ask the alliance for more support in securing its eastern flank. The exercises will go ahead as planned, Kosiniak-Kamysz said. The Russian embassy in Warsaw did not immediately respond to a request for comment from Reuters.

The incursion came during a large overnight Russian attack on Ukraine that killed three people, injured nine and damaged bridges and transport infrastructure, according to the Associated Press. Drones and missiles have repeatedly strayed into the airspace of NATO's eastern-flank countries during the war, fuelling fears the conflict could spread. Investigations are under way at the three Polish sites, and prosecutors have opened an inquiry into the airspace violation.

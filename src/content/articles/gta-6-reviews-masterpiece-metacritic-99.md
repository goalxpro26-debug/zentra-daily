After 13 years of waiting, the verdict on Grand Theft Auto 6 is in — and, according to the first reviews, it was worth it. The game averages 99 out of 100 on Metacritic, the review-aggregation site, tying the record held since 1998 by Nintendo's The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time.

The reviews landed on Wednesday, a day before the game's worldwide release on Thursday, November 19. Critics were near-unanimous in their praise, describing an open-world game of unusual scale and polish from Rockstar Games, the studio behind the series.

IGN called the game "unprecedented", "unbelievable" and "unmissable". Eurogamer awarded it five stars, with reviewer Chris Tapsell writing that Rockstar had pushed back the horizon of video games once again, while noting there were still nits to pick. Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, also in a five-star review, called it a "masterpiece" and wrote that there is nothing else like it. The Washington Post said the game "raises the bar at every level".

Much of the praise centred on Leonida, the Miami-inspired setting, and on the two lead characters, Jason and Lucia. The Guardian described Leonida as so lifelike that at times it beggars belief, and said the leads feel like real people rather than puppets dancing to the player's whim. Forbes reviewer Paul Tassi wrote that he had never seen a better video game relationship than the one the pair share. The New York Times' Yussef Cole struck a more measured note, saying the game had matured beyond its memorable car crashes and stickups, while questioning whether its realism might dull some of the fun chaos of its predecessors.

Gaming journalist Jordan Middler, who played for around 100 hours to reach the credits, called it the "game of a generation", adding that it can slow down at times because of how much is happening on screen.

The scale of the production is reflected in its cost, though Rockstar has not disclosed a figure. Some estimates put it north of $1 billion (£760 million). Take-Two Interactive, Rockstar's parent company, saw its shares rise on the news, and analysts estimated preorders at $1.65 billion, according to Sensor Tower figures cited by Barron's.

The game has already been recognised by the industry before a single copy has been sold: it received eight nominations at The Game Awards, including game of the year and best music, announced on Tuesday. One of those nominations also confirmed Manni L Perez as the actress behind Lucia.

Not everyone is swept along. Some posters on gaming forums have pushed back against what they see as ultra fan-boy hype. But with more than 140 critic reviews counted and the score holding at 99, the early consensus is clear: the most anticipated game in years has, for most critics, lived up to it.

China will launch its Shenzhou-24 spacecraft on Wednesday morning, carrying a Pakistani astronaut who will become the first foreign national to visit the country's Tiangong space station.

The three-person crew consists of commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi and Pakistani payload specialist Zeeshan Ali. The spacecraft is scheduled to lift off at 8:18 a.m. local time (0018 GMT) aboard a Long March-2F rocket from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, the China Manned Space Agency announced on Tuesday.

Ali's participation is part of bilateral cooperation under Beijing's Belt and Road Initiative, according to the agency. His flight marks the first time a non-Chinese astronaut has boarded Tiangong, a station China has operated with all-Chinese crews since it began hosting long-duration missions in 2021.

## A short visit, then a handover

Ali is scheduled for a short-term stay rather than a full six-month rotation. He will return to Earth with two members of the outgoing Shenzhou-23 crew, while the third, Zhu Yangzhu, will remain in orbit for a year-long mission, authorities said.

Shenzhou-24 is the 41st flight mission of China's crewed space programme. Once in orbit, it will complete a handover with the Shenzhou-23 crew, and the new arrivals will carry out space science experiments alongside planned station research.

The mission is the latest step in a programme that has sent trios of Chinese astronauts to Tiangong for six-month stays since 2021, steadily expanding the station's role as a platform for science and for international cooperation.

## A wider race to the Moon

The launch comes as the contest to return astronauts to the Moon accelerates. The United States has alleged that Beijing plans to colonise and mine lunar territory and resources, claims Beijing has strongly rejected.

China has not set out a crewed lunar landing date in its public announcements, but its space programme has moved quickly from building Tiangong to planning deeper exploration. For Pakistan, Ali's flight is a milestone of its own: a national first, achieved through a partnership with its closest regional ally.

The crew is reported to be in good condition, and launch preparations are complete, the agency said.

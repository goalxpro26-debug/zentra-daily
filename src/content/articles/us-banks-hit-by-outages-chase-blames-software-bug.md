Customers at some of the largest banks in the United States reported problems reaching their accounts and moving money on Monday afternoon, in an outage that appeared to be easing by the early evening.

JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America and US Bank all drew customer complaints, with the disruption showing up in online banking, mobile apps and, in some cases, in branches and at ATMs. By 6 p.m. Eastern time, the volume of reported problems had fallen for Chase, Bank of America and US Bank.

Chase, the country's largest bank, said a software bug in its hardware systems may have been behind the glitch. The bank described the problem as temporary and said it was not systemic or cyber-related. It said some transactions were taking longer than usual, but that customers still had access to their accounts and funds. The exact nature of the bug was not clear.

Outage tracker Downdetector recorded almost 5,000 reports about Chase alone. Customers said they struggled to sign in, view balances and complete transactions, including transfers and wire transfers. One customer in Texas said she could not deposit a cheque at a Chase branch in Dallas and was told the transaction could not be completed. Another was told by a teller that the system was down.

Bank of America customers reported trouble at ATMs and with mobile banking. US Bank customers using the Zelle payment service received error messages saying payments could not be sent. Zelle said its own service was operating as it should, and that problems at other banks could affect payments. US Bank said some of its systems were not directly affected.

Customers also reported problems at Truist, PNC and Capital One, but all three told the BBC they had not been affected.

The scale of the disruption reflects how central the affected banks are to everyday American finance. Chase serves more than 86 million customers through 5,000 branches and 7.4 million small businesses, and is the top credit card issuer in the United States.

## What is still unknown

Service had not been described as fully restored for all of the banks by Monday evening, and it was not clear how long the remaining problems would take to clear. Chase has not said when it expects the bug to be fully fixed.

The banking outage came on the same day as a separate technology problem at United Airlines, which disrupted departing flights for a few hours before being resolved. That outage was also described as a technology issue rather than a cybersecurity incident, and was unrelated to the banking problems or to the nationwide ground stop ordered by the Federal Aviation Administration on Friday.

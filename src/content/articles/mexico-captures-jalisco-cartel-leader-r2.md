Mexican security forces arrested Rafael Alvarez, known as "R2", an alleged leader of a Jalisco New Generation Cartel cell, in a raid near Guadalajara on Sunday that drew on intelligence shared by the United States Drug Enforcement Administration.

The operation took place in Zapopan, in the Guadalajara metropolitan area, and was carried out by the Mexican Navy's special operations unit together with the federal security ministry. Mexico's National Intelligence Center also took part. Security Minister Omar Garcia Harfuch said Alvarez was wounded in a shootout that broke out during the arrest and is receiving medical care. Nineteen other people were detained in the same operation.

## Who the authorities say he is

Alvarez is suspected of heading "Los Rs", a cell of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, known as the CJNG, operating in the states of Jalisco and Michoacan. Harfuch said the cell was "mainly linked to extortion, drug trafficking, homicide, illegal deprivation of liberty, kidnapping and attacks against authorities", and that its extortion victims included business owners and avocado producers.

The Navy linked Alvarez to the killing of at least 19 people, including Carlos Manzo, the popular crime-fighting mayor of Uruapan in Michoacan state, who was murdered last year. Those are allegations made by the authorities; Alvarez has not been convicted of any offence, and no response from him or from the group has appeared in the reporting.

Alvarez faces an outstanding arrest warrant in Mexico for aggravated homicide and a separate warrant for extradition to the United States. The DEA said he is wanted in the US on federal drug trafficking and firearms charges, describing him as a senior leader of the CJNG "responsible for drug trafficking, extortion, kidnapping, and deadly violence across Mexico". CBS News reported the same account of the arrest on Monday.

## Why the arrest matters

The CJNG is one of Mexico's two main criminal groups and competes with the Sinaloa Cartel for drug trafficking into the United States. The US government designated it a "foreign terrorist organisation" in 2025. Since the death of the group's founder, Nemesio "El Mencho" Oseguera, in a military operation in February, several of its top leaders have been apprehended.

The arrest is the latest in a run of high-profile detentions, but it does not by itself change the security picture in the region. Reports of kidnapping for ransom continue in the areas where the cartels are active, and an estimated 134,000 people are believed to be missing across Mexico, with Jalisco recording the country's highest numbers of disappearances.

Harfuch described the information released so far as preliminary and said it would be updated as the investigation advances. Officials have not said whether the Mexican case or the US extradition request will proceed first.

Waymo has paused driverless taxi rides on freeways across the United States, days after California's motor vehicles department banned its self-driving cars from the state's highways over safety concerns.

The Alphabet-owned company said it has "temporarily restricted commercial freeway rides in all markets where we operate that service". The pause, implemented last Wednesday, covers the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles County, Phoenix, Miami and Austin — the five areas where Waymo offered freeway rides. Rides on city streets continue as normal.

The California Department of Motor Vehicles imposed its ban effective November 11, after two incidents in early November. On November 5, three Waymo vehicles drove into an active construction zone on US Route 101 near Burlingame, south of San Francisco. The next day, a Waymo car on Highway 280 in San Francisco sped towards two California Highway Patrol cars and tried to pass them while their lights were flashing during a traffic break. Video of the second incident spread widely online.

The department said Waymo's driverless freeway operations pose "an imminent hazard to public safety" because of "recurring incursions" into active construction zones, putting road workers, vehicles and equipment at risk. It said the restrictions will remain in place until Waymo demonstrates it has taken "effective corrective actions". The regulator noted that similar incidents had occurred on May 18, and that this is the second time this year Waymo has faced highway restrictions in California — it paused freeway operations in May in San Francisco and Los Angeles to improve how its vehicles respond to construction zones.

Waymo said the November 5 construction-zone incident caused no injuries or collisions, and that it is working with the California DMV and other state officials to resume freeway operations. Freeway testing continues with trained autonomous specialists behind the wheel, and the company says the pause will let it "evaluate our performance". Waymo, which completes about half a million trips a week, points to its own data showing 95 percent fewer crashes resulting in serious injuries than human drivers.

The pause matters because freeway driving was a key step in Waymo's expansion beyond city streets, and because it is the sharpest regulatory intervention the company has faced. Robotaxis have already drawn scrutiny from safety investigators over red-light running, driving into oncoming traffic and failing to respect emergency road closures — a June CNN analysis found hundreds of such incidents. Waymo has described most of those as isolated, and says the overwhelming majority of its rides are smooth and uneventful.

## What is still unknown

Neither Waymo nor the DMV has given a timeline for when freeway rides will resume, or said what specific corrective actions would satisfy the regulator. It is also unclear whether other states will follow California's lead, or whether the pause will slow Waymo's plans to expand into new cities.

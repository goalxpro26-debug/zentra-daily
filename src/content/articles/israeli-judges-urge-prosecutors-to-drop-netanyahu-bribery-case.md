The judges hearing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's corruption trial told prosecutors on Wednesday that the most serious case against him should be dropped — a dramatic intervention in a prosecution that has run for more than six years.

The three-judge panel of the Jerusalem District Court summoned Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to the hearing and said the bribery case, known as Case 4000, was unlikely to end in a conviction. The presiding judge, Rivka Friedman-Feldman, said the charge presented "considerable difficulties" and that the prospects of a conviction were low enough to justify reconsidering the case.

In Case 4000, prosecutors allege that Netanyahu granted regulatory favours worth around 1.8 billion shekels — about $500 million — to the telecoms company Bezeq, and in return sought favourable coverage for himself and his wife, Sara, on the Walla news website, then controlled by Bezeq's former chairman, Shaul Elovitch. Netanyahu faces charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust in the case, the most serious of the three against him.

The judges went further than they had before. They had twice previously urged prosecutors to reconsider the bribery charge, in June 2023 and again in June of this year. On Wednesday, they said their doubts extended to the fraud and breach-of-trust charges in the same case as well.

They also recommended that prosecutors reconsider a second case, Case 2000, in which Netanyahu is accused of negotiating an arrangement with Arnon Mozes, the owner of the Yedioth Ahronoth newspaper, under which he would receive more favourable coverage in return for legislation slowing the growth of a rival paper. The judges said they were not convinced the conduct described in the indictment amounted to a criminal offence.

On the third case, Case 1000, the judges struck a different note. That case concerns gifts, including cigars and champagne, that Netanyahu is alleged to have received from wealthy businessmen. The judges did not suggest dropping it; instead they said the prosecution and defence should try to reach an agreement and find a procedural path to bring it to a conclusion.

The court stressed that its comments were recommendations, not a verdict. Baharav-Miara told the court she would review the judges' statements with the prosecution team. The judges also made clear that their assessment concerned Netanyahu alone; dropping the charges against him would not automatically end the prosecution of Shaul and Iris Elovitch, whose liability would need separate consideration.

Netanyahu, who was indicted in 2019 and whose trial began in May 2020, welcomed the development and said the trial "must end". He has denied wrongdoing throughout.

The hearing came after the completion of Netanyahu's own testimony, which the court said had brought the proceedings to a point requiring a fresh assessment. There is also a practical clock running: Friedman-Feldman, who heads the panel, is due to retire in March 2028.

No charges have been withdrawn yet. But the judges' message was unmistakable: two of the three cases may not be sustainable.

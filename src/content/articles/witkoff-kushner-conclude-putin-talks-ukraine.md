U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner ended talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, closing a week of diplomacy that restarted direct negotiations between Russia and Ukraine but produced no breakthrough on ending the war.

The Moscow meeting followed two days of U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi between Russian and Ukrainian delegations — the first direct contact between the two sides since February. That round ended on Wednesday with an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange, though the exact number of detainees has yet to be settled, and no clear resolution on the war itself.

Speaking to reporters at Moscow's Vnukovo Airport shortly before departing, Witkoff struck an upbeat note. "I think progress was made. I think it was made earlier today," he said. "People didn't think that we were going to restart trilateral. We've restarted it. We've set a new meeting."

Kushner, who holds no official role in the Trump administration despite acting as one of its top diplomats, described the discussions as "very fulsome and detailed" and said they would inform the next round of multilateral talks.

## Europe's seat at the table

One of the envoys' goals in Moscow was to secure Putin's backing for European representatives to join the negotiations, which are also looking at the continent's broader security arrangements. Witkoff said European officials would take part in the next round. "They're our partners, and it's important for them to be involved," he told reporters.

Putin's foreign policy aide, Yuri Ushakov, confirmed that the three-way talks involving Russia, Ukraine and the United States would continue, but appeared to play down the prospect of European participation. "They are making every effort to get involved in the negotiations, but, as our president has said, they need to decide whether they want peace or a continuation of the war," Ushakov said.

Putin himself told the envoys that Russia had "never objected (to) pursuing dialogue" with Europe. "You want this peace to be a durable and lasting one, and I confirm that I subscribe to that, and I'd say more. I want this peace to be eternal, for good," he said.

Moscow has so far resisted European governments sitting at the main negotiating table. Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov criticised the presence of European officials in Abu Dhabi earlier in the week, calling it not a "promising approach," and Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said no contacts had taken place between the Russians and any EU representatives there.

## What happens next

The U.S. envoys are now expected to fly to Dublin, where they will meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and brief him on the Moscow meeting. Zelenskyy was already in the Irish capital on Thursday for a summit of the European Political Community, where the Abu Dhabi talks and additional sanctions on Russia were on the agenda.

The diplomacy has not brought Ukraine any respite from the fighting. Moscow launched a ballistic missile and drone attack overnight into Thursday, according to the Ukrainian Air Force, and at least one person was killed in Kyiv on Thursday morning, local authorities said.

Zelenskyy also warned on Thursday of renewed disruptions to Starshield, the military-grade version of Elon Musk's Starlink service that is critical to Ukraine's military operations, saying the service had faced "significant disruptions" that were hampering Kyiv's ability to shoot down Russian drones. SpaceX, which owns Starshield, told CNN there had been no change in its service.

The war is approaching its fifth year: large-scale hostilities began on February 24, 2022, following fighting that first broke out in eastern Ukraine in 2014.

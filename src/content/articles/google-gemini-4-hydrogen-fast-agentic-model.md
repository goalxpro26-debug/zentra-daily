Google on Wednesday introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a new model in its Gemini 4 family built for speed rather than maximum reasoning power, aimed at everyday coding and the multi-step jobs AI agents carry out alone. It is rolling out now across Google's developer tools, the Gemini app and Google Search.

Hydrogen sits a tier below the family's frontier models, Carbon and Argon, designed for deep, long-horizon reasoning on hard problems. The idea is that the most powerful model is not always the right tool: for routine work — writing code, running tests, verifying changes, carrying research through several steps — a faster, cheaper model that is still capable can be the better choice. Google calls Hydrogen its daily driver for agentic workflows.

On Google's own numbers, the trade-off is small. At its default high thinking setting, Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, edging out Argon at 50, and reaches 54 at its highest level, a new "xhigh" tier. It completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, the fast model it replaces, and on Terminal-Bench 4.1, a test of agentic terminal work, it scores 57 percent against Flash's 18.2 percent, cutting fabricated claims by more than five times. On multi-step tasks such as deep research and broad code navigation, it ranks fifth on Agent Arena.

## Search gets the biggest upgrade

The most widely felt change may be in Google Search, where Hydrogen is rolling out to all users — Google calls it its biggest model improvement to Search yet. The model is meant to handle complicated questions better — ones with several parts, highly specific requirements or confusing typos — and to give more helpful links and visual responses in AI Mode. Its improved reasoning also sharpens Search's fan-out technique, which breaks one prompt into several subqueries, and users should see more images, structured answers, comparison tables and custom tools as visual responses.

## Pricing, thinking levels and teamwork

Hydrogen offers four thinking levels — low, medium, high as the default, and the new xhigh tier — so users pay and wait only for the reasoning depth a task needs. Pricing is flat across its full one-million-token context window — $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens — with native one-million-token input and up to one million output tokens.

The model is also designed to work in teams. Alongside it, Google introduced Orchestrator in Antigravity, its development platform, which switches between models inside one session: Carbon can coordinate while Hydrogen powers worker and verifier agents building and testing changes in parallel. Hydrogen is available now in all tiers of Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini for business, the Gemini app, Search, and for paid AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in Antigravity.

One caution: every benchmark cited here is Google's own, measured on tests Google chose. Independent evaluations have yet to publish, and they will be the real test of whether the speed comes with the reliability Google claims.

Kilmar Abrego Garcia left the United States voluntarily on Tuesday, boarding a flight to Spain and bringing to a close a legal fight that has lasted nearly 20 months and, at one point, reached the Supreme Court.

Abrego Garcia, a 31-year-old Salvadoran national who has lived in Maryland for years, departed under an arrangement approved by a federal judge, who had told him to leave the country by Tuesday. His departure was confirmed by a person familiar with his travel, who spoke on condition of anonymity.

His case became one of the defining sagas of the Trump administration's mass-deportation drive. He was arrested in Baltimore in March 2025 and flown to a maximum-security prison in El Salvador, even though a 2019 immigration judge had ruled he could not be returned there because he faced danger from a gang that had threatened his family. An administration official later described the removal as an "administrative error."

Facing public pressure and a court order, the administration brought him back in June 2025 — but only after securing an indictment charging him with human smuggling in Tennessee. A federal judge dismissed that case in May, finding that the prosecution had been designed to punish Abrego Garcia for challenging his deportation. The government is appealing.

The administration then moved to deport him to a third country, at various points naming Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana and, most recently, Liberia. Abrego Garcia argued that if he had to leave, it should be to Costa Rica, which had agreed to take him. But Todd Lyons, the former acting director of Immigration and Customs Enforcement, wrote in a March memo that sending him to Costa Rica would be "prejudicial to the United States," saying Liberia should be the destination because Washington had spent government resources and political capital negotiating for it to accept third-country nationals.

The case took an unusual turn on October 6, when lawyers for both sides walked out of a Maryland courtroom five minutes before a hearing on the planned Liberia deportation. When they returned, the hearing was cancelled without explanation.

Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, who travelled to El Salvador last year to press for Abrego Garcia's release, welcomed the outcome. "I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free," he said in a statement, describing Abrego Garcia as "a free man" travelling to a country of his choice.

What comes next is not settled. The New York Times reported, citing his lawyers, that he ultimately plans to make his way to Mexico, and he may not remain in Spain long term. The Department of Homeland Security did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The leaders of the United States, China and Russia used their first three-way summit on Thursday to take aim at the International Criminal Court, agreeing in a joint declaration that the Hague-based court "must not abuse" its jurisdiction.

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met for 80 minutes at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in China. Afterwards they issued a four-point "declaration toward peace" — the first statement the three leaders have ever signed together.

The declaration covered reform of the United Nations, increased supplies of refined oil, a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks, and the court. On the court, the three leaders said the consent of states and respect for the immunity of state officials were of paramount importance, and agreed that the court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction."

Trump went further in the meeting's public opening, describing the court as a rogue globalist organisation that had exceeded its authority and was "out of control", and grouping it with other bodies he said undermined national sovereignty.

The language carries a pointed edge for Putin. The court issued an arrest warrant for the Russian president in March 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children, and the warrant remains in force. None of the three countries — the United States, China or Russia — is a member of the court.

For all its symbolism, the summit settled little else. The declaration named no nuclear cap, mentioned neither Ukraine nor Iran, and no deal emerged on the wars that filled the agenda. The statement was released through Chinese state media and later by Russia; no US version had been issued when the meeting ended.

The three leaders reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought", and promised talks on reducing nuclear risks. Whether the declaration marks a durable alignment — or a one-day show of unity — will be judged by what, if anything, follows it.

US stocks fell sharply on Wednesday as the sell-off in artificial-intelligence shares resumed, wiping out much of the relief rally that followed Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang's return to the company two days earlier.

The Nasdaq Composite was down about 3 percent shortly after the opening bell, and closer to 4 percent in early trading, while the S&P 500 fell 2.6 percent and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped more than 900 points, or about 1.9 percent. The losses spread well beyond technology: chipmakers, optical-networking firms, industrial suppliers and energy companies tied to the AI buildout were all under pressure.

Nvidia led the decline, falling about 8 percent in early trading after sliding nearly 6 percent on Tuesday. The chipmaker had surged more than 30 percent on Monday — one of its best days on record — after its board reinstated Huang as chief executive, only for the rebound to fizzle within a day. Anthropic, the AI company behind Claude, fell about 6 percent to around $77 a share, slipping further below its IPO price.

The renewed selling reflects growing doubt about whether the AI boom can sustain itself. Investors have questioned the scale of spending on data centres, much of it funded by debt, and the returns companies will earn from it. The cost of insuring technology companies' debt against default has risen, and credit spreads across AI-linked borrowers have widened — signs that lenders, not just shareholders, are growing cautious.

Two other worries added to the pressure. Fresh inflation data due on Wednesday morning fuelled expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates again, and new tariffs on major US trading partners threatened to lift costs further. Gold fell below $3,500 an ounce, its lowest since September 2025, as traders braced for higher rates.

The slide caps a volatile week. Monday's rally had lifted the Dow by more than 1,000 points and the Nasdaq by about 7 percent; on Tuesday the Nasdaq gave back 1.5 percent to close at 23,733.54 and the S&P 500 slipped 0.5 percent to 7,201.09, even as the Dow rose 0.85 percent to 50,018.70 — its first close above 50,000 in about a week — on strength in health care and consumer stocks.

With US markets closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving and trading ending early on Friday, investors face a thin, holiday-shortened stretch in which moves can be amplified. Whether the AI trade steadies or keeps falling may depend less on any single company than on the inflation data and the market's verdict on how much more the data-centre boom is worth.

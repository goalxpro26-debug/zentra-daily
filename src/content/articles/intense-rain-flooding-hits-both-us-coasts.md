A slow-moving storm system dumped heavy rain on both American coasts on Wednesday, flooding roads across South Florida and grounding flights in San Francisco, with forecasters warning more rain is coming in Florida.

In South Florida, preliminary figures from the National Weather Service showed 15.3 inches of rain fell in a 24-hour period ending Wednesday in parts of Broward County. The city of Hollywood declared a state of emergency, and Mayor Josh Levy urged motorists to stay off the roads, calling it a historically significant rainfall event. Portions of Interstate 95 and the Florida Turnpike in Hollywood were closed because of high water and stalled vehicles, and the city said it received more than 300 calls for service overnight.

Heavy rain caused the roof of Temple Sinai in Hollywood to collapse. No injuries were reported. Cars were submerged along roads across the region, and in Fort Lauderdale the 5.72 inches that fell in a downpour ending early Wednesday broke the daily record of 1.8 inches set in 1959. Farther north, a warning was issued for minor flooding along the St. Johns River.

The Florida Division of Emergency Management warned on Tuesday night that potentially catastrophic amounts of rainfall could cause flash flooding and life-threatening rip tides. The threat was expected to last through Friday along Florida's Atlantic Coast, with another 12 inches of rain possible in places. NBC News reported that about 11 million people between Miami and Jacksonville faced flooding risks.

Forecasters link the deluge to El Niño, the warming cycle that has disrupted Florida's typical October-to-May dry season, steering low-pressure systems toward the southeastern United States and bringing repeated rounds of heavy rain. It is the second major flooding episode to hit Florida this month, after record rain in early November.

A new concern looms ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. A low-pressure system is expected to form near the northern Bahamas and could become a subtropical or tropical storm, bringing more rain to parts of the southeast and mid-Atlantic coasts into early next week, according to the National Hurricane Center in Miami.

California was hit by the same coast-to-coast pattern. Heavy rain and high winds grounded planes at San Francisco's airport, disrupted ferry service and flooded roads in Napa County and other wine-country areas north of the city. Along San Francisco's Embarcadero, waves crashed over flood barricades near the Ferry Building, and several ferries were canceled. Highways were blocked by flooding or debris in Napa, Marin, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties.

The Los Angeles County Fire Department issued an evacuation warning on Wednesday for the Palisades, Hurst and Sunset burn-scar areas, citing a risk of debris flows as the storm approaches. The warning runs from Wednesday evening through Friday morning. Wind gusts up to 50 mph were forecast for San Francisco's East Bay and 70 mph for the Altamont Pass, with as much as four feet of snow expected through Friday in the highest Sierra Nevada.

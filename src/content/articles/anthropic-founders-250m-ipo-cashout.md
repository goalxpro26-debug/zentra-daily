Anthropic's seven co-founders will each take home about $250 million when the artificial intelligence company goes public next week, according to an updated filing — a modest cash-out next to the stakes they will keep, worth roughly $18 billion apiece.

The filing, published on Tuesday, shows each founder plans to sell 3,378,378 shares in the offering. At the midpoint of Anthropic's $70-to-$78 price range, that works out to just under $250 million each, or about $1.75 billion combined. At the low end of the range each would receive just over $236 million; at the high end, just under $264 million. Anthropic itself receives none of the money from the founders' sales.

The seven are chief executive Dario Amodei; his sister Daniela Amodei, the company's president and chair of the board; chief compute officer Tom Brown; Jack Clark, who heads the company's public-benefit institute; chief science officer Jared Kaplan; chief architect Sam McCandlish; and interpretability research lead Christopher Olah.

## A small slice of very large stakes

The sales amount to only about 1.5 percent of each founder's holding. After the offering, each will still own about 3.2 percent of Anthropic's Class A shares — roughly 22 percent between them — with the stakes valued at around $18 billion each at the midpoint of the price range, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index.

The founders have also agreed to transfer restrictions lasting up to a year after the offering, limiting further sales in the near term.

## A record-setting debut

The cash-out figures underline the sheer scale of the listing. Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares, which would raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion, and is seeking to raise as much as $90.4 billion in total once a private placement to large investors is included. At the top of the price range, the company would be valued at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

The company said its run-rate revenue reached $81 billion by the end of October, about $10 billion more than a month earlier. The offering is expected to be priced on November 18, with shares beginning trading on the Nasdaq the following day under the ticker "ANT."

Board member Reed Hastings has indicated he wants to buy $300 million of shares through his donor-advised fund, according to the filing.

## What comes next

The listing will be one of the largest public offerings ever and the biggest test yet of investor appetite for AI companies at trillion-dollar valuations. For the founders, the message of the filing is restraint as much as reward: they are selling a sliver of their holdings while keeping the overwhelming majority — and the voting control that comes with it — for the long term.

Canada and China agreed on Tuesday to widen tariff relief between the two countries, as Prime Minister Mark Carney met President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.

Under the agreement, China will provide additional tariff relief worth about 400 million Canadian dollars (US$280 million) on Canadian exports, including C$116 million of frozen pork cuts and C$285 million of fish and seafood — among them shrimp and prawns, geoduck, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut. In return, Canada will extend for two years tariff remission measures on certain Chinese steel and aluminium products that were due to expire at the end of 2026, and add remissions for 32 more steel and aluminium items for which Canadian supply is insufficient.

The measures build on a preliminary trade arrangement the two leaders struck in January, after Carney's visit to Beijing — the first by a Canadian prime minister since 2017. Under that deal, Canada cut its 100 per cent tariff on Chinese electric vehicles to 6.1 per cent, allowing an initial 49,000 vehicles a year into the country, a cap set to rise to 70,000 over five years. China, in turn, lowered tariffs on Canadian farm goods and recently extended relief for canola meal, peas, lobster and crab for two more years. Carney's office said Canada has now restored market access for more than 90 per cent of the trade previously hit by tariffs — nearly C$7.5 billion of exports.

The deal comes as Carney makes diversifying Canada's trade beyond the United States a central part of his economic strategy, with US tariffs hitting Canadian auto, steel and aluminium producers and trade talks between Ottawa and Washington having broken down in August. A day earlier, in Cambodia, Carney said Canada could turn to Chinese carmakers if talks with Washington fail to hold together the closely integrated North American auto industry — remarks that drew a sharp response from White House trade adviser Peter Navarro, who asked whether Carney was threatening to "play China off the US".

Xi said stronger ties served both countries' interests and supported global stability and prosperity, calling it "the right choice". Carney praised the greater predictability in the relationship and said Canada was ahead of schedule on its goal of increasing exports to China by 50 per cent by the end of the decade. Carney said on Saturday he had spoken with US President Donald Trump and senior officials and that, while differences remained, there was progress towards a possible deal.

Carney also met Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum on Tuesday, with both countries reaffirming their commitment to renew the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement. The two leaders noted a 19 per cent rise in merchandise trade in the first nine months of the year compared with the same period last year.

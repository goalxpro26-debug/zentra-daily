Finland's military said on Thursday that its forces had detected a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland the day before, in what would be the first attack of its kind on a Russian vessel in the Baltic Sea since the war in Ukraine began.

A spokesperson for the Finnish Defence Forces said in an email to Reuters that its forces "detected … a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland area and has monitored the development of the situation." The spokesperson added that Finland was not under "any immediate military threat," and declined to give further details about where or when the strike happened, or how it was detected.

Ukraine's Security Service has said it carried out the attack, according to Finnish public broadcaster Yle. The service said it targeted the Naro-Fominsk, a Buyan-M class missile corvette of Russia's Baltic Fleet, as part of a wider drone operation against the oil export port of Ust-Luga on the Russian side of the gulf. It did not say what weapon was used or provide evidence of damage.

## What the neighbours saw

Estonia's defence minister, Martin Herem, told public broadcaster ERR that an explosion occurred aboard a Russian Baltic Fleet vessel in the gulf at about 5:30 a.m. local time on Wednesday. Estonian media reported that the ship later returned to St Petersburg.

A senior Finnish official told Yle that the attack took place near Gogland, a Russian-held island in the gulf about 40 kilometres off the Finnish coast near Kotka. Brigadier General Markku Viitasaari of Finland's defence ministry told the newspaper Helsingin Sanomat that Finnish sensors recorded flashes and smoke coming from the ship in international waters, and that officials at first thought the vessel was firing at Ukrainian drones before sensor data pointed to at least one explosion on board.

Neither Finland nor Estonia has named the attacker, and Russia has not publicly confirmed that any warship was hit.

## A wider night of drone attacks

The strike came during a broader Ukrainian drone operation against Russia's north-western Leningrad region, which includes St Petersburg and the Baltic port of Ust-Luga, a major export outlet for oil and grain. Russia said it had intercepted at least 146 Ukrainian drones in the region in recent days, and the regional governor confirmed a fire in the Ust-Luga port area after a drone attack.

The same night, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania issued warnings telling residents to take shelter after drones entered their airspace, and NATO warplanes were scrambled in response. Some Ukrainian drones have strayed into the airspace of Finland and the Baltic states before, raising fears that the war is spilling over into NATO countries bordering Russia.

## Diplomacy in parallel

The incident came as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy praised mediation efforts led by US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, who have been shuttling between Kyiv and Moscow in an effort to advance talks on ending the war. The envoys' diplomacy has so far produced no breakthrough, and the fighting — including long-range drone strikes deep inside Russia — has continued alongside it.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear whether anyone was hurt in the strike, how badly the vessel was damaged, or whether the ship Finland and Estonia observed was the Naro-Fominsk. The Finnish military has not said how it detected the strike, and Russia has not confirmed a hit on the warship by name. Until those details are confirmed, the episode stands as a detected strike with a claimed attacker — and a sign of how close the war now runs to NATO's northern flank.

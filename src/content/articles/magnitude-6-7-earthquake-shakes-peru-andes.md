A magnitude 6.7 earthquake shook the Andes in central Peru on Friday evening, sending residents of the capital, Lima, rushing out of their homes and into the streets. Authorities reported no immediate casualties or major damage.

The US Geological Survey said the quake struck at 7:09 p.m. local time, with its epicentre in the Andes about 34 kilometres north of Junín, at a depth of 108 kilometres. Peru's Geophysical Institute put the epicentre slightly differently — about 30 kilometres north-west of Junín — and measured the depth at 129 kilometres. The shaking was felt across a wide area, including Lima, Huánuco, Callao and the Andean regions of Áncash, Ica, Ucayali and Cerro de Pasco.

Peru's Geophysical Institute said no damage or casualties had been reported in the hours after the tremor. The mayor of Carhuamayo, a town near the epicentre, told a local television station there were no reports of victims or material damage there. Later updates pointed to minor damage to some homes, power cuts and landslides in places near the epicentre, but still no injuries or deaths, as emergency officials continued monitoring vulnerable areas.

In Lima, hundreds of people evacuated buildings in the Miraflores district after the tremor, and television footage showed lawmakers leaving the Congress building as the shaking was felt in the capital. Peru's National Emergency Operation Center said the quake was felt with moderate to strong intensity.

Peru lies along the "Ring of Fire", a 40,000-kilometre arc of volcanoes and fault lines around the Pacific Basin where most of the world's earthquakes occur. The country is no stranger to strong tremors: in August, a magnitude 6.7 earthquake in the southern Andes injured at least two people and damaged homes, health centres and schools in the Ayacucho and Cusco regions, and in July, a 5.5-magnitude quake in the Junín region killed six people and injured 30.

The depth of Friday's quake — more than 100 kilometres below the surface — likely helped limit the damage at ground level, even though the shaking was felt far from the epicentre. Authorities said monitoring would continue through the night.

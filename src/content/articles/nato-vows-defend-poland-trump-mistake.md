NATO's secretary-general pledged on Saturday to defend "every inch" of Polish territory, a day after Russian missiles and a drone came down in south-eastern Poland during a heavy bombardment of Ukraine.

Mark Rutte made the promise during a visit to Warsaw, telling Polish media he could guarantee the alliance would defend Poland, according to the Polish Press Agency. The head of NATO forces in Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, would do everything necessary to identify threats and adjust deterrence if needed, Rutte said. NATO's North Atlantic Council, the alliance's main decision-making body, will discuss the incident in Brussels on Monday.

The pledge came after Polish officials said two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone fell on Polish territory during Russia's overnight attack on Ukraine on November 20. One missile, believed to be a Kh-101 cruise missile, came down near Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border; a second suspected missile fell near Rudnik nad Sanem, and the drone near Molodycz. No injuries or damage were reported in Poland.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incursion appeared to be "an intentional provocation" by Russia, likely timed to coincide with the Coalition of the Willing military exercises under way in Poland. The multinational exercise, involving troops from 11 countries, is preparing forces for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine.

The response from Washington has been strikingly different. President Donald Trump, speaking by phone to Fox News aboard Air Force One on Thursday, said of Russia's earlier incursions: "Putin has been landing drones in Poland and a couple of little missiles, by mistake." The remark came hours before the latest objects fell — and it was aimed at Tusk's criticism of Trump for greeting Vladimir Putin as "my friend" at a summit in Shenzhen. Tusk had answered on social media: "God save us from such friends."

That night, Russia launched one of its heaviest bombardments of Ukraine, firing more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. At least eight civilians were killed and more than two dozen were wounded.

Poland has not invoked Article 4, the clause under which allies hold emergency consultations — it did so in September 2025 after Russian drones entered its airspace. Invoking Article 5, which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all, requires agreement from all 32 members, and analysts say the bar remains high: it would take a sustained attack on Poland, not a handful of stray weapons.

For now, Warsaw is asking for more NATO support under the alliance's Eastern Sentry mission, which already patrols the eastern flank. What form that takes — possibly an airborne early-warning aircraft — is still to be decided.

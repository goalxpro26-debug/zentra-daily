A UPS cargo plane and a Copa Airlines passenger jet collided while taxiing at Miami International Airport on Saturday night, leaving two people with minor injuries and both aircraft damaged, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) said.

The collision happened at about 9:15 p.m. local time at the intersection of two taxiways. Copa Airlines Flight 427, a Boeing 737-800 that had just arrived from Panama City with 166 passengers and six crew members aboard, was taxiing toward its gate. UPS Flight 358, a Boeing 767 freighter, was taxiing for departure to Bogotá, Colombia. The wingtip of the Copa jet struck the nose of the UPS aircraft, according to the FAA.

Air traffic controllers had instructed the UPS crew to give way and let the arriving Copa flight pass before continuing toward the runway. Radio recordings of the exchange show the UPS crew acknowledging the mistake moments after the impact. After the Copa crew reported that somebody had hit them, the controller asked the UPS crew whether they had hit the aircraft. The UPS pilot replied: "Yeah, we went too far and then he... he clipped us with the wing."

The Copa pilot later told controllers: "It seems that we are missing the left wingtip."

## Two treated at the scene

Miami-Dade Fire Rescue treated two people at the scene for minor injuries. Neither needed to be taken to hospital, airport communications director Greg Chin said. All 166 passengers and six crew members aboard the Copa flight later disembarked after the aircraft was moved back to the terminal, the airline confirmed.

The airport said the collision had no effect on its overall operations, with aircraft redirected to other taxiways while the two planes were dealt with. UPS said its crew was safe and that it was committed to working with the authorities to determine what happened.

## Investigation under way

The FAA has opened an investigation into the collision. No official cause has been established yet, and the extent of the damage to either aircraft has not been officially confirmed, although images posted on social media show significant damage to the nose of the UPS jet.

There is a suggestion that a runway change may have added to the UPS crew's workload shortly before the collision. During the radio exchange, one of the pilots said the crew was in the middle of a checklist for a runway change. That has been reported as a possible factor, not as a finding — the investigation will determine what happened.

The incident came a day after a rare nationwide ground stop brought departures across the United States to a halt for more than four hours on Friday, following problems with the air traffic control network.

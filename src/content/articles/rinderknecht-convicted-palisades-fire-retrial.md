A federal jury in Los Angeles has convicted the man accused of starting the blaze that became the Palisades Fire, one of the deadliest and most destructive wildfires in the city's history.

Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, was found guilty on Friday of two federal charges: destruction of property by means of fire, and arson affecting property used in interstate commerce. Each count carries up to 20 years in prison. He is due to be sentenced on March 17, 2027, when he faces a mandatory minimum of five years and a maximum of 40 years in federal prison.

The verdict came at a retrial, five months after a first jury deadlocked 10-2 in favour of acquittal and a mistrial was declared. The second trial, before U.S. District Judge Anne Hwang, lasted 14 days, with jurors deliberating for nearly three days.

Prosecutors said Rinderknecht deliberately lit a small brush fire shortly after midnight on January 1, 2025, in a clearing on Hidden Buddha Hill near the Skull Rock Trailhead above Pacific Palisades. The fire, known as the Lachman Fire, was quickly suppressed by firefighters — but it kept burning underground, smouldering in the dense root systems below the surface.

Six days later, on January 7, fierce winds drove the hidden fire back above ground. It spread rapidly through Pacific Palisades and Malibu, killing 12 people and destroying 6,837 structures, with damage estimated at about $25 billion. Prosecutors called it the most destructive wildfire in Los Angeles history.

At trial, prosecutors relied on surveillance footage and location data from Rinderknecht's phone, which they said showed he was the only person near the ignition site when the fire began. They also told jurors that in a January 2025 interview with investigators, he lied about where he had been when he first saw the blaze; phone records placed him in a clearing about 30 feet from the fire as it grew.

Rinderknecht, a former Uber driver and onetime Pacific Palisades resident, has been in federal custody since his arrest in Florida in October 2025. He declined to testify in either trial, but said in a court declaration filed in March that he had not wilfully started any fire. His defence argued that holiday fireworks had triggered the New Year's Day blaze, and that the inferno a week later was a separate fire set by someone else. His lawyer also argued that prosecutors lacked enough physical evidence to prove the case, and suggested his client was being blamed for failures by the Los Angeles Fire Department.

The Palisades disaster struck on the same day as another catastrophic wind-driven fire in Eaton Canyon, about 30 miles to the east, which killed 19 people in Altadena. Investigators later concluded that fire was sparked by an electrical transmission tower. Together, the two blazes rank as the deadliest wildfire calamity in Los Angeles County history. Rinderknecht is the only person criminally charged in connection with either fire.

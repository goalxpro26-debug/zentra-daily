President Donald Trump said on Friday that American companies building data centres outside the United States will face steep penalties, in a warning aimed squarely at the tech firms driving the artificial intelligence boom.

"We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country," Trump said at a White House news conference, his first since returning from two days in China, where he held talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin alongside the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen. "We're going to fight very hard for the data centers."

He said companies that build abroad anyway would face a "tremendous tax". He gave no detail about how such a penalty would work — no rate and no timetable — and it was not immediately clear whether the threat would become a formal policy.

Trump's argument was about competition with China. If American firms are allowed to build overseas, he said, they will all build in China, and the United States would lose the industry in the same way he said it lost chip manufacturing when production moved abroad. "The next thing we know, they'll build them in space," he added. "That could happen, too."

He said the facilities should be built in the United States instead, naming Texas and California and adding that if firms would not build there, "they're going to go in West Virginia. We have a lot of places that want them badly, and they're great places."

The warning lands as several large American firms are expanding abroad. Alphabet announced earlier this month that it plans to invest $15 billion in data-centre infrastructure in Malaysia in 2027 and 2028, and CoreWeave has said it plans to expand across Australia, India, Japan, South Korea, Singapore and the United Arab Emirates. Oracle has also been building capacity outside the United States. Data centres supply the computing power the AI buildout runs on, and developers often place them close to users to keep delays and transmission costs down.

At home, the facilities have become a political flashpoint of their own. Data centres have faced bipartisan backlash in communities that blame them for noise and higher electricity prices, and a Gallup poll in August found that 7 in 10 Americans do not want one in their community. Industry groups have countered that the facilities do not raise costs. Trump told communities to take advantage of the boom rather than shut it down: "Make them pay you. Make them pay your community."

Trump also praised Anthropic's stock-market debut and said he and Xi had agreed to work on joint guardrails for AI.

For now, the threat is words, not policy. Whether it becomes a tax, a restriction or simply pressure to keep the next big builds at home — and whether the administration can legally penalise a company for where it builds — is what firms and investors will be watching.

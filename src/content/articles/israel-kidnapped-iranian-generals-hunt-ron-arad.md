For nearly forty years, Israel searched for Ron Arad, an air force navigator who vanished over Lebanon in 1986. A New York Times investigation published on Sunday reports that the search included the abduction of two senior Iranian generals — and was built on a premise Israeli officials now say was mistaken: that Iran was holding him.

Arad bailed out of his plane during an operation in southern Lebanon and was captured by the Shiite Amal movement. Signs of life reached his family in the first two years, the last a letter dated May 5, 1988. Israel declared him dead in 2008, but the search never stopped.

The investigation, based on interviews with dozens of Iranian, Israeli and American officials, says Israeli intelligence became convinced Iran had taken Arad from Lebanon. In 1994, commandos abducted Mustafa Dirani, a senior Amal member who had held Arad. Dirani told interrogators he had taken Arad to the village of Nabi Chit, where guards left him alone to fight nearby Israeli troops — and when they returned, he was gone. He also claimed an Iranian official had told him the Revolutionary Guards had taken Arad to Iran. Some in Israel treated that as proof; others dismissed it as uncorroborated and extracted under duress.

## Two generals, two abductions

In 2006, Mossad agents kidnapped General Alireza Asgari, a senior Quds Force officer who had served in Lebanon shortly after Arad disappeared, from Istanbul. Three former American officials told the Times that agents questioned him for weeks aboard a boat, then killed him and dumped his body in the Black Sea after finding no evidence he knew anything about Arad. Israel has denied any involvement.

Fifteen years later, in September 2021, agents abducted General Abdullah Saberi in Damascus while he was out on a morning run. Saberi had been tasked by Iran with overseeing the search for evidence of Arad's whereabouts. He was taken to Israel and interrogated, then released in Johannesburg, South Africa, according to one account. Then-Mossad chief David Barnea later acknowledged authorising a daring but unsuccessful operation.

## The lead that did not hold

A 2006 review by Israel's Shin Bet concluded that neither Iran nor Hezbollah had held Arad, but the government and the Mossad largely set its findings aside. A joint investigation by Iran and Hezbollah concluded that Arad had died in Nabi Chit in 1988 and was buried there. A 2016 report by the Mossad and Israeli military intelligence said he had likely been dead since 1988.

Israeli officials told the Times the prevailing assessment now is that Iran never held Arad — and that the assumption was likely mistaken from the outset. The leading theory is that he was killed in 1988 in revenge for the death of a fighter involved in his capture.

In March this year, Israeli commandos raided Nabi Chit looking for his remains, with airstrikes that local officials said killed at least 41 people. The raid found nothing. Arad's widow later said the family did not want soldiers' lives put at risk to recover his remains.

Two people were killed and at least 17 others were injured on Wednesday afternoon when a three-story building in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood partially collapsed into an open excavation pit at the construction site next door, fire officials said.

The collapse happened at about 4 p.m. near the intersection of Flatbush Avenue and Bedford Avenue. The building, at 1321 Flatbush Avenue, had a business on the ground floor and apartments above. It fell into the excavation at 2625 Bedford Avenue, the lot beside it, where construction work was underway.

The first 911 call came in at 4:01 p.m., and firefighters arrived within a minute, the Fire Department of New York said. Two people were pulled from the rubble soon after the collapse, and crews kept searching through the night with drones, search dogs and their hands, looking for anyone still trapped.

At a news conference on Wednesday night, the fire department said two people had died and at least 17 people were hurt, four of them in critical condition. Seven children and a firefighter were among the injured, officials said. The others suffered less serious injuries. The identities of the two people who died had not been released.

Officials warned the toll could still rise. The fire department said it did not know how many people were inside the building when it fell, or whether everyone had been accounted for.

## What caused it is not yet known

No cause had been determined as of Wednesday night, and an investigation is underway. The city's Department of Buildings said inspectors were at the scene to help with the recovery and to check the structural stability of surrounding buildings. Law enforcement is leading the investigation into the cause, NBC New York reported.

Attention is likely to fall on the excavation next door. City records show work at the site began in September and included blasting rock, NBC New York reported, and investigators have not yet established whether the excavation or the work supporting it played any role in the collapse. The news outlet said it had left messages with the architect, the property owner and the general contractor listed in city records.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said in a post on social media that people should avoid the area so rescue crews could keep working. The city's Office of Emergency Management also advised drivers to expect road closures and delays around the site. ABC7 reported that a reception center for displaced families was opened at the Flatbush YMCA.

For the neighborhood, the collapse is a sudden rupture in an ordinary street: a corner building that held homes and a shop, gone into a pit in an afternoon, with the search for survivors stretching into the night and the question of why it happened still unanswered.

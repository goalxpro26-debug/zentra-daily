Russian ballistic missiles damaged three bridges across the Dnipro River in Kyiv overnight, striking crossings that link the two halves of the capital and disrupting traffic across the city.

The Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and South bridges were all hit in the early hours of Friday, November 20, as a series of explosions sounded across Kyiv and Ukrainian air defences responded. The strikes damaged the bridge decks and sparked fires in at least three districts.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said traffic from the right bank to the left bank was suspended on the Podilskyi Bridge while engineers assessed the damage, and the Darnytskyi Bridge was also under assessment. The South Bridge stayed partly open, carrying vehicles only from the left bank to the right bank, with weight limits of 10 tons for ordinary vehicles and 18 tons for public transport. Klitschko urged residents to avoid unnecessary car travel and called on businesses to allow remote work where possible.

Falling missile debris set fire to a three-story building in the Obolonskyi district, in the north of the city. Another building caught fire in a nonresidential area of Podilskyi, and in Holosiivskyi a strike hit the grounds of an infrastructure facility, with preliminary reports that an office building there had caught fire. No casualties from the overnight attack were reported in the Kyiv Post's account.

Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces had struck road and rail bridges in Kyiv used to transport weapons and military equipment to the battlefield, Reuters reported. The ministry said the strikes were part of a wider round of attacks on energy and transport infrastructure across Ukraine, including port facilities in the Odesa region and sea vessels used for military purposes.

The three crossings carry the daily flow of commuters and freight between Kyiv's banks, and the damage leaves the city with one bridge closed, one under assessment and the third running in a single direction under weight limits. The full extent of the structural damage, and how long repairs will take, was not yet clear as authorities continued their assessments on Friday morning.

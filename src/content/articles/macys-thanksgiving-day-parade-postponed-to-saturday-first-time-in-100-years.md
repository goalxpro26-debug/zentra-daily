The 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will not march on Thanksgiving Day. Organisers announced on Wednesday that the parade is postponed until Saturday, November 28, because of a major storm forecast for New York City — the first time in the event's history that it has been moved to another day.

The parade will now begin at 8:30 a.m. ET on Saturday. It is the first postponement since the parade began in 1924. The only other times it has missed Thanksgiving Day were 1942 to 1944, when it was suspended during World War II — but it has never before been rescheduled because of weather.

"The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration," Macy's said in a statement.

The decision was driven by the giant balloons, the parade's signature feature. A strong coastal storm, or nor'easter, is expected to track up the East Coast through Thanksgiving Day, bringing heavy rain, strong winds and significant coastal flooding. The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for New York City for Thursday into Friday, with gusts forecast to reach 50 to 60 mph.

City rules require the balloons to be grounded if sustained winds exceed 23 mph and gusts exceed 34 mph. That threshold was set after high winds sent a balloon crashing into a lamppost during the 1997 parade, seriously injuring a spectator. Weather has grounded all of the parade's balloons only once, in 1971.

This year's parade was set to be the biggest yet, with a record 100 balloons, performances from nine Broadway shows and appearances by musicians including Raye, Megan Moroney, Teddy Swims, Natasha Bedingfield and Keke Palmer. By Saturday the storm is expected to have cleared, with milder weather forecast for the rescheduled march.

NBC, which broadcasts the parade, will air a rerun of the 2025 parade on Thursday morning instead. The 2026 parade will be televised live on Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET on NBC, Peacock and Telemundo.

For many Americans, watching the parade is as much a part of Thanksgiving as the meal itself, and millions line the Manhattan route each year. This time, the wait will be two days longer — and the 100th parade will make its debut on a Saturday instead.

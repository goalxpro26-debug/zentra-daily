A fire aboard a Russian cargo ship struck by drones in the Baltic Sea burned through the night, Finland's coast guard said on Sunday, as firefighting vessels worked to douse the flames and cool the containers stacked on its deck.

The vessel, the Sparta II, was hit on Saturday morning in international waters west of Gogland island in the Gulf of Finland. It later drifted into Finnish waters, where it remains at anchor. All 16 crew members were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship.

Finland's military said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine, though it did not say why the vessel had been targeted. Ukraine has sent waves of military drones towards Russia's north-western Leningrad region this year, including some that strayed into the airspace of nearby Baltic countries — raising fears that the war is spilling onto NATO territory.

The Sparta II is a 121-metre Russian-flagged cargo vessel owned by Oboronlogistika, a shipping company that provides logistics services for the Russian Defence Ministry, according to the company's website. The vessel is subject to US sanctions. It left St Petersburg on Friday, bound for the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad.

What the ship was carrying remains unknown. The commander of the Finnish Border Guard, Ilja Iljin, said it could not be ruled out that military materiel was on board. The vessel had been damaged in its bridge and cargo area, and at least two drones struck it, according to Finnish officials. The governor of Russia's Leningrad region said the attack took place about three nautical miles from Russia's border.

Hours after the strike, Finland's defence minister said a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace — an incident he suggested may have been linked to the rescue or monitoring of the damaged ship.

Finland's prime minister, Petteri Orpo, said the episode showed the war in Ukraine was spilling over. The risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland, a narrow arm of the Baltic Sea between Finland and Estonia, cannot be ruled out, Estonia's navy commander told public broadcaster ERR on Sunday.

Finland's military said last week it had also detected a strike on a Russian warship in the same area during Ukrainian drone attacks. Finnish authorities are leading the rescue operation while the ship sits in their waters, although Russian vessels may eventually tow it home. Officials said the incident posed no known danger to surrounding shipping, and no oil had been reported entering the sea.

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket carrying 40 Amazon internet satellites failed about 10 minutes after liftoff from Florida on Sunday, destroying its payload in the first outright failure of a ULA rocket since the company was formed in 2006.

The 214-foot Vulcan lifted off from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 12:40 p.m. EST. Its six strap-on solid rocket boosters appeared to work normally, burning out and falling away about a minute and 40 seconds into flight. But the first stage's methane-burning BE-4 engines appeared to fire longer than planned, and something went wrong during the burn of the hydrogen-fueled Centaur upper stage.

About 50 minutes after liftoff, ULA confirmed the loss. "Based on data received by our Launch Director, we confirm that the Vulcan vehicle launched from Space Launch Complex 41 suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit," the company said in a statement. It said it would work with Amazon and other organizations to find the cause, determine the corrective action and return to flight as quickly as possible.

Sunday's flight was only the fifth for Vulcan, ULA's replacement for its Atlas 5 fleet, and its first in nine months. ULA had spent that time fixing problems with the nozzles on the strap-on boosters that marred two earlier flights, in 2024 and this past February. The boosters appeared to work normally on Sunday. Instead, the trouble came later: the first stage separated from the Centaur later than expected, and telemetry showed the upper stage making unusual orientation adjustments early in its burn.

The launch had already been difficult: Friday's attempt was scrubbed after problems knocked out a plant supplying gaseous nitrogen to the pad, and Saturday's was called off over network problems between the rocket and ground systems. On Sunday, controllers held the countdown at T-minus 20 seconds over an unexplained technical issue before clearing the rocket to fly. It is not yet known whether that issue played any role in the failure.

## A setback for Amazon's satellite internet plans

The mission, known as LV-01, was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has purchased to build out Leo, its satellite internet constellation and rival to SpaceX's Starlink. The 40 satellites were the largest batch Amazon has flown on one rocket, and their loss leaves the constellation waiting on ULA's investigation before it can try again on Vulcan. Amazon has also booked Leo launches on Blue Origin's New Glenn, Europe's Ariane 6 and SpaceX's Falcon 9; its next Leo launch is an Ariane 6 mission with 36 satellites, targeted for November 23 at the earliest.

More than 3,300 Leo satellites will be needed to complete the constellation. Sunday's flight would have pushed the total launched to date to 436.

What caused the failure is not yet publicly known. ULA said it is committed to full transparency with its customers during the investigation, and NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman offered the space agency's help with the investigation and remediation.

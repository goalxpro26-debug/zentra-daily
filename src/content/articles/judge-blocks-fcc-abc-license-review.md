A federal judge on Wednesday barred the Federal Communications Commission from continuing its early review of ABC's broadcast licenses, ruling that the agency's action appeared to be retaliation for programming critical of President Donald Trump.

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan in Washington, D.C., granted Disney and ABC a preliminary injunction halting the review of the network's eight company-owned television stations. "This is not a close case," she wrote, adding that ABC had a weighty interest in vindicating its right "to be free of retaliation for (their) protected speech."

Disney and ABC sued the FCC in August after the commission ordered all eight stations to apply for license renewals years ahead of schedule. The stations' licenses were not otherwise due for renewal until between 2028 and 2031.

The timing was central to the judge's reasoning. The FCC launched the early review on April 28, one day after Trump criticised remarks by late-night host Jimmy Kimmel and called for his firing. The commission has maintained that the review was prompted by Disney's diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives and its response to an investigation into the company's employment practices. AliKhan wrote that she did not find that explanation credible, and said FCC Chair Brendan Carr "has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders."

The judge found that ABC was likely to succeed on its First Amendment claim and that the retaliatory proceedings themselves caused irreparable harm, even before any license was revoked. She also denied the FCC's motion to dismiss Disney's lawsuit.

## A win with limits

The ruling does not end the dispute, and it does not stop the FCC from continuing its investigations into Disney's employment practices or its scrutiny of the daytime show "The View." AliKhan also declined ABC's request for a broader injunction barring the FCC from threatening the company over its editorial decisions.

"No one doubts that the Commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit," she wrote. "But in doing so, Defendants may not retaliate against or punish Plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech."

The case has been closely watched as a test of whether a regulator can use broadcast licensing — a power that can, in theory, take a station off the air — as leverage over a network's editorial choices. Press advocates called the decision an important win not only for ABC but for all broadcasters.

## What happens next

The FCC has until December 2 to report back to the court on its compliance with the injunction. Both sides are due to submit a joint proposal for the next steps in the case by December 9. As a condition of the injunction, ABC and Disney were ordered to post a bond of $1.

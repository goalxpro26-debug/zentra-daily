Millions of people were cut off from their email and workplace tools on Thursday after a major Microsoft 365 outage knocked out Outlook, Teams and several other services for much of the day.

The disruption began on Thursday morning in the United States and spread worldwide, hitting Exchange Online — the cloud service behind business Outlook accounts — along with Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, Office for the web, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR. Microsoft first described the problem as affecting the Americas before widening it to "any region."

Outage tracker Downdetector recorded just under 130,000 reports for Outlook around 1:09 p.m. Eastern Time, the day's peak. Reports for Microsoft 365 itself topped 46,000, while Teams peaked at about 4,600 reports and Copilot at nearly 6,000. By late afternoon the numbers were falling, with roughly 54,000 Outlook reports still outstanding at 4:20 p.m.

## Servers "unexpectedly unavailable"

Microsoft said the failure was not caused by a surge in demand. Instead, it said "a large number of servers" had become "unexpectedly unavailable," pushing extra load onto the systems that remained and triggering automated health-check failures.

"We've determined that the elevated load isn't driven by an overall increase in traffic, but by a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable, which is shifting load onto the remaining infrastructure," the company said in its service-health updates.

The company logged the incident under reference EX1550543 and labelled it a "service degradation" for Outlook. It said it was redistributing traffic across its infrastructure, moving users off the affected systems and restarting the failed servers. By around 4 p.m., Microsoft said some servers had been "successfully returned to service," though it had not declared the incident over at the time of reporting.

## A day of disruption

The outage reached well beyond office workers. OpenAI said it was investigating broken Microsoft connectors, and Anthropic said an issue with an upstream provider was affecting its own Microsoft 365 connector. The State of Michigan said its email and other Microsoft applications were affected by the widescale outage.

Microsoft also said it had paused deployment and configuration changes while it reviewed the incident's telemetry.

There is no evidence the outage was caused by a cyberattack. Nothing Microsoft has published points to one, and the company has described the cause in infrastructure terms — servers failing, not an external intrusion. A full root cause will only come with Microsoft's post-incident review, which it has not yet published.

## What is still unknown

Microsoft has not said what made so many servers unavailable at once, nor given a firm time for full recovery. Separate from the main outage, the company said earlier on Thursday that it had restored a shorter Teams disruption in North America, although some users continued to report connectivity problems.

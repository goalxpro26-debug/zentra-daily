A powerful coastal storm is set to strike the East Coast on Wednesday and Thanksgiving Day, bringing heavy rain, damaging winds and coastal flooding as millions travel for the holiday — and forecasters say it could rapidly strengthen into a "bomb cyclone."

NOAA's Weather Prediction Center said it has high confidence in an impactful holiday storm, likely a nor'easter and perhaps a bomb cyclone, hitting the East Coast on Wednesday, November 25, and Thanksgiving, November 26. A bomb cyclone forms when a storm's central pressure falls by at least 24 millibars in 24 hours. The system is expected to develop off the Southeast coast and track north along the seaboard.

## Heavy rain on Thanksgiving Day

The heaviest rain is forecast for Thanksgiving Day along the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast coasts: 1 to 4 inches in the Carolinas and the Mid-Atlantic, and 1 to 2 inches across the Northeast. Local flash flooding is still possible, especially in flood-prone urban areas.

## Winds, outages and coastal flooding

Powerful winds will accompany the rain through at least Friday night, when the storm moves into the Canadian Maritimes. Gusts of more than 50 mph are forecast along the coast and above 40 mph inland into the Interstate 95 corridor, raising the risk of power outages and tree damage.

Strong onshore winds, high surf and high tides could also bring significant coastal flooding and beach erosion from Georgia through the Northeast. Forecasters warn of moderate to major flooding around Charleston, South Carolina, and Tybee Island, Georgia, on Wednesday, and at least moderate flooding along the Virginia Tidewater and Delaware coast at the Thanksgiving high tide.

## Macy's parade balloons at risk

The storm also threatens the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City, starting at 8:30 a.m. on Thursday with a record 100 inflatables. Under city safety rules, the giant balloons must stay grounded if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts top 34 mph. Winds are expected to strengthen through the morning, and rain could arrive around parade time, though the exact timing remains uncertain. No cancellation has been announced.

## A second storm on the West Coast

On the West Coast, record-high king tides are expected to coincide with an incoming Pacific storm, and the National Weather Service had issued coastal flood warnings from San Francisco to the southern coast of Washington as of Monday morning. That storm is expected to bring rain, high-elevation snow and dense fog to northern and central California.

More than 81 million Americans are expected to travel for Thanksgiving, and forecasters warn of expanding airline delays, first in Orlando and Miami from Tuesday into Wednesday, then in Atlanta and Charlotte on Wednesday. The National Weather Service urged travellers to monitor its forecasts and follow local guidance.

Snow is not expected to be a major issue, as temperatures are too warm for much to accumulate, though snow showers are possible on the back side of the system on Friday into Saturday, with only minor impacts likely.

Democrat Mary Peltola defeated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska, the Associated Press called on Tuesday night, resolving the last uncalled Senate race of the 2026 midterm elections and completing a Democratic sweep of nine Republican-held seats — the party's largest midterm gain since 1958.

With nearly all ballots counted, Peltola held about 50 percent of the vote to roughly 46 percent for Sullivan, two weeks after Election Day on November 3. The result gives Democrats a 56-44 majority in the Senate when the new Congress convenes in January, reversing the 53-47 Republican edge the party entered the midterms with.

Peltola, who is Yup'ik, will be the first Alaska Native ever elected to the United States Senate and only the fifth Democrat to represent Alaska in the chamber since statehood. The most recent was Mark Begich, whom Sullivan defeated in 2014. Peltola previously held Alaska's lone House seat, becoming the first Alaska Native to serve in Congress, before losing her re-election bid in 2024.

The campaign was one of the most expensive and contentious of the cycle, with more than $140 million spent, and it was upended in its final days by an allegation that members of Peltola's campaign helped recruit a second candidate named Dan Sullivan to siphon votes from the incumbent. The New York Times first reported the claim less than a week before the election. Peltola denied any involvement, and the retired teacher at the centre of it, Dan J. Sullivan Jr., finished with about 1.7 percent of the vote and has denied running at the Democrats' behest.

President Donald Trump called for Peltola's prosecution after the report, and the Justice Department filed a lawsuit seeking to block certification if Sullivan lost, pending an investigation into possible voter confusion. A federal judge dismissed the case, though the department appealed, and it has confirmed a criminal investigation is under way. Trump and Vice President JD Vance each travelled to Alaska in the month before the election to campaign for Sullivan.

## An "Alaska first" campaign

Peltola ran as a centrist under the slogan "fish, family and freedom," leaning into Alaskans' frustrations with high fuel prices and the cost of food and supplies that must be barged or flown into remote communities. She sought to distance herself from national Democrats, while Sullivan and his supporters tried to tie her to "Lower 48 liberals" they said wanted to restrict oil, gas and mining development.

The result is striking in a state Trump carried by wide margins, including about 13 percentage points in 2024. Alaska also elected a Democrat as governor for the first time since 1998. The Associated Press has not yet called the state's House race, where independent Bill Hill is challenging Republican Representative Nick Begich. Legal challenges to the Senate result remain possible, but with the count effectively complete, the contest that drew national attention for a ballot quirk and a federal lawsuit has delivered Democrats their ninth pickup — and a majority larger than any they have held since 2008.

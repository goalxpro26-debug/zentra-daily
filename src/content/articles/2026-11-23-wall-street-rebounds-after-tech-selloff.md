US stocks looked set to open sharply higher on Monday, November 23, as futures pointed to a rebound after one of the worst weeks for technology shares in years.

Futures tied to the S&P 500 rose about 2.3 percent ahead of the open, while the Nasdaq Composite jumped 3 percent in premarket trading, according to the New York Times. Dow futures climbed roughly 1.9 percent. Nvidia, the chip maker at the centre of the artificial intelligence boom, rose about 7 percent before the bell, and Alphabet, Intel and Micron were each 4 to 5 percent higher.

The bounce followed a punishing week. The S&P 500 fell 8.6 percent over the week, its worst weekly performance since April 2025, when President Trump announced sweeping global tariffs. Friday was the sharpest day of the rout: the Nasdaq dropped 5.3 percent, erasing its gains for 2026, while the S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow have now fallen more than 10 percent from their record closing highs, putting them in correction territory.

The sell-off was driven by growing doubt about the AI trade. Investors questioned whether the trillions of dollars pouring into artificial intelligence infrastructure would ever pay off, and those worries spilled into a broader market rout. Pressure built on Nvidia through the week amid leadership turmoil and reports of a Securities and Exchange Commission investigation into its accounting, and its shares fell about 14 percent on Friday alone. Anthropic, the AI company that began public trading last week after a record debut, also pulled back, while voters in several US communities rejected new data-centre projects.

Analysts cautioned that Monday's move looked largely mechanical, a bout of bargain hunting after an oversized fall, rather than a sign that doubts about AI spending had gone away. Asian technology stocks fell on Monday as traders there caught up with Friday's heavy selling in the United States, and authorities in China, Taiwan and South Korea rolled out measures to support their markets.

The week ahead is short. US markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving and shut early on Friday. Bitcoin, which slid to about $55,500 over the weekend, climbed back above $61,000 on Monday as stocks steadied.

What is still unclear is whether the rebound holds once regular trading resumes, and whether the debate over AI spending deepens or fades in the weeks ahead.

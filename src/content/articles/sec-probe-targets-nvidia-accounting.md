The US Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting practices, according to two people briefed on the matter, adding a new front to the legal troubles facing the chipmaker at the centre of the artificial intelligence boom.

The Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating Nvidia, without establishing the focus. The two sources told Reuters that enforcement staff are scrutinising potential accounting issues at the company. Neither the SEC nor Nvidia would comment. It is not known when the inquiry began or which accounting issues are under review.

The report landed on Friday, and Nvidia's shares fell about 14 percent to $125.53, extending a slide that has wiped more than a third off the company's value.

The accounting inquiry is only the latest of Nvidia's problems. On Thursday, California Attorney General Rob Bonta served the company with an investigative subpoena. His office is examining whether Nvidia or its former chief executive, Jensen Huang, allowed advanced chips or confidential information to reach China — and whether the company misled investors and the public about that risk.

"My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law," Bonta said.

The investigations follow Huang's sudden resignation on November 11, days after he admitted to extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a businesswoman US authorities suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. China has denied she is a spy. Alice Huang formerly led Megaspeed, a Singapore-based firm that was once a large Nvidia chip customer. US authorities have investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over alleged shipments of Nvidia AI chips to China in breach of export controls, the Justice and Commerce departments said this week.

One area drawing renewed attention is how Nvidia reports revenue by region. Last year it switched to customer headquarters rather than billing addresses, removing Singapore from its geographical breakdown — even as sales booked through Singapore soared to almost half its global revenue, the Financial Times reported this week. It is not known whether that change is part of the SEC inquiry.

The Justice Department is also reviewing Nvidia's proposed $12.9 billion purchase of open-source platform Hugging Face as part of a broader antitrust investigation.

Nvidia's board has launched its own independent investigation and set up a committee on national security and export-control compliance. Colette Kress, the longtime chief financial officer, is interim chief executive, and Huang is due to testify before Congress on December 1.

Investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing, and many close without enforcement action. But for the company whose chips power most of the world's AI data centres, the pile-up — an SEC accounting probe, a state subpoena, a federal export-control inquiry and a shareholder lawsuit — is now a test of how far investors still trust the AI boom's most important supplier.

Net migration to the UK fell to 77,000 in the year ending June 2026, according to provisional figures published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS) on Thursday — the lowest level since 1997, outside the coronavirus pandemic.

The figure is less than half the 204,000 recorded in the previous 12 months. An estimated 738,000 people arrived to live in the UK long term, down 17% from 893,000 a year earlier, while 661,000 left, broadly unchanged from 689,000. Net migration has now fallen by about 92% from its peak of 944,000 in the year to March 2023.

The fall was driven almost entirely by fewer arrivals from outside the EU. Net migration among non-EU nationals fell to 258,000, from 379,000 a year earlier, as work-related arrivals fell by 45,000, study-related arrivals by 33,000 and asylum-related arrivals by 19,000. Study remained the most common reason for coming to the UK, accounting for 257,000 arrivals, followed by work at 135,000 and asylum at 73,000.

More British and EU nationals left the UK than arrived. Net migration for British nationals stood at minus 137,000, with an estimated 245,000 British nationals emigrating during the year. Net migration for EU nationals was minus 45,000, and has been negative since the year to June 2022.

The figures reflect successive tightenings of immigration rules for workers, students and asylum seekers. The ONS said net migration had fallen to "levels not typically seen since the 1990s", as arrivals from outside the EU for work and study continued to decline.

The numbers are provisional and will be revised as fuller data arrives. The ONS warned that estimates for British nationals carry greater uncertainty than usual, because the latest registration data was unavailable for this release and the figures rest on a 15-month projection. Updated estimates are due in May 2027.

For now, the picture is a sharp reversal: after the post-pandemic surge that pushed net migration close to a million, arrivals and departures have nearly converged, leaving a net flow close to the levels of the late 1990s.

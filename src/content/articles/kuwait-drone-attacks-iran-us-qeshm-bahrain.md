Kuwait's military said on Tuesday night that its air defences were intercepting hostile drone attacks, as the United States and Iran traded strikes across the Gulf in a widening exchange.

The Kuwaiti armed forces said in a statement posted on X that any explosion sounds heard across the country were the result of air defence systems intercepting hostile attacks, and urged the public to follow safety instructions. It did not say where the drones came from, or whether there were casualties or damage.

Earlier on Tuesday, the United States carried out several attacks on Iran's southern island of Qeshm, in the Strait of Hormuz, hours after Iran said it had launched a drone attack on a US radar system in Bahrain. A US official told Al Jazeera the strike hit a missile launch site on Qeshm, in response to Iran's earlier attack on a US base in Bahrain.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had carried out a "precision operation" with drones on the US-operated radar at the Sheikh Isa air base, describing it as a response to what it called repeated violations of Iranian airspace by US military drones.

US Central Command said Iranian drones aimed at a US radar system at an air base in Bahrain "were intercepted without any damage to US equipment or personnel". Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, said its air defences intercepted the attack, and blasts were heard over the capital, Manama, according to the AFP news agency.

It was the third Iranian drone attack on Bahrain in five days, after a lull of several weeks. Friday's strike was the first Iranian attack on a Gulf country since early September, AFP reported.

Iranian media reported that the US strikes on Qeshm caused no damage to infrastructure. Ahmad Nafisi, deputy governor of Iran's southern Hormozgan province, told the official IRNA news agency that the situation was under control and relief agencies were on full alert. IRNA reported five to six explosions on the island on Tuesday evening.

The exchange comes as shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil, faces growing disruption. Iran's armed forces said three oil tankers struck naval mines while trying to cross the strait on Tuesday, while the US military said it had cleared the main routes of mines. The war began on February 28, and the US has responded to Iranian pressure on shipping with a naval blockade of Iranian ports and tanker escorts.

Diplomacy continued alongside the fighting. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, and his Omani counterpart, Badr Albusaidi, discussed safeguarding shipping and diplomatic options in Muscat. A third US aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, arrived in the Middle East on Monday, and US President Donald Trump is due to hold a three-way summit on Thursday in China with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, where the Iran war is on the agenda.

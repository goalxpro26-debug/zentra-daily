Haiti has announced an agreement with El Salvador to build three new prisons, deepening a security partnership that has already put Salvadoran troops on the ground against the gangs controlling much of the capital.

The announcement came on Friday from Haiti's Minister of Justice and Public Security, Patrick Pélissier, after an official mission to El Salvador. The first prison will be launched soon, with the prime minister ordering it to be expedited, and will be one of three built by a Salvadoran company working with a Haitian firm, the minister said.

Haiti's need for prison space is acute. Gangs control an estimated 70% of the capital, Port-au-Prince, and swaths of the country's central region. In 2024, gang raids on the country's two biggest prisons released thousands of inmates, and the system has struggled ever since to hold those arrested. Haiti's prisons have long been notorious for severe overcrowding, with human rights experts warning that prisoners sleep standing and that hundreds have died in recent years. A 2023 visit by The Associated Press to one of the prisons found inmates thirsty and starving.

Pélissier praised the Salvadoran maximum-security prison CECOT, which he toured during his visit to El Salvador for an international conference. El Salvador has been under a state of exception since 2022 that has imprisoned more than 90,000 people with no access to family visits. The United States has also deported Venezuelans and Salvadorans to CECOT, and some have denounced torture there after their release.

"Once you enter, there is no hope of ever seeing the outside world again," Pélissier said of the facility.

The minister did not say how much the prison projects would cost, whether El Salvador would help finance them, or whether it would play any role in managing them.

The announcement came on the same day El Salvador's armed forces said two of its soldiers were killed and two others injured in a detonation while handling explosives at a camp in Haiti. The soldiers were among the 2,500 Salvadoran troops President Nayib Bukele has deployed in a military operation to rescue four technicians who disappeared on October 18 after coming under fire from gangs in the Croix-des-Bouquets area northeast of Port-au-Prince.

The deployment runs alongside a United Nations-backed gang suppression force, authorised by the Security Council in October 2025, that is meant to have 5,500 security personnel working to dismantle armed gangs, protect infrastructure and facilitate humanitarian access in collaboration with the Haitian National Police. The force replaced the Kenya-led multinational mission and has not yet deployed its full strength. Bukele's additional troops are not part of the UN-backed force, which unlike its predecessor has the power to arrest suspected criminals.

Haiti has not had an elected government since President Jovenel Moïse was assassinated in 2021. Elections are set to take place next year.

CrowdStrike said on Monday it will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal valuing the company at about $43.9 billion, one of the largest acquisitions in the history of the cybersecurity industry.

Under the terms, which both companies' boards unanimously approved, Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike for each Zscaler share they own. Based on CrowdStrike's closing price on Friday, November 20, that implies a value of about $260.41 per Zscaler share, a premium of roughly 34 percent. CrowdStrike's existing shareholders would own about 84.1 percent of the combined company, with Zscaler holders owning the rest.

The deal pairs CrowdStrike's Falcon platform, built around protecting computers and servers, with Zscaler's zero-trust network security, which controls who and what can reach a company's systems. The combination would create one of the world's largest pure-play cybersecurity providers, with more than $10 billion in combined annual recurring revenue. The companies said they are targeting $1 billion of annualised revenue synergies.

Investors reacted quickly. Zscaler shares jumped more than 13.4 percent in premarket trading on Monday, while CrowdStrike fell about 5 percent, a familiar pattern when a buyer pays in stock.

The timing reflects two pressures reshaping the industry. High-profile hacks and fast-evolving artificial intelligence threats have made digital defence a boardroom priority, and large enterprise customers are trying to cut the number of security vendors they use, favouring providers that can deliver several products through a single relationship. CrowdStrike estimates the deal will expand its addressable market by more than $185 billion in calendar 2029.

The announcement came just three days after Bloomberg and Reuters reported that CrowdStrike was in early-stage talks to buy Zscaler, with no agreement then signed. CrowdStrike unveiled the deal alongside its fiscal third-quarter results, which it said set company records for new business and cash flow, and held a joint call with Zscaler on Monday morning to discuss both.

The transaction is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, subject to approval by Zscaler shareholders and regulators. The companies said they will continue to operate independently until then. Whether regulators scrutinise the combination closely remains to be seen, given its size and the overlap between two of the sector's largest vendors.

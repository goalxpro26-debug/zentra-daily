President Donald Trump said on Thursday that he has posthumously pardoned Pete Rose, baseball's all-time hits leader, more than two years after the player's death and a year after Major League Baseball restored his eligibility for the Hall of Fame.

Trump announced the pardon in a social media post written aboard Air Force One as he flew home from a trip to Asia. "I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND," he wrote, citing Rose's record of 4,256 hits — a career total collected over 24 seasons, not in a single season as the post suggested.

Rose, who died on September 30, 2024, at the age of 83, was one of the most accomplished and most controversial figures in the sport. A three-time World Series champion with a .303 career batting average, he was nicknamed "Charlie Hustle" for the way he played. But he was banned from baseball in August 1989 after an investigation led by John M. Dowd concluded that he had bet repeatedly on the Cincinnati Reds while playing for and managing his hometown team from 1985 to 1987.

The pardon covers only the federal case that followed. On April 20, 1990, Rose pleaded guilty to two counts of filing false federal income tax returns, admitting he had failed to report $354,968 over a four-year period. He was sentenced that July to five months in prison, served his time at a federal camp in Marion, Illinois, between August 1990 and January 1991, paid a $50,000 fine, completed 1,000 hours of community service and repaid the Internal Revenue Service $366,042.

Trump had been promising the pardon for more than a year. In March 2025 he said he would sign one "over the next few weeks," and he repeated the pledge on November 9 — yet no pardon for Rose appeared on the Justice Department's list of clemency grants, last updated on October 20, 2026.

The pardon does not, by itself, change Rose's standing with baseball. That question was settled separately in May 2025, when Commissioner Rob Manfred removed Rose from the permanently ineligible list, ruling that a lifetime ban ends with a person's death. Manfred later acknowledged that Trump's urging, in an April meeting at the White House, had influenced the decision. The next opportunity for Rose to be voted into the Hall of Fame comes in December 2027, with the earliest possible induction in 2028.

Trump used the announcement to renew his criticism of baseball's handling of Rose, writing that he had been "treated terribly" by the league. The pardon closes the legal chapter of a career defined as much by its ending as by its record. For the Hall of Fame, the wait continues.

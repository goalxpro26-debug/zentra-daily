Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, was damaged on Thursday by a suspected Houthi missile, in the second strike on the capital's financial district in less than two weeks.

The strike hit the Public Investment Fund Tower, an 80-storey building standing 385 metres high in the King Abdullah Financial District. Witnesses said the blast blew out windows across seven to eight floors and bent parts of the tower's metal facade. Saudi Arabia's civil defence agency confirmed the attack caused material damage but no casualties, and said damage to several floors of the tower and nearby buildings was being assessed.

The early-morning blast was heard across the capital, and journalists in Riyadh reported a powerful explosion before Saudi authorities issued an emergency warning that was lifted minutes later. Entrances to the financial district were closed and people were evacuated, while ambulances and fire trucks were sent to the scene.

The Saudi-led military coalition fighting the Houthis said its air defences had addressed a missile threat launched towards Riyadh. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said an initial assessment pointed to the impact of falling debris, with material damage still being surveyed. Three sources familiar with the matter told Reuters the building had been hit by an incoming Houthi missile. The Houthis issued no immediate statement on the strike, and the Public Investment Fund acknowledged receiving a request for comment.

The tower is the centrepiece of the King Abdullah Financial District, a showcase for Saudi Arabia's economic ambitions that also houses the Saudi stock exchange and offices of international firms. The fund it hosts manages assets worth more than $900 billion and finances major projects intended to reduce the kingdom's dependence on oil. Witnesses told AFP that most of the building's employees had been working remotely since early September.

The attack follows a strike on state oil giant Aramco's building in the same district on November 8, and comes amid a surge in Houthi attacks since fighting reignited over the summer in neighbouring Yemen's civil war, where Riyadh backs the internationally recognised government against the Iran-backed Houthis. Saudi Arabia has also launched retaliatory strikes in Yemen in recent days, with Houthi media reporting deadly strikes across territory the group controls.

It was not immediately clear whether the tower had been evacuated before the attack, and officials did not immediately confirm the full extent of the damage. The strike underlines how the renewed fighting in Yemen is reaching into the heart of Saudi Arabia's financial centre, eleven days after the previous landmark was hit.

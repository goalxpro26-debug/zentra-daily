China's space station has welcomed its first foreign visitor. Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistan Air Force pilot, boarded the Tiangong station on Wednesday after his Shenzhou-24 spacecraft launched from the Gobi Desert and docked about three and a half hours later — a first for Pakistan, and a first for China's crewed space programme.

The Long March 2F rocket lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre at 8:18 a.m. Beijing time, carrying Ali alongside Chinese commander Tang Hongbo and flight engineer Wu Yi. The spacecraft separated from its rocket roughly ten minutes after launch and entered orbit, and the China Manned Space Agency confirmed the docking later that morning. The three men entered the station on Wednesday afternoon, where the Shenzhou-23 crew already living aboard greeted them.

Ali is serving as a payload specialist on the mission. He is expected to spend around a week at the station — reports put the stay at five to ten days — running scientific experiments, including work in biotechnology and materials science, before returning to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft. He is the first Pakistani to travel into orbit, and the first foreign national to set foot on Tiangong.

The flight is the product of a space cooperation agreement signed by China and Pakistan in February 2025. Ali and his backup, Khurram Daud, have been training at the China Astronaut Research and Training Center in Beijing since April. The mission also coincides with the 75th anniversary of diplomatic relations between the two countries, and Pakistan's prime minister, Shehbaz Sharif, congratulated the nation as the launch went ahead, saying Pakistan had begun its journey "beyond the stars."

For China, the mission extends a programme that has moved quickly since Tiangong became fully operational in late 2022. Shenzhou-24 is the country's 18th crewed spaceflight, and for Tang Hongbo it is a record of sorts: he has now visited Tiangong three times, more than any other astronaut.

Tiangong — "Celestial Palace" — has until now been crewed exclusively by Chinese astronauts, in contrast to the International Space Station, which has hosted visitors from many nations. Beijing has said it wants to open its station to international partners, and Ali's flight is the first concrete step: a guest astronaut, on a short stay, doing joint science rather than simply visiting.

What comes next is still open. Pakistan's space agency, Suparco, says Pakistani science payloads will now begin work in the station's Wentian and Mengtian laboratories. Whether other countries follow — and whether short guest missions become a regular feature of Tiangong — will be watched closely by space agencies weighing how to work with China's station in the years ahead.

The bridge linking Crimea with Russia has been closed after reports of a Ukrainian strike, in an overnight exchange that also shut two of Kyiv's main crossings over the Dnipro River.

Crimea's Moscow-backed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, confirmed on Telegram that traffic across the bridge had been suspended. Images circulating online, shared by a former adviser to President Volodymyr Zelensky, purport to show a section of the span collapsed into the water, but the BBC said it had not independently verified them, and the full extent of the damage remained unclear on Tuesday morning. Russian state media reported that officials were due to meet during the day to discuss the situation.

The reported strike came a day after Ukraine began its latest attempt to disable the crossing. Two people were killed in a strike overnight on Sunday, at the start of that attempt, according to the Russian-installed governor.

Built by Russia after its annexation of Crimea in 2014 and opened by President Vladimir Putin in 2018, the bridge is both a supply route and a potent symbol of the occupation. It has been attacked repeatedly during the war and is protected by layers of air and sea defences.

## Kyiv crossings hit

In the Ukrainian capital, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the Northern and Southern bridges — two of the eight major spans carrying about three million residents across the Dnipro — were closed after Russian ballistic missile strikes.

Loud explosions were heard in Kyiv shortly before midnight. Klitschko said the strikes damaged critical infrastructure and disrupted heating and water supplies, and officials said some above-ground subway services were also affected after residents were told to take shelter.

Three people were injured in a separate Russian missile attack in the Cherkasy region, according to local officials.

## Diplomacy continues

The exchange came as diplomats pressed on with efforts to end the war. President Zelensky had warned of major attacks in the run-up to a meeting of the Chinese, US and Russian leaders in Shenzhen on Thursday.

After talks on Monday between US, Ukrainian and European officials, US envoy Jared Kushner said Washington was committed to strong, credible security guarantees for Ukraine and a path toward EU membership, adding that NATO would remain a critical pillar of European security. Zelensky described the meeting as "difficult" and said more was needed to defend against Russian ballistic missile strikes, which he said were being supplied by North Korea.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet confirmed how badly the Crimea bridge has been damaged, how long it will stay closed, or precisely what struck it. The images said to show the collapsed section remain unverified, and neither side has given a full account of the overnight exchange.

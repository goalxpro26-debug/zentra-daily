U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff will press Russia to give Europe a direct role in future negotiations over Ukraine when trilateral peace talks open in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday — the first such meeting between the three sides since February.

The two-day session, mediated by Kushner and Witkoff, is set to open with security guarantees for Ukraine and the future shape of Europe's security arrangements, before turning to territorial questions on the second day. The aim, U.S. officials said, is to keep momentum going rather than to deliver a final ceasefire this week.

## Washington wants Europe in the next round

The American envoys plan to argue that this week's meeting should be the last one held without Europe's full participation, and to propose bringing European governments into the discussion once the trilateral round is over. The case, as U.S. officials put it, is that Europeans should hear directly what Washington hears from Moscow, and be able to put their own messages to Russia in person rather than through intermediaries.

Senior European officials are due in Abu Dhabi this week — security advisers from the U.K., Germany and France, plus officials from Italy, NATO, the European Commission and the European Council. But Russia objects to European involvement in the main negotiations, so they will be consulted by Kushner and Witkoff on the sidelines rather than seated at the table.

Who should speak for Europe is itself a divisive question. EU leaders are expected to discuss it at an informal summit in Dublin later this week.

## A wider argument about European security

The push for a European seat is part of a broader American argument that ending the war will require a rethink of Europe's security relationship with Russia. European governments worry that President Donald Trump could accept President Vladimir Putin's long-standing demands to roll back NATO's influence in the region. U.S. officials insisted that NATO would remain central to Europe's collective defence, while arguing that the current arrangements are not working and need to be rebuilt. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte has been closely involved in the discussions with the U.S. administration, according to the report.

Territory remains the hardest question for Ukraine. Both sides have shown some flexibility in private about the fate of Russian-occupied territory, the report said, and the territorial and security tracks will have to be resolved together.

## What is still unknown

Whether Moscow will agree to widen the format at all, who would represent Europe if it does, and what form any new European security arrangement would take are all still open. No agreement is expected from this week's talks, and previous rounds earlier this year produced no visible progress while fighting continued along the front line.

Elon Musk has said every child in the United States will receive two shares in SpaceX — one from the company and one from him personally — in a pledge worth more than $18 billion.

Musk announced the plan in a brief post on X on Wednesday, without giving details of how or when the shares would be delivered. "Next year, I will give one of my SpaceX shares to every kid in America. This will be the first of many," he wrote.

SpaceX had said earlier in the day that it would contribute one newly issued share of its Class A common stock to every currently active Trump Account, and that Musk would personally match the number of shares the company donates. Trump Accounts are a government programme designed to give Americans born between 2025 and 2028 a stake in the stock market and help them build wealth from an early age.

With about 72 million children under 18 in the US, and SpaceX shares closing at $127.44 on Wednesday, the combined pledge is valued at around $18.35 billion. On the roughly 70 million active Trump Accounts reported by the White House, the figure would be about $17.8 billion.

"Giving shares, which are stakes in the future economy, directly to the people is the way," Musk added in his post.

It is not the first such gift from inside SpaceX. President Gwynne Shotwell and her husband had already pledged a share of their SpaceX stock to each of more than two million children under the Trump initiative. Other companies have joined the programme too: Dell Technologies chief executive Michael Dell and his wife pledged a $6.25 billion cash gift.

The pledge is a small fraction of Musk's fortune, which Bloomberg estimates at about $813 billion, most of it tied to his SpaceX stake. He owns 849.4 million shares of SpaceX's Class A common stock, according to regulatory filings, alongside billions of Class B shares with greater voting power and a large holding in Tesla.

Some details remain unclear. SpaceX did not immediately respond to a request for comment on whether Musk's personal share donations would be linked to Trump Accounts or come with any kind of lockup period — a restriction that would stop recipients selling the shares for a set time. Musk had already said last month that he would give one of his SpaceX shares to every child in America, and Wednesday's announcement turns that idea into a matching commitment from both the company and its chief executive.

The presidents of the United States, China and Russia opened the first trilateral meeting of the three countries' leaders on Thursday in Shenzhen, a gathering Donald Trump described as "really making history."

Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin sat down at the Kylin Villa on the sidelines of the two-day APEC leaders' meeting in the southern Chinese tech hub. Xi, hosting the talks, called them of "groundbreaking significance" as the session began. Trump said the three leaders had a "great deal to discuss" and carried a "special responsibility" as leaders of three of the world's largest countries.

The meeting is the first time the three leaders have met as a group. Trump held separate bilateral meetings with Putin and with Xi earlier in the visit. He called Putin his "friend" and said the two have a "great relationship," and said after meeting Xi that the United States and China "want to see an end to the Ukraine war."

The agenda is crowded. The leaders were expected to discuss the wars in Ukraine and Iran, nuclear proliferation, trade and artificial intelligence. Trump said the talks would cover "a lot of things including denuclearisation," and said after the Xi meeting that Iran "cannot have a nuclear weapon."

There are limits to what the meeting is expected to produce. US officials have said they do not expect a formal agreement, describing the session as an initial conversation. China has long been reluctant to join nuclear disarmament talks, pointing to the gap between its arsenal and those of the United States and Russia, and Beijing has resisted efforts to put the Ukraine war on the agenda.

The timing adds weight to the encounter. The last US-Russia nuclear treaty expired in February, leaving the world's two largest nuclear arsenals without caps for the first time in more than half a century. The three leaders' talks come as the APEC summit brings 21 Pacific Rim economies together in Shenzhen, a forum that takes no binding decisions but has long served as a place to build consensus on trade and cooperation.

Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday from the Philippines, where he attended the ASEAN summit. His bilateral meeting with Xi was their fourth since he returned to office in 2025, and their third this year. Putin's appearance at the APEC gathering was his first in person since 2017.

Whether the three-way format becomes a regular fixture, or a one-off piece of pageantry, will depend on what follows the handshakes. For now, the meeting itself is the news: three leaders who have never sat down together have done so, in a room where the world's largest economies, its biggest nuclear arsenals and two ongoing wars were all on the table.

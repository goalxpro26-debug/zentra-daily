A federal judge on Wednesday declined to extend a ban on taxpayer-funded television ads that promoted President Donald Trump ahead of the midterm elections, ruling that Democrats had not shown they were likely to win an immediate court order — even as she said they may ultimately succeed in proving the ads broke the law.

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan in Washington, D.C., refused a preliminary injunction sought by the Democratic National Committee, which sued the Trump administration in October after it began airing ads paid for at least in part by a $20 million Department of Homeland Security contract. The DNC argued the spots were illegal government-sponsored propaganda designed to promote the president's campaign message in the weeks before November's midterm elections.

AliKhan noted that her earlier temporary restraining order had been issued when the election was less than a week away. "But the November 2026 midterm elections have come and gone," she wrote. The DNC argued it could still be harmed by the ads in December congressional runoff elections, or because the 2028 presidential cycle has already begun. "At this juncture and on this record, the court is unpersuaded," she wrote.

She also rejected the party's argument that campaigns are effectively permanent. Taken to its logical conclusion, she wrote, that reasoning "would allow the DNC to seek preliminary relief in perpetuity because it could always point to the occurrence of some election somewhere in the country."

The Justice Department argued there was no threat of harm because the government had already stopped using public funds to air the ads and the midterms had passed. AliKhan's earlier order had blocked the administration from developing, airing or distributing some of the challenged ads through November 25.

The ruling follows a similar decision in a separate case in New York, where another judge also declined a long-term ban on the ads — though that judge blocked a broader collection of the advertisements and required the government to remove three of them from official government websites and social media accounts.

The decision is not the end of the case. AliKhan said the DNC was likely to succeed on its claim that using public money for the ads violated the law, and the lawsuits continue. For now, though, no court order stops the ads from airing again.

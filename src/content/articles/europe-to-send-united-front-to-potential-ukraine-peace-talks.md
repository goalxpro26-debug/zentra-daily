Europe will take a seat at any future talks on ending the war in Ukraine, speaking with one voice through the European Union and five of its biggest states, European leaders agreed in Dublin on Friday.

European Council President Antonio Costa announced it after an informal European Council meeting and a European Political Community summit that brought 47 European leaders to the Irish capital. Under the plan, the EU will jointly represent Europe alongside France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the United Kingdom.

It answers a demand European and Ukrainian leaders have pressed for years: no settlement about Ukraine's future without Europe in the room. Russia has long resisted a European role, and the United States under President Donald Trump had previously appeared indifferent. That position now appears to be shifting.

## A shift after Moscow

The announcement followed a diplomatic sequence that began with trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday. On Thursday, US envoys Jared Kushner and special envoy Steve Witkoff met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow, then travelled to Dublin to brief Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Kushner said late on Thursday that a deal could not be concluded without the Europeans at the table, and that Putin was willing to give the expanded format a try. Witkoff told reporters that Europeans would be present at the next round of meetings.

But the shape of Europe's presence is far from settled. Russia and the United States have called for a single European representative, while several European nations favour a diplomatic team. Who would speak for the EU itself is also open. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said she is the one representing Europe on foreign and security policy; she and Costa are the two names most often tipped.

Moscow is sending mixed signals. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said European participation was not discussed during the Moscow talks. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia needed to understand the EU's real intentions before deciding on an expanded format, while chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky claimed Russia had received no concrete peace proposal from European countries after the Abu Dhabi talks.

## Merz warns Trump against surprises

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz used the Dublin meeting to send Washington a public message. With Trump expected to meet Putin again at an APEC summit in China next week, Merz asked for no surprises, warning against a repeat of last summer's Russia-US talks in Anchorage, Alaska, whose outcome Europe viewed as favouring Putin. He said Germany's involvement in any future talks was a given.

Kallas struck a similar note of caution, saying a ceasefire must come first and warning against walking into what she called Russian traps. She argued that Moscow does not want a strong figure at the table because it wants to push through its maximum demands.

The Dublin agreement puts Europe's claim to a seat on the record. Whether Russia accepts it, and in what form, is the next question — nearly five years into the war.

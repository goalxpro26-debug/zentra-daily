An international panel of health experts meets behind closed doors in Washington this week to decide whether the United States keeps the measles elimination status it has held since 2000. The review comes as the country has confirmed at least 5,182 measles cases so far this year, spread across 45 states and Washington, D.C.

The meeting, scheduled for November 10 to 13, is run by the Pan American Health Organization's independent commission for monitoring measles and rubella elimination in the Americas. The commission, made up of experts in epidemiology, public health, virology, pediatrics and immunization, meets at least once a year to assess whether countries in the region still qualify as having eliminated the disease.

## The question is whether the spread never stopped

Elimination does not mean measles vanished entirely. It means the virus was not spreading continuously within the country for at least 12 months. The United States earned that designation in 2000, when annual cases had fallen to 85.

The review turns on what happened after the first cases of the current surge were confirmed in a small town in western Texas in January 2025. From there, measles spread to neighbouring Texas counties and to other states, including Utah, Arizona, South Carolina and Pennsylvania.

If the evidence shows genetically identical strains linking the large outbreaks back to the Texas strain, that would support a finding of a full year of continuous transmission — and the loss of elimination status. If the outbreaks turn out to be separate and unlinked, the country would keep its status.

## A record year, by a wide margin

The scale of the resurgence is now clear. The United States recorded 2,289 confirmed cases in 2025, the first time the annual total had passed 2,000 since 1992. This year's count of more than 5,000 is already more than double that.

Pennsylvania alone has recorded more than 1,500 cases this year, according to the state's health department. Eleven measles deaths have been confirmed over the past nearly two years, largely among people who were unvaccinated or too young to be vaccinated.

Canada lost its own measles elimination status last year after a year-long outbreak it could not contain, and the wider Americas region has already lost its measles-free designation as a result.

## A decision that is largely symbolic — and still stings

Health experts who spoke to ABC News said losing the status would change little in practice, because measles transmission is already widespread. But they described the moment as a serious marker of how far the disease has come back.

Dr. Paul Offit called the possible loss "a tremendous loss" and, "at best… a call to arms." Dr. Peter Hotez put it more bluntly: "Measles is back."

## What is still unknown

The commission will weigh the evidence during its four-day meeting, but there is no public timetable for a decision. Its conclusions go to PAHO's director, who formally communicates the classification to national authorities, so a public announcement may come days after the meeting ends.

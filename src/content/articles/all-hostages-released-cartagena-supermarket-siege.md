Colombian security forces stormed a supermarket in the Caribbean city of Cartagena early on Wednesday, freeing the last 20 hostages held after a failed armoured-vehicle robbery turned into a day-long siege. One of the captors was killed in the assault, President Abelardo de la Espriella said.

The gunmen had tried to rob an armoured vehicle on Tuesday before fleeing into an Éxito supermarket in the La Castellana neighbourhood as police closed in. Security camera operators had alerted officers to the attempted robbery, and the men took about 30 staff and shoppers hostage inside the store. Police, troops and special forces surrounded the building and cut power to the area as negotiations began.

By nightfall, 10 of the hostages had been released, including five elderly women. Hours later, shortly after midnight, explosions were heard and security forces moved in. "In an extraordinary joint operation between the National Police and the Armed Forces, all the hostages were rescued," de la Espriella said early on Wednesday. Cartagena's mayor said all of those freed were "safe and sound" and would be reunited with their families before undergoing medical checks.

De la Espriella travelled to the scene, appearing in fatigues and carrying a walkie-talkie as he met commanders from a specialist anti-kidnapping unit. He was quick to rule out the involvement of guerrilla or paramilitary groups. "It's not that a terrorist group carried out a kidnapping — no, these are criminals who were caught off guard, had no way out, and then kidnapped a group of people," he said.

The siege unfolded on the 10th anniversary of Colombia's 2016 peace deal with the FARC, and it adds to a run of high-profile security incidents facing a president who campaigned on a promise to crack down on crime. Former President Gustavo Petro weighed in on social media, blaming the violence on the collapse of the tourism sector. De la Espriella defeated Petro's handpicked successor, Senator Iván Cepeda, in a run-off election on June 21.

Cartagena, a Caribbean port famed for its colonial walled old town, beach resorts and cruise-ship tourism, was brought to a standstill for much of the day. The city's Rafael Núñez airport briefly suspended landings while the armed forces used one of its runways in connection with the operation.

Latvia and Lithuania warned residents to take shelter on Tuesday night after an unidentified object was detected in Belarus flying towards the Baltic states, and NATO fighter jets on the Baltic air defence mission were scrambled in response.

In Latvia, the National Armed Forces issued an orange-level air threat alert for Ludza municipality, on the border with Belarus, telling residents to take cover immediately. The alert was later extended to the Krāslava and Augšdaugava regions before being called off about an hour later. No injuries were reported.

Latvia uses a two-level mobile alert system. A yellow alert is an informational message about a possible threat that requires no immediate action; an orange alert means a confirmed threat in the airspace and residents in the affected areas must follow instructions at once. People in the areas covered by the warning received the alert on their mobile phones.

## Lithuania's alert

Lithuania issued its own warning after the object was spotted in Belarus heading towards its territory. A code yellow alert was broadcast at around 11pm local time, urging people in the counties of Vilnius and Utena to head for their nearest shelter.

Vilnius Airport and the airspace around it were shut down while the alert was in force, and reopened once it was called off. The head of Lithuania's National Crisis Management Centre, Vilmantas Vitkauskas, told Lithuanian public broadcaster LRT that the object had yet to be identified.

A drone detected in Belarusian territory was also reported to have crossed the border into Poland, according to reports carried by Israeli outlet Ynet, and Lithuania later widened its alert in the border area with Belarus.

## A recurring pattern

The incident is the latest in a series of airspace scares across northern Europe. Drones have strayed into the airspace of Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania in recent months, and Latvia reported a brief intrusion near its borders with Belarus and Russia just over a week ago, on 1 November.

Ukraine has been attacking Russian oil shipping ports on the Baltic Sea, and some of its drones have missed their targets and triggered security warnings in neighbouring countries. Moscow has accused Kyiv of using NATO airspace as cover for attacks, a charge Kyiv rejects.

Neither Latvian nor Lithuanian authorities said on Tuesday night what the object was, where it came from, or whether it ever entered their airspace. Both countries lifted their alerts within about an hour, and residents in Ludza were told they could leave their shelters.

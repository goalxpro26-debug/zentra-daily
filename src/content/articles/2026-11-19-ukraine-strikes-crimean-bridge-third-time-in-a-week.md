Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia to Crimea for the third time in a week on Thursday, forcing a halt to train services across the crossing hours before President Vladimir Putin met President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping at a trilateral summit in China.

The latest attack was the third on the Kerch Bridge in four days. The first, on Monday, caused more minor damage. On Tuesday, Ukrainian drones and missiles destroyed the central road section, and video and images verified by Reuters showed the main road arch crumbling into the sea and leaving a yawning gap in the roadway.

Russian authorities said the Thursday attack forced a suspension of train services across the bridge. Sergei Aksyonov, the Russian-installed governor of Crimea, said in a video message that rail travel would resume later in the day, adding that Crimea would overcome the damage. Russia's transport ministry said nobody had been hurt in the latest attack and that specialists were assessing the damage, without giving an extent for Thursday's strike.

Satellite images from Monday and Thursday show a collapsed section of the bridge, according to the report. The Tuesday strike was described as likely to make it harder for Russia to supply the peninsula, home to nearly 2.5 million people, and to put pressure on Russian military logistics in parts of southern Ukraine held by Moscow.

The 12-mile bridge is the longest in Europe. Opened by Putin in 2018, it has become a symbol of Russia's attachment to Crimea, which Moscow annexed from Ukraine in 2014 and which hosts its Black Sea Fleet. The crossing carries both road and rail traffic and has been a repeated target since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began.

There was no immediate comment on the latest attack from Ukraine or from President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, according to the report. Zelenskyy was due to hold talks in Berlin later on Thursday with United States peace envoys, while the three leaders in Shenzhen were meeting for the first time in the trilateral format.

The bridge attacks have come in a week of intense diplomacy around the war, with the Shenzhen summit and the Berlin talks both taking place on the same day. Whether the damage to the crossing changes the course of those discussions remains unclear, and neither side has said when full road and rail traffic across the bridge will be restored.

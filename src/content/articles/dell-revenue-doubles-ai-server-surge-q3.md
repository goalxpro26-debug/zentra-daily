Dell Technologies more than doubled its quarterly revenue as demand for artificial intelligence servers surged, reporting record results on Tuesday and raising its full-year outlook for the third time this year.

Revenue for the fiscal third quarter rose 103 percent from a year earlier to $54.9 billion, comfortably ahead of the $49.3 billion analysts had expected. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $9.45, well above the $6.59 forecast, while net income rose to $5.67 billion.

The growth was driven almost entirely by Dell's infrastructure business, which sells servers, storage and networking gear to companies and cloud providers building AI systems. Revenue in that division nearly tripled to $42.0 billion. Sales of AI-optimised servers jumped 291 percent to $22.0 billion, and traditional servers and networking rose 235 percent to $15.0 billion.

Orders kept piling up faster than Dell could ship them. The company booked $60.2 billion in new AI server orders during the quarter and ended it with a record backlog of $132.3 billion. More than 7,500 customers are now deploying Dell's packaged AI systems, known as Dell AI Factories, which bundle servers, storage, networking and software for running AI models.

Dell now expects full-year fiscal 2027 revenue of $202 billion, up from its previous target of $192 billion and above Wall Street's estimate of about $194.5 billion. It also raised its adjusted earnings forecast to $29.68 per share, from $25.50, and lifted its projection for AI server revenue this year to $78 billion, from $74 billion. For the current fourth quarter, Dell guided to revenue of $56.3 billion and adjusted earnings of $8.40 per share, both above expectations.

The personal computer business, once Dell's core, grew far more modestly, rising 3 percent to $12.9 billion — a reminder of how far the company's centre of gravity has shifted toward data-centre infrastructure.

Dell also returned a record $4.9 billion to shareholders in the quarter through buybacks and dividends, and its board approved an additional $25 billion share repurchase authorisation. Shares rose about 3 percent in after-hours trading following the report.

The results underline how much of the AI spending boom is flowing through hardware makers. Dell's servers are powered largely by Nvidia chips, and enterprises and cloud providers are buying them to run complex AI workloads. The open question for investors is whether the $60.2 billion of orders booked in a single quarter converts into shipped revenue without straining Dell's cash, since the company must buy expensive components and fund assembly before delivery.

Sales of existing homes in the United States fell sharply in October to their lowest level in more than 16 years, the National Association of Realtors reported on Thursday, as high mortgage rates and still-elevated prices kept would-be buyers on the sidelines.

Existing-home sales dropped 5.5% from September to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million units, the realtors' group said. That was the weakest pace since August 2010, when sales were falling after the end of a homebuyer tax credit, and it came in below the 3.85 million rate economists polled by Reuters had expected. Sales were down 8.8% from a year earlier.

The drop ended a three-year stretch in which sales had hovered near the 4 million mark, moving within a narrow range from month to month. The main culprit, by the realtors' own account, is the cost of borrowing: the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.33% in October, the highest since 2000 apart from a single month in 2023.

"Home buying is difficult when mortgage rates have climbed to their highest levels since 2000, aside from a single month in 2023," said Lawrence Yun, the association's chief economist. "As a result, home sales fell to their lowest level since August 2010."

## More homes, fewer buyers

The market is not short of homes to buy. There were 1.59 million homes on the market in October, up 4.6% from a year ago, a supply of about 5.1 months at the current sales pace. The median price of an existing home held at $416,700.

That combination — more choice, but fewer deals — points to affordability rather than supply as the binding constraint. Sales are counted when a contract closes, so October's figures largely reflect deals agreed in August and September, when rates were already climbing.

Economists do not expect a quick rebound. Nancy Vanden Houten, lead U.S. economist at Oxford Economics, said she expects sales to stay below 4 million until late next year. Yun said he expects total sales for 2026 to come in somewhat below last year's, and is waiting until December, after the midterm elections, before forecasting 2027.

## What is still unknown

Whether rates ease from here is the open question. If they stay near current levels, the freeze is likely to persist into the winter, traditionally the slowest season for housing. If they fall, the homes already sitting on the market could move quickly — the demand, by most accounts, is waiting rather than gone.

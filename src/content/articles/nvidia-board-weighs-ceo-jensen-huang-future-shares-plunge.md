Nvidia's board met late on Wednesday to decide the future of Jensen Huang, the founder and only chief executive the chipmaker has ever had, after a week of damaging reports about his conduct sent the company's shares sharply lower.

Shares in Nvidia fell more than 8% in after-hours trading on Wednesday, dropping to about $158.78 from a regular-session close of $173.73. The slide followed a report in the Wall Street Journal, citing people familiar with the matter, that the board was preparing to remove Huang. Reuters, citing a source familiar with the matter, reported that the board was meeting late on Wednesday to decide whether to oust him.

The immediate trigger was a New York Times report, published earlier on Wednesday, that Huang had a relationship with a Nvidia executive who reports directly to him. The executive is Kimberly Powell, the company's vice president of healthcare. The Times cited unidentified people who worked at the company, and the Journal subsequently confirmed the reporting. Neither Nvidia nor Powell had responded to requests for comment by late Wednesday, and the board had issued no statement.

## A crisis that has built over days

The reports cap a week in which Huang's position has come under growing pressure. On Sunday, Huang told Nvidia employees in a letter that he had been unfaithful to his wife, without disclosing details of the relationship. The same day, the board opened an independent investigation into reports that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation.

That investigation concerns Huang's reported relationship with Alice Huang, a businesswoman US authorities have described as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The board has retained the law firm Wachtell Lipton to lead the inquiry, and the FBI is also examining the matter. The board had previously said it had no reason to believe company secrets or national security had been compromised, and had expressed confidence in Huang.

The political fallout has spread beyond the company. Lawmakers in Washington have called for briefings and new restrictions on advanced chip exports to China, and at least one senator has publicly called for Huang's resignation. President Donald Trump said on Monday that he had spoken to Huang, who told him he had no idea the woman was a spy, and that he believed him.

Investors have reacted swiftly. Nvidia's shares had already fallen a combined 9.5% on Monday and Tuesday, wiping hundreds of billions of dollars from its market value, before recovering 2.9% in Wednesday's regular session — only to plunge again after the closing bell. Shares in rival chipmakers Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom rose in after-hours trading on Wednesday.

## What is still unknown

No decision has been announced. The board has not said when it will conclude its deliberations, and it is not clear whether a successor has been identified. That matters: analysts note Nvidia has no public succession plan, and Huang has led the company since founding it in 1993, overseeing a rise in its share price of more than 700,000% as it became the dominant supplier of the processors that power artificial intelligence.

For now, the company's leadership — and the centre of the AI industry — hangs on a board decision that had not been made public by late Wednesday night.

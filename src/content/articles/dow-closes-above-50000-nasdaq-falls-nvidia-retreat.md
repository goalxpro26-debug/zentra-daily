The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed above 50,000 on Tuesday, gaining 423.39 points to finish at 50,018.70, while the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite both fell as technology stocks gave back part of Monday's sharp rally.

The Dow rose 0.85 percent, its second straight daily gain and its first close above the 50,000 mark in about a week. The S&P 500 slipped 0.50 percent to 7,201.09, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48 percent to 23,733.54. The split session showed investors rotating out of the technology shares that had powered the previous day's surge and into defensive and value stocks instead.

The reversal was led by Nvidia, which fell nearly 6 percent on Tuesday after surging about 31 percent on Monday. The chipmaker's rebound a day earlier had been driven by the reinstatement of chief executive Jensen Huang, and it lifted the Nasdaq more than 6 percent and the S&P 500 nearly 5 percent in one of the strongest single-day rallies in years. On Tuesday, chip stocks fell broadly, with several semiconductor names among the day's biggest decliners.

Smaller companies fared better than the big technology names. The Russell 2000 index of small-cap stocks rose 0.57 percent to 2,684.94, suggesting the selling was concentrated in the large technology stocks rather than spreading across the whole market.

The Dow's advance was helped by gains in health care and consumer stocks, which investors tend to favour when they grow cautious about growth shares. MarketWatch reported that investors rotated into defensive stocks and away from technology, leaving the blue-chip index higher even as the broader market weakened.

Tuesday's session capped a volatile stretch for American equities. Markets have swung sharply in recent weeks as investors weigh the outlook for artificial intelligence spending, corporate earnings and the path of interest rates. The Dow's return above 50,000 gives the index a symbolic milestone, but the renewed slide in technology shares underlines how quickly sentiment can turn.

Attention now turns to the next round of corporate results, with Dell among the companies reporting, and to economic data due before the Thanksgiving holiday, when US markets close on Thursday and trade for a shortened session on Friday.

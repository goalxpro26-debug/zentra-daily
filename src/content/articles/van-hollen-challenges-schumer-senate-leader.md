Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland announced on Thursday that he will challenge Sen. Chuck Schumer for the job of Senate majority leader, opening a leadership contest inside a Democratic Party that has just won back the Senate.

Van Hollen, 67, made the announcement in an interview with MS NOW host Jen Psaki and in a post on X, saying voters had sent a clear message in the midterm elections. "This is not about any one individual, but it is about answering the call of the American people," he said, describing the midterms as a "huge outburst of democracy" and a rejection of the status quo.

Democrats flipped eight seats in the midterms and will enter January with at least 55 senators, possibly 56. The caucus will choose its leader by secret ballot, with the vote scheduled for December 1.

## A challenge from the left

Van Hollen framed his candidacy as a break with business as usual. He said he would use the "full power" of the Senate to respond to voters' demands to end the war in Iran and to lower the cost of living — two issues he has pressed for months.

The Maryland senator has emerged as a vessel for progressives and newer members who want more forceful leadership, or at least want to push Schumer toward a more aggressive posture. Sen. Bernie Sanders said this week he would not support Schumer for another term as leader, and Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan, who has said for weeks he could back Van Hollen, welcomed the announcement.

Van Hollen's move is a sharp reversal from just days ago. On Saturday he told CNN that seeking the leadership job was "not my focus," noting that he was also interested in running for president in 2028. As of Thursday afternoon, he said he was "still actively listening to members" — before declaring his candidacy hours later.

## Schumer says he has the votes

Schumer, 75, a five-term senator from New York, has led Senate Democrats for nearly a decade and says he is confident he will win. In a statement on Thursday, he said he welcomed a discussion about the caucus's future and repeated his assurance that he had the votes to continue as leader.

"I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic Leader," Schumer said. "I look forward to leading our new Democratic majority and delivering for the American people."

Schumer has faced sustained criticism from parts of the party over his handling of the Democratic caucus during the Trump presidency, and the leadership race will test how much of that discontent reaches the ballot box. Even if Schumer wins, the contest could raise questions about his authority at the start of a new majority.

## What is still unknown

No other senator has entered the race, and it is not yet clear how many of the incoming freshmen — most of whom have declined to publicly back Schumer — will vote for Van Hollen. The closed ballot means the outcome will not be known until the December 1 vote.

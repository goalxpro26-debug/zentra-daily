Rivian's chief executive, RJ Scaringe, says 2027 will be "an inflection year" for the electric-vehicle maker, as it ramps up production of its cheaper R2 SUV and rolls out new driver-assistance technology. He made the remarks in an interview with CNBC on Tuesday, five years to the day since Rivian listed on the Nasdaq.

The anniversary is a sobering one for shareholders. Rivian's shares have fallen more than 80% from their initial public offering price of $78, including a drop of nearly 30% this year. Scaringe's argument is that the next twelve months change the story: the R2, a midsize SUV aimed at a far broader market than Rivian's first models, is now the company's best-selling vehicle by volume, and its cheapest version is expected to cost about $45,000.

The numbers behind that claim are the strongest the company has reported. Rivian delivered a record 19,248 vehicles in the third quarter of 2026, up 46% on a year earlier, with the R2 driving the growth in its first full quarter of sales. The company has raised its full-year delivery forecast to between 67,000 and 70,000 vehicles. The R2 has also collected early honours, including TopGear's SUV of the Year award for 2027.

## Autonomy is the second bet

The other half of Scaringe's plan is self-driving technology. Rivian is preparing a limited release of "point-to-point" driving — in which the vehicle navigates between destinations while the driver supervises — before the end of this year, with a general release to subscribers of its Autonomy+ service in 2027. The company says it will share more detail about its artificial-intelligence and autonomy plans at an investor event on December 9.

Rivian is also building a multibillion-dollar factory in Georgia to support the R2's expansion, and it estimates its available future liquidity at about $13 billion, including credit lines, funding from its partnerships with Volkswagen and Uber, and a $4.5 billion US Department of Energy loan for the Georgia plant.

## What is still unknown

Investors have not yet seen Rivian's 2027 delivery or profit targets, and funding remains a live concern: Deutsche Bank analyst Edison Yu flagged the company's cash needs after its October 30 earnings report. Rivian's automotive division is on course to end 2026 with positive gross margins, its interim finance chief told investors last week — a milestone that would give the 2027 plan a firmer footing, but one the company still has to deliver.

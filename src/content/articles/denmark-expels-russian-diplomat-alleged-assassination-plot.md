Denmark has ordered Russia's defence attaché to leave the country after its intelligence service said Moscow planned to assassinate a Ukrainian-American rapper during a concert near Copenhagen in September.

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen announced the decision on Friday, saying he had summoned the Russian ambassador and told him the attaché must leave Denmark within seven days. Denmark is also considering suspending Schengen visas for Russian citizens.

According to PET, Denmark's security and intelligence service, three people were stopped in a car near the concert venue on 25 September, where police found firearms and knives. Two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service. Court documents showed they were arrested five minutes before the concert was due to begin, and one of them had bought a ticket to the performance. The concert was cancelled.

PET suspects the banned street gang Loyal to Familia helped plan the attack. Danish public broadcaster DR identified the intended target as Kyivstoner, a rapper who holds both Ukrainian and US citizenship.

PET chief Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen said the case showed that Russian intelligence had tried to carry out a killing on Danish soil. "A Russian assassination attempt is not something we've seen in Denmark before, and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia," he told journalists.

Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen echoed that warning, saying Denmark faced "a cornered Russia, acting more aggressively and with a greater propensity to take risks". European Council President António Costa called the alleged attempt "unacceptable" and said it "will not go unanswered", promising Denmark the European Union's full backing against what he called Russian hybrid attacks.

Moscow rejected the accusation. The Russian embassy in Copenhagen called the expulsion "vengeful" and said Denmark had presented no evidence of Russian involvement. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Moscow would give "an appropriate response". Russia's security service had previously accused the rapper of helping Ukrainian intelligence plan a drone attack on a defence facility near Moscow, an allegation he denied.

Denmark has blamed Moscow for other hybrid attacks — operations short of open war, such as sabotage and disruption. Drones shut down Copenhagen airport for hours in September 2025, and PET said in September that Russia was recruiting Danish citizens to help plan sabotage against defence firms with ties to Ukraine. Russia denies involvement in such operations.

What evidence Danish prosecutors hold beyond the arrests has not been made public, and the charges remain allegations to be tested in court. But the expulsion is already Denmark's sharpest diplomatic response to Moscow since the war in Ukraine began — a signal, in Rasmussen's words, that Copenhagen wants to send a "very, very strong" message without breaking off diplomatic relations entirely.

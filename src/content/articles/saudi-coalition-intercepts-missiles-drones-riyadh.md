Saudi-led coalition forces said early on Sunday they had intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh, allegedly fired by Yemen's Houthis, in the latest cross-border attack as fighting intensifies across several front lines in Yemen.

Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said in a post on X that air defences had intercepted and destroyed the two missiles. He said on Saturday that two suspected Houthi drones had been brought down in Riyadh and the southern regions, and on Sunday the coalition said three more drones were shot down in the Eastern region — five drones in all over the weekend.

Saudi authorities say at least 26 people have been killed in suspected Houthi drone and missile attacks inside the kingdom since September 23. The Houthis have repeatedly targeted Riyadh's King Khalid international airport, as well as oil infrastructure in eastern and southern Saudi Arabia.

## A downed aircraft, claimed by the Houthis

The Houthis gave their own account of the weekend's fighting. Their military spokesman, Yahya Saree, said the group had shot down an armed coalition reconnaissance aircraft operating over al-Jawf, describing it as the fifth aircraft brought down since the escalation began. He said Houthi fighters had repelled Saudi formations using Yemeni-made surface-to-air missiles, and that the group had fired ballistic missiles at Saudi-backed forces in the Kharaz area of Lahij governorate.

On the ground, Yemeni government forces said they had made gains in the al-Hazm and Madghal districts of al-Jawf as fighting intensified around Marib. The government said 321 Houthi leaders had been killed or wounded in 723 operations across Taiz, Lahij, Marib and al-Jawf, and that 117 military vehicles had been destroyed. The Houthis, for their part, said the coalition had launched 72 air strikes against them in the past 24 hours. Houthi-affiliated media reported that two women and two children were wounded by artillery shelling in a residential neighbourhood of the Shuaub district in Sanaa.

## Displacement and a U.S. warning

The International Organization for Migration says more than 340,000 people have been displaced by fighting in Yemen since August, as the conflict grinds on with no settlement in sight.

As the fighting escalated, the U.S. Embassy in Riyadh issued a nationwide security alert on Saturday urging Americans in the kingdom to exercise "heightened vigilance", citing an increased threat of Houthi attacks. The embassy said attacks on airports and other critical infrastructure had caused civilian casualties and severe travel disruption, and warned that hostilities could spread rapidly.

Millions of workers across Spain stopped work on Wednesday in the country's first general strike in 14 years, demanding that the government act on a housing crisis that has pushed rents out of reach for many — less than three weeks before a snap national election.

The strike, backed by Spain's two largest unions, UGT and CCOO, along with tenants' associations and other labour groups, was expected to disrupt public transport and slow or close businesses, factories and shops across the eurozone's fourth-largest economy. Picket lines formed in the early hours despite stormy weather in much of the country, and the unions reported widespread participation.

## A decade of rising rents

Housing is at the centre of the protest. The average rent in Spain has almost doubled over the past 10 years, eating into household budgets and angering voters. According to the Youth Council of Spain, the average rental price of €1,176 a month is equivalent to about 98.7 percent of a young person's average monthly salary.

Spain also faces a shortage of roughly 750,000 homes, according to the central bank — a gap expected to grow to more than a million by 2028.

The strike was called by the tenants' movement and quickly won union backing. Its official slogan is "Salaries. Housing. Prices." The housing activists are demanding several measures, including the elimination of tourist apartments, a crackdown on real estate speculation, and protection for renters through automatic lease renewals. The unions are also seeking more government intervention in the housing market and a new minimum-wage agreement negotiated with employers.

## A death that became a symbol

The campaign gained force after the eviction of an 87-year-old woman known as Maricarmen, who was removed from her home in Madrid's Retiro neighbourhood on September 23. Her subsequent death drew further public anger and made her a symbol for Spaniards who feel they pay too much on rent and cannot imagine ever owning a home.

The last general strike in Spain was on November 14, 2012, called against the austerity measures of the government of the time. This time the initiative came from the housing movement rather than the unions alone.

## Politics days away

The timing is pointed. The strike comes 18 days before a November 29 general election, and on Thursday — the day after the strike — the Congress will vote on two housing decrees, including measures that would allow some tenants to request up to two additional years of rent and suspend evictions of vulnerable households without housing alternatives.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has defended the right to strike. Analysts say the protest puts housing — and the cost of living more broadly — at the centre of the election campaign, in a vote that could bring the far right to power.

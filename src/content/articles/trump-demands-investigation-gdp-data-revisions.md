President Donald Trump has demanded an immediate investigation into the government's economic statistics, after the Commerce Department revised third-quarter growth sharply higher in the weeks after the midterm elections.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) said on Wednesday that the US economy grew at an annualised rate of 4.3% in the third quarter, up from the 3.4% advance estimate it published on 29 October — five days before the 3 November midterms, in which Trump's Republican Party suffered heavy losses. The 0.9 percentage point upward revision was driven by stronger investment and consumer spending than first measured.

Trump seized on the revision in a post on his Truth Social network on Thursday, Thanksgiving Day, calling the figures published before the election "FAKE" and the revision a "massive, and purposeful, error". He offered no evidence for the claim that the numbers were deliberately understated.

"It was just revealed that the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE," he wrote. "Why wasn't the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation."

In the same post, Trump argued that the corrected figure showed the economy under his administration was "doing FAR BETTER than the good numbers already released", adding: "Third Quarter GDP Growth has now been corrected to be 4.3%, instead of 3.4%, a major difference!"

He returned to the subject in a second Thanksgiving post, wishing a happy holiday "even [to] those who issued fake Economic Numbers, just prior to the Election, and corrected them yesterday, three weeks late", and calling the revision a "RECORD SETTING ERROR, and one so grave that it should be immediately investigated."

The BEA says revisions of this kind are routine. Its advance estimate for each quarter is based on incomplete source data, particularly on inventories, trade and consumer spending on services, and is updated as fuller figures arrive. The agency said this revision largely reflected upward adjustments to investment and consumer spending, after updates to the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Producer Price Index showed stronger spending than first recorded.

The BEA publishes three estimates for each quarter — an advance, a second and a third reading — and the final third-quarter estimate is scheduled for release on 23 December. Economists note that revisions of close to a percentage point, while large, are not unusual when the advance estimate is based on partial data.

The episode comes as Trump has repeatedly questioned official figures that cut against his political interests, including in the wake of the midterm results. For now, there is no indication of what form any investigation would take, or which body would conduct it.

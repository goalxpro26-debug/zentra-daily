New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced on Monday that the New York Police Department's Strategic Response Group will be disbanded, delivering on one of the central promises of his 2025 mayoral campaign.

"During our campaign for mayor, I promised to disband NYPD's Strategic Response Group, known as the SRG. Today, we are keeping that promise," Mamdani said in a video address announcing the changes, delivered alongside Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch.

The SRG was created in 2015 as a specialised unit handling both counterterrorism and protest response. Over the years it drew sustained criticism from protesters and civil rights groups, who described its tactics at demonstrations as heavy-handed — criticism that led to lawsuits against the nation's largest police department. Mamdani has long argued that the two jobs should never have been combined. "New Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat," he said.

Under the plan, the split is now explicit. The unit's counterterrorism responsibilities, including deployments of heavy weapons at critical infrastructure, will move to the NYPD's Intelligence and Counterterrorism Bureau. Protest response will be handled differently: a new civilian-led Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division, housed in the Mayor's Office of Community Safety, will prepare for and respond to demonstrations through dialogue and engagement. Its staff will not be police officers and will carry no weapons and hold no arrest powers.

The NYPD will also create a First Amendment Support Team of 30 uniformed officers trained in communication and de-escalation, alongside a new Patrol Support Division staffed with vetted officers focused on patrol duties. Officials said the changes will not reduce NYPD headcount, and SRG officers will be reassigned to other roles.

The announcement also brings new restrictions on crowd-control tactics. Officers will be barred from using tear gas and pepper spray for crowd management at protests, and from deploying long guns during lawful demonstrations. The reforms also prohibit kettling, the practice of corralling protesters into enclosed spaces.

Mamdani also endorsed the CURB Act, a City Council bill sponsored by Council Member Chi Ossé that would place stronger legal limits on protest policing and prevent a similar unit from being recreated under a future mayor. The bill had 25 council sponsors as of last week, one short of a majority.

The move had been anticipated since last week, when Politico reported that Mamdani was preparing to unveil the plan. It is the mayor's most sweeping police reform action to date, and it comes after months of pressure from supporters who questioned the pace of change in his first year in office. Tisch, whom Mamdani kept on from the previous administration, supports the changes.

What remains to be seen is how quickly the new civilian division can be built and staffed, and whether the council passes the CURB Act to lock the changes into law. For now, the administration says the restructuring will be carried out without additional funding or an increase in NYPD staffing.

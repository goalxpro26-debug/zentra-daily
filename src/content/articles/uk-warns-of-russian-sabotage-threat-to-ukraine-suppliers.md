Britain's defence ministry and its domestic intelligence service MI5 have warned companies providing direct support to Ukraine's military that they face a growing threat of sabotage from Russia, in an unusually public alert issued on Tuesday.

The warning came jointly from the Ministry of Defence and the National Protective Security Authority, the part of MI5 that advises businesses on security. It told firms to take action now to improve their defences against what it described as the risk posed by operatives acting for Russia.

"In recent months Russia has increased attempts to conduct sabotage against European defence-related facilities," the alert said. "This activity is typically characterised by the use of proxy actors employing low-sophistication sabotage methodologies, such as arson."

The warning applies broadly. Any organisation that supports military procurement or supplies equipment to Ukraine's armed forces could be a target, the alert said — including companies further down the supply chain. Firms were urged to reduce the chances that hostile actors can identify them or their people as targets, carry out reconnaissance, or attack their staff, sites and operations.

The advice is practical rather than dramatic. Companies were told to cut their digital footprint by reviewing publicly available information and removing unnecessary detail about their products, facilities and staff, since hostile reconnaissance usually begins with internet research. They were also urged to install visible protective security measures, train employees to recognise and report suspicious activity, and identify their most critical assets.

The government is holding closed-door threat briefings for chief executives from the defence sector and critical national infrastructure in Whitehall on Tuesday and Wednesday, bringing together senior intelligence officials and ministers.

Security Minister Dan Jarvis said the recent attempts across Europe showed how serious the threat had become. "It's truly shameful that actors are seeking to disrupt our way of life and that of our allies, especially targeting organisations doing vital work in support of Ukraine," he said.

Armed Forces Minister Louise Sandher-Jones said Britain's adversaries were not limiting themselves to military targets. "They are prepared to use cyberattacks, espionage, sabotage and hostile reconnaissance against the companies, technology and supply chains that underpin our national security," she said.

The alert follows a series of suspected Russian sabotage operations across Europe. Estonia has investigated suspected sabotage targeting Milrem Robotics, a maker of unmanned military systems used in Ukraine, and in November said it had foiled an alleged attack directed by Russian military intelligence against drone manufacturer Threod Systems. Last year, a group of British men were jailed for an arson attack on a Ukraine-linked business in London that UK authorities said was carried out at Moscow's behest.

There was no immediate comment from Russia's embassy in London. The alert did not name specific companies or plots, and no details of individual incidents were published.

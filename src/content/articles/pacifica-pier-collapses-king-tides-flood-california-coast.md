A section of the historic Pacifica Municipal Pier collapsed into the Pacific Ocean on Wednesday, as record king tides and waves of 20 to 30 feet battered California's coast, breaching seawalls and flooding homes from the San Francisco Bay Area to Orange County.

The damage was worst in Pacifica, just south of San Francisco, where relentless waves sent seawater streaming down several city blocks. A massive swell struck the perpendicular end of the pier's L-shaped section, and its vertical supports suddenly buckled. The pier, built in 1973, had been closed since June, when inspectors found cracks running through the landmark structure. The same section had come down in earlier storms, so officials said the collapse was not entirely unexpected.

Waves overtopped the seawall along Beach Boulevard, turning the street into what one resident described as a river. Floodwater reached knee-high on some streets by midday, pouring over sandbag lines and into homes in the Sharp Park neighbourhood. Pumps meant to clear the water were overwhelmed, an evacuation warning was issued, and several sections of the seawall were damaged.

The tides were driven by a rare combination: a historic El Niño, record-hot ocean temperatures and strong swells rolling in from the west. "This El Niño is unprecedented with its strength," said San Mateo County Supervisor Ray Mueller. "For this to be happening when it is a beautiful day on the coastside today, in clear sunshine and no wind, is really significant."

Tide gauges set or tied records along the coast: La Jolla hit 8.11 feet, beating the 7.98-foot record set on October 27; Santa Barbara reached 8.21 feet, breaking its old record of 8.04 feet; San Pedro tied its record at 8.19 feet; and Monterey posted a record 8.02 feet, breaking a mark that had stood since January 27, 1983. San Francisco peaked at 8.21 feet, just short of Tuesday's 8.23 feet.

The flooding stretched well beyond Pacifica. In Orange County, several homes flooded in Sunset Beach after tides of 8.3 feet deluged the coastline, breaching seawalls and docks. Evacuation warnings went out for beachfront homes in Stinson Beach, a shelter-in-place order was issued for the Pajaro Dunes resort, and waves flooded streets in Capitola village. Monterey County closed multiple beaches and warned waves could reach 50 feet.

California Governor Gavin Newsom called the damage in Pacifica "heartbreaking" and warned it was a sign of what lies ahead. "This is a preview of things to come," he said. "This is anomalous today. This is normal tomorrow."

The danger is not over. Forecasters warned of more extreme coastal flooding through Thanksgiving Day and into Friday, and urged residents to stay away from the shoreline and never drive through standing water. Another round of king tides is expected around Christmas and again on Martin Luther King Jr. Day — and officials say residents should prepare for worse if a storm arrives on top of the next high tides.

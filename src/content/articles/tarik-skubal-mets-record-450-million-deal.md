Tarik Skubal has agreed to a nine-year, $450 million contract with the New York Mets, the richest deal ever given to a pitcher, landing the two-time Cy Young Award winner in Queens just days before baseball's offseason is expected to shut down.

The agreement was reached on Sunday, November 22, according to multiple reports, and had not yet been formally announced. The $450 million total shatters the previous record for a pitcher, Yoshinobu Yamamoto's 12-year, $325 million contract with the Dodgers, and carries an average annual value of $50 million, second only to Skubal's new teammate Juan Soto among Mets players.

The terms are heavily front-loaded. Skubal will receive a $45 million signing bonus, giving him a large sum up front before a potential work stoppage, and $30 million of the contract is deferred. Reports say the deal contains no opt-out clauses and includes full no-trade protection.

Skubal, who turned 30 on November 20, has been the best pitcher in baseball over the past four seasons. He has a career 3.02 ERA and 1.02 WHIP, and has posted an ERA between 2.39 and 2.80 in each of the last four years, winning the American League Cy Young Award in 2024 and 2025 with the Detroit Tigers. His fastball-changeup combination has produced 10.9 strikeouts per nine innings over that span.

He arrives in New York after a whirlwind four months. Detroit traded him to the Dodgers at the trade deadline, and he helped Los Angeles reach the National League Championship Series before the Brewers swept them out. The Mets will be his third team since July.

The signing continues the Mets' runaway spending under owner Steve Cohen. New York will have four players, Soto, Skubal and infielders Francisco Lindor and Bo Bichette, earning between $32 million and $51 million next season. Yet the spending has not yet bought success: the Mets have missed the playoffs in both seasons since Soto's 15-year, $765 million arrival, finishing last in the National League East at 74-88 this year.

The timing was deliberate. A lockout is expected to shut down all offseason activity on December 2, and Skubal's deal was completed before the freeze. The size of the contract is also certain to feature in the coming labour fight, with owners expected to use the Mets' payroll as evidence in their push for a salary cap.

Skubal now becomes the centrepiece of a rotation the Mets have been trying to build around Soto's bat, and the highest-paid pitcher in the game's history.

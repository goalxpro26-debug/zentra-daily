At least 31 people were killed and 21 injured after a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on a major ring road near Cairo on Wednesday evening, Egypt's health ministry said.

The tanker, which was carrying petroleum products, crashed on the Ring Road in the Al-Warraq district of Giza, on the western edge of the capital. The fire broke out above a tunnel on the busy highway, which links the governorates of Cairo, Giza and Qalyubia.

State-owned media reported that a high-voltage cable fell in front of the tanker, causing the driver to lose control. The vehicle crossed the central reservation, overturned and caught fire. The tanker belonged to a private contractor, according to the reports.

Thirty-nine ambulances took part in the rescue operation, the health ministry said. Hospitals nearby treated patients suffering from burns and smoke inhalation. Emergency crews worked through the night at the scene, and the highway was later cleared and reopened.

Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar said the death toll could still rise. DNA testing was under way to identify victims, after the fire left many of those killed unrecognisable.

The crash is one of the deadliest road accidents in Egypt in recent years. The country's roads have a long record of serious crashes, driven by speeding, poor road conditions and lax enforcement — a problem the authorities have repeatedly pledged to tackle, with limited success.

Investigators from the public prosecution are examining the circumstances of the crash, including the fallen cable and the tanker's condition. No official cause has yet been confirmed beyond the initial reports of the cable falling into the road.

For the families of those killed, the wait for identification will be agonising. For Cairo's commuters, the fire was a stark reminder of how quickly an ordinary evening journey can turn catastrophic on one of the region's busiest highways.

A third American aircraft carrier has arrived in the Gulf, sharpening a nine-month standoff with Iran, as Tehran steps up threats against U.S. forces and shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

The USS Theodore Roosevelt entered the region on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington. Three carrier strike groups in one theatre is an unusually large concentration of American naval power, with more than 50,000 U.S. personnel now in the region, per The Wall Street Journal.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy warned shipping companies off the U.S.-backed southern route through the strait, calling it fully closed and a "minefield of fire." Army spokesperson Brig. Gen. Mohammad Akrami-Nia said the strait would not reopen until "the enemy accepts Iran's conditions."

## A tanker struck

On Tuesday, the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations agency said a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and subsequently caught fire. The agency did not say who was responsible and urged vessels to transit with caution. No casualties were reported. Security monitor Vanguard identified the vessel as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX, struck three nautical miles off Oman.

Separately, the Iranian navy said four oil tankers had struck mines and caught fire in the eastern strait over the previous 24 hours. Those claims could not be independently verified. Iran has also said it will not allow inspectors into its damaged nuclear sites until the conflict ends.

The escalation follows an Iranian drone attack on Sunday against a U.S. air-defence radar at Bahrain's Riffa air base — the second attack Iran has claimed against the kingdom in three days. Bahrain said it intercepted several of the attacks, and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned them on Monday.

CNN also reported new U.S. intelligence indicating Iran was developing plans that could target senior Trump administration officials with a so-called dirty bomb or a chemical weapon in Washington. CNBC said it could not immediately verify the report, and CNN characterised it as developing options rather than evidence of an imminent attack. The White House said there were "no credible threats to the homeland."

## Mixed signals from Washington

President Donald Trump has sent mixed signals, alternating between declaring the war could soon end and warning of renewed large-scale strikes if talks fail. The conflict began in late February, when the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran; indirect talks through Qatar have since appeared to stall.

Ian Bremmer of Eurasia Group said Iran was likely to keep disrupting shipping to pressure Washington into concessions, while Washington would probably expand its blockade of Iran's trade routes. Brent crude slipped 0.7% in Asian trading, to $91.8 a barrel.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet known who fired the projectile that struck the tanker, or whether the mine strikes claimed by Iran's navy took place as described — or whether the third carrier's deployment signals preparation for renewed strikes or deterrence while talks stall.

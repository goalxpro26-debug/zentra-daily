Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo, the trafficker known as the "Boss of Bosses" who built the Guadalajara cartel into the dominant force in Mexico's drug trade, has died in hospital in Guadalajara. He was 80.

Jalisco state security authorities said Félix Gallardo was transferred to a private hospital in the city on November 13 and died of multiple organ failure at around 2 p.m. on Sunday, November 15. Prison police had secured the hospital where he was admitted earlier in the week.

Born in 1946 in Culiacán, Sinaloa, Félix Gallardo worked as a police officer and a bodyguard for a local governor before co-founding the Guadalajara cartel. Rising to prominence in the 1970s, he became one of the architects of Mexico's modern drug trade.

## An alliance that reshaped the trade

Félix Gallardo's defining move was to forge an alliance between Mexican trafficking groups and the Colombian cartels, above all Pablo Escobar's Medellín cartel. The partnership combined cocaine production in the Andes with Mexican transport routes across the border into the United States.

At one point, an estimated 80 percent of all cocaine entering the United States came from the Medellín cartel, and Félix Gallardo's organisation played a major role in smuggling it in. At its height, the Guadalajara cartel's influence reached deep into Mexican politics and law enforcement, controlling entire police networks and state governorships, according to the BBC.

The cartel's power began to unravel after the 1985 torture and murder of Enrique "Kiki" Camarena, an agent of the US Drug Enforcement Administration. Camarena had angered the cartel after discovering and destroying a vast marijuana plantation in Chihuahua. His death set off one of the biggest manhunts the US government has ever launched in North America.

## Arrest, prison and a lasting legacy

Félix Gallardo was captured in 1989 and sentenced to decades in prison. He remained in state custody from then on, and in ill health he was placed under house arrest in 2022. A federal court later imposed a further 37-year sentence in connection with the Camarena case.

His arrest broke up the Guadalajara cartel, but the organisation he built did not disappear. In the 1980s he had introduced Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán to the trade and put him in charge of handling logistics. The Sinaloa cartel emerged from the break-up of Félix Gallardo's organisation, led by Guzmán, who was sentenced to life in prison in the United States in 2019.

His story later reached a wide audience when actor Diego Luna portrayed him in the Netflix series Narcos: Mexico. The BBC's Will Grant wrote that his name "will echo through the annals of drug-trafficking history more than any other except perhaps Pablo Escobar's."

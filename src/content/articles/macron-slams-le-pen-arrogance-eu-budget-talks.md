French President Emmanuel Macron publicly rebuked far-right leader Marine Le Pen on Thursday, accusing her of "arrogance" for acting as though she already leads France, after she attacked efforts to settle the European Union's next long-term budget before the country's 2027 presidential election.

"It takes a peculiar kind of arrogance to think she's already in charge of France's destiny. Last I checked, she wasn't elected," Macron told reporters on the sidelines of an EU summit in Brussels.

The exchange came as EU leaders ended more than 12 hours of talks without bridging deep divisions over the bloc's 2028–2034 budget. Leaders agreed only that reaching a deal by the end of the year would be an uphill battle, and they will meet again on December 17.

Earlier on Thursday, Le Pen — the frontrunner to succeed Macron next spring — accused the European Commission and Macron of trying to rush a budget deal through before the French vote. "The apparent aim is to deter us from defending France's interests in EU budget negotiations if the French people entrust us with the responsibility of governing," she wrote in a post on X, calling the push "underhanded political manoeuvring to thwart the democratic will of the French people."

Macron, whose term runs until May 13, 2027, said he was taking the election timetable into account. If no deal is reached by December 31, he said, negotiations would have to resume in the second half of 2027, under his successor. "You have to be more humble when you're campaigning, even if you already see yourself in the job," he said.

It was an unusual step for Macron, who typically avoids commenting on domestic French politics while meeting fellow EU leaders in Brussels. It was also the second time in about a month that he has publicly told Le Pen to be humble: last month he used the same word after she set out plans to cut France's aid to Ukraine and its contribution to the EU.

The budget fight itself remains wide open. Leaders have spent most of 2026 arguing over the size of the long-term budget after the European Commission proposed a spending plan of €2 trillion, which most capitals rejected. European Council chief António Costa's latest compromise would cut the budget by €204 billion and introduce a tax on digital companies. France wants more money for farmers and argues Europe needs a bigger budget to meet its growing challenges; Macron called cutting it a "strategic error".

The talks were further complicated after German Chancellor Friedrich Merz linked any budget deal to agreement by all 27 EU countries on funding for Ukraine. Member states are being asked to back an extra €45 billion loan to keep Ukraine funded through 2027, but the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia want to opt out. Merz said that was unacceptable, insisting aid for Ukraine "must be borne by the entire European Union".

With France's election approaching, the prospect of a Le Pen victory hung over the summit. "The prospect of a potential Le Pen victory — and its consequences — is always in the back of everyone's mind," one EU diplomat said.

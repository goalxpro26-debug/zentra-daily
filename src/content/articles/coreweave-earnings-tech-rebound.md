Technology stocks steadied on Wednesday after CoreWeave, the AI data-centre company at the centre of recent market anxiety, reported quarterly results that beat expectations and raised its outlook for the year. Nasdaq 100 futures climbed 0.8 percent, S&P 500 futures rose 0.4 percent and Dow futures were higher by 33 points, or about 0.1 percent, according to CNBC's live market coverage.

The rebound followed a rough Tuesday on Wall Street. All three major averages fell more than 1 percent: the Dow dropped 553 points, or 1.2 percent, the S&P 500 fell 1.1 percent and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.6 percent. The trigger was hotter-than-expected September inflation data, which raised doubts about whether the Federal Reserve will be able to cut interest rates as quickly as investors had hoped.

CoreWeave's numbers gave the market something firmer to hold on to. The company reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168 percent from about $1.36 billion a year earlier and ahead of the $3.55 billion analysts had expected. Its adjusted loss narrowed to 95 cents a share, better than the $1.16 loss Wall Street had forecast, although the company still posted a net loss of $709 million, or $1.27 a share.

## A raised outlook

The company also lifted its guidance for the full year. It now expects 2026 revenue of $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion, up from $12.4 billion to $13.2 billion in August, and adjusted operating income of $1.275 billion to $1.375 billion. Its order backlog reached about $133 billion, more than double the level a year ago, according to market reporting of the results. CoreWeave shares advanced in extended trading after the report.

The relief spread beyond the United States. In Asia, Japan's Nikkei rose 1.6 percent, South Korea's Kospi gained 1.2 percent and Hong Kong's Hang Seng climbed 1.8 percent. Europe's Stoxx 600 was up 0.6 percent.

## Why this report mattered

CoreWeave has become a bellwether for the AI infrastructure boom — and for the doubts about it. The company rents out computing power for training artificial-intelligence models, and its heavy borrowing to build data centres made it a focal point when investors began questioning whether AI spending would pay off. Those doubts helped drive last week's sell-off, which pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory and erased trillions of dollars in market value.

A single earnings report does not settle that debate. CoreWeave remains loss-making, its debt load is still large, and the broader market still faces the same questions about inflation and interest rates that sank stocks on Tuesday. But the beat-and-raise gave investors evidence that demand for AI computing is still growing, and for one morning at least, that was enough to steady the trade.

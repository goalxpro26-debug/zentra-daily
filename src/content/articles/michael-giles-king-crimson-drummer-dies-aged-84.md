Michael Giles, the drummer and co-founder whose playing powered King Crimson's landmark debut and helped define progressive rock, has died aged 84.

Giles died on Monday, November 23, his record label Discipline Global Mobile (DGM) announced on Wednesday. No cause of death was disclosed. In a statement, the label called him a "visionary drummer, composer and co-founder of King Crimson" and praised his "extraordinary rhythmic imagination, musical sensitivity and highly inventive drumming."

Born on March 1, 1942, in Winton, Bournemouth, Giles began playing with guitarist Robert Fripp in 1967, forming the trio Giles, Giles and Fripp with his brother Peter. The following year, the group evolved into King Crimson, with Fripp, singer and bassist Greg Lake, multi-instrumentalist Ian McDonald and lyricist Peter Sinfield completing the original lineup.

The band's debut album, *In the Court of the Crimson King*, released in October 1969, became one of the defining records of progressive rock. Giles's drumming drove its opening track, "21st Century Schizoid Man," a jagged, jazz-inflected performance that set a new standard for what a rock drummer could do. The album reached the top five in Britain and has rarely left lists of the most influential records of its era.

Giles left King Crimson in 1970, shortly after the debut, but returned as a guest drummer on the follow-up, *In the Wake of Poseidon*. He went on to record the album *McDonald and Giles* with Ian McDonald in 1971, worked widely as a session musician, and later played with the 21st Century Schizoid Band, a group formed by former King Crimson members to revisit the band's early material.

His influence outlasted his brief time in the band. The precision and swing of his playing on those first two King Crimson records became a reference point for generations of progressive-rock drummers, and his work is still studied by musicians drawn to the band's early sound.

Fripp is now the only surviving member of King Crimson's original lineup. Lake died in 2016, McDonald in 2022 and Sinfield in 2024.

Tributes from fellow musicians and fans have focused on the same quality: a drummer who could be both fiercely powerful and remarkably musical, and whose playing helped turn a debut album into a genre's founding document.

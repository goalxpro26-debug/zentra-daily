Andy Taylor, the guitarist whose riffs helped make Duran Duran one of the defining bands of the 1980s, has died at the age of 65.

Taylor died at home on Monday, surrounded by his family, after living with metastatic prostate cancer. His death was announced on Wednesday in a joint statement from his family and his former bandmates.

Taylor was diagnosed with the disease in 2018. He went public with it four years later, when his health prevented him from attending Duran Duran's induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.

Born in 1961 in Tynemouth and raised in Cullercoats, Northumberland, Taylor picked up the guitar at the age of 11 and left school to tour England and Europe with a series of bands. He moved to Birmingham and joined Duran Duran in 1980, a year before the group released its debut album. Global fame followed with 1982's *Rio*, and Taylor co-wrote some of the band's biggest hits, including "Hungry Like the Wolf", "Rio" and "Girls on Film", as well as "The Reflex".

His guitar work — muscular, melodic and unmistakably his own — gave the band's polished pop a harder edge, and helped carry Duran Duran from the Birmingham club scene to stadiums and the early days of MTV. The group went on to win two Grammy Awards, and Taylor was later inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with his bandmates.

Away from Duran Duran, Taylor recorded as a solo artist, releasing his debut album *Thunder* in 1987, and played in the supergroup The Power Station alongside Duran Duran bassist John Taylor and singer Robert Palmer.

In the family statement, his wife, Tracy Wilson-Taylor, said: "To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather."

The band's surviving members — Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes, John Taylor and Roger Taylor — said Taylor had been there "at the very beginning of our story", arriving in Birmingham from the north-east "with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become". He brought, they said, "an indelible fire and swagger to our music", and faced his illness "with courage, honesty and grace". Le Bon added that there was now "a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart".

Tributes arrived quickly from across the music world, including from Sir Rod Stewart, who called Taylor a dear friend, and singer Belinda Carlisle. Taylor is survived by his wife, their four children and a grandson.

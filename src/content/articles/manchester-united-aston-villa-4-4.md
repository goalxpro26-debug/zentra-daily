Manchester United twice came from two goals down to salvage a remarkable 4-4 draw against Aston Villa on Sunday, November 8, and potentially save manager Michael Carrick's job. Goals in stoppage time from Joshua Zirkzee and Patrick Dorgu rescued a point — but United will spend the two-week international break in the relegation zone.

It was, by any measure, a wild game. United conceded twice inside the opening four minutes as Alysson and Nicolas Jackson punished errors from goalkeeper Senne Lammens. At that point, with the team already in the bottom three, the afternoon looked like it might become a rout. Instead, United rallied to level at half-time thanks to a Bruno Fernandes penalty and Youri Tielemans's first goal since moving to Old Trafford from Villa.

The second half brought more of the same: Villa pulling clear, United dragging themselves back. And then, in stoppage time, Zirkzee and Dorgu scored the goals that turned a defeat into a draw. It was the kind of finish that keeps a manager in a job for another fortnight — and the kind that also tells you how deep the problems run, because a team that needs two stoppage-time goals to draw at home is a team in trouble.

## A season in the bottom three

The draw keeps United in the bottom three with just eight points from 10 games. Saturday's results had seen them slip into the relegation zone after August for the first time in 40 years — a statistic that captures, in a single line, how far the club has fallen from the standards it set for itself over decades.

Eight points from 10 games is relegation form by any measure. It is not a blip, or a bad month, or a run of hard fixtures. It is a third of a season, and it has left one of the biggest clubs in world football fighting for survival rather than for trophies. The international break now gives Carrick two weeks to work with the players who are not away with their countries, and two weeks for the club's hierarchy to decide whether he is the manager to lead the fight.

Carrick arrived with goodwill and a brief to steady the club. A thrilling fightback from 2-0 down to beat Roma 3-2 in the Champions League on Tuesday had given him a lifeline, and the Villa draw may have given him another. But lifelines are not the same as a recovery, and the table does not care about spirit or fightbacks. It cares about points, and United do not have enough of them.

## The goalkeeper question

The opening four minutes will haunt Senne Lammens. Two goals conceded, both punished from errors by the goalkeeper, set the tone for the afternoon and put United in a hole before the game had properly begun. Goalkeepers live with the knowledge that their mistakes are the most visible on the pitch — a striker can miss three chances and score the fourth, but a goalkeeper's error is a goal. Lammens will need the international break as much as anyone.

The broader defensive picture is no better. A team that concedes four goals at home, even in a game it draws, has a defensive problem that no amount of attacking spirit can cover up indefinitely. United scored four and still could not win. That is the sentence that should worry Carrick most.

## What the draw does — and does not — change

The draw does not change United's league position. It does not change their points total in any meaningful way — one point instead of none, when the gap to safety is already a concern. What it changes is the mood, and the time Carrick has. A defeat, after the opening four minutes, would have made his position very difficult to defend. A draw, achieved the way it was, gives the club a reason to wait until after the international break before making a decision.

That is how football works at a club in crisis: the manager survives one game at a time, and each survival is presented as a turning point until the day it isn't. Whether the Villa draw is a turning point or merely a postponement will be decided by the games after the break, not by the stoppage-time goals themselves.

For Aston Villa, the draw will feel like two points dropped. They led twice by two goals, and they led in stoppage time. To leave Old Trafford with a point from a game they had won twice is the kind of result that stays with a team. But Villa, unlike United, are not in a crisis. They can afford to be frustrated. United cannot afford anything at all — except, for now, the hope that a 4-4 draw is the start of something rather than the latest chapter in a season that has already made unwanted history.

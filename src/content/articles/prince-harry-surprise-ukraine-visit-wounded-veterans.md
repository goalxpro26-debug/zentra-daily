Britain's Prince Harry made an unannounced weekend visit to Ukraine, meeting wounded veterans and medics in the western city of Lviv and telling them they would win the war against Russia.

The Duke of Sussex, 42, arrived on Saturday for a two-day trip that was kept secret until he had left the country for security reasons. It was his fourth visit to Ukraine since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022, and his second this year.

In Lviv, Harry visited the Superhumans Center, a specialist clinic that treats wounded military personnel and civilians, many of whom need prosthetic limbs. He also spoke at a conference for medics organised by the clinic, where he praised the work being done for injured soldiers and acknowledged the risk of travelling to a country at war.

"You're going to win the war and then you're going to be set up for success," he told the conference on Saturday, adding that he had "taken a risk to come here."

## Trials for the Invictus Games

Harry also attended Ukraine's National Invictus Games trials, watching wheelchair rugby and pickleball players compete for a place on the team for next year's games in Birmingham, England. Around 500 athletes registered to compete for the 60-member squad — the largest team Ukraine has ever planned to send to the event.

Harry founded the Invictus Games in 2014 to support wounded, injured and recovering military personnel through adaptive sport. He told veterans in Lviv that when the war is over and Ukraine wins, he would be ready to support holding an Invictus Games in the country.

During the trials he met competitors including Valery Odainyk, a leg amputee and still-serving soldier who competed at the games in Canada last year, and Kostiantyn Vlasenko, who was the first Ukrainian soldier airlifted by drone from the front lines.

## A growing veteran population

Ukraine has about 1.4 million combat veterans, more than 10 percent of whom have disabilities, according to figures cited during the visit. Lviv, close to the Polish border, has become a hub for the medical treatment and rehabilitation of wounded soldiers.

Harry, a British Army veteran who served in Afghanistan, described the wounded veterans he met as role models and said they must continue to receive support from Kyiv. His visit wrapped up on Sunday, with the details of his movements only reported after he had departed.

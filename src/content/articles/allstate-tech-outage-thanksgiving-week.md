Allstate's technology outage entered its fifth day on Friday, leaving customers across the United States unable to use the insurer's website and mobile app during one of the busiest travel weeks of the year.

The disruption began on Monday morning, according to user reports tracked by Downdetector, and has kept policyholders from accessing their accounts, reviewing policy information, paying bills and filing claims. People seeking roadside assistance through Allstate's website were still hitting an error message on Friday, CBS News reported.

## What customers cannot do

The outage has struck at the heart of Allstate's digital services. Customers have reported being locked out of online accounts and unable to complete routine tasks — checking coverage, paying a premium or submitting a claim — just as millions of Americans took to the roads for Thanksgiving. Local agents have been fielding calls and emails from customers who cannot get through online.

Allstate, based in Northbrook, Illinois, acknowledged the problem on Wednesday, after customers had spent days complaining on social media and on Downdetector. In a statement, the company said some access had been restricted over the previous 48 hours, but that it continued to respond to and settle claims.

"To the extent service interruptions impact a customer's ability to make a payment, they won't lose coverage or be charged late fees," the company said. It added that customers could still reach it by phone and through local offices.

## No cause, no fix date

As of Friday, Allstate had not publicly disclosed what caused the outage or said when full service would be restored. Its website told customers trying to log in that services were unavailable and that the company was "working to restore access as soon as possible."

The timing is awkward for the insurer: Thanksgiving is one of the heaviest driving periods of the year, when accidents rise and drivers are most likely to need roadside help. Allstate reported about 26.2 million auto insurance policy items and 1.65 million roadside memberships in its third-quarter figures.

## Part of a wider run of outages

Allstate's failure is the latest in a string of technology disruptions to hit major American services in recent weeks, including Microsoft's Outlook and Teams, airlines, Apple Pay and several banks. Trump administration officials have said the incidents are not cyberattacks, but the result of companies patching vulnerabilities and upgrading systems that run on older technology.

For Allstate customers, the practical advice for now is old-fashioned: call the company or visit a local agent. Whether the digital services return before the holiday travel rush ends remains an open question.

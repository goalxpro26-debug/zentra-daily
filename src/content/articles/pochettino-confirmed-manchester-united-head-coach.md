Manchester United have confirmed Mauricio Pochettino as their new head coach, ending a week of speculation over who would succeed the sacked Michael Carrick.

The 54-year-old Argentine has signed a two-and-a-half-year deal and will officially take charge on Wednesday, November 18. He will first complete his final duties with the United States men's national team, coaching their two Concacaf Nations League quarter-final ties against Haiti on Saturday and Tuesday, before crossing the Atlantic.

His first match in charge will be one of the biggest in the English calendar: a Premier League trip to Liverpool on Sunday, November 22.

## A club in crisis

Pochettino inherits a side in deep trouble. United sacked Carrick on Thursday after the club's worst start to a league campaign in four decades, with one win and eight points from their opening ten games leaving them in the relegation zone.

The club said it had reached an agreement with the U.S. Soccer Federation for Pochettino and his staff to be released after their national-team commitments. In its announcement, United described its new head coach as "renowned for his high-intensity, attacking style of football, his dedication to building strong, cohesive teams and his outstanding record of developing individual players."

According to The Times, the appointment was recommended by director of football Jason Wilcox and chief executive Omar Berrada, and approved by minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe. British media reported United will pay U.S. Soccer £7.5 million in compensation to release Pochettino from a contract that had been due to run until the 2030 World Cup.

## Return to the Premier League

Pochettino is no stranger to English football. He previously managed Southampton, Tottenham Hotspur and Chelsea before taking over the United States in 2024. With the U.S., he guided the World Cup co-hosts to the last 16 on home soil earlier this year before their campaign ended in defeat by Belgium.

He was the only coach United spoke to directly about the job, according to the Press Association, and his appointment is understood to have the unanimous endorsement of the club's leadership and ownership.

United said details of the staff who will join Pochettino at Old Trafford will be confirmed in the coming days. His long-time assistant Jesus Perez is widely expected to be among them.

## What is still unknown

The full composition of Pochettino's backroom team has not been confirmed, and neither the club nor U.S. Soccer has disclosed the exact compensation figure officially. How quickly the Argentine can lift a squad short on confidence — starting with a trip to Anfield — will be the first real test of the appointment.

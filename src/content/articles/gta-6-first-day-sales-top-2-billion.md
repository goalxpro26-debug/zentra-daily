Grand Theft Auto VI has made an estimated $2.36 billion in its first day on sale, according to analytics firm Sensor Tower, putting Rockstar Games' long-awaited sequel on course to be the biggest video game launch in history.

Sensor Tower estimates that 24.58 million copies were sold by Thursday evening, with the total still climbing as players rushed to download the game after it went live at midnight. The figure dwarfs the record set by its predecessor: Grand Theft Auto V sold about $800 million worth of copies on its first day in 2013, widely regarded as the best-selling video game in its first 24 hours.

The numbers make GTA 6 the fastest-selling entertainment release ever measured on day one, comfortably ahead of the biggest film openings. Much of the total came from pre-orders, which Take-Two Interactive, the game's publisher, has described as unprecedented.

Take-Two has remained tight-lipped about demand since pre-orders opened in June, limiting its public comments to saying demand has been "unprecedented" or "far exceeding expectations". Chief executive Strauss Zelnick struck a cautious note on the company's earnings call earlier in November, saying: "The title is so widely anticipated, consumers appear very, very excited, and all the press so far has been universally positive, but we still aren't ready to declare victory."

"We tend to avoid claiming success before it occurs," he added.

The launch is the payoff for one of the most expensive entertainment projects ever made. Take-Two is estimated to have spent $1 billion or more on producing GTA 6. Its marketing campaign was equally sprawling: Sensor Tower estimates that the game's promotional trailer, "Extended Look", released on Netflix in August, helped drive a sixfold jump in pre-orders. In Miami, the city Vice City is based on, the local NBA team celebrated the release with a Vice City-themed match, and the company is hosting a launch-weekend music festival featuring Cardi B, Metro Boomin and Travis Scott.

The figures come with an important caveat. They are third-party estimates, not official sales data: neither Rockstar nor Take-Two has released its own numbers, and the totals do not account for refunds.

Attention now turns to what comes next. Investors have been waiting for Take-Two's plans for a PC version of GTA 6 and the rollout of its online multiplayer mode, which analysts expect in 2027. The online component matters most for the long term: it turns a one-off purchase into a recurring revenue engine, and brokerage TD Cowen has named Take-Two one of its "Best Ideas" for 2027 on that basis.

For now, though, the first-day numbers alone put GTA 6 in a league of its own — a launch thirteen years in the making, and one that has already rewritten the record books.

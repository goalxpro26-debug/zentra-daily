Russian and Ukrainian negotiators are set to meet in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday for two days of U.S.-brokered talks, their first direct meeting since February, as Washington signals it is open to a wider agreement with Moscow on European security — a shift that European officials see as an early concession to Russia.

The talks are scheduled to begin at around 6:30 a.m. GMT on Tuesday, November 10, and run for two days, according to officials in Kyiv. Security guarantees are expected to top the agenda, with both sides still far apart on the core questions of territory and the shape of any deal.

## A broader deal on the table

The Trump administration believes the only way to end the war is to reach a broader agreement with Russia on Europe's future security arrangements at the same time, U.S. officials said on Monday — accepting a long-standing Kremlin demand the United States and its NATO allies had previously resisted. European officials and experts worry the American call for a wider deal echoes Kremlin talking points.

President Donald Trump raised the prospect himself last week after meeting Russian President Vladimir Putin's envoy, Kirill Dmitriev, at the White House on November 4. Trump said a broader deal was being discussed. "It's going to be a big agreement, and it's going to involve more than Ukraine," he said, adding that it might involve all of Europe. On Sunday, he told "60 Minutes" the two sides were "very close" to a deal.

## Who is at the table — and who is not

Russia's delegation will again be headed by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky. It will include Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov for the first time, along with senior military officer Yuri Zhigarlovsky. The Ukrainian delegation is headed by Rustem Umerov.

France, Germany and the European Commission are sending officials to Abu Dhabi, but Russia has made clear it does not want the Europeans at the negotiating table. "No one is going to invite them to the negotiating table," Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov said. Ukrainian delegates will instead meet separately to brief their European allies on how the discussions are going.

## What is still unresolved

The two sides remain far apart. Putin wants Ukraine to pull its troops back from the four regions Moscow illegally annexed early in the war but never fully captured, and has refused a general halt in hostilities. U.S. negotiators believe both leaders may be willing to compromise on territory, a U.S. official told Reuters. Under the proposed deal, Ukraine would get an end to the war, security guarantees and a roadmap to European Union membership.

No details of the broader European security agreement Trump described have been made public, and it is not clear what form it would take or whether European governments would accept it. Previous rounds of talks early this year produced no visible progress, and fighting has continued along the front line.

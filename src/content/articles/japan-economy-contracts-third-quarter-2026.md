Japan's economy shrank in the July–September quarter, government data showed on Monday, ending a year of growth as households pulled back on spending while prices stayed high.

Gross domestic product contracted at an annualised rate of 0.1%, the first decline in four quarters. The reading missed the median market forecast of 0.3% annualised growth in a Reuters poll, and followed a revised 1.7% expansion in the previous quarter. On a quarterly basis, the economy shrank 0.02%, against expectations of a 0.1% rise.

The weakness came from consumers. Private consumption, which accounts for more than half of Japan's economic output, fell 0.3% — a sharper drop than the 0.2% decline economists had expected. Household spending was squeezed by persistent price pressures and disrupted by poor weather, including heavy rain and typhoons during the quarter. Demand for cars and air conditioners also faded after a temporary boost in the previous quarter from tax and regulatory changes.

## Business investment held up

Not everything in the report pointed down. Capital spending, a key driver of private demand, rose 1.6%, slightly above the 1.5% forecast, supported by investment in labour-saving technology and artificial-intelligence-related projects. Policymakers are unlikely to read the contraction as a break in Japan's moderate recovery, with resilient corporate activity and rising real wages still underpinning growth.

Trade was the other drag. Net external demand — exports minus imports — knocked 0.6 percentage point off growth, worse than the 0.4-point drag economists had expected. Imports rose sharply as oil and other energy purchases recovered after Japan diversified its procurement following disruptions linked to the war in the Middle East.

## What it means for the Bank of Japan

The data will be closely watched by the Bank of Japan as it judges whether the economy can withstand further interest-rate increases. With stubbornly high energy costs and rising import prices from a weak yen adding to inflation, the central bank has tightened policy this year, raising rates in June and September.

Economists still expect only modest growth ahead. A monthly survey by the Japan Center for Economic Research of 37 economists put growth at 0.89% in fiscal 2026 and 1.02% in fiscal 2027. Longer-term challenges remain in focus too: the International Monetary Fund projects Japan will slip to the world's sixth-largest economy in nominal dollar terms in 2027, overtaken by India and Britain. Japan is currently the world's fourth-largest economy, after Germany.

Six months after Ebola was declared in the Democratic Republic of Congo, the outbreak is still out of control, Médecins Sans Frontières warned on Thursday, with cases going undetected, hospitals in some areas spreading the virus, and front-line workers left without pay, fuel or basic equipment.

MSF's international president, Javid Abdelmoneim, said staff were "often left speechless by dysfunctions" in the response, and called for urgent improvements in oversight, staffing and operational support. The organisation said the resources mobilised for the outbreak were not reaching the patients, communities and medics who need them.

The numbers underline the warning. More than 11,000 confirmed cases have been recorded, with over 5,400 deaths, according to Congo's National Institute of Public Health. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo species of the virus — for which there are no approved treatments or vaccines — is the second-largest ever recorded, behind only the 2014–16 West African epidemic.

But the case count is only part of the picture. Epidemiologists tracking the outbreak believe only about half of infections are being detected. MSF's emergency coordinator, Anna Halford, said most cases arriving at treatment centres were "surprise cases" with no known link to another infection — a sign, she said, that contact tracing is not keeping up with the spread.

"While that remains the situation, it is an absolute sign that we do not have this under control," Halford said. "You should not have the majority of your cases coming out of nowhere."

## Money that never arrived

Figures cited from the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention indicate that of $962 million passed on to organisations dealing with Ebola, $700 million was unaccounted for. On the ground, MSF describes surveillance teams with motorbikes but no fuel, community awareness workers who have not been paid, and health workers without supplies or technical support.

The outbreak began in the eastern provinces of North Kivu and Ituri and has since spread to eight provinces, five of which are still active. The World Health Organization declared it a public health emergency in May.

## Conflict compounds the crisis

Insecurity has become one of the biggest obstacles to containing the virus. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus acknowledged delays in paying health workers this week and pointed to attacks on health facilities. An attack in the town of Cantine-Aloya in North Kivu last week killed 66 civilians, including three health workers.

Despite the failures, MSF said there had been progress — better information flow, new diagnostic facilities and stronger community engagement. The organisation employs nearly 1,500 staff in the country, more than eight in ten of them Congolese.

For the wider world, the risk remains low: Ebola is not airborne and is not highly contagious. But inside Congo, the spread is slowly growing rather than slowing — and six months in, the response is still catching up.

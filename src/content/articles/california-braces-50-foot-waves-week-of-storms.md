California is bracing for a week of storms that will bring the season's first prolonged stretch of rain, wind, snow and towering surf, with waves of up to 50 feet expected along parts of the coast this weekend.

The storms are arriving as the waters off California are unusually warm and El Niño continues to strengthen, a combination forecasters say is pushing tides higher than normal and making the surf hit the shore harder.

## Towering surf this weekend

The National Weather Service has issued a high surf warning for the Bay Area coast from 6 p.m. Friday, November 13, to 4 a.m. Sunday, November 15, with breaking waves of 25 to 35 feet and sets of 40 to 50 feet at favoured spots. The warning covers nearly all of the Northern and Central California coast and much of the Oregon coast.

Waves at Ocean Beach in San Francisco could top 40 feet, and at Mavericks, the famed surf break about 20 miles south of the city, they are expected to reach 60 feet or higher. Beaches in Central California could see waves of 25 feet, while Los Angeles and San Diego are forecast to get waves of eight to 12 feet.

Forecasters warned of dangerous conditions at beaches across the state, especially in Northern California, where an unexpectedly large wave can pull a person standing on shore into turbulent water. Sneaker waves and rip currents are expected through the weekend, and a coastal flood advisory covers the Bay Area coast from midnight Friday night to 6 p.m. Saturday, with waves topping seawalls and flooding low-lying roads.

## Rain and Sierra snow

The first storm will bring rain up and down the state on Friday, arriving in the North Bay as early as late Thursday night. The heaviest rain is likely in the North Coast region, where up to six inches could fall over the next week. The San Francisco Bay Area could record up to two inches, while Los Angeles is likely to get less than an inch.

The storms are also expected to deliver the first significant snow of the season in the Sierra Nevada, with half a foot to more than a foot expected through Sunday morning. The Weather Service issued winter storm warnings across the mountain range, including for the Tahoe area, and warned of travel delays on mountain roads through the weekend.

## More storms on the way

After a brief break, a second storm is expected to move across the state between Sunday and Tuesday, with the rain again focused over Northern California. A third storm is likely next week, though forecasters said on Thursday it was not yet clear when it would arrive.

The flooding risk from the rain is predicted to be low. The storms will replenish reservoirs and rivers and soak the landscape enough to reduce the threat of wildfires, experts said.

The House of Representatives voted 211–204 on Tuesday evening to send Rep. Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, effectively ending the effort for the remainder of this Congress.

The referral means there will be no floor vote on whether to impeach Hegseth. The articles now sit with the Republican-led Judiciary Committee, which is not expected to take them up before the current Congress ends on January 3, when they will formally expire. Democrats would need to start over with a new resolution if they want to pursue the matter next year.

Massie, a Kentucky Republican, was the only member of his party to vote against sending the articles to committee. He had reintroduced the eight-article resolution on Monday as a privileged resolution, a procedural move that forced House leaders to act on it within two legislative days.

## What the articles alleged

The resolution accused Hegseth of waging war in Iran without congressional approval, in violation of the War Powers Resolution, and of continuing the campaign even after both chambers of Congress passed a measure directing U.S. forces to withdraw. The remaining articles covered lethal U.S. strikes on suspected drug-trafficking boats in the Caribbean, military action in Yemen, and the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in a military raid.

The push came a week after Republicans suffered heavy losses in the midterm elections, with some in the party blaming Hegseth's handling of the Iran war. Ahead of the vote, Axios reported that House Republican leaders expected enough defections from lawmakers with little left to lose to make the outcome uncertain. In the end, the party held together, with Massie alone breaking ranks.

## Hegseth and the White House pushed back

Hegseth dismissed the effort as a "farce" and a "political spectacle" earlier on Tuesday, and the Trump administration issued a statement opposing the impeachment push shortly before the vote. Vice President JD Vance weighed in on X, calling the question of whether Hegseth had committed impeachable crimes "absurd." President Donald Trump celebrated the result on Truth Social after the vote, saying the attempt had failed and praising every Republican except Massie.

## Why it matters

The vote spares Hegseth an impeachment vote for now, but the reprieve is likely short-lived. Democrats won majorities in both chambers in the midterm elections and take control in January, and they are widely expected to revive the impeachment effort in the new Congress. Even if the House were to impeach Hegseth then, removing him would require a two-thirds vote in the Senate — a much higher bar.

Only two cabinet secretaries have ever been impeached by the House in American history, and neither was convicted by the Senate. If Hegseth were impeached, he would become the third.

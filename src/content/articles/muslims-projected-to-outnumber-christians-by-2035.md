Islam is on course to become the world's largest religion by 2035, according to new projections from the Pew Research Center, ending centuries in which Christianity has held that position. The shift is expected to happen within a decade — but it is not a story of one faith shrinking while another grows. Both are getting bigger; one is simply growing faster.

Pew projects that by 2035 there will be about 2.57 billion Muslims worldwide, compared with 2.52 billion Christians. In 2020, the starting point for the study, the figures were roughly 2.02 billion Muslims and 2.27 billion Christians. Looking further ahead, the gap widens: if recent trends continue, there will be more than 3.6 billion Muslims and just under 3 billion Christians by 2070. Muslims would rise from 26 percent of the world's population in 2020 to 36 percent, while Christians would hold fairly steady at 29 percent.

The main driver is births, not conversion. Muslims have the highest fertility rate of any major religious group, at 3.1 children per woman in the 2020–2025 period, compared with 2.5 for Christian women and 1.6 for all other women combined. Muslims also tend to be younger on average, which means more women of childbearing age in the decades ahead.

The numbers behind that are stark. Between 2020 and 2070, Muslims are projected to gain 1.6 billion people through natural population change — births minus deaths — against an increase of 792 million for Christians. Religious switching plays a much smaller role: Pew expects Islam to lose a net 8 million people through switching over the period, and Christianity to lose a net 105 million, mostly people becoming religiously unaffiliated. Movement directly between the two faiths is expected to roughly cancel out.

Most of the growth will happen where each faith is already established. About three-quarters of the projected growth in the Muslim population is expected in the 53 countries where Muslims were already the majority in 2020. Pakistan's Muslim population is projected to double, from 227 million in 2020 to 461 million in 2070, making it home to the world's largest Muslim population. In the Democratic Republic of Congo, the Christian population is expected to roughly triple, from 92 million to 299 million.

Not every Christian population is growing, however. The United States, which had the world's largest Christian population in 2020 at 217 million, is expected to see the biggest decline, falling to 174 million by 2070, as deaths outnumber births among an older population and some people leave the faith.

Pew is careful to describe its figures as projections, not predictions. The study covers 201 countries and territories, accounting for 99.98 percent of the world's population, and assumes recent patterns of births, deaths, migration and switching continue. It does not model wars, epidemics, economic shocks or religious revivals, any of which could change the picture.

Pew's 2015 projections had the two religions reaching near parity only around 2050 — about fifteen years later than the new crossover date.

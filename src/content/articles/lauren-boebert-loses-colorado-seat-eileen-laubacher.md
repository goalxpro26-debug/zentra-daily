Democrat Eileen Laubacher defeated Rep. Lauren Boebert in Colorado's 4th Congressional District on Friday, the Associated Press projected, unseating a three-term Republican and one of Congress's most prominent MAGA voices in a seat Donald Trump carried by 18 points in 2024.

With nearly all votes counted, Laubacher, a retired Navy rear admiral, led Boebert 48.8% to 47.9% — a margin of roughly 3,400 votes out of more than 384,000 cast. She becomes the first Democrat to win the eastern Colorado seat since 2008.

Laubacher declared victory on Monday, days before the race was called, and was so confident of the outcome that she travelled to Washington to attend orientation for incoming members of Congress. Boebert, by contrast, has not conceded. She has said ballots remained uncounted, including military and overseas votes, and her campaign attorney, former Colorado Secretary of State Scott Gessler, said he expects the race to head to a recount "one way or another," citing what he called unusual voting patterns.

The margin is outside Colorado's threshold for an automatic recount, but state law allows Boebert to request one at her own expense. She has until November 25 to do so.

## A stunning upset in a deep-red seat

Boebert was first elected to Congress in 2020, representing western Colorado's 3rd District, and survived re-election in 2022 by just 546 votes. In late 2023 she switched to the more conservative 4th District, which had been in Republican hands since 2011, and won it in 2024. Trump endorsed her 2026 run, calling her "a MAGA Warrior" days before the election.

Her defeat is one of the most striking results of a midterm cycle shaping up as a sweeping Democratic wave — the Associated Press described it as one of the most lopsided elections in half a century. Colorado's House delegation will now favour Democrats 7–1, and Boebert is the third Colorado Republican to lose a re-election bid this cycle.

Boebert has said she would not run for the seat again in 2028 if she lost, and attributed her defeat to the financial backing Laubacher received. Laubacher, who served on President Joe Biden's National Security Council, is expected to join the new Congress when it convenes in January 2027.

Bahrain said on Sunday that its air defences intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks before they could reach their targets, in the first such interceptions by the kingdom since September.

The Bahraini military said the attacks were stopped before reaching their targets and accused Tehran of continuing a hostile approach towards the kingdom. The interceptions followed two warnings of potential threats that urged residents to seek shelter.

Tehran gave a different account. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had attacked a U.S. military base in Bahrain in retaliation for what it called hostile American actions, according to Iran's Fars news agency. Iranian state-run Press TV said the Guards launched six drones targeting U.S. air-defence radar at the Al-Rifaa helicopter base.

There was no immediate confirmation of that claim from U.S. or Bahraini authorities, and no casualty or damage figures were reported for Sunday's incident. Bahrain said the attacks were intercepted before reaching their targets.

Bahrain's interior ministry urged citizens and residents to remain calm and to move to the nearest safe location. The Gulf kingdom is home to the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet, which oversees American naval operations across the Middle East.

## A second incident in three days

It was the second reported Iranian attack on Bahrain in three days. Two days earlier, the Revolutionary Guards said they had struck a U.S. early-warning radar facility in the kingdom. The U.S. military denied that account, saying it had intercepted two Iranian drones without any damage.

The exchanges come amid uncertainty over efforts to end a conflict now in its ninth month. Diplomatic efforts have yet to produce a breakthrough, and sporadic attacks have continued despite a broader lull in fighting between the United States and Iran.

The war began with U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28 and then spread across the region, with Tehran launching missile and drone attacks on U.S. military bases in Gulf countries.

President Donald Trump said in recent days that he might hold off from attacking Iran if the United States faced no new provocations from Tehran or the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen, but that he would maintain a U.S. naval blockade, Reuters reported on Saturday.

## Fighting continues on a separate front

The Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition continued exchanging attacks over the weekend. The coalition said on Sunday it had intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles launched towards the Saudi capital, Riyadh, and three drones in the kingdom's oil-rich Eastern Province.

## What is still unknown

The central dispute remains unresolved: Tehran says it struck a U.S. base in Bahrain, while Bahrain says the attacks were intercepted and Washington has not confirmed any strike. Until U.S. or Bahraini officials give a fuller account, it is not possible to say whether any drone reached its target, or what, if anything, was damaged.

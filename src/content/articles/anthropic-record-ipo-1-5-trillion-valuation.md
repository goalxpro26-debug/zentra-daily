Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, has raised more than $107 billion in the largest initial public offering in history, valuing the five-year-old company at about $1.5 trillion. Its shares begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol ANT.

The company said on Wednesday that it sold 956 million shares at $83 each, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed to investors. Its seven co-founders sold a further 23.6 million shares. The public sale alone raised about $81.3 billion. A separate $26 billion private placement, with shares bought by companies including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, lifts the total to more than $107 billion.

The deal tops the previous record set by SpaceX, which raised $75 billion when it listed in June. On a fully diluted basis — counting stock options and restricted shares as well as shares outstanding — Anthropic is valued at about $1.5 trillion. Counting only shares outstanding, the figure is about $1.08 trillion.

The listing is the first by a frontier AI lab, a company building the most powerful general-purpose AI models. Anthropic was founded in 2021 by researchers who left OpenAI over concerns about AI safety, led by chief executive Dario Amodei. It launched Claude in early 2023, months after OpenAI released ChatGPT, and the two companies have competed closely ever since.

The financials show remarkable growth. Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate reached about $81 billion by the end of October, up $10 billion in a single month. Revenue in the first half of the year was $16.3 billion, up 1,300 percent on the same period a year earlier, and adjusted operating income was $1.6 billion, against a loss of $1.8 billion the year before.

But the debut also arrives amid doubts. Tech shares linked to the AI boom have been volatile, and the Nasdaq is down more than 11 percent since early October as investors question whether the vast spending on AI infrastructure will pay off. Analysts quoted by NPR called the listing a test of sentiment towards the AI trade. Anthropic's own prospectus runs to dozens of pages of risks, including the possibility that people could lose control of AI systems.

The company has also drawn political scrutiny. Earlier this year it clashed with the Pentagon over restrictions on how its models could be used by the military, and the Pentagon later designated it a "supply chain risk". Its chief executive has himself warned that frontier AI development should slow down — even as his company races ahead.

For now, investors have voted with their wallets. Whether the valuation holds will be decided from Thursday, when the shares start trading and the market gets its first chance to price the most expensive AI bet yet made.

A senior American soldier assigned to NATO who was arrested in Britain last week on suspicion of spying for Russia has been transferred to United States military custody, British police said on Tuesday.

The 41-year-old, identified by American officials as Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Tyner, was arrested by British counterterrorism police on November 10 near Gloucester in southwest England. He had been held in British police custody since then, and was handed to the US military over suspected national security and espionage allegations.

Tyner was based at Imjin Barracks near Gloucester, the headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, a 20-nation high-readiness force led by the British Army. He served there as a senior human resources officer. He joined the US Army in 2008 and had previously been deployed to Afghanistan, Qatar and Iraq.

He was arrested on suspicion of offences under Sections 1 and 3 of the UK's National Security Act 2023. Section 1 covers obtaining or disclosing protected information; Section 3 covers assisting a foreign intelligence service. British police had previously said the foreign state linked to the allegations was Russia.

Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing, said London had passed the investigation to its American partners "in line with existing international protocols", after an extensive investigation and discussions between British prosecutors and US officials.

"These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place," Taylor said. He added that British police would continue to support their US colleagues as they take the matter forward.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the officer was in US custody as a result of a joint espionage investigation involving the FBI's Philadelphia field office and its London legal attache, alongside UK Counter Terrorism Policing, MI5, UK Defence Counter Intelligence and the US Army Counterintelligence Command.

Brigadier General Sean Stinchon, who commands the Army Counterintelligence Command, said the case sent a "stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath". The case is believed to be the first time a US soldier has been suspected of spying for a foreign government while in the United Kingdom.

The Kremlin has denied any connection to the officer. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow had "nothing to do with it" and that he was unaware of what the arrest was connected to.

It remains unclear what information, if anything, was passed on, and no charges have been publicly announced. The case now proceeds under US military jurisdiction, with British authorities continuing to cooperate.

The case is the latest involving suspected Russian espionage in Britain and elsewhere in Europe, and comes amid heightened tensions between Moscow and European capitals since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The reviews are in, and Grand Theft Auto VI appears to have lived up to its hype. The game has averaged 99 on Metacritic across 100 critic reviews, putting it — at least for now — level with the original The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, which scored 99 when it launched in 1998.

IGN awarded the game a perfect 10 out of 10, describing it as "exquisitely detailed" and "uncompromisingly engineered". The New York Times called it a "dense game filled with friction", saying it "matures beyond its memorable car crashes and stick-ups with a game about love, relationships and nurturing your partner". The lowest confirmed reviews so far give the game 90, according to the BBC. Slant Magazine was less effusive, giving four and a half stars and noting "flaws" in its creative approach.

The verdicts arrived as the game began its staggered worldwide release. It unlocks at midnight local time in each region, so players in New Zealand were the first to get in, at 11:00 GMT on Wednesday. The full release follows the clock around the world, reaching the UK at midnight GMT on Thursday, November 19.

Early players in New Zealand were quick to praise it. Connor Cattermoul, a 27-year-old from New Zealand who finished the game's 45-minute introductory mission in the early hours, told the BBC it was "amazing" so far with "beautiful" graphics. Staying up, he said, was "pretty worth it".

The launch has already drawn a huge live audience. More than 2.4 million people were watching gamers play GTA VI on Twitch within hours of the New Zealand release, according to the BBC.

The game is the first new entry in the series in 13 years, and it carries the weight of that wait. Set in Leonida, Rockstar's fictional version of Florida, it returns players to Vice City, its take on Miami, and follows two protagonists — Lucia, the series' first playable female lead in a 3D and HD setting, and her partner Jason — on what the BBC describes as a "Bonnie and Clyde-esque adventure". It is available on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X and S, priced at £69.99 in the UK and $79.99 in the US. There is no news yet on a PC version, or on whether it will come to Nintendo Switch 2.

The release follows two delays, after the game was initially due to launch in autumn 2025. Its predecessor, GTA V, sold around 235 million copies and made Rockstar $1 billion in just three days.

Not all reviews are in yet, and the final score may shift as more critics publish their verdicts — some outlets want more time with a game whose main story is estimated to take around 50 hours. But with countless reviewers handing out perfect marks, Rockstar has delivered on what the BBC called its "truly monstrous hype".

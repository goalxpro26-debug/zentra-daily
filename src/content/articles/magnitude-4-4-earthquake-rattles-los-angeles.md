A magnitude 4.4 earthquake shook the Los Angeles area in the early hours of Friday morning, waking residents across the region and sending fire department helicopters into the sky to check for damage.

The tremor struck at 3:25 a.m. local time, with its epicentre about a kilometre north-northwest of Commerce, a city roughly five miles east-southeast of downtown Los Angeles, according to the U.S. Geological Survey. It originated about 10.6 miles (17 kilometres) below the surface.

The quake was first measured at magnitude 4.2 and later revised upward to 4.4 after seismologists at the California Institute of Technology's seismic network reviewed the data. Shaking was reported across Los Angeles County, from Whittier and the San Gabriel Valley to communities nearer the coast, and social media filled quickly with posts from people woken by the jolt.

There were no immediate reports of injuries or serious damage. The Los Angeles Fire Department sent helicopters over the city shortly after the shaking to look for signs of structural damage, and the Geological Survey's automated assessment issued a green alert, indicating a low likelihood of casualties or significant economic losses. Officials urged anyone who felt the tremor to report it through the survey's "Did You Feel It?" programme, which helps scientists map how widely shaking is felt.

The earthquake was the strongest to shake the Los Angeles area in about a year, according to Geological Survey records. Southern California sits atop a network of active faults, and moderate quakes of this size are not unusual — but one centred so close to the city centre is enough to rattle nerves and set phones buzzing across the region.

The Los Angeles tremor was not the only seismic activity in California overnight. A separate cluster of more than a dozen earthquakes rattled Central California near Pinnacles National Park late on Thursday into early Friday, the strongest of them also measuring magnitude 4.4, according to the Geological Survey.

Damage assessments were continuing on Friday morning, and it was not immediately clear whether the Commerce quake had caused any injuries or structural harm. Seismologists typically watch for aftershocks after a quake of this size; smaller tremors in the hours and days that follow are common, though most are too weak to be felt.

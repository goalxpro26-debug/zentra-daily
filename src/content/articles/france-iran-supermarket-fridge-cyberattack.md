France summoned Iran's ambassador on Tuesday after accusing Tehran of a cyberattack that disabled refrigerators in hundreds of supermarkets across the country, in a diplomatic escalation that Paris says could lead to European sanctions and a cyber response of its own.

Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said France would push for EU sanctions over what he called an "attack of unprecedented scale and aggression in our country". Speaking on France Info television, he warned that Paris would take whatever measures it judged proportionate, including some it would not discuss publicly, to deter further attacks and punish those responsible.

## Tehran blamed for October disruption

French officials said on Monday that Iran's Revolutionary Guards were behind the attacks, which disrupted commercial refrigeration systems in early October. The foreign ministry confirmed that the ambassador's summoning, announced the previous day, took place on Tuesday morning.

The supermarket hacks were among the most disruptive cyberattacks since the war with Iran began in February, a conflict marked by sporadic episodes of suspected digital sabotage. Other incidents in that pattern have included hacks targeting the Los Angeles transit system in March and US water treatment plants in July.

The attacks were relatively unsophisticated in technical terms — the refrigeration systems were connected to the internet, which left them exposed — but their scale made them a national problem. Hundreds of stores were affected, and the disruption put chilled and frozen stock at risk across the country.

## A relationship already under strain

Iran's mission to the United Nations did not respond to requests for comment on Monday. Iran routinely denies carrying out malicious cyber operations.

Relations between Paris and Tehran have been strained for years over Iran's nuclear and ballistic missile programmes, its regional activities and the detention of several French nationals. Ties improved after those detainees were released and France distanced itself from the US war on Iran, but they deteriorated again over the summer, after Iran arrested and later expelled two French diplomats. France responded by expelling Iranian diplomats.

French officials say Tehran has become increasingly hostile towards France, notably over its support for Iranian civil society.

## What is still unknown

France has not published technical evidence linking the attacks to the Revolutionary Guards, and no details of any planned sanctions or cyber response have been disclosed. Barrot said France's cyberdefence doctrine gave authorities a range of options, but he did not say which, if any, had been chosen.

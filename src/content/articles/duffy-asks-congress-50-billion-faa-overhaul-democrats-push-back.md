Three days after a single faulty router in rural Arkansas grounded every departing flight in the United States, the fight over fixing the country's air traffic system has moved to Capitol Hill — and it is already turning into a partisan standoff over money.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy spent Sunday pressing Congress for tens of billions of dollars in new funding for the Federal Aviation Administration. He had previously asked for around $30 billion. After Friday's nationwide ground stop, he raised the figure to $50 billion. President Donald Trump backed the demand in a social media post on Sunday, urging Congress to "give the money, FAST!"

Democrats, who are set to take control of Congress in January, are pushing back. They point to $12.5 billion that Republicans allocated to the FAA last year in the president's sweeping spending and policy bill — legislation no Democrat supported — and argue the agency already has the funds it needs.

Duffy disputes that. Appearing on CNN's "State of the Union" on Sunday, he said none of that money can be used for the work that matters most: a new telecommunications architecture, new software to manage aircraft in the airspace, or new control towers. The software the system still runs on dates back to the 1990s, he said, and the towers are old. Of the $12.5 billion, $4.75 billion was earmarked for telecommunications modernisation and systems upgrades, and Duffy said on Saturday that about a third of that modernisation budget has been spent so far. He told CNN the telecom work could be finished by "the first part of 2029" — if the money arrives.

"All I need is the money to finish it," Duffy said. "And my hope is that if they give me money today, I'll be going tomorrow to build it."

The Democratic response has been pointed. Reps. Rick Larsen and André Carson, the top Democrats on two House panels overseeing aviation, asked the administration on Saturday to explain to Congress why it needs more money while funds remain unspent, and said FAA and Transportation Department staff would brief their teams on Monday. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the House Appropriations Committee's top Democrat, called the $50 billion request a "ransom" and said the country's air traffic controllers "have been failed by Secretary Duffy."

Others turned the argument toward spending priorities. Hawaii Sen. Brian Schatz noted there is $60 billion in extra funding for immigration enforcement and argued some of it could go to the FAA instead. Illinois Sen. Tammy Duckworth said the administration had controlled Congress for two years and still found money for what she called vanity projects, but none to fix air traffic control.

Not every Democrat is opposed. Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen, who launched a bid for Senate majority leader last week, said on Sunday he would support "whatever level of money is necessary to fix this problem" on a bipartisan basis — but he warned the administration must come to Congress and justify the request. "It's really rich to hear the secretary pointing the fingers at Congress when Congress has given the administration what they asked for," he said.

Republicans may try to move quickly while they still hold the majority. Outgoing House Speaker Mike Johnson said on Saturday he would add FAA funding to an upcoming legislative package, telling Fox News that "we don't really need any investigation to understand we need further investment." GOP Sen. Eric Schmitt is also pushing his own bill, the Modern Skies Act, which would invest $30 billion in modernising the aviation system; he spoke with Trump on Friday about it.

How any new funding would pass is not yet clear. Congressional appropriators are already negotiating a government funding deal ahead of a December 11 deadline, and aides in both parties have not said how FAA money would fit into it. With the Thanksgiving travel rush approaching, the pressure to reach an answer is only growing.

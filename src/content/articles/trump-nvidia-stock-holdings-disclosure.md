President Donald Trump held at least $12 million in Nvidia shares at the end of 2025, according to his annual financial disclosure report reviewed by ABC News, making the chipmaker one of his three largest individual stock holdings.

The stake was worth between $12.5 million and $58 million at the end of last year, according to a CNBC analysis of the president's disclosures, up sharply from between $615,000 and $1.3 million a year earlier. The filings give holdings as broad dollar ranges rather than share counts, so the exact value is not public.

Trump's investment accounts have kept trading Nvidia since his return to the White House, buying or selling the stock at least 103 times in transactions worth a combined $27 million to $97.5 million, the CNBC analysis found. ABC News reported more than three dozen trades buying and selling millions of dollars of Nvidia stock this year through September, based on separate periodic filings.

The pace of trading is a marked change from Trump's first term. His accounts disclosed more than 21,000 trades in 2025 alone, compared with roughly 500 during his entire first term in office, according to CNBC's analysis.

## White House response

The White House maintains that the president does not direct the trading. A spokesperson said his stock and bond portfolio is independently managed by third-party financial institutions, held in discretionary accounts and invested through computer-based model portfolios that automatically replicate recognised indexes.

The disclosures offer no evidence that Trump directed the trades, or that his advisers had access to classified intelligence, CNBC reported.

## Why the holdings are in focus

The disclosure comes as Nvidia's co-founder Jensen Huang has become one of the president's closest business allies, meeting or appearing with Trump at least 15 times since he returned to office. Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive last week following revelations about his relationship with a woman suspected of spying for China and reports of affairs with company employees.

Trump stood by Huang as Nvidia's board weighed his future, urging him to fight to keep his job, and has since floated the idea of reinstating him. Nvidia's board is not considering a return, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC.

Nvidia's rise has also been central to the market Trump points to as a measure of the economy. The chipmaker alone accounted for about 13.5 percent of the S&P 500's gains in 2026 through September, according to an analysis by Choate Wealth.

## What is still unclear

Because the filings report holdings in wide value ranges and do not list share counts, Trump's current stake in Nvidia cannot be pinned down precisely. Based on the value he reported holding in August, the stake would have been worth between $4.5 million and $57.7 million at midday on Monday, with the stock priced around $157.

European leaders have settled the question that hung over their Dublin summit: who speaks for Europe at the next round of Ukraine peace talks. The answer is a team, not a single envoy — the European Union's institutions together with five countries: Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland.

European Council President António Costa announced the arrangement on Friday, at the end of an informal summit of EU leaders in Dublin. The five are the largest military powers in the loose "coalition of the willing" backing Ukraine.

The decision came a day after the same leaders had agreed that Europe must speak with one voice in any talks, but left the question of who would do the speaking unresolved. Costa said the team would work with all 27 EU member states.

Who exactly sits in the chairs is still to be decided. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas laid claim to the envoy role on Friday, saying she is the one who represents Europe on foreign and security policy, and that it is not up to Russia to decide who speaks for the EU. No individual names have been confirmed for the national seats.

## Moscow has not yet said yes

The bigger unknown is whether the Europeans will be admitted to the room at all. The Kremlin said on Friday that it would be open to European participation if it saw the Europeans were genuinely ready to contribute to the peace process — but it has not confirmed that they will join the next round.

U.S. negotiators had said on Thursday that Russian President Vladimir Putin had agreed to European involvement, but Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said no concrete discussions had taken place. Moscow continues to press for a broad agreement on Europe's security framework before tackling the specifics of the war.

European leaders pushed back. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the Kremlin's comments "the same old game" and said there would be no discussions about Ukraine without Ukraine, and none about Europe without Europe. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there could be no revision of Europe's security architecture — which, he said, was what Putin was demanding.

## A week of shuttle diplomacy

The decision caps a week of shuttle diplomacy. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow on Thursday, then flew to Dublin to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Representatives from Britain, France, Germany and Italy, along with the EU, attended Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi earlier in the week — the first direct meeting between the two sides in nine months. Both sides cited some progress, but no breakthrough on territory or a ceasefire.

A day earlier, 40 European countries meeting in Dublin signed a declaration saying any matter affecting European security should be decided with Europeans involved. EU leaders next meet on November 26–27 to discuss Ukraine's financing needs for 2027 and beyond. No date has been set for the next round of peace talks.

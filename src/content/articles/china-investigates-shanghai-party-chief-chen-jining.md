China has placed Chen Jining, the Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a member of its elite Politburo, under investigation — the fourth member of the party's top leadership body to fall since 2025, and the most senior civilian official yet caught in President Xi Jinping's widening anti-corruption campaign.

State news agency Xinhua announced on Tuesday that Chen, 62, is being investigated for "suspected severe violations of discipline and laws", the party's standard euphemism for corruption. The brief statement gave no details of the alleged wrongdoing.

Chen is the first serving Shanghai party secretary to be placed under investigation since Chen Liangyu in 2006, who was later jailed for 18 years. The post is one of the most prestigious in Chinese politics and has often served as a springboard to the very top: Xi himself briefly held it in 2007 before joining the Politburo Standing Committee.

His fall is striking because of the career it interrupts. An environmental scientist by training, Chen earned a doctorate from Imperial College London, rose through Tsinghua University to become its president, then served as China's environment minister and mayor of Beijing. He joined the Politburo in October 2022 and took charge of Shanghai, China's financial hub and largest city, the same month. Political watchers had seen him as a strong candidate for elevation to the Standing Committee at the party congress due in the second half of 2027, where Xi is expected to seek a fourth five-year term.

Before Chen, three members of the 24-strong Politburo formed in 2022 had already been removed: generals He Weidong and Zhang Youxia, both senior figures in the Central Military Commission, and Ma Xingrui, a provincial party secretary. With Chen under investigation, the body now has 21 active members.

Shanghai's senior party leadership moved quickly to endorse the probe. At a meeting on Tuesday evening, officials pledged loyalty to Beijing and said the investigation demonstrated the party leadership's determination, according to the city's official newspaper. No replacement for Chen has been named, and the city's mayor, its number-two official, could stand in until a permanent appointment is made.

Chen's last public appearance was on Thursday, November 19, when he inspected the headquarters of a Chinese shipping conglomerate.

The investigation caps a year in which Xi's disciplinary campaign has thinned the senior ranks. At the Central Committee's fifth plenum in late October, the party formalised the expulsions of 33 high-ranking members, nearly half of them military officers. Analysts say the pattern is unprecedented: the purge has now reached the civilian leadership of China's richest city, and it clears the way for Xi to reshape the ruling elite ahead of next year's congress.

What happens next is unclear. In past cases at this level, the announcement of an investigation by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection has generally signalled an official's eventual political downfall. Whether Chen faces trial, and what charges if any are brought, has not been disclosed.

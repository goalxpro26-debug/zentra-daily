Warren Buffett said on Monday he is donating about $5.5 billion of Berkshire Hathaway stock to four foundations run by his family, his largest Thanksgiving gift yet — and used the occasion to argue that generosity, like a good investment, grows over time.

The 96-year-old investor announced the gift in his annual Thanksgiving message to Berkshire shareholders. He converted 6,150 of Berkshire's high-priced Class A shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares and gave them to the four foundations: 3,690,000 shares to the Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation, named for his late first wife, and 1,845,000 shares each to the Sherwood Foundation, the Howard G. Buffett Foundation and the NoVo Foundation. The foundations are led by his three adult children — Susie, 73, Howard, 71, and Peter, 68.

With this gift, Buffett has now given more than $29 billion of Berkshire stock to the four charities. He said his confidence in the conglomerate he built over six decades remains "as strong as ever."

The scale of the donation marks a step change in Buffett's giving. Last year's Thanksgiving gift was worth about $1.3 billion, making this year's roughly four times larger. Buffett said the goal is to increase the size of the grants each year as he works toward giving away all of his Berkshire shares by the end of 2034.

The letter was notably short and said little about Berkshire's business. Instead, Buffett returned to a theme from last year, when he wrote that kindness "is costless but also priceless." This year he added: "A year later, I would only add that it compounds, and at a better rate than anything Charlie and I ever bought" — a nod to his late business partner Charlie Munger.

Buffett closed the message with a direct appeal to readers: "This Thanksgiving, thank one of those selfless people in your own life. Better yet, be a benefactor for someone else."

The gift is Buffett's first Thanksgiving donation since he stepped down as Berkshire's chairman and chief executive. Greg Abel now runs the company, while Buffett remains chairman emeritus. Even after the donation, he still holds 182,140 Class A shares, worth roughly $162 billion, which he has said will eventually go to the same four foundations.

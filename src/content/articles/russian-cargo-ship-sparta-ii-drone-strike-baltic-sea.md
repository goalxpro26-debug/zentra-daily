A Russian cargo ship was still burning in the Baltic Sea on Sunday, a day after drones struck it in the Gulf of Finland, in an attack Finland says was probably carried out by Ukraine — and which Helsinki warns shows the war reaching into its own neighbourhood.

The Sparta II, a 121-metre Russian-flagged cargo vessel owned by the shipping company Oboronlogistika, left St Petersburg on Friday bound for Kaliningrad, the Russian exclave on the Baltic coast. On Saturday morning it was hit by two drones in international waters west of Gogland island, about three nautical miles from Russia's maritime border, according to Russian officials and Finland's Defence Forces. The ship caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters, where it now sits at anchor.

Finland's coast guard said the fire burned through the night as several vessels sprayed the ship with water cannons, trying to douse the flames and cool the containers stacked on its deck. All 16 crew members were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship. No casualties have been reported. Helsinki has imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions in the eastern Gulf of Finland to protect civilians and give its forces room to act if drones need to be intercepted.

"The war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas," Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said on X. "Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, where a drone strike has hit a Russian merchant vessel. Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor." Finland's military said it was likely Ukraine was behind the attack, though it was not clear why the vessel had been targeted. Kyiv has not commented.

The ship's background is part of the story. The Sparta II is subject to US sanctions imposed in 2022, and to Ukrainian sanctions imposed earlier this year. Oboronlogistika provides logistics services for the Russian Defence Ministry, and the vessel has been suspected of carrying weapons from Syria to Russia. Finnish border guard commander Ilja Iljin said there was no confirmed information about what cargo was on board, but that military materiel could not be ruled out.

The Baltic has become a flashpoint in the war. Ukraine has sent waves of military drones towards Russia's north-western Leningrad region this year, some straying into the airspace of nearby Baltic states. Finland and Sweden joined NATO in 2023 and 2024 in response to Russia's full-scale invasion. Estonia's navy commander said on Sunday that the risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland could not be ruled out, and hours after the strike a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating Finnish airspace — an incident Helsinki said may be linked to the rescue operation.

Finland is leading the rescue operation, and its border guard says the plan is to enable the damaged vessel to be towed into Russia's area of responsibility. No environmental damage has been detected, and officials say there is no danger to maritime traffic or people.

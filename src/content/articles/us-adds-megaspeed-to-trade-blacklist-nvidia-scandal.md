The United States has added Megaspeed, the Singapore data-centre company at the centre of the scandal engulfing Nvidia, to its trade blacklist — closing the door on its access to advanced American chips.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security placed Megaspeed International and six other firms in South-East Asia on its Entity List on Monday, in a filing to the Federal Register. The bureau said the seven entities had been found to be acting against the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States.

The designation means American companies now need a government licence to ship the firms advanced technology, including Nvidia's top-end graphics processors used to train and run artificial intelligence systems. Such licences are generally denied. It is the first time since October 2025 that the Commerce Department has added new entities to the list.

The six other firms are MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix in Malaysia; Apex Enterprise Solutions and Swiftdata in Singapore; and Megaspeed in Thailand. All were linked to Megaspeed, according to a Commerce official.

The move came hours after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, less than two weeks after he resigned amid revelations about his relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. President Donald Trump said he had personally urged Nvidia's board to bring Huang back. Nvidia shares surged on the news of his return.

## A listing long resisted

The blacklisting follows weeks of open disagreement inside the administration. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's department had declined to place Megaspeed on the Entity List even as the investigation into the firm deepened, drawing public criticism from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and from lawmakers.

US authorities have been investigating Megaspeed since 2024 over suspicions that it diverted advanced Nvidia chips to China in breach of export controls. Last week the Justice and Commerce departments confirmed the criminal investigation and said they would pursue the matter to the full extent of the law.

US officials have accused Alice Huang, Megaspeed's controlling shareholder, of working with China's Ministry of State Security in an intelligence-gathering operation targeting the Nvidia chief. Beijing has rejected the claims as groundless. Megaspeed has previously denied wrongdoing and said it complies with all applicable US and local laws. It did not respond to requests for comment on the listing.

## What is still unknown

The criminal investigation into Megaspeed continues, and it is not yet clear how strictly the licensing regime will be applied in practice, or whether any of the seven firms will seek exemptions. Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand have not yet commented publicly on the designations.

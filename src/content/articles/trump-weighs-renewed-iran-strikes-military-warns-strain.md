President Donald Trump is weighing a new round of military strikes against Iran, even as American military planners warn that U.S. forces risk becoming overextended if the standoff drags into the new year, the Financial Times reported on Friday.

The report said hawkish officials in Washington and Israel are pressing Trump to resume military action, while planners caution that sustaining the current deployment is becoming harder the longer the confrontation continues.

A separate report in The New York Times, published on Wednesday, said Trump has not yet made a decision, but is considering fresh Israeli intelligence warnings that Tehran has rebuilt parts of its missile and drone arsenals and made progress on chemical and biological weapons programmes.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is urging Washington to join Israel in a four-to-seven-day air campaign, according to the Times report. A senior Israeli official disputed that Netanyahu had pressured Trump to act.

The discussions intensified last week, when Mossad director Roman Gofman travelled to Washington for meetings with senior administration officials, including CIA director John Ratcliffe. Israeli military intelligence chief Maj. Gen. Shlomi Binder has also advocated resuming operations, the report said.

The prospect of another major campaign has exposed divisions inside the administration, both over the reliability of the intelligence and over the wisdom of renewed strikes. Some officials question whether the emerging threats justify a new offensive, particularly after the Republican Party's defeat in the midterm elections. The war, which began on February 28, has lasted nearly eight months, contributing to rising prices at home without delivering the swift victory Trump initially promised.

Trump recently instructed Vice President JD Vance to abandon efforts to reach a negotiated settlement with Tehran, the Times reported, favouring sustained economic pressure aimed at forcing Iran to give up its nuclear programme. One U.S. official argued, however, that further major military operations were unnecessary because the economic pressure campaign had already severely weakened Iran.

The report came hours after Trump told Fox News that American intelligence had indications Iran was exploring chemical and biological weapons as alternatives to nuclear weapons.

"Well, there is indication that Iran is looking at gases, looking at bio because we've shut them out with the nuclear," Trump told the network's White House correspondent, Jacqui Heinrich.

The president also confirmed that contacts with Tehran were continuing. "They want to make a deal, but I want a deal that can be lasting," he said. Asked whether he would keep American troops in the region until an agreement was reached, he replied: "Yeah, I'm going to finish it off. Yeah, soon. I mean, one way or the other, we'll finish it off."

His remarks left open whether Washington intends to end the confrontation through diplomacy or renewed military action — whether the president will embrace Israel's proposal for another concentrated air campaign, or keep pursuing an agreement through economic and diplomatic pressure.

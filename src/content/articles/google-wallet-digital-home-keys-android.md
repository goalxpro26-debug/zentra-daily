Your house key can now live in Google Wallet. Google has begun rolling out support for digital home keys on Android, letting a phone unlock a compatible smart lock — a feature Apple has offered in Apple Wallet for years.

Once a home key is added to Wallet, tapping the phone against the lock opens the door. The tap uses NFC and works even when the phone's screen is locked, by default — although that can be changed in the Wallet app to require a screen lock or biometric check first. Any Android phone running Android 9 or newer can use the tap-to-unlock mode.

There is also a hands-free option, called Unlock on Approach, which opens the door as you walk up. That needs an Aliro-compatible phone with ultra-wideband, plus an Aliro-compatible lock — Google names the Pixel 10 Pro series and above, and selected ultra-wideband Samsung Galaxy phones. Smartwatches are left out for now: Wear OS is not supported at launch.

## One key for the whole home

The key is tied to the home in the Google Home app, not to a single lock. That means one digital key in Wallet works with every compatible Aliro lock in the household, and it syncs automatically across other Android phones signed into the same Wallet account. Keys are added through the Home app, which prompts you to save one after a lock is set up, or from Home settings under Google Wallet key. The card in Wallet shows a Google Home logo with the home's name in the top-right corner.

Aliro is the open smart-lock standard from the Connectivity Standards Alliance, the group behind Matter. Its arrival in Wallet matters because it breaks the pattern of each lock brand needing its own app: any lock that follows the standard should, in principle, work with any phone wallet that supports it.

## A short list, for now

The catch is how few locks qualify at launch. Only three models are supported to start: the Aqara u400, which handles both ultra-wideband and NFC; the Schlage Sense Pro, with NFC now and ultra-wideband to follow; and the Nuki Keypad 2 NFC. Google says support for more models is coming.

The rollout is also staged. It reaches Google Home Public Preview members first, then widens to all users over the coming weeks. So most Android owners with a smart lock will have to wait — and, if their lock is not one of the three, wait longer still.

Still, the direction is clear. Car keys, hotel keys and transit passes already live in phone wallets; the front door was the obvious next step. For the small group with the right lock and the right phone, the keyring just got one item lighter.

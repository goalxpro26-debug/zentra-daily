Toyota is bringing back Scion, but not as the youth-focused car brand Americans remember. The revived nameplate, now called Scion by Toyota, will serve as an experimental mobility brand — and its first vehicle is a tiny Japanese-style kei truck, assembled in Indiana and limited to 25 mph.

The truck, the KEI-01, is scheduled to reach customers in the second half of 2027. Toyota will assemble it at its plant in Princeton, Indiana, backed by a $34 million investment and about 50 new jobs. The vehicles will arrive as kits from Japan for final assembly in the United States.

## Small by design

The KEI-01 follows the Japanese kei-truck formula almost to the letter. It measures just over 11 feet long and less than five feet wide, with a 660cc three-cylinder engine, a continuously variable transmission and standard four-wheel drive. Its cargo bed folds down on three sides and can carry up to 600 pounds. It keeps its right-hand-drive layout, just like the kei trucks sold in Japan.

The catch is the speed. Toyota designed the KEI-01 to meet the federal safety standard for low-speed vehicles, FMVSS No. 500, which caps its top speed at 25 mph and restricts it to roads with speed limits of 35 mph or less. It is a work vehicle for campuses, farms and local roads — not a highway pickup.

That trade-off is the point. Kei trucks have become a quiet cult favourite in the United States, with more than 30,000 used examples imported since 2020, largely for farm and utility work. Toyota is now the first major automaker to offer one officially, built and sold through its own network rather than through independent importers.

## A fleet experiment first

Most Americans will not be able to buy a KEI-01 at first. Toyota is launching it as a fleet-focused pilot programme, aimed at corporate campuses, farms, municipalities and local delivery operators, sold through a small group of existing Toyota dealers. The company says limited retail sales could follow if demand supports it. No price has been announced.

The revival is a sharp turn for a badge that once meant cheap, quirky cars for young buyers. Scion launched in 2003 and was discontinued in 2016 after sales faded. Toyota now describes the new Scion as a "freedom lab" — a place to test vehicles and ideas that would not fit its mainstream lineup. Alongside the KEI-01, it showed two concepts: a hybrid side-by-side off-road vehicle and a compact electric mobility concept.

## What is still unknown

Toyota has not said what the KEI-01 will cost, how many it plans to build, or when — or whether — retail buyers will get access. The pilot's results will decide whether the tiny truck becomes a niche fleet tool or the start of a broader Scion experiment.

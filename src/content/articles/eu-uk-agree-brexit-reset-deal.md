The United Kingdom and the European Union have sealed the first stage of a reset in their relations, a decade after British voters chose to leave the bloc.

After a summit in Brussels on Friday, Downing Street said negotiations on three agreements had concluded. "The deal is done," a UK government spokesperson told Al Jazeera. "Negotiations have concluded. Now both sides will work towards implementation."

Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa at the summit, the second such meeting since the two sides launched a renewed partnership in May 2025. "This is a good day for Britain. This is a good day for Europe," Burnham told the EU chiefs as the talks began.

The three agreements cover food trade, young people and carbon markets. A sanitary and phytosanitary deal will remove checks and paperwork on food and animal goods traded between Britain and the EU, a change the UK government estimates is worth £5.1 billion ($6.7 billion) a year to the British economy. A youth mobility scheme will allow up to 100,000 young people from each side to live, work or study in the other's territory for up to three years. And linking the two sides' emissions trading systems will exempt British and EU goods, such as steel, from each other's carbon border levies.

Burnham said the package could add 0.5 percent to Britain's economy. Downing Street said both sides had agreed timelines for putting the measures into practice, with details to be published in a joint statement.

The deal is not the end of the story. University tuition fees remain unresolved: Brussels wants students from EU countries to pay the same rates as British students at UK universities, rather than the far higher international fees charged today. The two sides have given themselves six months to reach agreement, and the deals will be signed once that is settled. Britain is also seeking access to the EU's "Made in Europe" rules, notably for cars.

Legal processes in both the UK and the EU must still run their course, and Al Jazeera reported that months or years of negotiations lie ahead before any deeper reset takes effect.

The summit comes ten years after the 2016 referendum in which 52 percent of British voters backed leaving the EU. Burnham has previously said all options for the future relationship should be on the table, including rejoining the now 27-nation bloc, while stressing that reversing Brexit is far from imminent.

The agreements drew a swift attack from Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, who called Burnham "naive" and accused the EU of strong-arming the prime minister. But for Burnham, who has argued that Britain lost more than it gained from Brexit, the summit marks the most concrete step yet towards rebuilding ties with Brussels.

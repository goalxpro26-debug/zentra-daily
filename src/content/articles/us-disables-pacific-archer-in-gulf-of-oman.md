The United States military says it disabled a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman after the vessel tried to run the American naval blockade of Iranian ports, in the latest confrontation over a campaign that has now stopped more than 150 ships.

US Central Command said in a statement on Friday that a military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier M/V Pacific Archer on Thursday, disabling the ship's propulsion. The civilian crew was unharmed, the command said.

According to the statement, the Pacific Archer had departed from the Iranian port city of Bandar Abbas and attempted to evade the blockade despite multiple warnings. Ship-tracking websites list the vessel's destination as Singapore.

The Pacific Archer is at least the 15th vessel the US military has publicly acknowledged disabling since the blockade began in April, and the sixth since it resumed this summer. The previous vessel was disabled on November 7. US forces have redirected 158 commercial vessels that complied with warnings not to run the blockade, according to Central Command.

The blockade itself has been on and off all year. American ships began blockading Iranian ports in April, but the campaign was lifted during the summer while talks with Tehran were under way, then reimposed in July. Earlier this month, Washington announced Task Force Iron Gate, a new effort to restrict the movement of weapons and sanctioned materials across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea. Diplomatic efforts to end the standoff have stalled.

Tehran answered the latest strike with defiance. The commander of the Iranian navy, Shahram Irani, said on state television on Friday that Iran would carry out operations "that will not only break the naval blockade but also drive the enemy out of the region's waters." Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's supreme leader, said Tehran would neither reopen the Strait of Hormuz nor allow the blockade to remain in place, though he gave no details of how Iran planned to break the embargo.

Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Friday that two tankers had tried to cross the Strait of Hormuz through an unauthorised route, claiming one of them struck a naval mine while the other was forced to turn back. Those claims could not be independently verified.

For now, the pattern is set: Washington enforces the blockade ship by ship, and Tehran promises to break it. Neither side has shown a way out of the cycle.

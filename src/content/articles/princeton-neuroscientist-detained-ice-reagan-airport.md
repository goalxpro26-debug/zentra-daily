A Princeton neuroscience doctoral student was detained by US immigration officers on Thursday at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport as she travelled home from a conference where she had just presented her research.

Eva Yi Xie, 24, a third-year PhD student at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, had presented a poster on how the brain makes decisions at the Society for Neuroscience annual meeting in Washington, D.C., on Wednesday. On Thursday, as she boarded a flight home, she was taken into custody by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

Her colleagues learned what had happened from a message Xie sent on Slack asking for help, then lost contact. Her research group, the Brody Lab, later confirmed she was being held at an ICE facility in Chantilly, Virginia.

Her adviser, professor Carlos Brody, posted on Bluesky and X that Xie is "a brilliant computational and experimental systems neuroscientist" who "went to a conference to present her work, and now she's in a detention center. She should be home."

Princeton has arranged legal counsel for Xie. Rodney Priestley, dean of the Graduate School, called the news distressing and said the university would stay in contact with her legal team and family: "We hope Eva will soon be able to resume her studies at Princeton."

Xie's lawyer, Kerry Doyle, told *The Daily Princetonian* that Xie is an asylum seeker with an immigration court date set for early 2028. Her lawyers have filed a habeas corpus petition in the US District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, arguing the detention is illegal. On Friday, District Judge Anthony J. Trenga ordered that Xie not be removed from the judicial district without further direction, and required immigration officials to respond by 5 p.m. on Wednesday, November 25.

The Department of Homeland Security has given a different account. It told Newsweek it conducted a targeted enforcement operation at Reagan National on November 19 to arrest Xie, whom it called "an illegal alien from China." The department said Xie was admitted in March 2016, was required to leave by September 2016, and never held valid authorisation to study. A pending application, it said, does not give her legal status, and she would remain in ICE custody pending removal proceedings.

Xie's lawyers dispute that reading. They say her pending asylum application allows her to remain in the United States legally while her case is heard, even though her visa had expired.

Xie, a 2024 MIT graduate, emigrated from China in 2016.

The detention has rattled foreign-born academics, who say the case crystallises fears that travelling to conferences has become risky. Xie is the first known Princeton student to be detained by ICE, according to *The Daily Princetonian*.

Why Xie was detained at this moment remains unclear: no reason was made public until the Homeland Security statement on Friday, and ICE did not answer repeated requests for comment. For now, the court order keeps her in Virginia while her lawyers challenge the detention.

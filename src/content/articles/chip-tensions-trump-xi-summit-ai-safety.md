The prospect of tighter American curbs on China's access to advanced AI chips is expected to overshadow talks when US President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping meet in Shenzhen on Thursday, even as the two sides look for common ground on AI safety.

The meeting comes days after Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned following reports of an affair with a suspected Chinese spy. The scandal has spurred a push by US lawmakers to tighten China's access to advanced AI chips, a move analysts said Beijing would be bracing for.

"President Xi may not name or mention Jensen Huang, but I'm pretty sure export controls will be high on the agenda when he meets Trump," said George Chen, chair of digital practice at The Asia Group, a consultancy. He added that Beijing's key concern went beyond technology controls to whether Washington would seek to contain China's broader technological and economic rise.

"If we are moving from strategic stability to strategic containment, that's a big shift and that's what Beijing is watching," Chen said.

Despite the rivalry, Washington and Beijing have been exploring cooperation on the risks posed by increasingly powerful AI systems. Three people briefed on preparations for the talks said the two sides had planned further AI discussions around the Shenzhen summit, covering safeguards against misuse of the technology.

One proposal under discussion is an emergency mechanism for the two countries to notify each other of serious AI incidents. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng discussed the idea in September, but it has yet to become operational, according to several people familiar with the discussions. The mechanism is intended to help prevent AI-related incidents or misunderstandings from escalating into national security crises. Communication has so far relied largely on direct contacts between senior officials.

Bessent said in New York last week that both sides would discuss communication protocols for AI safety incidents at the APEC AI talks and expected to exchange best practices for testing advanced models.

"Right now, I don't think it's in the interest of either side to let the potential export control squeeze sour the leaders' summit," said Bo Zhengyuan, a Shanghai-based partner at research consultancy Plenum.

The two leaders are not expected to announce further progress on the Board of Trade initiative agreed at their summit in Washington in September, which aimed to lower reciprocal tariffs on $30 billion worth of goods, a senior US administration official said.

China has identified AI and the digital economy as priorities for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum in Shenzhen, with Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning saying Beijing hoped leaders would reach practical agreements in both areas. Chinese organisers said more than 2,000 political and business leaders were expected at the APEC CEO Summit on November 17–18, including representatives from 115 Fortune Global 500 companies.

Trump is not bringing a formal business delegation, according to the administration official. Elon Musk is the main chief executive travelling with Trump, while other corporate leaders were attending at China's invitation. Musk, Mastercard CEO Michael Miebach and FedEx CEO Raj Subramaniam were expected to attend the summit dinner, a person familiar with corporate preparations said.

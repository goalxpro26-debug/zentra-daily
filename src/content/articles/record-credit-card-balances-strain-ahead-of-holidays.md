Americans are heading into the holiday shopping season carrying record credit card balances. Total card debt reached $1.29 trillion in the three months ending in September, up $22 billion from the prior quarter, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's latest quarterly report on household debt and credit, released November 10.

The figure is 67% higher than in the first quarter of 2021, when pandemic restrictions were still limiting spending on travel and dining out. Total household debt — including mortgages, student and auto loans, credit cards and home equity lines of credit — rose by $334 billion in the third quarter to $19.1 trillion, about $5 trillion more than at the end of 2019.

The buildup comes as household budgets face pressure from several directions at once. Prices are up roughly 30% since 2019, and the savings rate has dipped below 4% of total income, well below pre-pandemic levels. Disposable personal income, adjusted for inflation, grew less than 1% in September, the slowest reading since April and the third consecutive monthly slowdown, according to Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis data.

## Signs of strain

Spending is showing signs of slowing after a resilient year. A monthly gauge published by CNBC and the National Retail Federation found retail sales rose just 0.2% last month, the slowest pace in nearly a year, with the figure held up by a strong Halloween season and early holiday promotions.

Even McDonald's has struggled despite $5 and $6 meal deals: U.S. same-store sales fell 0.2% in the third quarter, its first quarterly drop since early last year. Chief executive Chris Kempczinski said lower-income customers in particular were looking for entry-level value the chain had not fully delivered.

The pressure is also visible in demand for help with debt. Ted Rossman, principal consumer finance analyst at Money Management International, a nonprofit credit counseling agency, said the past two months had been the busiest for debt management plan sign-ups in more than 15 years. "Household budgets are reaching a breaking point," he said.

## A labour market holding the line

There is one counterweight: jobs. The labour market remains in a low-fire, low-hire pattern, and job growth has shown signs of stabilising — employment grew by an average of 137,000 a month over the past three months, compared with an average loss of 45,000 a month during the same period in 2025. Many economists believe that as long as most people stay employed, consumer spending is unlikely to fall off a cliff.

The picture is not uniform, though. About 2 million Americans have been out of work for six months or more, accounting for 27.6% of all unemployed workers — the highest share since December 2021.

What happens over the holidays will be the next test: with balances already at a record, how much more households put on their cards may say a lot about where the economy heads next.

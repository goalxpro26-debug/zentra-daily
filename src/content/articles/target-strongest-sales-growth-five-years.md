Target reported its strongest quarterly sales growth in more than five years on Wednesday, a result the retailer credited to new products, price cuts and faster delivery — and a sign that its long turnaround effort is finally gaining traction with shoppers squeezed by inflation.

Net sales rose 7.1% in the three months ended October 31, to $27.07 billion from $25.27 billion a year earlier, the company's best performance since the fourth quarter of 2021. Comparable sales — from stores and digital channels open at least a year — climbed 5.6%, well ahead of the roughly 3.8% analysts had expected, as comparable traffic rose 5%. Digital comparable sales jumped nearly 10%, while in-store comparable sales rose 4.6%.

Profit rose sharply. Target earned $1.21 billion, or $2.65 a share, against $689 million, or $1.51 a share, in the same period last year. The figure includes a 50-cent-per-share benefit from government tariff refunds. Analysts had expected earnings of about $2.07 a share on sales of roughly $26.5 billion, according to FactSet.

## A turnaround finding its footing

The quarter overlapped with the back-to-school season, and the company said a refreshed product lineup, expanded same-day delivery and lower prices on thousands of items brought customers back. Chief executive Michael Fiddelke, a 20-year veteran who took the top job in February, has made reviving the stores and merchandising the centre of his turnaround plan.

Target's shares have risen 63% so far in 2026, though the stock remains about 40% below its record closing high of $266.39, set in November 2021. The question now is whether the momentum survives the holiday season, the retailer's toughest test of the year.

## Outlook raised again

Encouraged by the quarter, Target raised its full-year guidance for the second time this year. It now expects net sales growth of around 6%, one percentage point higher than the guidance it gave in August, and full-year earnings per share of $10.65 to $11.40, up from a prior range of $9.90 to $10.90. That earnings range includes about $2.14 a share in tariff refund benefits recognised so far.

The company also projected a full-year operating income margin of about 6.2%, including roughly 1.1 percentage points of benefit from tariff refunds, and said it plans to spend $5 billion to $6 billion on capital investments in 2027.

Target said November sales so far are in line with its full-year outlook.

## What is still unknown

The headline beat comes with a caveat. A large part of the profit jump — the 50 cents a share in tariff refunds this quarter, and $2.14 across the full-year guidance — is a one-off benefit, not a gain from selling more goods. Average transaction amounts grew by less than 1%, suggesting shoppers are visiting more often but still spending carefully. Whether the traffic gains hold once the refund boost fades will decide whether this is a durable recovery or a strong quarter flattered by timing.

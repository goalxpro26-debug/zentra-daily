The leaders of the United States, China and Russia will meet together for the first time next week, the White House confirmed on Friday, in an unprecedented gathering of the heads of the world's three biggest nuclear powers.

President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin are scheduled to sit down on Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, the southern Chinese technology hub hosting this year's gathering. The meeting will close Trump's visit to China.

The White House said Trump wants to use the session to discuss reducing nuclear weapons stockpiles and to raise the war in Iran. A senior U.S. official, briefing reporters on condition of anonymity, said Trump does not plan to discuss Russia's war in Ukraine at the three-way meeting, and that those negotiations are on a separate track.

## Low expectations, high symbolism

Officials on all sides have been careful to play down what the meeting might produce. The senior U.S. official described it as the "beginning of hopefully a conversation" on nuclear disarmament, and said no agreements are expected to be announced. Analysts quoted by the Associated Press said the talks are likely to be heavier on symbolism than substance.

The backdrop gives the symbolism weight. The last nuclear arms pact between the United States and Russia expired in February, removing caps on the world's two largest atomic arsenals for the first time in more than half a century. China, which holds more than 600 nuclear warheads according to Pentagon estimates — far fewer than the estimated 5,000-plus held by each of the United States and Russia — has long resisted joining formal arms-control talks, and U.S. officials acknowledged that persuading Beijing to engage will be difficult.

For the Kremlin, the meeting is a coveted chance to present Russia as a superpower on par with Washington and Beijing. For China, it carries a complication: the trilateral could undercut Beijing's effort to portray itself at APEC as a defender of free trade and multilateralism.

## The Ukraine question

The decision to keep Ukraine off the agenda has already drawn a reaction in Kyiv. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has expressed concern that the meeting could become a venue for discussing a possible Ukraine settlement behind his country's back, and Ukrainian officials have objected to any suggestion that the three leaders might decide the war's outcome between themselves.

The Kremlin has pushed back on comparisons to the 1945 Yalta conference, where the Allied leaders redrew Europe. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said any understandings reached in Shenzhen could not be imposed on other countries.

The APEC summit itself runs on November 18 and 19, and the trilateral meeting is expected to overshadow much of the gathering's trade agenda.

Alaska voters have elected a Democrat as governor for the first time since 1998, the Associated Press projected on Friday, handing former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins a win in one of the most Republican-leaning states in the country.

Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, defeated three Republicans — Bernadette Wilson, Dave Bronson and Treg Taylor — and will become the youngest governor in Alaska's history. He will also be the youngest sitting governor in the United States when he takes office. His running mate, independent Zac Johnson, will serve as lieutenant governor.

The result was decided on first-choice votes. Kreiss-Tomkins cleared just over 50 percent, enough to avoid a ranked-choice runoff under Alaska's voting system. Final ballot counting is scheduled for November 18, with certification expected by November 25, and he is due to be sworn in on December 7.

## A win built on crossover appeal

Kreiss-Tomkins, a former state representative from Sitka, won in a state where Democrats make up only about 11 percent of registered voters. He campaigned as a pragmatic coalition-builder, pledging to work across party lines and to appoint a cabinet drawn from Republicans, Democrats and independents.

It is a political comeback three years after he left the state House, where he served for a decade after first winning a seat at age 23. He finished first in August's open primary; the second Democrat in the race, Tom Begich, later withdrew.

He succeeds Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy, who was barred by term limits from seeking another four years. Wilson, a former talk-show host who owns a garbage-collection company, was the Republican backed by President Donald Trump.

## Part of a wider midterm wave

The Associated Press described the Alaska result as part of a national midterm wave that delivered sweeping wins for Democrats in state governments. With Alaska, Democrats will control the governor's office in 30 states, including several they flipped after years of Republican control, among them Iowa, Georgia, Ohio and Texas.

The state's last Democratic governor, Tony Knowles, left office in 2002 after serving two terms. Kreiss-Tomkins told supporters that Alaskans had "voted for change," and said he would take office facing a short-term budget surplus driven by higher oil prices.

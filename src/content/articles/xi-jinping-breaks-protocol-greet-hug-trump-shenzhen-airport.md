China's President Xi Jinping broke with decades of diplomatic convention on Wednesday, waiting on the tarmac at Shenzhen's airport to greet Donald Trump in person as the US president stepped off Air Force One. The two men shook hands at the foot of the aircraft's stairs, then embraced, before walking together along a red carpet lined with performers waving pom-poms, flowers and flags.

The welcome at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport was a deliberate piece of stagecraft, and a pointed one. Xi paid Trump a special honour by meeting him on arrival, reciprocating the gesture Trump made in September, when he personally greeted Xi on the tarmac at Joint Base Andrews during the Chinese leader's state visit to Washington. American presidents typically receive visiting heads of state at the White House, not on the runway.

Chinese leaders almost never greet foreign counterparts at the airport. Xi did not do so when Trump visited Beijing in May, and his surprise appearance in Shenzhen made him the first Chinese paramount leader in decades to welcome a visiting leader on arrival, according to AFP.

The contrast with the day's other arrival was hard to miss. Russian President Vladimir Putin landed in Shenzhen earlier on Wednesday, but he was met by Cai Qi, Xi's chief of staff, rather than by Xi himself — despite the two leaders' longstanding friendship and their frequent public displays of closeness.

The warmth on the tarmac sets the tone for a consequential 24 hours. On Thursday, Xi is due to host Trump and Putin for a first-of-its-kind trilateral meeting, expected to touch on nuclear arms control, Iran, Ukraine, trade and the AI chips at the heart of the US-China technology rivalry. Trump arrived in Shenzhen from the Philippines, where he had attended the annual summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations.

Whether the personal rapport on display at the airport translates into anything concrete is an open question. The greeting was choreographed to signal that Beijing values its relationship with Washington — and that Xi is willing to break his own protocol to say so. The talks that follow will show whether the gesture was the start of a deal, or only a photograph.

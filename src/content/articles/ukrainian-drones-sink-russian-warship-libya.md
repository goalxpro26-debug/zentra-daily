Ukraine says its forces sank a Russian warship off the coast of Libya in a naval drone operation on Sunday, in Kyiv's furthest strike yet on Russian military assets beyond the Black Sea.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that seven Ukrainian-made naval drones sank the corvette Merkury, one of Russia's newest warships, and destroyed a Ka-27 combat helicopter carried on board. The attack took place in the port of Benghazi, in eastern Libya, some 2,400 kilometres from the front line in Ukraine.

"A exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia's war," Zelenskyy said in a post on X, praising the crews behind the attack.

The Merkury, a 1,800-tonne Steregushchiy-class corvette about 344 feet long, was commissioned in 2023, a year after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Libyan authorities said the ship had stopped in Benghazi to refuel and resupply while en route back to Russia. Video released by Ukraine showed sea drones speeding toward the vessel before a large explosion erupted at the dock.

Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces also hit a second target, the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said Moscow used to transport weapons. Ukraine's security service said the drones were carried to the area aboard a mothership and launched from there, and that Russian forces tried to intercept them from the air, sea and shore.

Libyan officials said at least four drones were intercepted while two struck the corvette, and that at least 10 people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, were wounded. Libya's eastern health ministry said all crew members had been rescued and were receiving treatment. Satellite images showed the warship smouldering and partially submerged in the water at Benghazi.

Russia made no immediate comment on the attack. There was no independent confirmation of the full extent of the damage to the two vessels, and the United Nations has called for an independent investigation into the drone attacks.

The operation is the latest in a series of long-range Ukrainian strikes on Russian assets far from the battlefield. The Merkury was the second Russian ship hit over the weekend, after a cargo vessel was struck in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, and the Russian Black Sea Fleet has lost more than 20 vessels since the start of the war.

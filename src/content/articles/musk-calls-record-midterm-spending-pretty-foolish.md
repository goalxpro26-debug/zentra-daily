Elon Musk has said he feels "pretty foolish" about the record sum he spent backing Republicans in the US midterm elections, after voters handed the party sweeping defeats.

Musk was the election's biggest individual donor, giving $446 million (£339 million) to support President Donald Trump's party, much of it aimed at races in Texas, where many of his companies are based. Speaking virtually on Wednesday at the New York Times DealBook Summit in New York, he said the money had bought him little.

"I feel pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seeing much for it," he said, adding that the result showed campaign cash alone could not decide an election. He added that, given the chance to do it again, he would probably have spent less.

It was a striking change of tone from a man who, at a Trump rally in 2024, joked that he was "not just Maga, I'm dark Maga". On Wednesday he distanced himself from the movement altogether. "I'm not Maga," he said, adding that if the label meant "blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don't have that."

The scale of the spending was without precedent for a donor who was not a candidate. According to BBC analysis, at least $45 million of Musk's money went to support Ken Paxton, the Republican candidate for a US Senate seat in Texas — who lost to Democrat James Talarico. In the Texas governor's race, Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated Republican Governor Greg Abbott, ending more than three decades of Republican control of the state. Musk described Hinojosa as "going to be quite moderate".

The defeats were part of a wider Democratic sweep. Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress, flipping seats in places Republicans had held for decades, in a bruising defeat for Trump.

Musk had backed Trump's second White House bid and served for several months at the start of Trump's second term as an unpaid special government employee, leading the Department of Government Efficiency.

The new Democratic majorities have pledged greater scrutiny of tech companies, including controversial data centres. Democratic lawmakers have also suggested that SpaceX — which has gained an estimated $10 billion in new government contracts since Trump was re-elected — should be investigated.

For Musk, the midterms were an expensive lesson in the limits of political money. He spent more than any private donor in history on a single election cycle, and the candidates he backed most heavily lost anyway. Whether he returns to big political spending is now an open question — and one his own remarks suggest he will think twice about.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear whether Musk will scale back his political donations permanently or simply redirect them — he gave no firm commitment about future spending. How the new Democratic Congress handles its promised scrutiny of SpaceX also remains to be seen.

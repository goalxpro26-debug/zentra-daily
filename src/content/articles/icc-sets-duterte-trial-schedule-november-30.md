The International Criminal Court has set out the full schedule for the trial of former Philippine president Rodrigo Duterte, with proceedings due to open on November 30 and evidence hearings running into April 2027, under an order issued by Trial Chamber III on November 26.

The trial will begin with opening statements, followed immediately by the first prosecution witness. Prosecutors have been given two hours and 15 minutes for their opening statement, the Common Legal Representatives of Victims one hour and 30 minutes, and Duterte's defence one hour to outline its case. The court will sit until its judicial recess on December 18, then resume in the new year.

Each trial day will run in four one-hour sessions: 10 to 11 a.m., 11:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m., 1:30 to 2:30 p.m., and 3 to 4 p.m. Hague time. In Manila, that is 5 to 6 p.m., 6:30 to 7:30 p.m., 8:30 to 9:30 p.m., and 10 to 11 p.m. The timetable was adjusted on expert advice to build in regular breaks during the day.

The chamber has also fixed the first three blocks of evidence hearings for 2027: January 12 to February 19, March 1 to 24, and April 5 to 30. Dates for hearings after April 30 will be set in due course. Each side must provide a schedule of its forthcoming witnesses at least four weeks before a block begins, and the chamber has told the parties to organise appearances so there are no gaps between one witness finishing and the next beginning.

About 100 prosecution witnesses are expected to testify, the court's spokesperson, Oriane Huber Maillet, said on Tuesday. Duterte faces three counts of alleged crimes against humanity, tied to incidents during his time as mayor of Davao City and in the early part of his presidency. He was arrested on March 12, 2025, so the trial will open roughly 20 months later. His defence team has contested the charges.

The schedule gives both sides and the victims' representatives a clear runway for the first time since the case was confirmed. The opening statements on November 30 will be the first public test of how each side frames a trial that has been closely watched in the Philippines, where families of those killed during Duterte's drug war have been seeking justice at home and abroad.

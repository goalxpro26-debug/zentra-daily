British comedian Russell Brand has been sentenced to ten years and six months in prison, after a jury in London convicted him of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

The sentence was handed down at Southwark Crown Court on Monday, November 9, 2026. Brand, 51, was convicted on Thursday, November 5, following a trial that lasted around four weeks. He had pleaded not guilty and has denied engaging in any non-consensual sexual activity.

The jury found that Brand raped one woman and sexually assaulted three others, in offences committed between 2004 and 2009. He was acquitted of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault.

## The sentence

Judge Joel Bennathan imposed separate sentences for each conviction: nine years for the rape, and nine months, 18 months and eight months for the three sexual assaults. The assault sentences run concurrently with each other and consecutively with the rape sentence, giving a total of ten years and six months.

Brand must serve two-thirds of the sentence in custody before he is eligible for release on licence.

In his sentencing remarks, the judge said Brand had believed that "fame meant you could act how you liked, when you liked, unhindered by the rules by which decent people live." He also praised the determination and bravery of the women who came forward, noting that they had been confronted and told they had lied or twisted the truth, on Brand's instructions.

The judge took some circumstances into account when setting the sentence, including Brand's lack of previous convictions, the fact that 17 years had passed since his last offence, and the time he has spent helping people struggling with addiction. He said the sentence for the rape would otherwise have been a year longer.

## The trial

The trial began on October 12 and featured testimony from six accusers, as well as from Brand himself. Prosecutors told the court that Brand used his celebrity status to pursue women, describing him as a "calculated sexual predator" — a description the judge echoed in his sentencing remarks. Brand testified that he had "misused" his fame to have sexual encounters with women, while maintaining that everything was consensual.

The case began with a joint investigation by The Sunday Times, The Times and Channel 4's Dispatches programme in 2023, which reported allegations against Brand spanning 1999 to 2012. Charges were brought in 2025, covering offences said to involve six women between 1999 and 2009.

## What happens next

In a statement on November 9, Metropolitan Police Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy said Brand "has been held accountable for his crimes" and that he hoped the outcome would give the victims "some measure of closure and allows them to continue rebuilding their lives."

One victim said in a statement that she was grateful to the police and the prosecution "who have been with me throughout this process," while another said she finally felt believed.

London's Metropolitan Police has said Brand remains under investigation over two further allegations of rape, including one involving a woman who was 16 at the time.

A military-run court in Jordan sentenced three men to death on Sunday for a gun attack on a Greek Orthodox church last month that killed nine people, the Jordanian military said in a statement.

The State Security Court handed down death sentences by hanging after convicting the men on terrorism charges. They were found guilty of carrying out terrorist attacks that resulted in death, as well as weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation.

The three men — two Jordanian brothers and a relative by marriage — fled after the attack and were arrested three days later, according to the authorities.

## The attack in Karak

On October 23, gunmen stormed a funeral at St George's Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud village, in Jordan's southern Karak governorate, and opened fire. Nine people were killed and 25 wounded, Al Jazeera reported.

The shooting was one of the deadliest militant attacks in Jordan in years. King Abdullah II vowed after the attack that those responsible would face the harshest penalties.

## A swift verdict

The ruling came 23 days after the attack, an unusually fast timetable for a terrorism trial. The Jordanian military announced the court's decision in a statement on Sunday, saying the State Security Court had sentenced to death "the three criminals who carried out the terrorist attack" at the church.

The verdict underscores how seriously Jordan's government has treated the attack, which struck a Christian congregation during a funeral and rattled a country that has largely been spared the kind of sectarian violence seen elsewhere in the region.

No date for the executions was announced, and the reports did not say whether the men would appeal.

Pete Crow-Armstrong is the National League's Most Valuable Player, and the vote was not close. The Chicago Cubs center fielder was named the unanimous winner of the 2026 NL MVP Award on Thursday, collecting all 30 first-place votes from the Baseball Writers' Association of America.

Crow-Armstrong, 24, put together one of the finest all-around seasons in recent memory. He hit .280 with 45 home runs and 41 stolen bases, becoming just the seventh player in major league history to reach the 40-40 mark in a single season — and the first Cub ever to do it. He tied for the major league lead in home runs, led baseball with 119 runs scored, and piled up 357 total bases and 84 extra-base hits.

The advanced numbers were just as emphatic. Crow-Armstrong led the majors with a 9.8 WAR by Baseball-Reference's measure and a 10.6 WAR on FanGraphs, finishing well clear of the rest of the league. He was also the game's best defensive center fielder, winning both the Gold Glove and the Platinum Glove, which goes to the top overall defender in each league. He is the first player ever to win an MVP and a Platinum Glove in the same season.

## Ending Ohtani's run

The award ends Shohei Ohtani's streak of three consecutive MVP wins. Ohtani, the Dodgers' two-way star, finished second after earning 26 of the 30 second-place votes, for 264 points. Milwaukee's Brice Turang placed third. Crow-Armstrong's 420 points were the maximum possible under the voting system.

It is the fourth consecutive season in which the National League winner was unanimous, and the 11th unanimous NL MVP in history. Crow-Armstrong is the ninth Cub to win the award and the first since Kris Bryant in 2016 — and the first in franchise history to win it unanimously.

The season also brought him the Players Choice Awards for Player of the Year and NL Outstanding Player, voted on by his fellow players, along with the Hank Aaron Award as the league's top offensive performer.

## A unanimous year across baseball

Crow-Armstrong's win came on the same night Houston's Yordan Alvarez won the American League MVP, also unanimously. The two are the third pair of unanimous MVP winners in the same year, and 2026 is the first season in which all four MVP and Cy Young winners were unanimous choices.

For the Cubs, the award is the payoff on a player they committed to early: Crow-Armstrong agreed to a six-year, $115 million contract in March, before the season that turned him from a promising young outfielder into the game's most complete player.

Apple is shutting down its official Support Community, the user-to-user forum where customers have traded troubleshooting advice for 28 years. The company posted a notice on the forum on Tuesday night saying it will close permanently on November 20, 2026, and that the site is now read-only: no new questions or replies can be posted.

"The Apple Support Community will permanently close on November 20, 2026. Starting today, the site will no longer accept new posts and is available in a read-only state," the notice said, directing users to Apple's official Support site for help. The final message posted to the forum appears to have gone up at 5:49 p.m. Pacific Time on Tuesday, minutes before posting was switched off.

## A forum that predates the iPhone

The community is one of the oldest fixtures of Apple's online presence. It launched in 1998 as the Apple Tech Exchange, when the company was still rebuilding after Steve Jobs' return and the iMac was brand new. It became Apple Discussions in 2000, was relaunched as the Apple Support Communities in 2011, and took its current name in 2022.

For most of that time it worked the same way: a user with a broken Mac, a misbehaving iPhone or a puzzling software bug could post a question and get an answer from another user, sometimes with an Apple employee stepping in with a fix or an update. Millions of threads accumulated into a searchable archive of fixes that often turned up in search results before Apple's own support pages did.

Apple has not given a detailed public explanation. The company told MacRumors it decided to retire the forum "after a careful evaluation of strategy and customer needs," and its notice points users toward official support channels instead. Alternatives for peer help include independent forums such as the MacRumors Forums and Reddit.

## What happens to the archive

What remains unclear is what happens to nearly three decades of archived discussions after November 20. Apple has not said whether the threads will stay readable, be taken offline entirely, or be preserved in some other form. The login button on the forum now routes to an internal employee page, suggesting staff access will continue even as public posting ends.

The move follows a similar step by Verizon earlier this year, which also switched its community forum to read-only before closing it. For Apple users, the practical effect is immediate: the fastest route to an answer from another customer is gone, and the official support site — with its articles, chat and phone options — is now the company's only front door for help.

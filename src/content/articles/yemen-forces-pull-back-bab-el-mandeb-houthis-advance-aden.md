Saudi-backed forces in Yemen have pulled back from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait to defend the temporary capital, Aden, in a major setback for the country's internationally recognised government and its Saudi backers.

The Southern Armed Forces, the main military force fighting for the government in southern Yemen, announced on Sunday that it was redeploying its troops from Dhubab Airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area, west of Taiz governorate. The statement said the move was taken "based on the priority of securing the capital, Aden, and the neighbouring governorates, as well as strengthening the front lines and addressing existing threats."

The announcement effectively ends a six-week government counteroffensive aimed at driving the Iran-aligned Houthis from the mouth of the Red Sea and securing a key oil route. The Houthis made rapid gains in September, capturing the coast along the Red Sea and the strait that controls access to it. A government offensive last month retook key positions around the strait, but failed to drive the Houthis entirely from the area or to retake the city of al-Mocha further up the coast.

Yemeni military sources said government forces around the strait had redeployed to al-Suqyah, about 150 kilometres (95 miles) west of Aden, though they were still trying to push Houthi fighters from coastal areas nearby. In the highlands north of Aden, the Houthis are now positioned just outside the district of Tur al-Bahah, about 80 kilometres (50 miles) from the city. They have besieged Taiz, Yemen's third-largest city, where humanitarian conditions are worsening: the city hosts about half of the 300,000 people displaced across Yemen by the latest fighting, aid supplies are unable to enter and civilians are unable to leave.

There was no immediate response from the Houthis or from Saudi Arabia.

The retreat raises the prospect of new Houthi advances toward Aden, the base of the internationally recognised government since the Houthis drove it from the capital, Sanaa, more than a decade ago. It also comes as the Houthis have intensified drone and missile attacks on Saudi Arabia, grounding flights with strikes on its main airports and putting global oil markets on edge by targeting energy facilities.

The pullback unfolded on the same day Iran fired drones at US bases in Bahrain, headquarters of the US Fifth Fleet in the Gulf. Bahrain said it intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks, the first such interceptions by its air defences since September. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had attacked a US military base in Bahrain with six drones in retaliation for what it called "hostile US actions." The twin pressures — in Yemen and in Bahrain — underline how the wider regional conflict is squeezing the United States and its Gulf allies on two fronts at once.

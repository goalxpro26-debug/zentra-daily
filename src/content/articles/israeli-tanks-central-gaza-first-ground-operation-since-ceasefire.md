Israeli tanks advanced into central Gaza on Wednesday, in the first broad ground operation since the ceasefire between Israel and Hamas was signed more than a year ago, hospital officials and witnesses said.

Three Palestinians were killed during the day's fighting, according to hospital officials. Al Jazeera correspondents on the ground reported Israeli tanks operating near Salah al-Din Street, east of the Nuseirat refugee camp and north of the Bureij camp, while witnesses told Reuters that some tanks had rolled onto the main road itself, west of the so-called Yellow Line.

The Yellow Line, established under the October 2025 ceasefire agreement, marks the boundary of the area of Gaza still under Israeli military control — roughly two-thirds of the territory. The tanks' advance across it was described by residents as the first ground raid since the ceasefire took effect.

## What the Israeli military said

Earlier on Wednesday, the Israeli army said the operation aimed to "eliminate a threat" to its forces operating in the area of the Yellow Line, and that it was targeting Hamas infrastructure outside the areas it controls.

"We reserve the freedom to act and manoeuvre whenever necessary to eliminate any immediate threat in the Gaza Strip," the military said in a statement.

An Israeli military spokesperson told NBC News the operation was aimed at Hamas infrastructure west of the Yellow Line in central Gaza, and said forces had also struck a militant in southern Gaza earlier in the day.

## Families flee as shells fall

Palestinians began fleeing parts of central Gaza as the operation got under way. Al Jazeera's Hind Khoudary, reporting from Gaza City, said people in eastern Maghazi and Bureij and those living close to Salah al-Din Street were evacuating amid explosions, live ammunition and artillery shelling.

An Israeli air strike targeted the town of al-Mughraqa, north of Nuseirat, killing one person and wounding several others, an official at al-Awda Hospital told Al Jazeera. Two further people were killed in separate drone attacks before the operation was announced — one in the Sheikh Ijlin neighbourhood of southwestern Gaza City and one in Deir el-Balah, hospital officials said.

Gaza's Ministry of Health said hospitals had recorded seven deaths and received 15 wounded people in the previous 24 hours.

## Hamas calls it a ceasefire violation

Hamas condemned the operation as a "flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement", saying forced evacuation orders and displacement under the threat of bombardment amounted to a war crime. The group said it held Israel and the United States responsible for the repercussions of the operation.

The October 2025 ceasefire halted major fighting in the Israel-Gaza war, but it has not stopped Israeli attacks or secured Hamas' disarmament. Whether Wednesday's ground push marks a limited raid or a wider return to ground operations remains unclear, and neither side has said how long the operation will last.

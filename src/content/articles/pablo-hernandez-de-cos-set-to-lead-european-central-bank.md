European leaders have agreed to nominate Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next president of the European Central Bank, choosing a career central banker to steer the eurozone through a period of stubborn inflation and strained public finances. The 55-year-old Spaniard will succeed Christine Lagarde, who steps down on February 7, several months before her term was due to end.

De Cos is currently general manager of the Bank for International Settlements, the Basel-based forum for the world's major central banks, and previously served as governor of the Bank of Spain. He will become the first Spaniard to lead the ECB, and its fifth president since the bank was founded.

The decision fell into place on Thursday after the Netherlands dropped its backing for its own candidate, Klaas Knot, the former Dutch central bank governor and the only other contender. Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten agreed to support de Cos, EU officials said, clearing the way for a consensus nomination. EU ambassadors were due to meet on Thursday afternoon to launch the written procedure under which capitals formally sign off on the recommendation.

The nomination is part of a wider reshuffle of the ECB's leadership. The same recommendation names Laurence Boone to run the bank's market operations and Markus Brunnermeier as chief economist. European Council President António Costa confirmed the agreement, and French President Emmanuel Macron welcomed what he called a balanced trio for the euro area.

Formal approval still lies ahead. The European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council must endorse the candidacy, with a final seal from EU leaders expected at December's European Council summit. The Parliament's opinion is not binding, and with political agreement now in place, the appointment is widely seen as assured.

De Cos takes over an institution facing a difficult inheritance. Inflation in the eurozone is still running above the bank's 2% target, government debt tensions in France have unsettled bond markets, and the war involving Iran has added to uncertainty over energy and growth. He is also expected to advance the ECB's digital euro project, with a pilot planned for 2027. He is regarded as more dovish on interest rates than Knot, his rival for the post, but he also has a record of preaching fiscal discipline.

Markets took the news in stride. The euro edged up 0.12% to $1.0988, while German 10-year government bond yields, the benchmark for the wider eurozone, rose 5 basis points to 3.268%.

De Cos will oversee monetary policy for 21 member states and about 350 million people. His appointment closes a closely watched succession race and hands the eurozone's most powerful economic institution to a technocrat who has spent his career inside central banking — first as an economist at Spain's central bank, then as its governor, and most recently at the helm of the BIS.

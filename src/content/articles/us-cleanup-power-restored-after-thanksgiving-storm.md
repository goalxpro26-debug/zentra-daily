Heavy rain that soaked the East Coast over Thanksgiving began to ease on Friday, as utility crews restored power to hundreds of thousands of homes and businesses and coastal towns started clearing up after days of flooding, high winds and water rescues.

The storms snarled holiday travel, brought down trees and power lines, and forced water rescues from New York to Virginia. Outages peaked on Thursday at more than 900,000 customers, and had fallen by about half by Friday afternoon, according to the tracking site poweroutage.us. Pennsylvania and New Jersey were the hardest hit, with about 90,000 customers still without power in Pennsylvania alone.

In New York City, preparations were under way to hold the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade on Saturday, after the parade was postponed for the first time ever because of rain and high winds. More than 1,100 flights were delayed and at least 116 cancelled by Friday morning, according to flight tracker FlightAware.

The damage along the coast was extensive. In Norfolk, Virginia, crews rescued 39 people and two dogs from floodwater. Part of a fishing pier collapsed in Virginia Beach, closing a three-mile stretch of beach and boardwalk. Roofs caved in at Ocean City and Rockville in Maryland, and in Hampton, Virginia, the storm toppled a weathered, leafless tree at Grandview Beach known locally as the "Grieving Tree". In Avalon, New Jersey, shopkeepers were pumping saltwater out of their stores after Thanksgiving brought some of the most significant tides in the seaside community's history. Baltimore recorded 3.04 inches of rain on Thursday, a record for the date at the city's airport.

In Maryland, nearly two million water customers in Montgomery and Prince George's counties were told on Friday that they could resume normal water use, after being urged to conserve during the storm. Coastal flood warnings remained posted from New Jersey to Virginia.

The bad weather was not confined to the coast. Wet snow fell as far south as the Tennessee Valley, freezing rain moved across northern New England, and parts of eastern Missouri received as much as 15 inches of snow. The National Weather Service said conditions were expected to improve across the eastern United States on Friday, "but this huge system will take some time to weaken."

Another round of rain and strong winds is expected to reach the East Coast by Sunday evening, potentially disrupting the Monday morning commute — a reminder, for towns still pumping out basements, that the clean-up may not be over yet.

Ukraine has defended a weekend drone attack on Russian ships in Libyan waters as a legitimate act of self-defence, after Libya's government in Tripoli condemned the operation and said it would not accept being drawn into the war.

Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said on Tuesday that both Russian naval vessels and cargo ships carrying military equipment were valid targets, and that Ukraine's right to self-defence is set out in Article 51 of the United Nations Charter.

The operation sank the Russian naval corvette Merkury in the eastern port of Benghazi, according to Libya's Tripoli-based Government of National Unity, which condemned the attack on Monday. Benghazi is controlled by military commander Khalifa Haftar, whose Libyan National Army has ties with Russia, while the internationally recognised government in Tripoli controls western Libya.

"Ukraine is not 'dragging' anyone into the war or 'transferring' the war anywhere," Sybiha wrote on the Telegram messaging app. "Ukraine respects the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Libya. We emphasize that Ukraine's actions are aimed at countering Russian armed aggression and are not intended to harm Libya or its people."

Sybiha argued that international law does not limit the right to self-defence to the territory where the aggression takes place, saying it can also apply on the territory of the aggressor or in international waters. Russia, he said, was the one endangering third countries by using their civilian infrastructure for its military purposes.

The Institute for the Study of War said the corvette had stopped over for several days at Benghazi to refuel and resupply under a cooperation agreement, while escorting cargo ships capable of carrying military supplies to Russia.

The eastern government in Benghazi said on Monday there were indications the naval drones had been launched from western Libya, and asked the United Nations to investigate allegations that Ukraine has deployed military experts there. Russia's defence minister has not commented publicly on the attack.

In a separate development, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy confirmed in an interview published on Tuesday that Ukraine was responsible for a drone strike on Saturday on a Russian cargo vessel, the Sparta II, in the international waters of the Gulf of Finland. He said the vessel was a legitimate target because it was carrying weapons. Russia's Transport Ministry said on Tuesday that the damaged vessel had been towed back to the Russian port of St Petersburg.

The Libya operation marks a widening of the war's geography: a Ukrainian strike carried out far from the front lines, against Russian naval assets using a foreign port. How far Kyiv is willing to press that logic — and how Libya's rival governments respond — remains to be seen.

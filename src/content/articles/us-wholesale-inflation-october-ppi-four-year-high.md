US wholesale inflation accelerated in October to its fastest annual pace in nearly four years, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, as rising food, fuel and electronics costs pushed producer prices higher for a second straight month.

The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.3% from September and 6.5% over the 12 months ended in October — the largest yearly increase since November 2022, when prices rose 7.4%. The monthly gain was slightly smaller than the 0.4% economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected, and the annual figure came in just below the 6.6% forecast. Even so, the yearly rate climbed from 6.3% in September, extending a run of hot readings.

Food did much of the pushing. Food prices rose 1.3% in October, the fastest monthly increase since July 2022, after a 1.1% rise in September. Wholesale prices for fresh and dry vegetables jumped 26.7%, following a 30.7% leap the month before. Goods prices overall rose 0.5% in October, after a 2.5% jump in September, and were up 10.2% over the year — their largest annual rise since October 2022.

Energy remained a pressure point. The war in Iran and the resulting supply shock have sent gas and diesel prices markedly higher in recent months, and high diesel costs are now lifting transportation and warehousing costs as well.

Stripping out food and energy, core producer prices rose 0.3% on the month and 5% over the year, matching a pace last seen in January 2023. One standout was electronic components and accessories, up 4.2% in the month — a six-month high — and 31.7% from a year earlier, the fastest annual rate in bureau records going back to December 1966. The surge is tied to massive investment in artificial intelligence technology and equipment.

## What it means for the Federal Reserve

The report is the last major inflation reading before the Federal Reserve's December meeting, and it points in the same direction as this week's hotter-than-expected consumer price data. Markets expect a quarter-point rate hike, although Fed Governor Christopher Waller has said a half-point move could be necessary. Nationwide senior economist Ben Ayers said lingering fuel-price effects could stretch into 2027 and warrant further tightening.

Producer prices tend to lead consumer prices, so the report raises the prospect that the factory-gate inflation now showing up in wholesale data will reach household budgets in the months ahead — a concern for families already stretching to cover groceries, rent and everyday costs.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear whether December brings a quarter-point move or something larger, or how much of the wholesale increase will be passed on to consumers rather than absorbed by businesses. The bureau is scheduled to publish its November inflation report on December 10 — one day after the Fed's decision — so policymakers will act on October's figures and their own projections rather than fresh data.

China's anti-corruption watchdog has placed Chen Jining, the Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a member of the ruling Politburo, under investigation — the most senior civilian official to fall in President Xi Jinping's widening purge of the party's top ranks.

State news agency Xinhua announced on Tuesday that Chen, 62, is suspected of "serious violations of discipline and law", the party's standard phrasing for corruption cases. The investigation is being conducted by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Supervisory Commission. No details of the alleged wrongdoing were given, and Chen could not be reached for comment.

Chen is the fourth member of the 24-member Politburo to be investigated since 2025, after generals He Weidong and Zhang Youxia and former Xinjiang party chief Ma Xingrui. The body, formed after the party congress in 2022, now has 21 active members. It is the largest purge at this level since 1976, according to The Wall Street Journal.

## A rising star, now fallen

Chen had an unusual path to the top of Chinese politics. An environmental scientist by training, he earned a doctorate from Imperial College London and rose through Tsinghua University to become its president, before serving as Beijing's mayor. He joined the Politburo in October 2022 and took over as party secretary of Shanghai — China's largest city and financial centre — succeeding Li Qiang, who went on to become premier.

The Shanghai post is one of the most powerful in China and has traditionally been a stepping stone to the Politburo Standing Committee, the pinnacle of party power. Chen was widely seen as a candidate for promotion to that body at the party's next congress, scheduled for the second half of 2027, when Xi is expected to seek a fourth five-year term as party chief.

His last public appearance was last Thursday, when he inspected the headquarters of the state-owned shipping group China Cosco Shipping in Shanghai. On Tuesday evening, the Shanghai party committee's standing committee held an expanded meeting to brief officials on the investigation. No replacement for Chen has been named; the city's mayor, its number-two official, could stand in until a permanent appointment is made.

Chen is the first sitting Shanghai party chief to be placed under investigation since Chen Liangyu was removed from the post in 2006.

## A widening campaign

The probe is the latest escalation in Xi's anti-corruption drive, launched after he took power in late 2012 and intensified in recent years. At the party Central Committee's fifth plenum in late October, the expulsions of 33 high-ranking members were formalised, nearly half of them military officers.

For officials of Chen's rank, the public announcement of an investigation by the discipline inspection commission generally signals eventual political downfall. What remains unknown is what specific conduct Chen is accused of, and how the party will fill one of its most important regional posts as it prepares for the 2027 congress.

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has ordered his country to spread its battlefield nuclear weapons more widely, state media reported on Thursday, days after he ignored President Donald Trump's offer of a meeting during Trump's visit to Asia.

North Korea's official Korean Central News Agency said Kim inspected nuclear-material production facilities on Wednesday, including two factories — one newly built and one being expanded — along with a nuclear-powered equipment production base and a nuclear research institute. The agency did not say where the sites are located.

Kim said production capacity for nuclear material was being systematically expanded and described North Korea's status as a nuclear weapons state as "absolutely inviolable." He called the deployment of low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons "across multiple domains for different missions" an important and urgent task, given what he called the inevitable reality of facing what he described as the most dangerous enemy state — a reference to South Korea.

Tactical nuclear weapons are lower-yield warheads designed for battlefield use, unlike strategic weapons built for longer-range strikes. North Korea has focused on developing them in recent years amid heightened animosity with South Korea. Photographs released by KCNA showed Kim and senior military officials walking between rows of what appeared to be centrifuges used to enrich uranium, and touring halls displaying what analysts said looked like miniaturised warheads, including the Hwasan-31 design first shown in 2023.

Kim justified the buildup by citing what he called variable nuclear threats approaching the Korean Peninsula "moment by moment." He accused the United States of pushing its regional allies toward nuclear-powered submarines and of committing to share nuclear weapons with them, and he called international demands for denuclearisation an infringement of sovereignty. He also warned that attempts to deny North Korea's nuclear status would amount to "an open declaration of war."

Analysts say the visit was meant to show off an evolving arsenal and to make clear that Kim will not discuss giving up his nuclear weapons in any future talks. The trip was his fourth visit to a nuclear facility since September 2024, and it came after a widely expected meeting with Trump failed to happen during the president's trip to China last week. The visit likely also served as a check on this year's progress in the nuclear programme ahead of a year-end Workers' Party conference.

South Korea's Defence Ministry said it takes Kim's comments on battlefield nuclear weapons seriously and maintains firm military readiness against Pyongyang's nuclear threats under its alliance with the United States.

What remains unknown is where the facilities Kim visited are located. Outside experts have assessed that a uranium enrichment site North Korea disclosed in June is likely an additional facility at its main Yongbyon complex, and analysts are still studying the new photographs. There is also no sign of renewed dialogue: with the talks offer unanswered, the visit reads as Pyongyang setting its terms — recognition as a nuclear power, with no discussion of disarmament.

US stocks fell sharply on Friday, dragging the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average into correction territory and wiping out the Nasdaq Composite's gains for the year, as a sell-off in artificial-intelligence shares gathered pace.

The S&P 500 fell 3.7%, its seventh straight daily decline — its longest losing run since 2020. The Dow dropped 1.9%, or more than 900 points. The Nasdaq plunged 5.2%, or more than 1,200 points, its second-largest one-day points fall on record.

Both the S&P 500 and the Dow are now more than 10% below their recent record highs, the threshold generally used to define a correction. The Nasdaq has been in correction territory since November 5 and is now down nearly 3% for the year. The other two major indexes have kept less than 1% of their 2026 gains.

The week was the market's worst since the first week of April 2025, when President Donald Trump's reciprocal-tariff announcement sent stocks into a tailspin. The Dow finished the week down 8%, the S&P 500 down 8.6% and the Nasdaq down 10%. Just six weeks ago, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq were trading at record highs.

The slide was driven by the unravelling of the AI trade. Anthropic, the AI lab whose stock-market debut on Thursday was the biggest initial public offering on record, fell nearly 9% to around $89 on its second day of trading, giving back much of its first-day gains. The shares debuted at $111.50, well above the $83 IPO price, but closed Thursday at about $97. The listing had been seen as a test of confidence in AI shares; investors took little comfort from it.

Chip stocks, hit hard in recent weeks, fell again. Nvidia, the AI bellwether, dropped 14% and led the Dow's decliners. The company has lost a third of its value in the past two weeks as investors weigh the impact of Jensen Huang's departure from the chief executive role. Advanced Micro Devices fell 13%, while Broadcom, Marvell Technology and Micron all posted double-digit declines. Memory-chip makers were among the day's biggest losers in the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq: SanDisk fell 16%, and Seagate and Western Digital each dropped 12%.

Wall Street's fear gauge, the Cboe Volatility Index, jumped 26% to near 36, its highest mark since last year's tariff turmoil.

The retreat spread beyond equities. Bitcoin traded at $58,100, its lowest level since June. Gold futures fell 4.5% to $3,525 an ounce, their lowest since September 2025. Oil moved the other way, with West Texas Intermediate crude up 1.1% to $84.10 a barrel, while the yield on the 10-year Treasury note fell to 4.94% from 5.05%.

What happens next will turn on whether AI shares stabilise and whether the S&P 500 can hold above 7,000 — a level traders have been watching as a line of support. For now, a rally that carried stocks to records six weeks ago has given back almost everything it gained this year.

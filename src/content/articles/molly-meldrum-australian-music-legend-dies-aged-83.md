Ian "Molly" Meldrum, the music journalist and television presenter who shaped Australian popular music for more than half a century, has died aged 83.

Meldrum died peacefully at 5.30am on Monday at Epworth Hospital in Richmond, Melbourne, after a long period of ill health, his family said. A state funeral will be held to honour him, Victorian Premier Ben Carroll said, honouring a wish made by his family.

Born Ian Alexander Meldrum on 29 January 1943 in Orbost, in eastern Victoria, he began writing for the weekly music paper Go-Set in 1966. When the ABC launched Countdown in November 1974, he joined as talent coordinator. Within a year he was in front of the camera, and for almost 13 years, until the show's final episode in July 1987, he was its beating heart.

In the days before streaming and music video channels, Countdown was how Australians discovered new music. The show brought the world's biggest acts into living rooms, from ABBA, David Bowie and Elton John to AC/DC, Kylie Minogue and INXS, and gave homegrown artists their first national exposure.

Meldrum's enthusiasm could make or break a career. His catchphrase, "do yourself a favour", attached to a record or an act he believed in, could move sales and fill venues. He became a household name in his trademark Stetson hat, known for his bumbling charm, his unpredictable interviews and his undisguised love of the music.

That unpredictability produced some of Australian television's most famous moments, including a 1977 interview with the then Prince Charles in which he referred to the Queen as "your mum". He was also a record producer, and a passionate supporter of the St Kilda Football Club, where he helped start the cheer squad, and the Melbourne Storm, where he was the club's number one ticket holder for many years.

His health had been fragile since December 2011, when he was placed in an induced coma after a fall from a ladder while hanging Christmas decorations. In 2014 he was inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame, together with Countdown, becoming the first non-artist to receive the honour.

Tributes poured in from across Australian music and television. His longtime friend and manager, Mark Klemens, called him a "true icon" who "opened doors for so many and entertained millions of people". Singer Kate Ceberano, who first sang in front of him as a judge at the age of 14, wrote: "He was the fan all artists wish to have." Marcia Hines thanked him for his contribution to the Australian music industry and worldwide.

Meldrum is survived by his long-term partner, Yan Wongngam, and his adopted son, Morgan Scholes. His family has asked for privacy as they grieve.

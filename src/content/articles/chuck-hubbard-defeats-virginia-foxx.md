Democrat Chuck Hubbard will unseat longtime Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx in North Carolina's 5th Congressional District, after Foxx conceded the race on Sunday following a machine recount that left Hubbard ahead by about 330 votes out of nearly 287,000 cast.

Foxx, 83, chairs the House Rules Committee, one of the most powerful posts in the chamber, deciding which bills reach the floor and how they can be amended. She has represented the district since 2005 and is the longest-serving member of North Carolina's congressional delegation. Hubbard, 64, a former journalist, will be the first Democrat to hold the seat since 1995.

Foxx requested the recount after the initial count showed her trailing by 331 votes. The recount, which wrapped up on Saturday, barely moved the numbers: Hubbard finished with 139,769 votes to Foxx's 139,439, according to the Associated Press results, a margin of roughly one tenth of one percent. Foxx called Hubbard on Sunday to congratulate him.

"After a hard-fought and thorough election process, the voters of the Fifth Congressional District exercised their Constitutional rights and made their voices heard," Foxx said in a statement. "While I am saddened that I did not emerge victorious in this election, I continue to have great faith in our nation, our state and the people of North Carolina."

The result was the last House race of the midterm elections to be decided, and it caps a sweeping Democratic performance in North Carolina and nationally. Democrats will control at least 259 House seats when the next Congress convenes in January, their largest majority since the early 1990s, according to CNN. One race remains uncalled, in Minnesota, where a recount is scheduled to begin on November 30, and runoff elections in Louisiana on December 12 will settle the final two seats.

Hubbard's win is a sharp reversal from two years ago, when he lost to Foxx by about nine points in the same district. He ran again this cycle in a year that saw Democrats flip seats in deeply Republican territory, and the contest tightened steadily after election night: Hubbard led by 99 votes in the unofficial returns, stretched the lead as provisional and cured ballots were tallied, and held on through the county canvasses and the recount.

The outcome still awaits formal certification by the North Carolina State Board of Elections, which is due to meet on Tuesday. But with Foxx's concession, the last outstanding question of the 2026 House map is effectively settled, and attention now turns to how the new Democratic majority will use its numbers in the 120th Congress.

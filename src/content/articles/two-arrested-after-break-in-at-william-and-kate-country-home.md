Two men have been arrested after a break-in at the country home of Prince William and Catherine, Princess of Wales, in what police are treating as a burglary at one of Britain's most closely watched royal addresses.

Norfolk Constabulary said officers were called just before 6pm on Monday after a report of suspicious activity at a property on the Sandringham estate in Norfolk, eastern England. The property was Anmer Hall, the couple's country retreat. Two men were arrested on the estate on suspicion of burglary and trespass and remain in police custody. The house was unoccupied at the time, and inquiries are continuing.

The Sun newspaper first reported the break-in, saying masked intruders had made their way into the house on Monday night. A search of the estate followed, involving police dogs, drones and a helicopter, according to reports of the police operation.

Nobody from the family was at Anmer Hall when the incident happened. William, Catherine and their three children — Prince George, Princess Charlotte and Prince Louis — were at their main home, Forest Lodge in Windsor, at the time. A spokesperson for William said the prince's office did not comment on security matters.

Anmer Hall is a ten-bedroom, Grade II-listed Georgian house set within the King's private Sandringham estate, where the royal family traditionally gathers for Christmas. The late Queen Elizabeth II gave the house to William and Catherine as a wedding present in 2011, and the couple use it for holidays and short breaks, while their main home is on the Windsor Castle estate west of London.

It is not yet known whether anything was taken from the house, or how the intruders gained access. Police said their investigation remains open, and the two arrested men have not been named.

The news emerged on Tuesday morning as William visited Prime Minister Andy Burnham at 10 Downing Street — a rare working visit by the heir to the throne to the prime minister's official residence. The timing turned a routine royal engagement into the backdrop for a security incident that will raise fresh questions about protection at royal homes outside London.

The Sandringham estate, in the east of England, is one of the monarch's private residences rather than a state-owned palace, and its grounds are patrolled by royal protection officers alongside local police. The break-in is the most serious security breach at a royal country home to become public in recent years, and it comes at a moment when the Wales family have made Forest Lodge their primary base, spending less time at Anmer Hall than they once did.

Police have asked anyone with information about suspicious activity around the estate on Monday evening to come forward.

Iran fired drones at a US radar installation in Bahrain on Tuesday, the third Iranian attack on American military targets in the Persian Gulf kingdom in five days — and the United States answered hours later with a strike of its own.

The exchange broke a roughly two-month lull in fighting in a war that began on February 28, when the US and Israel attacked Iran. Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy's 5th Fleet, said its air defences intercepted the drones. The US military said the attack caused no damage to American equipment and no injuries to American personnel.

Hours later, a US fighter jet struck a missile launch site on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, belonging to Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard, a US official said. The official, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military operations, said "precision munitions" were used and that the US was responding to "aggression" toward its personnel. A local official, Ahmad Nafisi, said the strike caused no damage to residential or commercial areas or infrastructure.

## A week of strikes

Tuesday's attack followed two earlier ones. On Friday, Iran targeted a US early-warning radar facility in Bahrain, and on Sunday it fired at a radar system at an American helicopter base there. Iran said it was retaliating for US strikes on Iranian communications towers and ongoing drone surveillance.

## Why the lull ended

Analysts say the renewed attacks reflect mounting pressure on Tehran. Iran has been living under a US-enforced naval blockade in and around the Strait of Hormuz, which is curbing the flow of Iranian crude and damaging its economy. Negotiations to end the war have stalled despite intensified mediation by Qatar, and Tehran-based analyst Mostafa Najafi told the Associated Press the attacks could signal a shift in Iranian strategy, from waiting and retaliating to targeting the operational capability of bases.

Egypt, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates each condemned the attack, calling it a violation of Bahraini sovereignty. Qatar said the continued attacks "undermine diplomatic efforts."

## A larger US naval presence

The US has also expanded its forces in the region. With the arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group, American naval assets across the region now exceed the force deployed when the war began, according to a new analysis by Israel's Institute for National Security Studies. The Roosevelt joins the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington, along with smaller Navy ships maintaining the blockade of Iranian shipping.

## What is still unknown

It is not yet clear whether this week's exchanges mark a brief flare-up or the start of a sustained escalation. "Tehran knows it needs to do something, but I wouldn't overestimate what we saw against Bahrain this week," said Sascha Bruchmann, a Bahrain-based defence analyst. Iranian officials have described the current situation as "no war, no peace" and vowed to end it, while saying they remain open to a negotiated deal.

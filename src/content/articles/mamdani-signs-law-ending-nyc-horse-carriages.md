New York City's horse-drawn carriages, a fixture of Central Park for more than 150 years, will disappear from the city's streets by June 2028. Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed the ban into law on Thursday at City Hall, closing a chapter that generations of visitors have associated with the park.

The law, known as Romanch's Law, stops the city from issuing new licences for carriage horses, horse-drawn cabs and their drivers, and every existing licence expires on May 31, 2028. From June 1, 2028, no horse-drawn carriage will be allowed to operate on New York's streets. The City Council passed the bill on October 22 by a vote of 40 to 7.

The law is named for Romanch Mahajan, an 18-year-old visitor from India who died on June 17, 2026, after the horse pulling his family's carriage bolted during a ride in Central Park. The bill was sponsored by Council Member Christopher Marte and had been debated for years before the fatal accident gave the campaign new urgency.

"For generations, horses lived and worked here in New York City," Mamdani said at the signing ceremony, adding that the city had changed and its laws needed to change with it. He signed the bill in City Hall's Blue Room, flanked by Council Speaker Julie Menin and former Mayor Bill de Blasio, who had pushed for the change for years.

The phase-out affects about 200 people: carriage drivers, stable hands and medallion owners. The administration says it will support them through a new Horse Carriage Worker Industry Program, with medallion buybacks for owners, stipends for drivers and stable hands, workforce training, career counselling and help starting new businesses. A full transition plan is due by April 2027. The law also requires the horses to be rehomed humanely, and forbids selling them for slaughter or transferring them to other carriage companies.

The ban is not going unchallenged. Transport Workers Union Local 100, which represents the drivers, filed a federal lawsuit on Thursday seeking to block the law, arguing that it is unconstitutional. The case will decide whether the phase-out timetable survives.

New York is now the largest metropolitan area in the United States to ban horse-drawn carriages. Supporters of the ban point to decades of accidents and the welfare of the horses; opponents say the city is ending a living tradition and the livelihoods that go with it. Both sides now wait on the courts — and on the transition plan due next spring.

Two oil tankers were struck by unidentified projectiles near the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, British maritime authorities said, the latest attacks in a sharp escalation of Iran's campaign to control shipping through the vital waterway.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations centre (UKMTO) said one tanker was hit on its stern about 20 kilometres north of Khasab, a port town on Oman's northern coast. A second tanker was struck in its engine room about 31 kilometres east of Khasab. No casualties were reported in either incident, and it was not immediately clear whether the two reports involved the same vessel.

Iran's navy said on Saturday that an oil tanker had caught fire after striking a naval mine in a restricted area east of the strait. In a statement carried by Iranian state television, the navy accused the vessel of ignoring warnings and travelling with its identification and navigation equipment switched off. That account could not be independently verified, and it was unclear whether the burning tanker was one of the vessels in the UKMTO reports.

The attacks followed a similar strike on Friday, when a tanker sailing out of the strait was hit by an unknown projectile about eight nautical miles north of Khasab and caught fire, according to UKMTO. No casualties were reported then either.

The incidents are part of a pattern that has intensified sharply over the past week. Citing statements from Iran's armed forces, Al Jazeera's correspondent in Tehran reported about 25 incidents since the previous Saturday — roughly three a day. Tehran says any ship passing through the strait must coordinate fully with Iranian authorities and follow lanes set by Iran, and warns that vessels that do not comply risk attack or striking sea mines laid by Iranian forces.

The confrontation is playing out against a war that began when the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Before the conflict, the strait carried nearly a fifth of the world's crude oil. The United States has responded to Iran's restrictions by blockading Iranian ports and deploying naval vessels to escort tankers through the waterway. A Qatari delegation visited Tehran on Friday night for two hours of talks with Iranian officials, according to Iran's deputy foreign minister — the latest diplomatic effort as the standoff grinds on.

Maritime security analysts say the threat level remains severe. UKMTO recorded six verified attacks across the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf in its latest reporting period, and weekly transits through the strait have fallen. For the crews and companies still moving oil through the narrow passage, each crossing now carries a risk that was unthinkable before the war.

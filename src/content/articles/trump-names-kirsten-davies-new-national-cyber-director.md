President Donald Trump has named Kirsten Davies as the White House's new national cyber director, replacing Sean Cairncross in a shake-up of the administration's technology leadership.

Davies currently serves as chief information officer at the Pentagon, a Senate-confirmed role she has held since December 2025. She will take over the cyber director's job in an acting capacity, according to multiple reports. Axios first reported the appointment on Thursday.

The national cyber director leads the Office of the National Cyber Director, which advises the president on cybersecurity policy and coordinates cyber strategy across the federal government. The position requires Senate confirmation, and Davies has already been through that process once, for her Pentagon post.

Cairncross had led the office since August 2025, after being confirmed by the Senate that month. Accounts of his departure differ. Two sources described it to Axios as amicable, saying Cairncross did not want to serve another two years in the role. But two White House officials told the outlet that Trump's new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, sought the change, saying Cairncross lacked the strong cyber credentials the job requires. A source close to the White House told CNN that Trump fully supported removing him. A White House spokesperson confirmed Cairncross is leaving the administration, but his exact last day has not been made public.

The change comes after a difficult week for American technology systems. Outages disrupted Microsoft services, air-traffic routing systems run by the Federal Aviation Administration, banking services at Chase, and internal systems at the National Security Agency, among others. The disruptions have cost hundreds of millions of dollars and rattled air travel, banking and workplaces. Administration officials have said there is no evidence the outages were the result of a coordinated cyberattack.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly congratulated Davies on Thursday, writing that he looked forward to working with her to strengthen American cybersecurity.

The appointment is part of a broader overhaul of Trump's artificial intelligence and technology team. The same day, the president's nominee to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, Michael LoSapio, appeared at his Senate confirmation hearing — a process that has drawn scrutiny of its own.

What happens next is not yet settled. The White House has not said when Cairncross will formally leave, or whether Davies will be formally nominated to hold the cyber director's job permanently rather than in an acting role. Her Senate-confirmed status at the Pentagon is expected to smooth any path to confirmation, but the timing remains unclear.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has ordered makers of every GLP-1 weight-loss and diabetes drug to add a warning about a rare eye condition that can cause sudden, permanent vision loss.

In a drug safety communication issued on Monday, November 9, the agency said it is requiring all glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists — including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound — to carry information about a potential increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, known as NAION, in the Warnings and Precautions section of their labels.

NAION occurs when blood flow to the front of the optic nerve drops, and it can cause sudden, painless vision loss in one eye, sometimes permanently. The FDA describes it as rare and says its underlying cause is not fully understood.

## What the FDA found

The review began after a study published in July 2024 in JAMA Ophthalmology examined the risk of NAION in people prescribed semaglutide. The FDA said that study had limitations, but that the potential risk warranted a closer look, and it undertook a broader evaluation of clinical trial data, animal data and postmarketing reports.

A meta-analysis of 126 clinical trials covering 134,253 patients found 15 probable NAION events — 10 among people taking a GLP-1 drug and five among those on a placebo. That translated to an incidence of 6.4 events per 100,000 person-years in GLP-1 users, compared with 3.8 per 100,000 in people on a comparator drug.

A separate study using the FDA's Sentinel real-world evidence system produced mixed results: it found no statistically significant increase in risk compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors, another class of diabetes drugs, but an increased risk compared with DPP-4 inhibitors.

Importantly, the FDA did not determine that GLP-1 drugs cause NAION. The agency said it found enough evidence of a potential increased risk to require the warning, even though the mechanism — how the drugs might raise the risk — remains unknown. The animal data it reviewed identified no mechanism for the effect.

## Which drugs are affected

The warning applies to the entire class, not just the best-known brands. The FDA's list includes semaglutide products (Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy), tirzepatide products (Mounjaro and Zepbound), liraglutide products (Victoza and Saxenda), dulaglutide (Trulicity), exenatide and the combination products Xultophy and Soliqua, as well as orforglipron (Foundayo), approved earlier this year. NAION cases have been reported for every GLP-1 product currently on the market, the agency said.

Warnings about a possible NAION risk are not new everywhere. European labels for semaglutide products have carried them since June, stating the condition may affect up to 1 in 10,000 people taking the drug.

## What patients should do

The FDA is telling patients to report sudden, painless vision loss, blind spots, tunnel vision, distorted colours or any other visual change in one eye, and it says doctors should refer patients promptly to an eye specialist if NAION is suspected.

The action is a labelling change, not a recall or withdrawal: no product is being taken off the market. Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly, the makers of the leading GLP-1 drugs, have denied a significant link between their products and NAION, and both companies face lawsuits from patients who say the risk was not disclosed to them.

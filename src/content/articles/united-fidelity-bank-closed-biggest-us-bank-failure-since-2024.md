Federal regulators closed United Fidelity Bank, an Evansville, Indiana lender, on Friday evening, in the largest failure of an American bank in more than two years. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency said the bank was in an unsafe or unsound condition and likely unable to pay its obligations, and appointed the Federal Deposit Insurance Corp. as receiver.

The FDIC moved quickly to sell the failed bank. First-Citizens Bank & Trust of Raleigh, North Carolina, agreed to take over substantially all of United Fidelity's deposits and buy substantially all of its assets. Depositors will automatically become First Citizens customers, keep all of their funds and their deposit insurance coverage, and can keep using checks, ATMs and debit cards over the weekend. The bank's 22 branches — in Indiana, Illinois, Florida, Colorado and the U.S. Virgin Islands — will reopen as First Citizens branches, starting Saturday at locations that normally open on Saturdays.

The numbers show how deep the trouble ran. United Fidelity held about $5.9 billion in assets and $5.4 billion in deposits as of September 30. First Citizens agreed to take on $4.5 billion of deposits and $3.1 billion of loans — but only at a steep discount of about $2.6 billion, a sign it expects many of those loans to be troubled. The FDIC estimates the failure will cost its Deposit Insurance Fund $1.83 billion, an unusually large loss equal to about 31 percent of the bank's assets. Some brokered deposits will not transfer to First Citizens; the FDIC did not say how many, or why.

It is the biggest US bank failure since Republic Bank of Philadelphia closed in April 2024 with $6 billion in assets, and the second-largest since the 2023 regional banking crisis that brought down Silicon Valley Bank and First Republic. First Citizens also bought Silicon Valley Bank's bridge bank in 2023. United Fidelity is the seventh US bank failure of 2026, up from two in each of 2024 and 2025 — a step up in number, though not on its own evidence of a wider crisis.

## What is still unknown

Neither the OCC nor United Fidelity has said what caused the bank's failure. The figures in the deal remain subject to adjustment as the FDIC and First Citizens settle the transaction, and the FDIC has not said how many brokered deposits were left behind.

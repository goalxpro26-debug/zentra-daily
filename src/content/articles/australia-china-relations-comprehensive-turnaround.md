China and Australia have completed a "comprehensive turnaround" in their relationship, President Xi Jinping said on Friday, after meeting Prime Minister Anthony Albanese on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

"Since you took office as prime minister in 2022, we have met four times. I think it is fair to say that together we have brought about a comprehensive turnaround in China-Australia relations," Xi said, according to Reuters.

Xi said China was willing to work with Australia to expand cooperation, set aside differences and build on the recent improvements, taking the partnership to a new stage of development. In the meeting he suggested greater cooperation in agriculture, energy, the green economy and artificial intelligence, as well as stronger exchanges between the two countries' peoples, according to China's official Xinhua news agency.

The reset caps a four-year repair job. Ties with China, Australia's top trade partner, soured under the country's previous centre-right coalition government, which called for an inquiry into the origins of COVID-19, angering Beijing. China responded with a series of barriers on Australian exports. It has since lifted many of them, though tensions have flared from time to time — including after a Chinese missile test into the Pacific and Beijing's arrest of Chinese-born Australian nationals on spying charges.

Albanese struck a careful note after the meeting. Differences would be discussed "constructively and directly", he said, and a stable relationship was in the interests of both nations.

"It is important that Australia continues to engage in a constructive way with China, manage our differences, but not be defined by them. And we, today, were able to discuss a full range of issues," Albanese said.

The diplomacy continues in Beijing. Albanese was expected to travel there later on Friday to meet Chinese Premier Li Qiang, accompanied by several top Australian business executives — including Rio Tinto's head of iron ore, Matt Holcz, and BHP's Australia boss, Geraldine Slattery — for a CEO roundtable with Chinese company executives.

But one hard negotiation still hangs over the reset. Australia's top miners are in increasingly fraught talks with China's state iron ore buyer, China Mineral Resources Group, whose centralisation of purchasing increases Beijing's bargaining power in contract negotiations. Australian Resources Minister Madeleine King said this week that any potential manipulation of trade in iron ore would be against international rules and a threat to government revenues.

The language from both leaders marks how far the relationship has travelled since 2022. The iron ore talks, and the differences both sides say they will keep managing directly, show the reset is still being negotiated in practice.

Anthropic has priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed, in one of the largest stock-market debuts on record and the first by a major developer of frontier artificial intelligence models.

The shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol ANT. The company had planned to sell 825 million shares, and a person familiar with the matter told Reuters the size of the offering has increased, though the final share count has not been made public. At $83, the original 825 million shares alone would raise about $68.5 billion, valuing the maker of the Claude chatbot at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

The pricing follows a roadshow that drew demand several times the shares on offer, despite questions about the company's ability to sustain its rapid growth. It is the second mega-IPO this year centred on AI, following SpaceX's record-setting listing in June. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are the lead underwriters, heading a 22-bank syndicate; the same two banks led the SpaceX offering.

Anthropic's growth has been driven by surging demand for its Claude models, particularly Claude Code, its coding agent that has become popular among software developers. Its annualised revenue run rate reached $81 billion as of October 31, up $10 billion from September 30, according to its IPO filing. It had 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 a year as of September 30, up from more than 1,500 at the end of last year.

But sustaining that growth is becoming expensive. Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure in the first half of 2026, nearly four times the amount a year earlier, and incurred a further $5 billion in computing costs in the third quarter. Revenue rose 47 percent in the third quarter to $16.96 billion, while the company reported an operating loss of $567 million, compared with operating income of $974 million in the second quarter.

The company has committed to nearly $900 billion in computing spending with infrastructure partners including Amazon and Alphabet's Google. Its prospectus warns that some of those partners depend on third-party financing to acquire chips and build capacity, and that if such financing became unavailable or more costly, contracted capacity could be delayed, repriced, reduced or not delivered.

The debut is also a test of the AI boom at a more difficult moment for the sector. Chipmakers and cloud companies central to the business have come under pressure in recent weeks over valuation concerns and mounting infrastructure costs. Anthropic's closest rival, OpenAI, is expected to pursue a public listing as early as next year.

Anthropic also faces questions over how it will balance its commercial ambitions with its emphasis on AI safety. Chief executive Dario Amodei has called for the industry to slow the pace of developing and releasing increasingly powerful models, warning that advances in AI could outstrip society's ability to manage the risks.

Anthropic declined to comment on the pricing. Bloomberg News first reported it.

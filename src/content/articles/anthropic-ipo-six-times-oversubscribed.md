Investor orders for Anthropic's initial public offering have reached about six times the shares available, Bloomberg News reported on Thursday, in a sign of intense demand for what could become the largest first-time share sale in history.

The AI company behind the Claude chatbot and some of its existing shareholders are aiming to raise up to $64.4 billion by selling 825 million shares priced between $70 and $78 each, according to the reporting. At the top of that range, the listing would value Anthropic at up to $1.4 trillion — slightly below what some investors had expected, but still a figure that would place the company among the most valuable ever to go public.

Bankers have told prospective investors that Anthropic will give priority to its existing backers when shares are allocated, the report said, citing people familiar with the matter. That means some would-be buyers are likely to receive smaller allocations than they asked for, even with demand running far ahead of supply.

## A test of confidence in the AI boom

The listing is shaping up as a key test of Wall Street's confidence in the artificial intelligence boom. Anthropic is one of the leading developers of large AI models, and its debut comes as investors weigh whether the sector's rapid growth — and the enormous spending behind it — can be sustained.

The company's filing showed a revenue run rate of $81 billion as of October 31, up from $65 billion at the end of July, according to CNBC TV18. A separate private placement of about $26 billion is running alongside the IPO, anchored by cornerstone investors including Alphabet's Google, Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices. Nvidia alone agreed to buy about $10 billion of shares, the filings showed. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering, with 20 other banks taking part.

Shares are expected to start trading on the Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker symbol "ANT."

## What is still unknown

The six-times figure is a snapshot, not a final result. The report cautioned that details of the IPO, including the level of demand, could still change before the shares are priced, and order books in a listing of this size can move quickly in the final days. Anthropic could not immediately be reached for comment on the report.

The final price, the exact allocation of shares and the first day of trading will show whether the early enthusiasm holds — and whether the market is willing to pay a record price for a stake in the AI race.

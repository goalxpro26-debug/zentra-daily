The dinner on Sunday was meant to reward the biggest holders of President Donald Trump's meme coin with an evening at his golf club. For one guest who had bought his way to VIP status, it ended outside in the rain.

Morten Christensen, a Dutch crypto investor who lives in Mexico and runs the website Airdrop Alert, attended the gala at the Trump National Golf Club in Potomac Falls, Virginia, with his sister Gabriëlle Felen. He had been to both previous dinners and said he bought enough of the coin to qualify as a VIP this time, with an email to prove it. But when he tried to enter the area reserved for the top guests, he was turned away — his name was not on the list.

Later in the evening he was approached and told to leave. He said he was told it was because he had been sharing pictures online and was in touch with the media. Organisers did not reply to NPR's email seeking comment. Christensen waited outside in the rain while his sister stayed inside.

Felen said Trump eventually appeared to cheers, but his speech jumped from topic to topic and made few important points, in her view. She was disappointed that VIP guests got a one-on-one selfie with Trump and probably a handshake — an opportunity her brother missed. Christensen said the evening will be his last Trump event.

Gift boxes for guests contained perfumes, a watch and a small golden statue of the president, and dinner was grilled filet mignon or pan-seared halibut. Kevin O'Leary of CNBC's Shark Tank, former boxer Floyd Mayweather and former NBA star Tristan Thompson all gave speeches, while mentalist Lior Suchard performed a trick for the siblings.

The event's website listed 185 qualifying wallets, with the top 29 invited to a VIP reception. It was the third such gala for the coin's top holders. Trump's meme coin, launched just days before his inauguration, has fallen from a peak of over $70 in January 2025 to less than $1, sharply reducing the cost of qualifying. Trump, who effectively has a royalty deal with the company behind the coin, earned more than $600 million from it last year, according to his disclosures.

The dinners have drawn sharp criticism. Anti-corruption activists say they effectively reward the largest coin buyers with access to the president, with the top buyers getting the closest access. Consumer group Public Citizen said about half of those who qualified for Sunday's event appeared to be foreign-based. Prominent Democrats, including Senator Adam Schiff of California and Senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut, have demanded more information about the latest dinner. The White House has defended the events as part of Trump's private business, not his public functions.

The dinner came weeks after corruption was a defining issue in the midterm elections, which handed Democrats control of Congress next year.

The United States is now working to arrange direct engagement between Russia and its European partners, U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said on Saturday, as Washington pushes to keep the Ukraine peace effort moving after a week of shuttle diplomacy.

Kushner said the focus had shifted to setting up talks between Moscow and European governments and preparing the agenda for the next round of three-way negotiations involving Russia, Ukraine and the United States. He made the remarks in a post on X, after he and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff held separate meetings this week with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

The meetings followed the first round of U.S.-brokered talks between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi, which concluded on Wednesday. It was the first direct contact between the two sides since February. The round ended with plans for a prisoner exchange and further talks, but no wider agreement on ending the war, which has now lasted more than four and a half years.

Kushner said the envoys had stayed in close consultation with Russia, Ukraine and European partners since the Abu Dhabi round, and that new ideas from the discussions were being explored. He added that a lasting peace would require credible security guarantees for Ukraine and detailed postwar arrangements to reduce the risk of another war.

## Europe's place in the process

European governments have been pressing for a seat at the table, and the question of their role has been one of the most contested parts of the diplomacy. Russia has long resisted European participation in the main negotiations, though Putin told the U.S. envoys this week that Russia had never objected to pursuing dialogue with Europe.

European leaders moved on Friday in Dublin to select five nations — the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — to send representatives to future rounds of talks, according to TRT World. The move suggests the European side is organising itself for a formal role even as the terms of its involvement are still being negotiated.

Zelenskyy, meanwhile, said on Saturday that American envoys had raised the prospect of Ukraine giving up territory in exchange for NATO membership, TRT World reported. The claim underlines how difficult the substance of any settlement remains, even as the format of the talks expands.

## What happens next

The next round of trilateral talks has not yet been scheduled publicly, and no date has been announced for the direct European-Russian engagement Kushner described. For now, the diplomacy amounts to a framework in search of content: the parties are talking again after a nine-month gap, and the circle of participants is widening, but the central questions — territory, security guarantees and the terms of any ceasefire — remain unresolved.

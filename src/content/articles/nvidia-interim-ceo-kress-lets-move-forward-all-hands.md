Nvidia's interim chief executive, Colette Kress, told employees to move on from the departure of founder Jensen Huang at the company's first all-hands meeting since his resignation, promising pay rises and extra equity in December as the board pushes ahead with finding a permanent leader.

"Let's move forward," Kress told staff at the meeting in Santa Clara on Wednesday, according to people familiar with the matter. The hour-long gathering was held in person and streamed to employees on Microsoft Teams. Kress was joined by Harvey Jones, the newly appointed chair of Nvidia's board, and Ian Buck, who leads the company's hyperscale and high-performance computing business.

Kress moved quickly from her opening message to pay. She told employees they could expect salary increases and additional equity awards in December, months ahead of the company's normal compensation cycle. "The best thing that we want to do is to take care of each and every single employee that is here and to make sure that you feel good about this company and you do not worry about your comp," she said, according to the reports.

Pay was the most common subject among roughly 700 questions employees submitted before the meeting, with board transparency second. Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer since 2013, has spent her first days as interim chief trying to steady a workforce rattled by the sudden exit of the man who built the company into the world's most valuable chipmaker.

Jones used his part of the meeting to repeat the board's position: it unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him. He told staff the board would not settle for a "Jensen clone" and would not compromise its requirements for the sake of speed, while accepting suggestions from employees during the search. The board has retained the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles and says it will consider internal and external candidates.

The message lands against open dissent inside the company. An online petition calling for Huang's return has gathered nearly 200,000 signatures, more than 10,000 of them from people claiming to be current Nvidia employees, and some staff have publicly pressed for his reinstatement. Huang himself said on Tuesday that he would be "honoured" to return. Kress told CNBC the same day that "the board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen."

Huang resigned after reports about his personal conduct, including an admitted affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese intelligence asset, which the board described as a lack of candour. His departure came days after Nvidia reported another record quarter, with third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion and guidance well above Wall Street expectations. Shares fell 2.9 percent on Wednesday, as investors weighed whether the company can sustain its momentum without its founder at the helm.

Kress closed Tuesday's earnings call, her first as interim chief, with an upbeat note: "Our customers and our partners are with us. And employees are committed. We've got this." Wednesday's meeting was the first test of whether employees agree.
